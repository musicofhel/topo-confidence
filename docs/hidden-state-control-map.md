# Hidden-state geometry for inference-time control in small LLMs

The strongest finding in this research program — that 60-dimensional Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) features predict Qwen2.5-1.5B correctness on MATH-500 at 0.811 AUROC and transfer symmetrically to BBH — sits at the convergence of three rapidly maturing literatures: residual-stream trajectory probing, activation steering, and cross-domain probe generalization. The other empirical results (PH ≈ Gaussian null, near-zero TwoNN predictivity, late-layer concentration, length confounds, and the asymmetric D2H transfer) each have direct published analogues, and together they tell a coherent story: **per-layer trajectory geometry is the genuine signal; covariance and global manifold statistics are largely epiphenomenal; and using such signals to close the 49-point Qwen2.5-1.5B → 7B gap on MATH-500 is plausible only when paired with strong verification or trained — not extracted — steering directions**. The planned experiment of repurposing the CoE probe-weight vector as an ITI-style steering vector is a novel test, but the published evidence on probe-weight steering predicts modest gains at best unless re-cast as a difference-of-means or trained bias.

## How CoE features fit the trajectory-probing landscape

The CoE feature definition — per-layer magnitude change ‖h_{l+1}−h_l‖ and angle change ∠(h_l, h_{l+1}) at the final-token position — was introduced by **Wang et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.13640)** as a label-free, training-free aggregator (CoE-R, CoE-C). Across 7 LLMs and 4 task domains, CoE outperformed ITI/MIND/SAPLMA-style detectors by **+8.3 AUROC points on average**, with its largest gains on math reasoning and its central robustness claim being cross-domain stability. The 0.811 AUROC on Qwen2.5-1.5B MATH-500 reported here represents the **trained-classifier extension** of that closed-form score — strictly more flexible than CoE-R/C and consistent with the regime where Wang et al. found math reasoning to give the strongest signal.

Several 2025 papers converge on the same general conclusion that **trajectory-shape information in the residual stream predicts correctness with AUROC in the 0.80–0.90 range on math tasks**. Sun et al. (arXiv:2604.05655) report AUROC up to 0.87 from per-step trajectory features on GSM8K. Zhang et al. (arXiv:2504.05419, NYU) achieve AUROC > 0.9 on AIME with R1-Distill-Qwen-32B using a small MLP probe on chunk-boundary hidden states. "Tracing the Traces" (arXiv:2510.10494) finds Cumulative Change negatively correlates with accuracy at Spearman r ≈ −0.38 on AIME-2025/Qwen3-14B. CoRE (arXiv:2507.06087) extends CoE to step-level for Large Reasoning Models, reducing CoT length by 13–33% while improving accuracy. **The 0.811 number is therefore neither anomalous nor saturated** — Zhang et al.'s 0.9+ ceiling on math suggests room to improve via richer features, layer/token-position selection (Orgad et al. 2025), or better probes.

The closely related **D2H-score** is published as Yang et al. (arXiv:2509.11569, Sept 2025), defining intra-layer dispersion (spread of token embeddings within a layer) and inter-layer drift (attention-weighted trajectory of key tokens) as joint hallucination signals; the within-domain 0.806 AUROC matches the literature's reported single-LLM performance. The closest covariance-based competitor is INSIDE/EigenScore (Chen et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2402.03744), which uses log-determinant of activation covariance over multiple sampled responses — a sampling-based method that beats single-pass probes by 5–9 AUROC points on QA but at 5–15× the inference cost.

## Why CoE transfers symmetrically and D2H does not

The cross-domain transfer asymmetry (CoE: 0.720/0.712 symmetric MATH↔BBH; D2H: 0.740 vs 0.529) maps cleanly onto the **MCI (Misalignment from Correlational Inconsistency) hypothesis** of Marks & Tegmark (COLM 2024, arXiv:2310.06824). Their analysis showed truth-direction probes generalize symmetrically only when the probed feature is the *dominant* correlated signal in both training distributions; asymmetric transfer arises when one dataset has additional confounded features that align with correctness on that domain but not the other. Orgad et al. (ICLR 2025) develop the same point empirically: their per-pair generalization heatmaps are visibly asymmetric, and they conclude **"truthfulness encoding is not universal but rather multifaceted"**.

Several mechanisms predict the specific MATH→BBH success and BBH→MATH failure of the D2H probe. BBH is a heterogeneous mixture of 23 tasks (Suzgun et al., ACL Findings 2023), some symbolic/arithmetic and some natural-language; **a probe trained on BBH must average over feature directions that may not isolate the math-specific correctness signal**, while a MATH-trained probe finds a feature that survives on BBH's symbolic subtasks. Sprague et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2409.12183) reinforce the structural asymmetry: in their meta-analysis of CoT across 100+ papers, gains were +12.3% on math but negligible on most non-symbolic tasks, indicating that the internal computation BBH triggers is bimodal in a way MATH is not.

The **CoE features' symmetric transfer is more striking and is consistent with Wang et al.'s original claim** that per-layer geometric trajectories capture a domain-general "computation regularity" signal — a feature derived from the *form* of layer-to-layer evolution rather than learned correctness directions. Bürger et al. (NeurIPS 2024, "Truth is Universal") provide a constructive related result: a *two-dimensional* truth subspace explains why one-dimensional probes fail on negation while a two-D probe transfers, suggesting that geometric features (M_l, A_l) span a small but generic subspace that survives domain shift. Kossen et al. (arXiv:2406.15927) report a parallel finding: **probes trained on semantic-entropy labels generalize OOD better than probes trained on correctness labels** — a methodological lever worth testing on the D2H asymmetry.

## Persistent homology and the Gaussian-null verdict

The finding that real PH features (AUROC 0.690) are statistically indistinguishable from Gaussian-null PH features (0.693) is **the single most important methodological result in the program**, and it is consistent with — though more direct than — several published cautions. Bobrowski-Borman (2012), Adler-Taylor random-field theory, and Pranav et al. analytically establish that persistent homology of Gaussian random fields has computable Betti number distributions that can dominate observed PH on real data. Bubenik (JMLR 2015) and Robinson-Turner (2017) develop the formal hypothesis-testing apparatus that the program implicitly invokes. Turkeš, Montufar, & Otter (NeurIPS 2022) test PH against simple SVM/distance-matrix baselines on tasks where topology *should* matter, finding PH wins only on hole-counting and curvature targets — **on most other tasks, distance-matrix baselines match it**. Otter et al. (2020) showed neural networks trained on raw data approximate PH features, suggesting PH adds little beyond second-moment statistics. The program's null-model finding therefore strengthens an existing — but rarely conclusively tested — concern: most PH-on-LLM papers (Tulchinskii et al. NeurIPS 2023; Kushnareva et al. EMNLP 2021; Perez & Reinauer 2022; Gardinazzi et al. ICML 2025) report apparent successes without Gaussian baselines.

A key clarification: **Birdal et al.'s (NeurIPS 2021) PH-and-generalization bound** operates on the SGD trajectory in *parameter* space, not on per-input hidden-state point clouds. The program's negative PH result therefore does not contradict Birdal — they are different objects.

The TwoNN intrinsic-dimension result (AUROC 0.407, ID rising from ~1.7 to ~6.1 then declining) is partially anomalous against the literature. Yin et al. (ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.18048) reported that local intrinsic dimension *does* predict truthfulness in Llama-2 7B/13B with 5–8 AUROC points over uncertainty baselines. The discrepancy is best explained by three factors: (a) the program uses vanilla TwoNN whereas Yin et al. use GeoMLE local-ID estimators; (b) Robinson, Dey, & Chiang (arXiv:2504.01002) demonstrate token-embedding spaces are stratified rather than manifold, so single-ID estimates collapse heterogeneous local geometry; (c) Brown et al.'s (ICLR 2023) Union-of-Manifolds Hypothesis predicts global ID averages over heterogeneous regions with low signal-to-noise for fine-grained discrimination. **The layer-wise ID profile (rise to layer 19, then decline) precisely matches the universal "expand-then-compress" pattern documented by Ansuini et al. (NeurIPS 2019), Valeriani et al. (NeurIPS 2023), and Cheng et al. (ICLR 2025)**, so the geometry is real even if it is not predictive of per-instance correctness.

## Late-layer concentration and length confounds

The peak per-layer PH AUROC at layer 24 of 28 (~86% depth) aligns with **Lad, Gurnee, & Tegmark (arXiv:2406.19384)** "stages of inference" — their proposed final two stages, "prediction ensembling" and "residual sharpening," occupy this depth band and are predicted to host maximum task-specific information. Skean et al. (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.02013) found best representations at 70–85% depth via matrix entropy and curvature; Belrose et al.'s Tuned Lens (arXiv:2303.08112) shows prediction depth typically resolves at 80–95% for confident predictions. **The empirical peak at 86% is therefore the textbook location**, and concentrating future probe training on layers 22–26 of the 28-layer Qwen2.5-1.5B (and analogous bands of the 7B) is well-supported.

The output-length confound is similarly well-grounded. **Singhal et al.'s "A Long Way to Go" (COLM 2024, arXiv:2310.03716)** showed a length-only reward reproduces most RLHF improvements, and Saito et al. (NeurIPS 2023 ITIF), Park et al. (ACL Findings 2024 R-DPO), Wu & Aji (COLING 2025), and Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2023) all document length biases in LLM-as-judge and preference systems. For chain-of-thought specifically, **"Between Underthinking and Overthinking" (arXiv:2505.00127) directly confirms the program's observation that shorter outputs correlate with correctness within a fixed question** on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B and DeepScaler-1.5B over GSM8K/MATH. Wu et al. (arXiv:2502.07266) characterize the relationship as inverted-U: accuracy first improves then degrades with CoT length, which together with Sprague et al.'s finding that CoT helps mainly on math/symbolic tasks implies the length-correctness relationship is sample- and task-dependent.

A ~0.050 AUROC drop from length deconfounding is **smaller than typical cross-task probe drops (0.10–0.30) but larger than within-domain noise** — qualitatively similar to LCoT2Tree (arXiv:2505.22148), which reports tree-based features improve over length-only baselines by 5.63%, implying length carries roughly 5% of the predictive signal. No published study reports an exact length-deconfounded AUROC delta at this magnitude; the program's quantification is novel. The standard methodological templates are **LEACE (Belrose et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.03819)** for representation-level erasure, INLP (Ravfogel et al., ACL 2020) as predecessor, and Length-Controlled AlpacaEval (Dubois et al., arXiv:2404.04475) for evaluation-level regression conditioning. Reporting both LEACE and regression-deconfounded AUROCs is the gold standard.

## Probe-weight steering: the literature predicts disappointment

The planned Experiment 1 — using the CoE probe-weight vector as an ITI-style steering vector — has a specific and discouraging history. **Li et al.'s original ITI paper (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.03341) explicitly compared probe-weight directions against mass-mean (difference-of-means) directions and found mass-mean outperformed probe weights**. Marks & Tegmark (2023) confirmed this for truthfulness directions: difference-in-means probes are more causally implicated in model outputs than logistic-regression weights. **AxBench (Wu et al., ICML 2025, arXiv:2501.17148)** ran the head-to-head benchmark across Gemma-2-2B/9B and reported the ranking **prompting > finetuning > difference-in-means > linear probe weights ≫ SAE features**. AdaRAS (arXiv:2601.19847) explicitly tested probing-classifier-based neuron importance and discarded it as "unreliable in practice due to sensitivity to feature selection and dependence on probe generalization," switching instead to mean-difference between correct/incorrect activations to achieve **+13% on AIME-24/25**.

The "No Answer Needed" study (OpenReview 2025) trains exactly the kind of question-time correctness probe the program plans to use and reports a critical caveat: **"generalisation falters on questions requiring mathematical reasoning"** — for trivia/knowledge probes transfer OOD, but for math they don't. This casts direct doubt on the durability of CoE-probe-weight steering for math reasoning, even though the CoE features' geometric (rather than semantic) basis may help.

The most encouraging recent precedent for closing a small-model math gap with steering is **Sinii et al. (EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2505.18706, "Steering LLM Reasoning Through Bias-Only Adaptation")**, which trained per-layer additive biases via RL on Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Qwen-2.5-Math-1.5B. On MATH-train→MATH-test, base 1.5% → full fine-tuning 44.5% → **steering vector 51.4%** (steering exceeded full FT). On GSM8K, steering reached 73.8% vs full FT's 78.9%. Crucially, **the vector was trained from scratch with RL, not extracted from a probe**. The follow-up "Small Vectors, Big Effects" (arXiv:2509.06608) localized the effect to layers 8–15 of Qwen2.5-Math-7B. SEAL (Chen et al., COLM 2025, arXiv:2504.07986) extracted a thought-type mean-difference vector that improves DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B on MATH-500 and GSM8K by up to 11%. **TTSV (arXiv:2512.04748)** optimizes a single test-time steering vector via entropy minimization on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B and Qwen3-4B-thinking, reporting +14.6% relative improvement on average and +69.6% relative on Olympiad Bench.

The tactical implication is sharp: **the probe-weight steering experiment should be run in parallel with mass-mean (correct-vs-incorrect activation difference) and trained-bias baselines**. If the literature pattern holds, mass-mean will outperform probe-weight, and an RL-trained per-layer bias will exceed both.

## Closing the 49-point gap: what is achievable

The Qwen2.5-1.5B → 7B gap on MATH-500 (20.8% → 69.6%, a 48.8-point delta `[256-tok, superseded; 1024-tok rebuild gives 48.6% → 73.2%, a 24.6-point delta]`) defines the program's headroom. The most direct existing evidence comes from **Liu et al.'s "Can 1B LLM Surpass 405B LLM?" (arXiv:2502.06703, Feb 2025)**, which evaluated Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B with compute-optimal test-time scaling using diverse beam search against an external Process Reward Model. **Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + TTS exceeds Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct on MATH-500**, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B + TTS beats o1-preview and o1-mini. The 1.5B → 7B+ gap can therefore be closed entirely at inference, but **the closing is "system-level" rather than "model-level"**: the verifier (typically Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B or Skywork-PRM, both 7B+) carries much of the burden. For the strict question "can a 1.5B alone close the gap to a 7B alone?", the published answer is no: self-consistency, contrastive decoding, DoLa, and current activation-steering methods close ≤15 points of the gap.

DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (Guo et al. 2025, arXiv:2501.12948) reaches **83.9% MATH-500** — exceeding even the user's Qwen2.5-7B baseline — but via SFT on 800K R1-generated traces, which changes weights. rStar-Math (arXiv:2501.04519, Jan 2025) reaches **87.8% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B** with MCTS + Process Preference Model, but trains the PPM via four self-evolution rounds. Snell et al. (arXiv:2408.03314) and Brown et al.'s "Large Language Monkeys" (arXiv:2407.21787) establish the general scaling laws — coverage scales log-linearly with samples for four orders of magnitude — and Setlur et al. (arXiv:2502.12118) prove verifier-based test-time compute is asymptotically optimal vs verifier-free distillation by O(H/n).

For pure activation-steering on small Qwen models specifically, the documented inference-time-only gains are modest: TTSV (+14.6% relative on Qwen3-4B-thinking math), AdaRAS (~+1% on saturated benchmarks, larger on AIME), SAE-feature steering on DeepSeek-Qwen-1.5B ("significant" ~single-digit-to-mid-teens absolute improvements per arXiv:2505.15634), and Cache Steering (Belitsky et al., arXiv:2507.08799) for KV-cache-based reasoning style induction. **These techniques typically deliver 5–15 absolute points on math benchmarks for ~1.5B models, far short of the 49-point headroom**. Combining steering with verifier-based test-time scaling is the most promising route based on current evidence.

## Synthesis and direct implications for the research program

Five concrete implications follow from the literature.

First, **the 0.811 AUROC CoE result is in the published range and likely improvable** — Zhang et al.'s 0.9+ ceiling on AIME with R1-Distill-Qwen-32B suggests headroom from Orgad et al.-style exact-answer-token probing, layer 22–26 concentration, and possibly two-dimensional truth-subspace extraction (Bürger et al.). Combining CoE with semantic-entropy labels (Kossen et al.) is the single best-supported path to better OOD transfer.

Second, **PH null-result publishability is high**: no prior paper has cleanly demonstrated PH-on-LLM features collapse to Gaussian baselines, despite a substantial body of work assuming otherwise. Framing the result as a methodological correction — with reference to Bubenik (2015) and Robinson-Turner (2017) testing — would extend Turkeš-Montufar-Otter (NeurIPS 2022) to a regime they did not cover.

Third, **the asymmetric D2H transfer is best explained by the MCI hypothesis** (Marks & Tegmark 2024) and BBH's known task heterogeneity (Suzgun et al. 2023; Sprague et al. 2025). Rerunning D2H training on BBH subsets restricted to symbolic/arithmetic tasks should improve BBH→MATH transfer if MCI is the right diagnosis.

Fourth, **the planned ITI-style probe-weight steering experiment should expect modest gains** based on Li et al.'s own ablation, AxBench's ranking, and AdaRAS's explicit rejection of probe-classifier-based neuron selection. The strongest version of the experiment runs probe-weight, mass-mean, and trained-bias steering as parallel arms, with the prediction that **mass-mean ≥ probe-weight and that all extracted directions trail Sinii et al.'s RL-trained biases**. Conditional/adaptive multipliers (LITO, CAST, KTS) are essential to mitigate the brittleness Tan et al. (arXiv:2407.12404) document.

Fifth, **closing the full 1.5B → 7B gap with the 1.5B alone at inference is unsupported by current evidence**. The pragmatic frame for the program is whether CoE-derived steering can match or exceed the 5–15 absolute-point gains documented for activation-steering on small Qwen math models, and whether stacking with verifier-based test-time scaling (Liu et al. 2025) can deliver headline improvements that compound rather than substitute. The CoE probe is also an obvious candidate as a *light verifier* in a beam-search pipeline — running each candidate's CoE-AUROC score as a per-example difficulty estimator — which would unite the program's diagnostic and intervention pathways.

The literature, in sum, strongly supports the program's diagnostic findings (CoE strength, PH null, late-layer concentration, length confound) and frames the planned interventions against well-documented obstacles. The novel contribution available is empirical: **a head-to-head test of probe-weight vs mass-mean vs trained CoE-derived steering on the same Qwen2.5-1.5B base for math reasoning, with proper length deconfounding and OOD transfer to BBH, would directly fill gaps in both the steering and probe-transfer literatures**.
