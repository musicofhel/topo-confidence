# Triage brief — 2605.21488 — Equilibrium Reasoners: Learning Attractors Enables Scalable Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21488
Triaged: 2026-05-24

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — mechanism: DoM as static linear probe is wrong; correctness signal is an attractor residual, not a direction.** EqR demonstrates that correctness in iterative models is tightly coupled with *convergence to fixed points* in latent space, measured by the residual ‖f_θ(z;x) − z‖. If the same dynamic applies to autoregressive transformer hidden states (where each layer is an implicit "iteration"), then our L19 DoM direction may simply be a linear projection of an underlying fixed-point residual — and the AUROC would be explained by proximity to a layer-wise attractor rather than by a directional signal per se. Falsifiable by: computing ‖h_{L20} − h_{L19}‖ (layer-to-layer residual norm at L19) and checking whether it predicts correctness with AUROC ≥ 0.7731, which would reduce DoM to a special case of residual convergence.

2. **Refutes F-4 (correct trajectories collapse harder, "asymmetric collapse") — mechanism: asymmetric collapse is not about correct/incorrect but about attractor basin depth.** The paper's landscape taxonomy (§4.2, modes a–d) predicts that trajectories reaching *any* deep attractor will exhibit low residual regardless of correctness. If the correct basin is simply deeper/broader than spurious basins in Qwen-2.5-1.5B, then asymmetric collapse is a basin-geometry artifact, not a correctness-encoding mechanism. Spurious attractors with comparable depth could yield incorrect solutions that also "collapse," breaking the correct/incorrect dichotomy. Falsifiable by: measuring the per-layer residual norm trajectory for correct vs. incorrect problems and checking whether some incorrect problems (e.g. confident-but-wrong) also show strong collapse — if so, F-4's clean dichotomy breaks.

3. **Refutes H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves accuracy) — mechanism: fixed-vector steering is incompatible with attractor dynamics.** EqR shows that correct solutions are *emergent* properties of iterative convergence to attractors shaped by training, not static direction-aligned states. Their Fig. 1 demonstrates that what matters is the *trajectory* toward low-residual states, not any single direction. A fixed steering vector added at one layer cannot meaningfully redirect a multi-layer attractor trajectory — it may move the state away from the attractor basin rather than toward it. This would explain P10's already-documented steering failures (direction-rotation refutes fixed-vector steering). Falsifiable by: applying DoM-direction steering at L19 and measuring whether (a) the residual ‖h_{L20} − f(h_{L19})‖ increases (pushed out of basin) and (b) accuracy doesn't improve, confirming attractor dynamics dominate over directional signal.

4. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) — mechanism: orthogonality is trivial if prefill and final tokens occupy different attractor basins.** The paper shows that different initializations can converge to different attractors (modes b, c). If prefill and final tokens are in fundamentally different dynamical regimes (different "iteration counts" from the model's perspective, since prefill processes all tokens simultaneously while final generates one), they would naturally land in orthogonal attractor basins — the cos=0.046 is not surprising information-theoretic content but simply reflects that two different computational phases have unrelated fixed points. Falsifiable by: showing that intermediate tokens (positions between prefill-mean and final) have DoM directions that smoothly interpolate between the two, or alternatively that they cluster into discrete basins (supporting the attractor interpretation).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core claim — lower fixed-point residual tracks lower prediction error — is isomorphic to our finding that DoM magnitude (projection onto a direction) tracks correctness. The DoM probe could be the linear approximation of a convergence diagnostic. The 0.7731 AUROC may represent partial capture of attractor-convergence information by a linear projection.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse):** EqR's landscape modes (§4.2) provide a formal vocabulary: F-4 describes mode (d) — a "well-aligned landscape" where correct trajectories converge strongly (collapse) while incorrect ones remain trapped in shallow/spurious basins (less collapse). The asymmetry maps directly to basin depth asymmetry.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM):** CoE-60's 60-dimensional trajectory features aggregate layer-by-layer changes — essentially a discretized version of measuring residuals along the trajectory. EqR predicts these should be redundant with single-layer convergence measures when the landscape is well-aligned (mode d), explaining why CoE-60 adds nothing beyond L19 DoM.

- **F-10 (PH adds negative signal beyond covariance):** EqR's attractor framework is purely about convergence/residual dynamics and makes no topological predictions. The attractor view is orthogonal to PH — it operates on trajectory-level dynamics rather than cross-sample geometry. F-10's null result is consistent with: attractor convergence is the real mechanism (captured by covariance/linear probes), and PH measures something else entirely that's uninformative for this task.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):** EqR's adaptive computation mechanism (ACT) learns to predict which problems need more iterations. This maps to H-5: "familiarity" = already in a deep attractor basin after prefill (few iterations needed), "decomposability" = problem requires many iterations to converge. The halting head's prediction is analogous to what DoM might encode.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Fixed-point residual as convergence diagnostic** — Compute ‖f_θ(z; x) − z‖ at each layer, use as proxy for whether the hidden state has converged to an attractor. EqR shows this correlates with task correctness.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (can compute from cached NPZs if we have adjacent layers).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — need L18 and L20 activations to compute the residual at L19, but if we have them cached this is trivial.

- **Depth–breadth Pareto heatmap** — Vary "depth" (iterations/layers) and "breadth" (restarts/samples) and plot residual + error as a 2D heatmap to characterize the attractor landscape.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2h H100 (need multiple forward passes with different random seeds).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires fresh forward passes with multiple random initializations, which isn't how standard autoregressive inference works.

- **Randomized state initialization (RI)** — Sample initial latent states from N(0, σI) instead of fixed initialization to improve coverage over attractor basins.
  *Replication cost*: conceptual adaptation only — not directly applicable to autoregressive transformers.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't control the hidden state initialization in a pretrained autoregressive LLM.

- **Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) with learned halting** — Train a halting head to predict when latent state has converged, enabling variable compute allocation by difficulty.
  *Replication cost*: would require fine-tuning (days of H100 time).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we're probing a frozen model.

- **Path independence metric ΔPI(B)** — Measures how sensitive accuracy is to restart randomness: E_x[|Acc_B(x) − Acc_1(x)|]. Quantifies stability of the attractor landscape.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (adaptation to our setting: measure variance of DoM across different prompt formulations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — adapted as "how stable is DoM across minor perturbations" (paraphrase stability).

- **Top-1 Converged selection** — Under breadth scaling, select the restart with smallest average residual over last L iterations as the prediction.
  *Replication cost*: conceptual — maps to "among K samples, pick the one whose L19 hidden state has lowest ‖h_L − h_{L-1}‖."
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — if we have multi-sample CoT outputs, select by residual convergence rather than by DoM score. Requires multi-sample generation.

## Approaches & framings

- **Attractor landscape as task-metric alignment:** Correctness = favorable attractors; failures = spurious attractors. This provides a vocabulary for explaining why DoM works (it's a convergence proxy) and why PH doesn't (it measures cross-sample geometry, not within-trajectory convergence).

- **Two-axis test-time scaling (depth × breadth):** Depth = more iterations/layers; breadth = more independent restarts. Maps to our regime as: depth = which layer to probe (L19 may be the "convergence point"), breadth = self-consistency / K-sampling. Provides a theoretical justification for why self-consistency (our AUROC-vs-coverage curve) works.

- **Four landscape modes (§4.2):** (a) no correct attractor, (b) correct + spurious coexist, (c) correct but hard to reach, (d) well-aligned. This taxonomy could classify MATH-500 problems: easy problems are mode (d), hard problems might be mode (b) or (c). The D-bucket (F-7) might correspond to problems in mode (b) — the model converges to *something* but it's spurious.

- **Bilevel optimization (Eq. 4):** The implicit differentiation framework (resolvent (I − J_z)^{-1}) connects to spectral properties of the layer-to-layer Jacobian — the eigenvalues of J_z at the fixed point determine attractor stability. This connects to our cov-spectrum finding (F-2 enhancement via eigenvalues).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2605.21488 — Equilibrium Reasoners: Learning Attractors Enables Scalable Reasoning (Huang, Geng, Kolter; CMU, 2026)

**Relevance:** Provides a formal attractor-dynamics framework for understanding when iterative latent computation converges to correct solutions. Their central claim — that fixed-point residual ‖f(z) − z‖ tracks task correctness — is directly analogous to our observation that single-layer prefill geometry (DoM) predicts correctness. The paper offers a mechanistic vocabulary (attractor basins, landscape modes, depth/breadth scaling) that could reframe F-2, F-4, and F-9 as special cases of attractor convergence in the layer dimension of a pretrained transformer.

**Key claim we tested:** Fixed-point residual tightly correlates with task correctness in iterative latent reasoning models (demonstrated on Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Unique).

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — The attractor-convergence hypothesis has not been tested on our frozen autoregressive transformer pipeline. The closest existing evidence is that DoM (a single direction) already captures 0.7731 AUROC, and the cov-spectrum (spectral features) lifts to 0.7928 — which is *consistent* with partial capture of attractor-convergence information, but doesn't prove the attractor mechanism is operating. Full test requires multi-layer residual extraction (P11-FE674).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE670, P11-FE671, P11-FE672, P11-FE673, P11-FE674, H-913, H-914

**Status:** TO TEST
