# Triage brief — 2406.19384 — The Remarkable Robustness of LLMs: Stages of Inference?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19384
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Layer-block redundancy refutes L19's claimed specificity (F-2).** Lad et al. find via CKA that representational similarity is *block-structured* across depth, and that middle layers are highly robust to deletion / swap (≤ 95% top-1 retained). For Qwen 2.5-1.5B (28 layers), L19 sits at 68% relative depth — squarely inside what their framework calls a "Stage 3 prediction-ensembling" block, not a singular feature peak. *Mechanism*: if L19 is one of several layers in the same CKA block sharing similar residual content, the AUROC=0.7731 prefill-DoM signal should appear with negligible degradation at L17, L18, L20, L21. *Falsifiable test*: re-run prefill DoM 5-fold OOF logistic regression on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` activations (we already cache hidden states across all 28 layers in some Stage-2 NPZs) at L15…L23. If AUROC is ≥0.75 across that whole window, "L19 DoM" is really "Stage-3-block DoM" — a stage-level rather than layer-level claim. Currently F-2 implicitly attributes specificity to L19.

2. **Stage 4 (residual sharpening) predicts the orthogonality F-3 reports — collapsing F-3's novelty.** F-3 reports cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 and frames this as a surprising orthogonality. Lad et al.'s framework predicts exactly this: prefill-token activations live in Stage 2/3 (feature engineering / prediction ensembling), while final-token activations live in Stage 4, dominated by *suppression*-neuron output norm growth and entropy collapse onto the predicted token. *Mechanism*: the stages operate on different sub-bases — semantic-feature subspace at prefill, token-identity-suppression subspace at final — so near-orthogonality is the prediction, not the discovery. *Falsifiable test*: project prefill L19 and final-token L19 activations into (a) the top-32 prediction-neuron MLP-out directions and (b) the top-32 suppression-neuron directions (both identifiable by Lad et al.'s `W_U · w_out` kurtosis/skew criterion, ~5h CPU on Qwen 1.5B from saved weights). If prefill loads on prediction directions and final-token loads on suppression directions with cos < 0.1 *between those neuron sets themselves*, F-3's geometric claim reduces to the standard prediction/suppression duality already in the universal-neurons literature.

3. **Sharpness Dimension / breathing universality (F-1) should align with their CKA block boundaries — and may collapse to it.** F-1 claims "dimensional breathing is universal across architectures and scales." Lad et al. test the same five families (Pythia, GPT-2, Phi, Qwen, LLaMA) and find universal block structure with sharp boundaries at the same relative depths. *Mechanism*: if breathing PR(layer) curves co-locate their inflection points with CKA block boundaries, then breathing is a corollary of the discrete-stage architecture — not an independent discovery. *Falsifiable test*: overlay our P11 PR(layer) curves for Qwen 1.5B / 7B against Lad et al.'s CKA heatmap (Figure 4) and KL(deleted) curves (Figure 1). If breathing peaks exactly at their reported Stage 2→3 boundary, F-1 needs to be re-stated as "the cross-architectural breathing curve is a layered re-statement of staged inference," weakening its standalone novelty claim.

4. **Layer-deletion robustness directly stress-tests F-2's *causal* reading.** Lad et al. find 72-95% top-1 retention when arbitrary middle layers are zero-ablated. F-2 is currently *correlational* (AUROC), not causal. *Mechanism*: if zero-ablating L19 in Qwen 2.5-1.5B preserves K=1 MATH-500 accuracy ≈48.6% (i.e., the headline number doesn't move), then L19 is a *readout* layer for correctness, not a *compute* layer. The DoM is a probe of state, not a steering target. *Falsifiable test*: run zero-ablation at L19 only, then L18+L19+L20, on the 500 MATH-500 problems we already have generations for. Compare K=1 accuracy and the prefill DoM AUROC under ablation. If accuracy holds and AUROC collapses, F-2 is purely epiphenomenal — directly bears on H-1 (per-position DoM steering).

5. **WiC probe peak ≠ correctness probe peak — implicit conflation in F-9.** Their WiC probing peaks at the model midpoint (≈50% depth) and *declines* afterward. F-9 reports CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM, treating both as "feature signal." But their finding suggests the L19 (~68% depth) probe should already be in *decline* if it were a semantic-feature probe — yet ours peaks. *Mechanism*: this implies F-2/F-9 are reading prediction-ensembling activity, not semantic features. *Falsifiable test*: run a WiC-style linear probe on Qwen 1.5B residual at L8, L14, L19, L23 to confirm the WiC curve has its midpoint peak in our model. If yes, the L19 correctness signal is post-feature, in the prediction-ensemble regime — a substantive reframing of F-2/F-9.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1** (dimensional breathing universal across architectures, anchor: the universality-across-Pythia/GPT-2/Phi/Qwen/LLaMA claim in P11 exp1_cross_model). Lad et al. test the *same five families* and find universal stages — strong methodological parallel; potential reduction of F-1 to staged inference (Refutation 3).
- **F-2** (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731, OOF 5-fold). L19 / 28 = 68% relative depth = early Stage 3 in their framework. They predict prediction-ensembling-region probes should peak here; refutations 1, 4 challenge specificity / causality.
- **F-3** (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046). Their stage 2/3 vs stage 4 separation predicts this orthogonality (Refutation 2).
- **F-4** (asymmetric collapse at final token). Their Stage-4 residual sharpening + suppression-neuron rise (~last 10% of layers) predicts a directional collapse — *correct* trajectories should show stronger sharpening if suppression-neuron activation correlates with confidence. Bears on whether F-4 is mechanistic or trivial.
- **F-9** (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM). Their decline of WiC probe accuracy past midpoint and rise of prediction-neuron density at ~85% depth implies the layer-trajectory feature has different content per stage — refutation 5.
- **F-10** (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null). Their CKA finds block structure, *not* uniform geometry — but CKA is second-order similarity, PH is first-order topology. Tangentially related; their result doesn't refute F-10 but suggests the block structure is in *correlation* not *shape*.
- **H-13** (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits). Their *subjoiner heads* and early-layer attention-locality analysis directly seed methodology for H-13.
- **H-17** (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical). Their layer-swap commutativity finding is the closest existing test; if attention/MLP order can swap with little harm, RoPE-position-induced rotation is constrained to specific layer roles.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Layer zero-ablation single-layer scan** — run forward pass with layer ℓ output replaced by zeros, sweep ℓ across all layers; record KL(intervened ‖ original), top-1 retention, benchmark accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: 30min H100 on Qwen 1.5B for full 28-layer sweep on 500 MATH-500 problems (we have the harness).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of F-2 causality (Refutation 4).
- **Adjacent layer swap** — exchange execution order of blocks ℓ and ℓ+1, sweep ℓ across layers; same metrics as ablation.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 on Qwen 1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — answers H-17 (RoPE mechanics) and the L19-block-vs-L19-specific question.
- **Layer repetition (sharpening experiment)** — duplicate a contiguous block (e.g. layers 5-7 → 5-5-6-6-7-7) and measure final-layer logit entropy + benchmark.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100; needs care with KV-cache re-use.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — interesting for F-4 confirmation (does extra "sharpening" widen the asymmetric collapse gap?).
- **Prediction-neuron / suppression-neuron identification** — for each MLP, compute `W_U · w_out` per neuron; classify by kurtosis (high) and skew (positive → prediction, negative → suppression).
  *Replication cost*: 5h CPU per model from saved unembedding + MLP-out weights, no forward passes needed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a neuron-level basis for projecting our DoM directions (Refutation 2).
- **WiC linear probe** — train a layer-wise linear probe to detect context-dependent word meaning on the WiC dataset; measure probe accuracy at each layer.
  *Replication cost*: 2h CPU on cached Qwen 1.5B activations; WiC dataset is HF-public.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a baseline curve to compare against the prefill-DoM curve (Refutation 5).
- **Logit lens at every layer + KL/entropy** — apply unembedding to each layer's residual, measure entropy and KL(intermediate ‖ final).
  *Replication cost*: trivial on cached activations (minutes).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — F-4 + F-9 sanity check.
- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) heatmap across layers** — CKA between every pair of layer outputs; reveals block structure.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests Refutation 1 (is L19 in a multi-layer block?).
- **Subjoiner-head identification** — a labelled dataset of "last 4 tokens form a multi-token word" vs. random non-multi-token last-4; record per-head attention to first-of-multi-token-word; identify heads where attention ↑ as position approaches multi-token-word end.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day CPU + dataset construction.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: probably no — orthogonal to current MATH-500 focus.
- **Local-attention-fraction-vs-depth** — fraction of attention each token directs at preceding ≤5 tokens, aggregated over layer.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached attention weights.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — ties to H-21 (D-bucket prefill attention narrowness).

## Approaches & framings

- **Stage-decomposition hypothesis**: argues transformer inference is *not* a flat iterative process but four ordered stages with distinct roles (detokenization → feature engineering → prediction ensembling → residual sharpening). *Intersects F-1 (universal breathing) and F-2 (L19 specificity) by giving them a stage label rather than a layer label.*
- **Robustness-as-redundancy**: layer-deletion tolerance is reframed as evidence for *overlapping computational pathways* / ensembling, not mere parameter excess. *Bears directly on H-13 (per-head attribution) and the entire P11 program — if pathways are redundant, there is no single "L19 circuit."*
- **Block-structured representation**: CKA reveals discrete layer blocks rather than continuous flow. *Directly intersects F-1 by suggesting breathing's "smooth dimensional curve" is artefactual smoothing of underlying step-function structure.*
- **Iterative-inference vs. circuit duality**: the four-stage view positions itself as a unifying framework over both views. *Useful framing for our P10/P11 narrative which oscillates between "iterative collapse" (P10 v1 directions) and "circuit" (L19 DoM) language.*

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **The Pile (1M tokens random subset)** — natural-language pretraining corpus; HF-public. Applicable? yes — but Pile-style next-token prediction is the main signal in their work, not MATH-500 reasoning. Our 1024-tok regime with reasoning prompts is a distinct distribution.
- **WiC (Word-in-Context)** — small (HF-public, ~5K pairs); applicable? yes — natural-baseline probe to compare against prefill DoM (Methodology #5).
- **HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, LAMBADA** — for ablation-survival benchmark. Applicable? partial — not our MATH-500 distribution, but useful sanity-check for the layer-ablation methodology before applying it to MATH-500.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models tested: Pythia (410M / 1.4B / 2.8B / 6.9B; 24-32 layers parallel-attn-MLP), GPT-2 (small/medium/large/XL; 12/24/36/48 layers sequential), Phi-1/Phi-1.5 (24)/Phi-2 (32), LLaMA 3.2 (1B/16, 3B/28), Qwen 2.5 (0.5B/24, 1.5B/28, 3B/36).
- **Qwen 2.5-1.5B = 28 layers**; L19 = 68% relative depth; their framework places 68% in Stage 3 (prediction ensembling), with prediction-neuron density peaking at ~85% (≈L23-24).
- Pre-processing: fold layer-norm into following weights; center unembedding; center MLP-out writing weights (their App. A.11).
- Zero-ablation rather than mean-ablation, "to maintain consistency with swap order."
- Prediction-vs-suppression neuron classifier: kurtosis(`W_U · w_out`) high; skew positive → prediction, negative → suppression.
- Sharpening experiment: repeat block of 5 contiguous layers once.
- All 16 models replicate the 4-stage signature.
- Phi-2 has the highest prediction-neuron density (~15%) and suppression density (~25%) — 5-8× GPT-2, 3-7× Pythia. Suggests stage-density is architecture-dependent, not just depth-relative.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Logit-lens entropy curve at L0…L27 on cached Qwen 1.5B prefill activations** — we have the residual stream cached for all layers in `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` Stage-2 NPZs (or can regenerate cheaply); apply unembedding, take entropy. Verifies their Stage 2→3 entropy-decline boundary in our specific model.
- **CKA heatmap of L0…L27 prefill activations** — same cached activations, ~1h CPU, immediately tells us whether L19 is a singular layer or one of several in a block (Refutation 1).
- **Single-layer DoM AUROC sweep across L0…L27** — same OOF 5-fold logistic regression as F-2, but at every layer rather than just L19. We almost certainly cached only L19, but Stage-1 NPZs in pathway 8/9 may have full-layer data; if not, one full pass of Qwen 1.5B on the 500 prompts to extract-then-discard is 30min H100.
- **Prediction- vs suppression-neuron classifier on Qwen 1.5B saved weights** — no forward pass needed; just load the unembedding and per-layer MLP `w_out` and compute the kurtosis/skew per neuron. Gives us a neuron-level basis for cross-checking F-3 (Refutation 2).
