# Triage brief — 1912.02164 — Plug and Play Language Models: A Simple Approach to Controlled Text Generation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02164
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the practical implication of F-3 (prefill / final-token DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046) that a *fixed* steering direction is the right object to steer with.** PPLM does not maintain a fixed direction at all — at every generation step it re-runs gradient ascent on `Δ Hₜ` (the cached K/V history) using the attribute classifier `p(a|x)`, with 3–10 inner update steps and step size α normalized per layer. If a *re-derived-per-token* gradient direction outperforms a fixed mean direction on attribute control, then by extension a re-derived-per-token correctness gradient could outperform the static L19 prefill DoM. **Mechanism:** the cos = 0.046 between prefill_DoM and final_DoM is interpreted in F-3 as evidence that a single direction misses the object the model is actually using; PPLM operationalizes the alternative — track the gradient instead. **Falsifiable test:** compute, per token, ∇H₁₉ log p(correct | H) using a probe trained on F-2's labels; sample with PPLM-style updates and measure K=1 MATH-500 accuracy lift versus static-DoM steering at matched α and matched perplexity penalty. If gradient steering ≤ static DoM, F-3's practical implication survives; otherwise the project should switch from "characterize a direction" to "characterize a vector field."

2. **Refutes H-1's premise that "per-position DoM steering moves accuracy" via *one* direction.** H-1 plans to apply a single (or per-layer) DoM as the steering vector. PPLM's central design choice — that *no* fixed direction is needed once you have a `p(a|x)` classifier and access to `Hₜ` — means the relevant baseline for H-1 is not "static DoM-α sweep" but "static DoM vs gradient-of-classifier vs attribute-LL-gradient at matched compute." If the project runs only the static-DoM arm of H-1 it cannot conclude DoM is doing useful work; it has merely confirmed *some* steering signal exists. **Mechanism:** mass-mean / probe-weight directions are special cases of low-rank approximations to the classifier gradient, but they are not equivalent — they ignore curvature and per-example structure. **Falsifiable test:** add a "PPLM-arm" to H-1's existing three steering arms (probe-weight, mass-mean, trained-bias). At matched fluency/KL budget, measure MATH-500 accuracy. If the PPLM arm is no better than mass-mean, the orthogonality story is robust; if it is, the static-direction framing is incomplete.

3. **Refutes the implicit "selectivity at 50% coverage = 71.6%" ceiling (F-8) as a property of the prefill probe rather than of the input distribution.** F-8 reports selective-prediction accuracy 71.6% on answered using the static prefill-DoM threshold. PPLM-style hidden-state perturbation suggests there is a third option besides "answer / abstain": *intervene*. If you can detect a likely-wrong prefill (low prefill DoM logit) and then push `H_prefill` along ∇log p(correct | H) before continuing generation, the relevant question is no longer "answer or not" but "what fraction of the abstained set becomes correct after a controlled perturbation?" **Mechanism:** PPLM shows hidden-state perturbations of bounded KL produce coherent attribute-conditioned text; the same machinery should work for correctness if a correctness classifier exists at L19. **Falsifiable test:** on the lowest-DoM-decile of MATH-500 (which F-8 abstains on), apply m=3 PPLM steps targeting `p(correct|H_prefill)` with γ_gm = 0.9 and λ_KL = 0.01, then continue greedy generation. If post-intervention K=1 accuracy on that decile rises above the unperturbed baseline by more than the KL-regularized perplexity-penalty noise floor, F-8's "abstention is the only option" framing is incomplete.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — PPLM operates on `Hₜ` (the K/V cache, equivalent to all-layer prefill key-value pairs) with gradient updates from a single learned linear layer (~1K params). The L19-DoM probe in F-2 is exactly such a single-layer classifier on a single-layer activation; PPLM's recipe is "use that probe's gradient to perturb the corresponding K/V entry." Numerical anchor: 0.7731 AUROC says the probe is informative enough that gradient ascent on it should produce *some* shift; the open question is whether the shift compounds into accuracy lift after KL/post-norm regularization.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — PPLM circumvents this orthogonality entirely by perturbing `Hₜ` at every step (not at one privileged position), so the "which direction at which token" question becomes "what gradient at this exact step." Whether the project should follow PPLM here is the live question of H-1.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves accuracy)** — direct prior art for a non-trivial baseline arm. PPLM 345M GPT-2 with α ∈ [0.02, 0.04], γ ∈ [1.0, 1.5], m ∈ [3, 10], λ_KL = 0.01, γ_gm ∈ [0.8, 0.95] is the hyperparameter search box.
- **F-8 (selective-prediction 71.6% at 50% coverage)** — refutation #3 above promotes PPLM-style perturbation from a controlled-text trick to a candidate intervention pathway for the abstained set.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive geometric signature)** — PPLM-style perturbation gives a causal probe: if D-bucket prefills can be pushed along ∇p(correct | H_19) and the K=1 generation flips to correct, the geometric signature was prescriptive, not just diagnostic.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Hₜ-space gradient ascent on log p(a|H)** — single-step or m∈[3,10] inner updates of `Δ Hₜ ← Δ Hₜ + α · g/‖g‖^γ` where `g = ∇_{Δ Hₜ} log p(a|Hₜ + Δ Hₜ)`, with `Hₜ` = all-layer K/V cache up to step t. Per-layer normalization. *Replication cost*: 4h H100 once we have a frozen L19 correctness probe (F-2 already provides one). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's the natural "PPLM-arm" of H-1 / P10-FE1.
- **KL-divergence regularizer to unconditional LM** — adds `λ_KL · KL(p̃_t‖p_t)` to the steering objective during the inner ascent; λ_KL = 0.01 in their main results. *Replication cost*: trivial, ~10 lines on top of the steering loop. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — fluency guard for any steering arm we add.
- **Post-norm geometric-mean fusion** — at sampling time, draw from `β⁻¹ · p̃_{t+1}^{γ_gm} · p_{t+1}^{(1−γ_gm)}` rather than from `p̃` alone, with γ_gm ∈ [0.8, 0.95]. Decouples steering strength from fluency. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be standard for any decode-time intervention we publish.
- **BoW attribute model = sum of indicator log-probs** — `log p(a|x) = log Σ_{w∈BoW(a)} p(w|x)`. The BoW arm needs zero training. *Replication cost*: trivial; we don't have an obvious BoW analogue for "correctness," so probably not directly useful — but a *PANL-token* BoW (post-answer-newline tokens that correlate with correctness markers) is a cheap experiment. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if the discriminator-arm fails.
- **Discriminator attribute model = single linear layer on mean-pooled hidden states** — linear classifier on top of frozen LM mean-pooled embeddings, ~1K params, trained on labeled data (SST-5 in their paper). *Replication cost*: 5min CPU; we already have this for L19 (F-2 probe). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it *is* our F-2 probe.
- **Window-of-recent-past update** — to save compute, only the latents in a recent token window get the gradient update (dotted-red region in their Figure 1). *Replication cost*: a config knob. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — at 1024-tok regime, restricting the update to e.g. the last 64 tokens makes the difference between feasible and infeasible.
- **Reranking baseline** — generate r unconditional samples, rerank by `p(a|x)`. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is best-of-N on the correctness probe, a useful PPLM-baseline baseline.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Steering = gradient on H, not movement along a fixed v"** — PPLM's central reframing. For our project this is the alternative null to F-3: "the reason prefill_DoM and final_DoM look orthogonal is that we're approximating two slices of one nonlinear gradient field with two linear directions."
- **"p(x|a) ∝ p(x) · p(a|x) sampled by Langevin in latent space"** — Bayesian framing makes the KL penalty principled rather than ad hoc; relates F-2's probe to a generator p(x|correct).
- **"Tiny attribute model + frozen big LM"** — orthogonal axis in the steering literature: scale of the controller versus scale of the controlled. Our F-2 probe is already on the tiny-controller side; PPLM validates this design choice empirically (1K-param linear discriminator suffices to steer 345M GPT-2).
- **"Multiple controllers compose at inference"** — sum log-gradients from independent attribute models. For us this means stacking a correctness probe with, e.g., a refusal probe (H-12) at decode time without any joint training.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Reference implementation:** https://github.com/uber-research/PPLM (PyTorch, GPT-2 medium 345M).
- **Hyperparameters used in main paper experiments:**
  - Step size α ∈ {0.02, 0.03, 0.04} (per-layer normalized)
  - Inner update steps m ∈ {3, 10}
  - Norm-power γ ∈ {1.0, 1.5} (denominator exponent in update)
  - λ_KL = 0.01 (works "in general across tasks")
  - γ_gm ∈ {0.8, 0.9, 0.95} (post-norm fusion strength)
  - Window size: short window of recent tokens, exact value not in body — see code.
- **The "weighted-embedding gradient trick"** — to backprop through the discrete-token bottleneck, they use a soft, weighted-sum embedding for the BoW objective rather than discrete sampling. (Cited in author-contribution footnote: "engineered how to obtain workable gradients via the weighted embedding approach.")
- **Per-layer normalization** of the gradient denominator `‖g‖^γ` — important; without it deeper layers dominate updates. Code shows independent normalization per transformer layer.
- **Gotcha:** PPLM does *not* update model weights, but it does require a backward pass through the LM at every generation step ⇒ ~5–15× slower than vanilla decoding at m=3–10.

## Replicable intermediates

- **PPLM-arm of H-1 (= P10-FE1's fourth arm).** We already have: F-2's L19 prefill DoM probe (the discriminator), Pathway 11 1024-tok prefill activations (cached NPZs in `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`), and Qwen-2.5-1.5B on H100. Smallest cross-check that fits in cached data: take the existing prefill activations, simulate a one-step PPLM update *offline* on the cached `H_prefill` for the lowest-DoM-decile (F-8 abstained set), re-run the *single forward pass* through L20–L27 (no new generation), and ask the probe at L19 again — if the probe AUROC after one PPLM step is meaningfully higher than before, the gradient direction is at least locally informative. This is a sanity check we can run on existing data without re-generating any tokens.
- **Reranking baseline.** Already implementable on cached K=8 samples (where they exist) — for each problem, score the 8 samples by F-2's prefill DoM probe and pick argmax; compare K=1-greedy accuracy to "best-of-8 by probe." This is a free baseline that should be run before any gradient-steering experiment.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 1912.02164 — Plug and Play Language Models: A Simple Approach to Controlled Text Generation (Dathathri, Madotto, Lan, Hung, Frank, Molino, Yosinski, Liu, ICLR 2020)

**Relevance:** Canonical prior art for the entire activation-steering family that includes ITI (2306.03341), ALS (2509.18116), Brain-Grounded Axes (2512.19399), and our own H-1 / P10-FE1. PPLM does *not* learn a steering direction; it uses gradient ascent on a tiny attribute classifier's log-likelihood w.r.t. the K/V cache `H_t`, with KL regularization to the unconditional LM and post-norm geometric-mean fusion to keep fluency. Directly relevant as the gradient-based alternative to our static-DoM approach: in our terms, "steer with ∇_H log p(correct|H), not with mean(H_correct) − mean(H_incorrect)."

**Key claim we tested:** A 1K-parameter linear discriminator on frozen-LM hidden states is sufficient to steer generation when used via per-token gradient ascent on the K/V cache (with KL/post-norm fluency guards). In our terms: "F-2's probe gradient should outperform F-2's mean-difference direction at correctness steering."

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — H-43 (full PPLM-arm of P10-FE1, BLOCKED on H100 pod) and the cheaper gating sanity checks H-44 (offline single-step gradient on cached prefills) and H-45 (best-of-K reranking by F-2 probe) make the prediction concrete and runnable. Until those experiments report, PPLM is prior art that *predicts* a gradient-steering arm should beat any static DoM arm; F-3's cos = 0.046 is the strongest piece of indirect evidence either way.

**Related experiments:** P10-FE71, P11-FE71, P11-FE72, H-43, H-44, H-45; method-comparison neighbor of P10-FE1.

**Status:** TO TEST
