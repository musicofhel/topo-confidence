# Triage brief — 2604.05655 — LLM Reasoning as Trajectories: Step-Specific Representation Geometry and Correctness Signals

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05655
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Lihao Sun, Hang Dong, Bo Qiao, Qingwei Lin, Dongmei Zhang, Saravan Rajmohan (Microsoft, ACL 2026 Main)
Code: https://github.com/slhleosun/reasoning-trajectory

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731) is allegedly *better than* final-token DoM (0.7186).**
   Sun et al. measure AUC on Llama-3.1-8B GSM8K and find the opposite hierarchy: late-step trajectory features = 0.87, **final-marker activation alone = 0.81**, but the **early-step feature (Step 1→2 transition only) = 0.61–0.63 — barely above chance.**
   *Mechanism for refutation*: their "early-step" activation is structurally analogous to our "prefill" — it captures state before the model has generated any reasoning content. If, on Llama-3.1-8B with a controlled CoT prompt, the prefill state is uninformative for correctness, then F-2's "prefill > final-token" hierarchy on Qwen-2.5-1.5B is a model-specific artifact, not a general property of transformer geometry.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Replicate Sun et al.'s probe construction on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with Qwen prompts ALSO frozen-extracted (extract activations at the *final question token before "Step 1:"*), train an ℓ2-regularized logistic regression with PCA-128 features, and report layer-wise AUROC on a 256-tok-equivalent label set. **If best-layer AUROC < 0.65 on Llama-8B**, F-2's universality is refuted.

2. **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) directly contradicts Sun et al.'s headline.**
   They explicitly show: "the strongest predictors are not raw activations but trajectory-based Step-difference features." Late-step trajectory features (concatenated final-marker + last-step transition, PCA dim=128) hit AUC 0.87 vs final-marker alone at 0.81 — a +0.06 AUC margin attributed to the transition geometry.
   *Mechanism*: F-9 was decided by comparing CoE-60's *layer-wise sequence* against single-layer DoM on the same MATH-500 1024-tok labels. Sun et al.'s comparison is along an *orthogonal axis* — *step-wise* trajectory differences within a single layer. Step-wise trajectory features may carry information that layer-wise CoE features do not.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Build "step-difference features" on cached Pathway 11 Qwen-1.5B Stage 2 activations: locate "Step k:" token boundaries in the K=1 generations, extract h(L19) at each boundary, take pairwise differences for the last two transitions, concatenate with PANL/final-marker, PCA-128, train OOF logistic. If AUROC > 0.81 (= our final-token + 0.04 margin matching their lift), F-9 needs revision: trajectory features beat single-layer DoM along a different axis from the one we tested.

3. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, "directions are orthogonal") implies a discontinuous jump, but Sun et al.'s data implies a *gradual* late-stage divergence.**
   They show distance differences accumulate monotonically: Step1→2 Euclidean Δ(I−C) = 0.28 (NS), Step(N−1)→N = 4.26 (sig), Step N→answer-marker = 13.39 (sig). The signal *grows* with step number rather than appearing only at the final-token boundary.
   *Mechanism for refutation*: F-3's interpretation of orthogonality between two endpoint states is consistent with two readouts of a smooth rotating direction at distant phases of generation. If we extract DoM at every "Step k" boundary inside a Qwen-1.5B trajectory and find DoM(k) rotates *smoothly* through SO(d), then F-3's "two distinct circuits" framing is a misreading of a continuous rotation — not two orthogonal subspaces but one rotating axis.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: On cached Pathway 11 generations, locate every Step-marker boundary, extract h(L19), compute step-indexed DoM(k) on a held-out fold, then compute cos(DoM(k), DoM(k+1)) for k = 1..K_max. If consecutive cosines are 0.6–0.95 (smooth rotation) rather than ~0 (orthogonal jumps), F-3's circuit-orthogonality framing is refuted in favor of smooth rotation. (This also speaks to H-17.)

4. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is *partially confirmed* in their data but with a much weaker effect than implied — possibly refutational at our planned magnitudes.**
   Their PROLONG-MID GSM8K-trained termination steering vector applied unchanged to MATH-500 yields **+1.80% accuracy** (36.40 → 38.20). At |α| ≤ 0.4 length-control they observe ≤1% accuracy change. Above |α| > 0.8 they observe **mode collapse / repetitive loops**.
   *Mechanism for refutation*: H-1 currently posits that DoM steering in our 1.5B/7B setting will produce a clear correctness gain. The paper's data suggests a small (<2%) gain on MATH-500 with mid-layer steering and a narrow viable α window before destabilization. If we observe >2% on Qwen, that's confirmation; if we observe ~0% or destabilization at α matching their reported viable range, H-1 is weakly refuted.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Implement the SHORTEN/PROLONG steering with s = E[h_term − h_step_k] computed from cached Qwen-1.5B Stage 2 activations, sweep |α| ∈ [0.1, 0.8] in steps of 0.1, applied to last 5 vs middle 5 layers, evaluate on MATH-500. The H-1 prediction now has a calibrated reference point.

5. **F-7 (D-bucket has a *collective*, not per-problem, geometric signature) faces a competing per-problem trajectory account.**
   Sun et al.'s steering helps most on 6-step (+7.60) and 7-step (+7.69) problems with ≥97% preserve rate, while preserving accuracy on shorter chains. Their "ideal trajectory" is a *step-indexed* mean μ_j with per-step σ_j, and intervention fires per-problem when local OR cumulative deviation exceeds threshold.
   *Mechanism for refutation*: F-7 was discovered as a population-level density anomaly. If their per-problem cumulative deviation D_j on D-bucket members of MATH-500 (the 6/500 problems where K=1 right but K=8 wrong) cleanly identifies them with the same recall as the collective PR/density measure, F-7's "must be analyzed collectively" framing is refuted: D-bucket geometry is per-problem-detectable, just along an axis (cumulative trajectory deviation) we hadn't tested.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: For the 6 D-bucket members, project Stage-2 activations into a step-by-step PCA-128 subspace using their construction (μ_j = step-wise mean over correct GSM8K-subset trajectories, σ_j threshold), compute D_j and δ_j per problem, ask whether D-bucket members have outlier (D_j, δ_j) compared to A/B/C buckets. If yes, F-7's collective-only framing is wrong.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on MATH-500)** ↔ Their MATH-500 *transferred* AUC = 0.73 from a GSM8K-trained predictor. Suggestively close to our number, indicating that our prefill probe sits near the cross-task transfer ceiling for current methods. Their *in-distribution* GSM8K probe = 0.87. **Implication**: a fairly trained MATH-500-in-distribution probe with their late-step features (vs our prefill) could push AUROC well above 0.7731. (Read FINDINGS.md F-2 to confirm prefill protocol.)

- **F-3 (orthogonality, cos = 0.046)** ↔ Their finding "early reasoning steps follow highly similar paths for both correct and incorrect, whereas late-step transitions systematically diverge" is mechanistically consistent with prefill carrying minimal correctness signal in their setup. But — see Refutation #1 — they did not run the prefill probe specifically.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse: correct trajectories collapse harder at the final token)** ↔ Their Euclidean Δ(I−C) = −13.39 at last-step → answer-marker, with **incorrect trajectories having SMALLER step-to-answer-marker movement** (101.62 vs 115.01). This is opposite-signed to F-4's "correct collapse harder" framing and warrants re-interpretation: in their data, correct trajectories move *more* in the final transition. Strong methodological cross-check needed before either claim is reified.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — see Refutation #5.

- **F-8 (Selective-prediction at coverage 0.5 → 71.6% on Qwen-1.5B)** ↔ Their gated interventions fire on 12.3% of examples with up to +35.4% improvement over always-on; same architecture (predict-then-intervene) but their predictor is GSM8K-trained, our coverage-50 results are MATH-500. Their predictor +0.06 AUC gain (trajectory vs final-marker) is the gain margin we'd convert into selective-prediction lift.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM)** — see Refutation #2.

- **F-10 (PH on trained residuals = Gaussian null)** ↔ Their step-specific *linear* separability reaches 0.99+ at deep layers; this is consistent with F-10 because linear structure does not imply non-trivial topology. Mildly **corroborative** of F-10 (the relevant signal is linear and discoverable by linear probes — no PH needed).

- **H-1 (DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — see Refutation #4. **Partial direct evidence**: their PROLONG-MID +1.80% on MATH-500.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** ↔ They explicitly defer this as future work ("Bridging trajectory-level observations with circuit-level interpretability — identifying which attention heads and MLP neurons implement the observed dynamics — remains an important open direction"). Independent confirmation of relevance.

- **H-15 (length-band PR control validates asymmetric collapse)** ↔ **Strong direct evidence**: Their length-balanced resampling test takes trajectory features from 0.852 to 0.847 (−0.005), confirming the predictor signal is not driven by length. We can now adopt their length-balanced resampling protocol as the H-15 control design.

- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed)** — see Refutation #3. Their step-by-step distance growth is the dataset on which to test smooth-rotation predictions.

- **H-22 (compressed/qualitatively different breathing under abstract-CoT)** ↔ Their length-control via SHORTEN steering at moderate |α| changes length without harming accuracy (≤1% loss for |α|≤0.4), suggesting reasoning can be compressed in representation space without correctness loss in a controlled regime — relevant but indirect.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Step-marker activation extraction** — Extract h(ℓ)_t at the token immediately preceding each "Step k:" marker and the termination marker (e.g., "####"). Captures state at decision boundaries.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached Pathway 11 generations (need to re-locate boundary tokens; activations already cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives us a step-indexed view of L19 activations on MATH-500, naturally extending Pathway 11.

- **Step-identity linear probe** — Binary one-vs-all LR classifier per step, layer-wise. Trains step-k vs all-other-step activations. Used to certify step-specific subspace separability.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sanity-checks whether Qwen-1.5B exhibits the same step-organized geometry. Connects to F-3 / H-17.

- **Cross-model transfer of step probes** — Train step probe on Model A, evaluate on Model B activations. Measures whether step-specific organization is shared across post-training regimes.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU once both models' step-marker activations are cached.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for Qwen-1.5B vs Qwen-7B comparison — we already have both extractions.

- **Trajectory distance features (Euclidean + cosine, between consecutive steps)** — d(h(L)_step_k, h(L)_step_(k+1)) at the final layer, paired by correctness label. Compares early-step vs late-step distance differences as correctness signal.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct probe of whether "late-step divergence" generalizes to Qwen-1.5B MATH-500.

- **Concatenated trajectory features for correctness probe** — Concatenate (h_termination_marker) ⊕ (h_step_(N−1) − h_step_N), apply PCA dim=128, train ℓ2-LR. Their headline 0.87 AUC method.
  *Replication cost*: 2h CPU on cached Pathway 11 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — tests F-9 directly (refutation #2).

- **Logit-lens correctness baseline** — At step boundaries: entropy, answer-marker token rank, top-1 probability. Their reported best AUC = 0.765 on GSM8K.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a low-cost baseline for our 0.7731 prefill AUROC.

- **In-sample steering vector construction** — s(ℓ) = E_k[h(ℓ)_term − h(ℓ)_step_k] computed within each prompt, averaged over training prompts. Used as termination steering direction.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly relevant to H-1 and provides a cleaner construction than cross-example DoM.

- **Trajectory-based steering with ideal-trajectory PCA** — From correct-only training trajectories, compute PCA-projected step-wise mean μ_j and dispersion σ_j. At inference, project current activation, compute local deviation δ_j and cumulative D_j; if either exceeds optimized step-specific threshold, apply low-rank steering update toward μ_j.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day (requires inference-time hooks).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for the Qwen-1.5B reproduction — high-value but pod-blocked.

- **Length-balanced resampling for predictor evaluation** — Resample examples to balance step count between correct and incorrect classes; verify predictor AUC barely drops. Used as their length-confound control.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly addresses H-15 framing.

- **Format-robustness probe** — Train on fixed-form ("Step X:") activations, evaluate on freeform ("\n\n"-separated paragraphs, numbered lists, etc.). Tests whether step probes capture reasoning vs surface formatting.
  *Replication cost*: 4h CPU + small extraction run.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — relevant for prefill-DoM generalization across prompt formats.

## Approaches & framings

- **"CoT generation as a trajectory through representation space."** Frames per-token decoding as sequential geometric motion rather than separate per-step computations. Intersects directly with H-17 (RoPE rotation framing) and our breathing/PR temporal curve framing — they treat the trajectory as a *PCA-projected step-indexed path*, while we treat it as a *layer-wise PR curve at fixed token position*. The two views are orthogonal and complementary.

- **"Ideal trajectory" as a per-step distribution rather than a fixed direction.** Reframes DoM/steering from "single learned direction" to "step-indexed mean with dispersion bands." Intersects H-1 (now a per-step DoM hypothesis), H-17 (now a smoothly-rotating reference path), and our F-3 orthogonality framing (now possibly two endpoints of the same path).

- **"Late-step divergence" as the locus of correctness signal.** Reframes correctness prediction from "any-state probe" to "trajectory-late-region probe." Intersects F-2 / F-8 / F-9: forces us to ask whether the prefill-L19 signal is the late-divergence signal extracted at an unusual token (the question prefill might already encode the eventual late-step divergence direction).

- **"Training reshapes depth-of-convergence, not new representations."** Sun et al. argue Base / Instruct / R1-Distill share linear subspace structure; distillation just moves termination structure earlier in layer depth. This is a strong corroborator for F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across architectures) and prediction for H-10 (breathing changes after fine-tuning) — the prediction would be: fine-tuning shifts where breathing peaks but not the breathing structure itself.

- **MATH-500** — 500 questions. License: MIT (Hendrycks). HF accessible. Applicable? **yes** — our canonical 1024-tok benchmark; their probe transfers here at AUC 0.73 (vs our 0.7731).
- **MMLU validation** — 1,531 questions. License: MIT. HF accessible. Applicable? **partial** — knowledge-intensive, less step-structured; their probe transfers at AUC 0.64. Only relevant if H-22 (abstract reasoning) is pursued.
- **Freeform-prompted GSM8K** — Same questions, minimal CoT prompt without "Step X:" formatting. Constructed by them. Applicable? **yes** — tests format-robustness of any probe we replicate.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Extract activations at the token *immediately preceding* the Step marker (i.e., last token of step-k content), not the Step marker itself. Boundary detection uses tokenizer string match.
- Linear probe: ℓ2-regularized logistic regression on PCA dim=128 features. Reported on a held-out test split.
- Three random seeds for AUC stability — they report single-seed curves but seed-averaged best-layer values in Table 3 (0.852 ± 0.039).
- Steering applied at MID = middle 5 layers OR LAST = final 5 layers, with strength α ∈ [−0.05, 0.8] swept; |α| > 0.8 induces mode collapse.
- Gated intervention fires when the predictor flags (top fraction tunable; they use ≈12.3% flag rate for 35% improvement gain).
- Code: https://github.com/slhleosun/reasoning-trajectory.
- Models: Llama-3.1-8B Base / Instruct / R1-Distill (Llama-3.1-8B distilled by Deepseek). Single architecture family — explicitly noted as a limitation. Cross-architecture replication is open.
- Step-count distribution after R1-Distill (their Appendix H, not pasted here) confirms the "faster convergence" observation refers to layer-depth, not output token count.

## 2604.05655 — LLM Reasoning as Trajectories: Step-Specific Representation Geometry and Correctness Signals (Sun et al., 2026)

**Relevance:** Closest sibling paper to Pathway 11. **Key claim we tested:** "Late-step trajectory features predict final-answer correctness pre-emission with ROC-AUC 0.87 (vs 0.81 for final-marker alone, vs 0.63 for early-step features)."
