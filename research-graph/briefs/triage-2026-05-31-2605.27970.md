# Triage brief — 2605.27970 — Geometry of Human Perceptual Domains Emerges Transiently in LLM Representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27970
Triaged: 2026-05-31

## Refutations

**R1 — Transient-emergence pattern challenges L19 localization (F-2)**
The paper finds that geometric structure in residual streams follows a universal arc: weak/diffuse in early layers → organized in intermediate → attenuated in later layers. For Qwen-3-4B/8B models, peak alignment occurs in early-to-middle layers. Our F-2 correctness signal peaks at L19 (layer 19 of 28 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B), which is firmly in the "later" zone where this paper shows geometric structure attenuates. If correctness-predictive geometry followed the same transient-emergence pattern, L19 should be *past* peak organization — implying our DoM signal is either (a) not a geometric phenomenon at all (it's a readout artifact), or (b) fundamentally different from representational geometry. Falsifiable: compute layer-wise RSA for correctness dissimilarity on our all-layer cache. If RSA peaks at intermediate layers (8–14) and is degraded by L19, F-2's "L19 is the geometry layer" interpretation is refuted.

**R2 — Domain-specific trajectories challenge universality of breathing (F-1)**
The paper shows that different perceptual domains (color, pitch, emotion, taste) have distinct emergence profiles — color peaks early-middle, emotion is sustained through late layers, taste emerges early but rapidly degrades. If representational geometry is domain-specific rather than architecture-universal, our F-1 claim that "dimensional breathing is universal across architectures and scales" may conflate several distinct per-domain geometric evolutions. The refutation fires if correctness-for-math follows a trajectory similar to one perceptual domain (e.g., emotion's sustained profile) but dissimilar to others — suggesting domain-specificity, not architectural universality. Falsifiable: compare per-layer PR (participation ratio) profiles for math-reasoning vs. factual-recall prompts in our 1.5B cache. If profiles diverge significantly by content domain, F-1's universality claim needs domain qualification.

**R3 — Attenuation provides a null explanation for asymmetric collapse (F-4)**
The paper's central finding is that ALL geometric structure attenuates in later layers as part of the prediction-head pipeline. F-4 claims correct trajectories "collapse harder" than incorrect at the final token, interpreting this as an informative signal. But if late-layer attenuation is a universal property of geometric structure (as this paper shows), then both correct and incorrect trajectories collapse — and the "asymmetry" might merely reflect that correct problems produce *more* organized intermediate structure (which then attenuates more dramatically by the time it reaches the final token). Falsifiable: compute layer-wise GPA alignment for correct-only vs. incorrect-only subsets. If both show similar peak-then-attenuation profiles with the difference being only the peak height (not collapse asymmetry at the final token specifically), F-4's "asymmetric collapse is informative" interpretation weakens — it becomes "differential peak amplitude" instead.

**R4 — RSA metric could outperform or subsume DoM as correctness predictor (F-2, F-9)**
Their RSA (Spearman correlation between cosine-dissimilarity matrices) and GPA metrics operate on population geometry rather than individual sample positions. If an RSA-derived per-problem metric (e.g., a problem's contribution to collective RSA with a correctness template) captures correctness signal as well as DoM AUROC 0.7731, it would suggest our single-direction DoM is a simplified projection of a richer geometric signal — and the "single direction" framing in F-2 and the "redundancy with CoE-60" in F-9 might both be artifacts of measuring with a 1D probe when the signal is higher-dimensional. Falsifiable: compute a per-problem RSA-leave-one-out score at L19 and evaluate its AUROC vs. 0.7731.

- **F-1 (breathing universality)**: Their multi-model comparison (LLaMA-3-8B, Gemma-7B, Qwen-3-4B, Qwen-3-8B) using the same RSA/GPA metrics provides a template for cross-architecture breathing comparison with standardized geometric measures rather than PR alone.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse)**: Their "attenuation in later layers" finding is the population-level version of what we observe per-trajectory. The GPA residual at each layer could quantify collapse magnitude in a geometry-preserving way.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA)** — Spearman rank correlation between vectorized upper-triangular entries of cosine-dissimilarity matrices. One matrix from model activations, one from a reference structure (human perceptual similarity or, in our case, correctness labels).
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (we have 500×1536 cached activations; cosine dissimilarity + Spearman is trivial).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our cached L19 prefill activations as a new correctness metric.

- **Generalized Procrustes Analysis (GPA)** — Optimal orthogonal transformation minimizing Frobenius norm between MDS-projected model geometry and reference geometry. Score = 1 − (residual/2).
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (scipy Procrustes + MDS on 500 samples).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could align our L19 activation manifold against a correctness reference and compare the GPA score to our AUROC as complementary evidence.

- **Layer-wise geometric profiling** — Computing RSA/GPA at every transformer layer independently, producing an "emergence profile" curve. Key insight: structure peaks at intermediate layers and attenuates later.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (iterate over 29 layers in our all-layer NPZ cache).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the highest-value experiment from this paper. Tells us whether L19 is at peak, post-peak, or pre-peak for correctness geometry.

- **Bootstrap CI for geometric metrics** — 1000 iterations of stimulus resampling with 95% percentile confidence intervals for RSA/GPA scores at each layer.
  *Replication cost*: 45min CPU (1000×29 layers with 500 samples each).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — provides significance bounds for the layer-wise profile.

## Approaches & framings

- **Transient emergence as a universal property of residual-stream geometry**: Any structured signal (perceptual, correctness, factual) may follow an "emerge → peak → attenuate" trajectory across layers. This reframes our L19 finding: rather than "L19 is special," the question becomes "where does correctness geometry peak, and is L19 at/near that peak?"

- **Domain-specific geometric trajectories**: Different types of structure have different peak layers and persistence profiles. Intersects F-1/F-5: breathing universality vs. content-dependence may be resolvable by measuring domain-specific emergence profiles.

- **Geometry as pipeline product, not stored representation**: The paper frames transient geometry as arising from the model's "internal transformation pipeline" rather than being stored knowledge. Intersects our F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality): the geometry at prefill is part of an active computation, not a static embedding.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Cosine dissimilarity computed as d_ij = 1 − (h_i · h_j) / (||h_i|| ||h_j||) on raw residual stream vectors (no normalization beyond the cosine formula itself).
- MDS for dimensionality reduction to 2D/3D before GPA alignment — important because GPA operates on low-dimensional projections, not full 1536-dim space.
- Minimal prompts to avoid semantic bias — stimuli presented with minimal framing (e.g., "The description of color given as #HEXCODE"). Analogous care needed if we construct reference dissimilarity matrices for math problems.
- Bootstrap resampling of stimuli (not layers, not dimensions) — the uncertainty quantification is over the problem set, which maps directly to our 500-problem MATH-500 set.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Layer-wise RSA for correctness**: Load `pathway8_layerwise/data/math500/problem_*.npz` → extract `prefill_all_layers[layer_idx]` for all 500 problems → build 500×500 cosine dissimilarity matrix → compute Spearman correlation with binary correctness dissimilarity matrix (d=0 if same label, d=1 if different) → plot across 29 layers. Compare peak layer to L19.
- **GPA at L19**: Extract L19 prefill vectors → MDS to 3D → Procrustes align against a 1D correctness axis embedded in 3D → compute GPA score. Compare to DoM AUROC 0.7731 as a complementary measure.
- **Per-problem RSA contribution**: For each problem i, compute RSA with problem i excluded vs. included. The change in RSA is a per-problem score. Evaluate as AUROC for correctness prediction.
