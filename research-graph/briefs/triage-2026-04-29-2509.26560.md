# Triage brief — 2509.26560 — Estimating Dimensionality of Neural Representations from Finite Samples

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26560
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Chun, Canatar, Chung, Lee — ICLR 2026. Bias-corrected estimator γ_both for the
participation ratio (PR) of covariance eigenvalues, derived from unequal-index
moment sums (not Marchenko-Pastur per se, but motivated by the same
finite-sample regime). Headline scaling law:

> E_Φ[1/γ_naive] ≈ 1/γ + 1/P + 1/Q

i.e. the naive PR is the **harmonic mean** of P (stimuli/sequence count), Q
(neurons/hidden dim), and γ (true dimensionality), up to a constant factor.
γ_both, γ_row, and γ_col use sums over distinct row and/or column indices to
remove the bias contributions from row sampling, column sampling, or both.
Extensions: (i) two-trial noise correction with O(1/P + 1/Q) residual bias
versus O(1/√N) for naive trial-averaging, (ii) importance-sampling weights for
local dimensionality γ_both^local(r) with a ball-radius r and a
Mahalanobis-distance neighborhood, (iii) sparse-matrix variant. Code at
https://github.com/badooki/dimensionality.

LLM experiment: Llama 3 base on FLORES+ (NLLB Team 2024), 9 languages × 483
sentences, last-token representation, Q = 4096. With P sampled at ratios down to
0.1, γ_naive plateaus far below γ_both, and γ_col tracks γ_naive (column
sampling already complete since they retain all neurons). The layerwise profile
shows the now-standard rise-then-fall that Valeriani et al. 2023 and Skean et al.
2025 also report. **The naive estimator under-estimates** dimensionality
whenever P or Q is comparable to or smaller than γ — relevant because the
admission note (link-forge) flagged our P/Q ratios as 0.14–0.33, exactly the
bias-significant regime.

## Refutations

1. **F-6 (7B prefill PR inversion driven by incorrect-group concentration) may
   be a within-model bias artifact, not a real inversion.** F-6 compares peak PR
   of the correct-group vs the incorrect-group within Qwen-7B and concludes the
   incorrect group concentrates more sharply. But γ_naive ≈ 1/(1/P_group +
   1/Q + 1/γ): if the correct and incorrect groups have unequal P
   (366 vs 134 at K=1 7B accuracy), the naive PR floor differs between groups
   even when the underlying γ is identical. Mechanism: P_incorrect=134 and
   P_correct=366 give floors of ~134 vs ~365 respectively (with Q=3584,
   γ_true comparable). Falsifiable: recompute peak PR per group with γ_both,
   or alternatively bootstrap to matched P_correct = P_incorrect = 100 with
   γ_naive — if the inversion vanishes after either treatment, F-6 was a
   sample-asymmetry artifact, not a geometric finding.

2. **F-1 (universal breathing across architectures and scales) confounds
   architecture with the per-model (P, Q, γ) bias triangle.** Cross-architecture
   amplitude comparisons in F-1 use γ_naive at fixed n=500 across Qwen-1.5B
   (Q=1536, P/Q=0.33), Qwen-7B (Q=3584, P/Q=0.14), Llama, and Phi-3. The
   paper's harmonic-mean law predicts that the *amplitude* (max − min over
   tokens) of γ_naive depends on whether γ is in the saturating or
   bias-dominated regime — and the regime differs across these four models.
   Mechanism: a model whose true γ is well below P,Q sees naive amplitude
   ≈ true amplitude; a model whose true γ is comparable to min(P,Q) sees
   amplitude *compressed* by the harmonic floor. Falsifiable: recompute the
   per-model breathing curve with γ_both. If 7B's amplitude grows
   proportionally more than 1.5B's after correction (because 7B was deeper in
   the bias-significant regime), the "universal" claim becomes "universal
   when bias-corrected, but the *naive* signal is partly an artifact of
   matching the same n=500 across different Q values."

3. **F-10 (PH on residual streams at the Gaussian null) might be wrong about
   *local* geometry.** F-10 measures persistent homology and finds H_0/H_1
   bottleneck spectra indistinguishable from a Gaussian null — but PH at this
   scale is itself heavily sample-size sensitive. The paper's γ_both^local(r)
   uses Mahalanobis-distance balls and explicitly demonstrates that TwoNN
   *overestimates* local dimensionality on noisy data while γ_both^local
   recovers truth. Mechanism: residual streams are noisy and finite-sample;
   PH inherits the same noise sensitivity that breaks TwoNN. A
   bias-corrected local-dimensionality scan could reveal manifold structure
   (e.g. layer-by-layer drop in local γ that PH missed) that PH integrated
   away. Falsifiable: run γ_both^local(r) on the same Stage 2 NPZs F-10 used,
   sweep r, compare against a matched Gaussian null. If local γ deviates
   from the null at any r where PH did not, F-10 is too strong a refutation
   of the topological-geometry framing.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) may be
   driven by D-bucket having the smallest P.** D-bucket = K=1-right but
   K=8-wrong on MATH-500, which is a small subset (single-digit-percent of
   500). Naive PR floors at ~P, so D-bucket's PR is artificially compressed
   relative to A/B/C-buckets that have hundreds of problems. F-7's "narrower
   spread / lower effective rank" finding is exactly the signature you would
   expect from sample-size bias alone. Falsifiable: bootstrap A, B, C buckets
   down to D's |P|, recompute γ_naive — if A/B/C now look like D, F-7's
   "geometric signature" was a small-N effect.

## Methodologies extracted

- **γ_both bias-corrected PR estimator** — unequal-index sums of v_ijkl =
  Φ_iα Φ_jα Φ_kβ Φ_lβ to form unbiased numerator A_both and denominator
  B_both, ratio gives γ_both. Vectorizable via einsum (paper Sec. 4.1).
  *Replication cost*: ~1 h CPU on cached Stage 2 NPZs (P=500, Q=1536–3584).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly executes H-16 / P11-FE16.

- **γ_row / γ_col partial corrections** — correct only row or column
  sampling bias. γ_row is what we want when we have full Q (all hidden
  units retained) but sampled P (subset of MATH-500). The paper's LLM
  figure (Fig 3a) shows γ_col = γ_naive when columns are complete,
  γ_row ≈ γ_both — implying γ_row alone is the relevant correction for
  our setup.
  *Replication cost*: same as γ_both, slightly cheaper.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — γ_row is the precise estimator for our
  P-sampling, full-Q setup.

- **Two-trial noise correction** — replace v_ijkl ← Φ^(1)_iα Φ^(2)_jα
  Φ^(1)_kβ Φ^(2)_lβ with two i.i.d. trials of the *same* (x_i, w_α). For
  generation noise (different decoded sequences from the same prompt), each
  K=1 sample at temperature > 0 is a "trial." Bias contributed by noise
  drops to O(1/P + 1/Q).
  *Replication cost*: requires re-generation at temperature > 0 with
  per-token activation extraction — H100, ~4 h.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if H-16 surfaces a residual
  noise-bias story after γ_both is applied.

- **γ_both^local(r) local dimensionality with Mahalanobis ball** — pick a
  point Φ_0, weight neighbors within radius r (uniformly inside, zero
  outside), run γ_both with weighted sums. Provides a noise-resistant
  alternative to TwoNN; explicit comparison shows TwoNN overestimates local
  dim on RFF + Gaussian noise.
  *Replication cost*: O(P²Q) for distance matrix; for P=500, Q=1536 this
  is ~1 GB RAM, ~30 min CPU per layer per radius sweep.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests refutation #3 above
  against F-10.

- **Importance-sampling weights** — r(x) = ρ_X(x) / ρ_X^obs(x), c(w) =
  ρ_W(w) / ρ_W^obs(w), inserted into unequal-index sums. Lets us correct
  for the fact that MATH-500 is a *biased* sample of the population of
  problems the model trains on.
  *Replication cost*: requires a density model for problem distribution —
  not currently feasible.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we have no access to the population
  density of pretraining-distribution problems.

## Approaches & framings

- **Harmonic-mean law as a diagnostic.** γ_naive ≈ 1/(1/P + 1/Q + 1/γ)
  flips the question from "what is the dimensionality?" to "are P and Q
  large enough relative to γ?" Three lines of code give us a diagnostic
  for every PR number we have ever reported.
- **Global vs local dimensionality as orthogonal axes.** The paper
  separates global γ (covariance-eigenvalue PR) from local γ (Mahalanobis
  ball + same estimator with weights). PR has been our default; we have
  not measured local dim. F-10's PH-at-null is implicitly a *local*-ish
  claim, but uses topology not eigenvalue spectra.
- **Task vs neuron centering.** The paper distinguishes column-centering
  (subtract mean across stimuli per neuron → "task dimensionality") from
  row-centering (subtract mean across neurons per stimulus → "neuron
  dimensionality"); these are not equal. Worth checking which we use in
  Stage 2 — if we mix them or use the wrong one, F-1 and F-4 numbers
  shift.
- **PR is the soft-rank floor.** γ ≤ rank, and γ ≈ "number of components
  explaining 70-80% variance" (Gao et al. 2017). Useful framing for
  interpreting the absolute scale of breathing curves.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Reference repo: https://github.com/badooki/dimensionality — this is the
  go-to for P11-FE16 implementation.
- For our setup (full Q hidden dim, sampled P problems), **γ_row is the
  correct estimator**, not γ_both. Computationally cheaper than γ_both,
  same answer when columns are not subsampled.
- γ_both uses einsum-vectorizable unequal-index sums; six index sets
  (four row {i,j,l,r}, two column {α,β}), each must be mutually distinct.
  Expanded forms in their Sec. A.
- Time complexity O(min(P,Q)² · max(P,Q)) — same order as naive — so the
  per-layer cost adds maybe 2× wallclock vs the naive estimator we
  already run.
- Centering: paper uses **task centering** (column-centering = subtract
  per-neuron mean across stimuli) by default. Verify what `pathway11_h100`
  uses; if it row-centers, F-1 and F-4 amplitudes are computing a
  *different* quantity ("neuron dimensionality") than the paper.
- Local estimator γ_both^local(r) requires ≥4 distinct points inside the
  ball, so r must be large enough — paper notes this as the smallest
  allowable radius.

  apply the two-trial noise correction — only after γ_both lands; not a
  first-pass cross-check.

**Relevance:** Provides the bias-corrected participation-ratio estimator γ_both
