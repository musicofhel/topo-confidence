# Triage brief — 2308.08742 — PMET: Precise Model Editing in a Transformer

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08742
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, framed as "prefill and final-token circuits are orthogonal") may be artifact of mixing FFN-stable + MHSA-churn components into a single residual-stream DoM.**
   - *Mechanism*: PMET's per-layer cosine + Jaccard analysis on 1209 factual statements (GPT-J-6B, 28 layers) shows a sharp regime change at layer 15: post-L15, FFN hidden states "stabilize in a specific direction" while MHSA hidden states keep "undergoing frequent changes [with] directions [that] remain uncertain throughout knowledge extraction." Our L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers) sits in the same post-stabilization band (L19/28 ≈ 0.68 vs PMET's L15/28 ≈ 0.54). Under PMET's framework, **the FFN-component of L19 should be a stable knowledge axis, the MHSA-component should be a churning extraction-pattern axis**, and any whole-residual-stream DoM averages the two. If both prefill and final-token DoMs were FFN-driven, PMET predicts they should be aligned (FFN converges to a "specific direction"). cos = 0.046 ≈ orthogonal is therefore evidence that **at least one DoM is MHSA-driven, not FFN-driven** — which contradicts the implicit framing of F-3 as "two stable knowledge circuits at different positions" and reframes it as "one stable FFN axis + one transient MHSA axis."
   - *Falsifiable test*: At L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B, decompose the residual stream at the prefill-last-token and final-token positions into MHSA-output (a^L19), FFN-output (m^L19), and pre-L19-residual contributions (re-running the cached forward pass on MATH-500 with a hook that saves all three streams). Fit OOF DoMs separately on each component and compute pairwise cos. **Predict** cos(FFN_prefill_DoM, FFN_final_DoM) ≫ 0.046 (PMET's stability ⇒ alignment), and cos(MHSA_prefill_DoM, MHSA_final_DoM) ≈ 0 or even negative (MHSA churns). If both component cosines are ≈ 0.05, F-3's orthogonality is robust to MHSA/FFN decomposition and PMET's framework is wrong for Qwen. If FFN-only cos is high while MHSA-only cos is low, F-3 needs a "MHSA-extraction-axis" reinterpretation.

2. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 framed as "correctness predictor") may be MHSA-extraction-pattern-driven rather than FFN-knowledge-driven.**
   - *Mechanism*: PMET claims (and Geva et al. 2021/2023 support) that FFN stores factual knowledge and MHSA stores "general knowledge extraction patterns." If "is this MATH-500 problem going to be solved correctly" is a knowledge-content question, F-2's high prefill AUROC should be carried by the FFN component at L19. If it's an extraction-pattern question (problem-routing, attention-pattern matching, "do I know how to do this kind of problem?"), it should be carried by the MHSA component. The current F-2 framing in narrative docs treats the prefill DoM as a unified "correctness direction"; PMET's framework predicts the AUROC must localize to one component or the other. **A null-result here (AUROC ≈ 0 on both components individually but high on the sum) would itself refute PMET's clean MHSA/FFN separation for this task.**
   - *Falsifiable test*: Train OOF logistic DoM probes separately on (a) MHSA-output a^L19_prefill, (b) FFN-output m^L19_prefill, and (c) pre-L19 residual h^L18_prefill, all from cached pathway11_h100 NPZs. Compare AUROCs. **Predict** if PMET is right and correctness = factual recall, FFN-only AUROC ≈ 0.77 and MHSA-only ≈ 0.55. If MHSA-only AUROC is materially higher than FFN-only, F-2 is reinterpreted as an extraction/routing signal — which then connects to H-21 (D-bucket has narrower prefill attention) and H-13 (head-level attribution).

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) is in tension with PMET's "MHSA hidden states change frequently across layers" finding.**
   - *Mechanism*: PMET's Figure 2 shows MHSA cosine + Jaccard similarity changing monotonically and frequently across all 28 GPT-J layers, never stabilizing — i.e., MHSA hidden state direction *encodes* a layer-time signature distinct from any single layer. CoE (Chain-of-Embedding, Wang ICLR 2025) is exactly the kind of trajectory feature that should pick up this MHSA churn. If MHSA-component variation across layers is a real signal, CoE-60 should encode information **not present in single-layer L19** — contradicting F-9's redundancy claim. F-9 currently rests on whole-residual-stream features; PMET predicts a non-redundant MHSA-trajectory component would emerge if CoE were decomposed.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Decompose CoE-60 (60-dim summary) into MHSA-only, FFN-only, and residual-only sub-trajectories by re-extracting per-component activations across all 28 layers from cached prefill activations. Re-fit the CoE→correctness probe on each. **Predict** if PMET is right, MHSA-only-CoE will lift AUROC above 0.7731 (the L19 DoM ceiling) by capturing extraction-pattern dynamics; FFN-only-CoE will be redundant with L19 DoM (since FFN converged after L15). If both decomposed CoEs match L19 DoM, F-9 stands universally. If MHSA-CoE > L19 DoM, F-9 is too strong and needs scoping to "FFN-component-only redundancy."

4. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) needs to specify *which component* is being steered to test PMET's "MHSA weights need not change" claim.**
   - *Mechanism*: PMET's central methodological move is to update FFN weights only, leaving MHSA untouched. Their ablation (Table 3, "w/ W^l_O MHSA") shows that *also* updating MHSA marginally lifts generalization but degrades specificity — so MHSA does store some factual knowledge, just less. By analogy, DoM steering at the residual stream conflates injection into MHSA-out and injection into FFN-out. PMET predicts that adding the steering vector to FFN-out should produce stronger reliable correctness shifts (FFN = factual knowledge), while adding it to MHSA-out should produce smaller, more diffuse, generalization-damaging shifts. If H-1 succeeds equally with either decomposition, PMET's separation is wrong for accuracy steering. If only MHSA-out works, the steering signal is extraction-pattern, not knowledge-content.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Implement two H-1 variants: (1) v_DoM added to a^L19 (MHSA output), (2) v_DoM added to m^L19 (FFN output), keeping the rest of the forward pass intact via a custom hook. Run 100 MATH-500 problems with each, compare K=1 accuracy delta. **Predict** the FFN-out variant produces larger, cleaner accuracy shifts on edge-of-correctness problems; MHSA-out is noisier or null. Either result gives a sharp claim about what the DoM direction *is*.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-layer hidden-state cosine + Jaccard top-k vocab projection (PMET Section "Investigating MHSA and FFN")** — for each layer l, take last-token hidden states h^{l-1} and a^l (after MHSA) and m^l (after FFN), compute (a) cos(h^{l-1}, a^l) and cos(h^{l-1}, m^l), (b) project all three into vocab space via the unembedding matrix, take top-50 tokens, compute Jaccard similarity J_50(T(h), T(a)). Average across N factual queries; plot per-layer.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZs if MHSA-out and FFN-out streams are saved; ~3h H100 to re-extract per-component streams if not.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly answers "where in Qwen-2.5-1.5B does FFN stabilize?", which gates Refutations 1–3 and is a perimeter test for using L19 as the canonical DoM layer.

- **TC (Transformer Component) hidden-state perturbation** — add learnable parameters δ_a, δ_m to a^L and m^L respectively, optimize via KL + cross-entropy loss to memorize target knowledge.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 to port from PMET's GitHub (https://github.com/xpq-tech/PMET) onto Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — full PMET editing is overkill for our use case, but the δ_a / δ_m optimization framework is the cleanest existing implementation of "intervention on MHSA-out vs FFN-out" and could be borrowed for H-1.

- **Square-root residual spread** — when distributing a target intervention across critical layers, scale R^l = (V_1 - W_0 K_1) / sqrt(L - l + 1) instead of even spread. Improves reliability at cost of specificity.
  *Replication cost*: trivial code change (1 line).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes if we ever do multi-layer steering** — direct improvement over uniform per-layer DoM injection in P10 v2 steering experiments.

- **MHSA vs FFN ablation by output-weight masking** — to test whether MHSA stores knowledge, additionally update W^l_O_MHSA via the same loss; observe specificity drop. Diagnostic for "is component X causally involved in this task?"
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 if porting PMET infra; trivially cheap as a forward-pass-only zero-ablation diagnostic (~30min CPU).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — diagnostic for D-bucket: zero-ablate MHSA-out at L19 on D-bucket vs A-bucket, see whose accuracy collapses harder.

- **Last-token-as-knowledge-summary protocol** — use the residual stream at the last token of a factual query as the canonical representation of the query's knowledge. Foundational assumption for all of PMET's analysis.
  *Replication cost*: zero — already our canonical protocol for prefill DoM extraction.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: already running this.

## Approaches & framings

- **Information-flow decomposition of the residual stream into MHSA + FFN + residual contributions.** PMET's central conceptual move: stop treating h^l as a unified hidden state, decompose into a^l + m^l + h^{l-1}. This intersects every F-N/H-N that currently treats L19 prefill as a single direction (F-2, F-3, F-7, F-9, H-1). Adopting this decomposition would give us a sharper attribution toolkit and a cleaner reformulation of "what is the prefill DoM."

- **MHSA-as-extractor / FFN-as-store dichotomy.** This is the dominant model-editing-literature framing (Geva et al. 2021/2022/2023; ROME; MEMIT). It's not novel to PMET, but PMET sharpens it into "MHSA weights need *not* update on new knowledge — only the directions of its hidden states matter." For us, this predicts: prefill DoM-as-correctness should localize to FFN-out at L19 if the picture is right; deviation indicates either (a) correctness is not factual-recall, or (b) the dichotomy doesn't hold for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.

- **Knowledge-clue / knowledge-point subject-centric model editing.** Less directly relevant — they're framing model editing around subject-relation-object triples and noting that current methods break ripple effects (Cohen et al. 2023, RIPPLEEDITS). This framing is orthogonal to MATH-500 reasoning. Cited only.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Code repo**: https://github.com/xpq-tech/PMET — has the δ_a / δ_m optimization implemented for GPT-J and GPT-NeoX. Likely adaptable to Qwen.
- **Critical layer set R**: PMET uses MEMIT's set R = {3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8} for GPT-J (early layers!) — i.e., they edit *early* FFNs even though their analysis says MHSA-stabilization happens around layer 15. The argument is that early FFNs serve as "key-value memories" and editing them is more efficient (Meng et al. 2022b). For our work, if we adopt PMET's framework, the natural Qwen analog is editing early FFNs (L3–L8 say), not L19. This is a non-obvious gotcha.
- **Loss balance hyperparameters**: μ (KL weight) and φ (1/P, prefix averaging weight) — set via grid search per dataset.
- **MHSA + FFN are parallel, not sequential, in GPT-J / GPT-NeoX**: the formula is h^l = h^{l-1} + a^l + m^l with a^l and m^l computed *in parallel* from γ(h^{l-1}). Qwen-2.5-1.5B uses *sequential* MHSA → residual → FFN → residual (Llama-style). The PMET decomposition still applies, but the per-layer dynamics will differ — when porting their analysis, we need to extract pre-MHSA, post-MHSA-pre-FFN, and post-FFN streams as three distinct snapshots, not two.

## 2308.08742 — PMET: Precise Model Editing in a Transformer (Li et al., AAAI 2024)

**Relevance:** Sharpens the MHSA-as-extractor / FFN-as-store dichotomy from Geva et al. 2021/2023 into a concrete editing recipe and presents the cleanest existing per-layer empirical analysis of where in a transformer FFN hidden states stabilize (L15/28 in GPT-J). The protocol — per-layer cosine + top-50 Jaccard (vocab-projected) similarity of last-token hidden states before vs after each Transformer Component — is directly portable to our MATH-500 prefill activations and gives us a tool for distinguishing "knowledge axis" from "extraction-pattern axis" at L19. Connects to F-2 (prefill DoM AUROC), F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality), F-9 (CoE redundancy), H-1 (DoM steering), H-13 (head-level attribution).

**Key claim we tested:** FFN hidden states stabilize past a critical layer while MHSA hidden states keep churning; therefore correctness/knowledge probes at "post-stabilization" layers should localize to the FFN-output component, not the MHSA-output component or the residual stream as a whole. Applied to F-2: our prefill L19 DoM should split into a strong FFN-only AUROC and a weak MHSA-only AUROC.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** Pending FE8742 (per-layer FFN-stabilization scan on Qwen) + FE8743 (component-decomposed L19 DoM AUROC). The methodology transfers cleanly; the empirical question of whether the GPT-J pattern holds in Qwen-2.5-1.5B is open. PMET also offers a concrete prediction we have not yet examined for F-3 (orthogonality should disappear in the FFN-only component, since FFN converges to a "specific direction").

**Related experiments:** P11-FE8742 (per-layer FFN-stabilization), P11-FE8743 (component-decomposed L19 AUROC), P11-FE8744 (MHSA-out vs FFN-out DoM steering), P11-FE8745 (component-decomposed CoE), H-8742, H-8743, H-8744.
