# Triage brief — 2505.00127 — Between Underthinking and Overthinking: An Empirical Study of Reasoning Length and Correctness in LLMs

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00127
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Jinyan Su, Jennifer Healey, Preslav Nakov, Claire Cardie (2025).
Models studied: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, DeepScaler-1.5B-Preview.
Benchmarks: GSM8K (7,473 q), MATH (7,500 q).
Headline measurement: on R1-Preview MATH, correct responses average 2,459 ± 30
tokens; incorrect average 6,106 ± 38 tokens — a ~2.5× ratio. They show models
overthink easy problems and underthink hard ones (length is highest where it is
*least* useful), then run SimPO (β=2, γ=1, lr=5e-7) preferring "always shorter
regardless of correctness" and recover 30–60% length reductions with little
accuracy loss. No hidden-state, residual-stream, layer-wise, or probe analysis
appears in the paper.

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731) may be a covert length predictor.**
   Mechanism: the "prefill" representation is built from question-only tokens.
   If response length is itself predictable from the question (Su et al. show
   incorrect-bound problems have 2.5× longer outputs and that this divides
   easy/medium/hard cleanly), then a linear probe at L19 may simply be reading
   the model's internal "I am about to ramble" prior — and that prior carries
   the correctness signal **because rambling correlates with wrongness**, not
   because L19 encodes a calibrated correctness direction. This would partially
   demote F-2 from "geometric correctness signal" to "covert length-prior
   probe." Falsifiable test: regress predicted-response-length (from prefill
   activations) on correctness; compute residual DoM AUROC after partialing out
   length. If 0.7731 collapses toward 0.5, F-2 is largely length.

2. **F-4 (asymmetric collapse: correct trajectories collapse harder at the
   final token) is confounded with raw trajectory length.** Mechanism: if
   incorrect responses are systematically 2.5× longer, the "final token" of an
   incorrect trajectory is harvested from a deeper, more-drifted point in
   sequence than the final token of a correct one. Participation ratio
   accumulates differently across length bins (longer trajectories integrate
   more diversity), so the correct-vs-incorrect PR gap may be a
   **trajectory-length artifact**, not asymmetric "collapse." Falsifiable test:
   length-band PR control (H-15 / P11-FE15 already proposes this; Su et al.
   sharpen the prediction). Within each length tertile, compute correct-vs-
   incorrect final-token PR. If the PR gap vanishes in length-matched subsets,
   F-4 is refuted.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) understates the
   redundancy — both may be subsumed by predicted-response-length.**
   Mechanism: F-9 already concedes that CoE-60 trajectory features add nothing
   over L19 DoM; Su et al. add the next layer of the onion, which is that
   shorter=correct is itself the dominant signal. If a *single scalar*
   (predicted output length from the question) tracks correctness as well as
   the L19 DoM probe, then both representations are vestigial w.r.t. a trivial
   token-count heuristic. Falsifiable test: train a length-from-prompt
   regressor on R1/DeepScaler-style traces, evaluate its correctness AUROC on
   our MATH-500 / Qwen-2.5-1.5B data. If AUROC ≥ 0.75 from length alone, F-9's
   "L19 DoM is the floor" is itself the wrong floor.

4. **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts problem difficulty) is reframed as
   length-confounded.** Mechanism: Su et al. show response length is the
   easiest difficulty proxy (and inverted on hard problems where models
   underthink). If breathing amplitude scales with sequence length (more
   tokens = more PR variance), then "amplitude predicts difficulty" reduces to
   "length predicts difficulty," which their paper already establishes without
   any geometric machinery. Falsifiable test: regress breathing amplitude on
   response length first; report partial correlation between residual
   amplitude and difficulty.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-9 (shorter=correct is the dominant axis)** — directly *corroborated* by
  Su et al.'s 2,459 vs 6,106 token gap on MATH (ratio 2.48). Their finding is
  the broader version of F-9's CoE-60 redundancy result. Anchors: F-9's
  redundancy claim, our prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731.
- **F-2 / F-8 (Prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731; selective-prediction 71.6% acc @
  cov=0.5)** — *threatened*. Headline AUROC is suspect if length-from-prompt
  alone reaches comparable AUROC. Need a length-baseline cross-check before we
  cite F-2 as a hidden-state result.
- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse, peak PR 67/88)** — *threatened* via
  length-confounding; sharpens H-15 motivation.
- **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics)** — *partially
  threatened*: their easy-vs-hard length split offers an alternative AR-style
  story (autoregressive accumulation drives both length and PR variance).
- **H-5 (prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)** —
  *reinforced and reframed*: prefill may encode "predicted output length,"
  which is a noisy proxy for familiarity (familiar = short, unfamiliar = long
  rambling).
- **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts difficulty)** — *reframed*: difficulty
  prediction may already be saturated by length alone.
- **H-15 (length-band PR control)** — *reinforced*; this paper makes the
  control mandatory rather than nice-to-have.

## Methodologies extracted

- **SimPO with shortness-only preference labels** — fine-tune with pairs
  (shorter, longer) ignoring correctness, β=2, γ=1, lr=5e-7, batch=1×4 grad-
  accum, 2 sampled responses per question.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day H100 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 sample pairs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — out of pathway scope (training intervention
  versus our probe-only program), and we already have the per-problem length
  data without needing to re-train.
- **K=10 sample-based difficulty stratification** — partition problems into
  Easy (10/10 correct), Medium (mixed), Hard (0/10 correct).
  *Replication cost*: 0 — already implicit in our K=8 MATH-500 data; recompute
  with K=10 trivially.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a cleaner difficulty axis than our
  current B/D-bucket scheme for cross-checking F-7.
- **Question-level Pearson/Spearman correlation between length and correctness
  rate** — table-1 style summary.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached Stage 2 outputs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct corroboration / refutation of F-9
  framing.
- **Length-from-prompt regression as a baseline correctness predictor** —
  implicit in their argument; we can build it explicitly.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the load-bearing refutation cross-
  check for F-2.

## Approaches & framings

- **Length as a self-awareness signal.** They argue generation length should
  be treated as a "meaningful signal for reasoning behavior" worthy of
  calibration study. Intersects our F-2/F-8 framing where the *internal*
  prefill geometry is the signal of interest — Su et al. say the *external*
  signal (length) may already carry most of the information cheaply.
- **Overthink-easy / underthink-hard asymmetry.** A specific claim that the
  length-vs-difficulty relation is non-monotonic and inverted at the hard
  end. This is a fresh framing for our F-7 D-bucket discussion (D-bucket = K=1
  right but K=8 wrong; their "underthink hard" suggests D-bucket may correspond
  to questions where the model *should* sample more but doesn't extend
  reasoning).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.00127 — Between Underthinking and Overthinking: An Empirical Study of Reasoning Length and Correctness in LLMs (Su, Healey, Nakov, Cardie, 2025)

**Relevance:** A pure output-level empirical study showing that on R1-Distill-1.5B and DeepScaler-1.5B-Preview, **incorrect responses are ~2.5× longer than correct ones** (R1-Preview MATH: 6,106 vs 2,459 tokens), models overthink easy problems and underthink hard ones, and SimPO with shortness-only preferences (β=2, γ=1, lr=5e-7) cuts length 30–60% with little accuracy loss. No hidden-state or layer-wise analysis. Threatens our F-2 / F-4 / F-9 framings by showing that a trivial **length-from-prompt baseline** may be the floor that all our hidden-state probes are quietly riding on.

**Key claim we tested:** "Generation length is itself a strong correctness signal, regardless of internal representation." In our terms: length-as-feature AUROC may approach prefill L19 DoM AUROC (0.7731), and the prefill signal may decompose into "predicted output length" + small residual.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — refutation experiments P11-FE73 / P11-FE74 are queued as HIGH/CRITICAL priority; they are cheap (≤1h CPU on cached data) and load-bearing for F-2's status as a geometric finding rather than a length-prior probe.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73 (length AUROC), P11-FE74 (partial-correlation control for F-2), P11-FE75 (K=8 difficulty tiers × D-bucket), P11-FE76 (cross-model length-correctness ratio); H-83, H-84.

**Status:** TO TEST
