# Triage brief — 2410.02707 — LLMs Know More Than They Show: On the Intrinsic Representation of LLM Hallucinations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02707
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Exact-answer-token probe could beat prefill L19 DoM on MATH-500 (refutes F-2's claim that *prefill* is the strongest probe location).** The paper shows on Mistral-7b-instruct + TriviaQA/Winobias/Math that probes trained on the *last exact answer token* (LEAT) reach AUROC 0.83–0.95, while prefill ("End of question") tops out at 0.72–0.82. Our F-2 reports prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B + MATH-500 1024-tok and explicitly claims prefill > final-token. **Mechanism**: if Qwen's internal "I know the answer" signal also concentrates on the exact-answer span (the boxed expression in MATH problems), then a LEAT probe on the cached P11 H100 1024-tok activations should beat 0.7731. **Falsifiable test**: train an L19 logistic probe on the activations at the post-`\boxed{...}` token (or the last digit before `\boxed` close) on the 500 MATH-500 problems, OOF 5-fold, and compare AUROC to F-2's 0.7731.

2. **Probes generalize poorly across tasks (refutes any reading of F-2/F-3 that treats L19 DoM as a "universal correctness direction").** Their Section 4 finding: cross-task probe AUROC, after subtracting logit-min-exact, is at-or-below zero in most off-diagonal cells; only same-skill clusters generalize (factual retrieval ↔ factual retrieval, common-sense ↔ common-sense). F-2 currently quotes a single AUROC (0.7731) on MATH-500 as if the prefill DoM is *the* correctness signal at L19. **Mechanism**: if MATH-500 prefill DoM is skill-specific, training the same probe on a non-MATH dataset (e.g., TriviaQA) and testing on MATH-500 should yield AUROC ≈ logit-baseline. **Falsifiable test**: train a Qwen-1.5B L19 prefill probe on TriviaQA correctness labels (cheap to generate, ~1h H100 for 1000 prompts), evaluate on MATH-500, and compare to logit-min-exact baseline. If the gap is ≤ 0, F-2 must be reframed as MATH-specific.

3. **Truthfulness is multifaceted, not single-direction (refutes the implicit single-direction reading of F-3 and any single-DoM steering in H-1).** Section 4 explicitly argues against "a single unified mechanism" of truth. F-3 (cos = 0.046 between prefill_DoM and final_DoM) already shows two orthogonal directions for *one* task — but H-1 ("per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy") still treats the prefill direction as a single axis to push along. **Mechanism**: if multiple skill-specific directions exist *within* MATH-500 (e.g., one for arithmetic, another for combinatorics, another for geometry), a single per-position DoM bank trained on the full MATH-500 will steer correctly on average but flip incorrectly on subskills. **Falsifiable test**: split MATH-500 by topic tag, fit per-topic DoM directions, compute pairwise cos. If pairwise cos < 0.3, H-1 must be reformulated as multi-direction or topic-conditional.

4. **Internal-external discrepancy (refutes F-8's framing that 71.6% at 50% coverage is "the" attainable selective-prediction ceiling).** Section 6 + Figure 5: using a probe to *select* an answer from K=30 resamples lifts accuracy by 30–40 points in error types C2/D/E1 ("model encodes correct answer but generates incorrect"). F-8 uses prefill DoM as a *gate* (answer/refuse) on K=2.5 average sampling, not as a *selector* across resamples. **Mechanism**: if Qwen-1.5B has the same internal-vs-external misalignment, then probe-as-selector across K=8 resamples should beat F-8's 71.6%. The 1.5B is currently at 48.6% K=1 → if the probe lifts it to ~65–70% K=8 *answered-only*, that's a different (and better) operating point than F-8. **Falsifiable test**: regenerate 8 samples per problem on MATH-500 cached P11 H100 pipeline, score each with the L19 prefill DoM probe, pick argmax probe score per problem, measure accuracy. Compare to F-8's gated K=2.5 = 71.6%-on-answered.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold)** ↔ paper Sections 3.3, Table 1. They find probes at "End of question" (= prefill) hit 0.72–0.82 on Mistral-7b-instruct, but the *exact answer token* hits 0.85–0.95. Direct numerical anchor: their Math-instruct exact-token AUROC = 0.92 vs end-of-question = 0.74. Our 0.7731 is in the same regime as their *prefill* number, not their best.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ paper Section 4 multifaceted-truthfulness. Their cross-task non-generalization is the macro-scale analogue of our within-task prefill⊥final orthogonality. The paper provides theoretical cover for "directions don't transfer" without invoking RoPE.
- **F-7 (D-bucket = K=1 right, K=8 wrong)** ↔ paper Section 5 error taxonomy (types A/B1/B2/C1/C2/D/E1/E2). Their type C2 ("consistently incorrect with correct answer appearing ≥1 in K=30") and type D ("two competing") are close cousins of D-bucket. Their Table 2 shows error types are predictable from greedy-decoding hidden states (AUROC 0.59–0.90).
- **F-8 (selective-prediction 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** ↔ paper Section 6, Figure 5. Their probe-as-selector framing on K=30 resamples is a strict generalization of our gated-compute selective prediction.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer L19 DoM)** ↔ paper Section 4 finding that single-token probing already saturates. Their result that *task-specific* probes plateau is consistent with our F-9 finding that ensemble features are redundant.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** ↔ paper's multifaceted truthfulness directly questions whether a single DoM is steerable at all.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** ↔ paper's logit-min-exact baseline already explains most cross-dataset transfer. Reinforces the H-12 motivation: if probe ≈ logits across tasks, label-free uncertainty methods may match supervised probes for free.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Last Exact Answer Token (LEAT) probing** — train linear classifier on the activation at the *last token of the canonical answer span* (e.g., the boxed final number in MATH, the entity in TriviaQA), not at last-generated or final-prompt.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached P11 H100 NPZs. Requires extracting the post-`\boxed{...}` token index per problem.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly probes the strongest refutation of F-2.

- **Logit-min-exact baseline** — minimum log-prob over exact-answer tokens as a uncertainty signal. Used as the "subtractor" for cross-dataset generalization analysis.
  *Replication cost*: 10 min CPU; we have logits cached in P11.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — important null model for any AUROC claim, and we don't currently report it.

- **Cross-dataset probe-transfer matrix** — train probe on dataset i, test on dataset j, fill an N×N AUROC heatmap, then *subtract a logit-baseline heatmap* to expose probe-specific generalization.
  *Replication cost*: ~4h H100 to generate hidden states across 3–4 datasets; then CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-3 and H-2 (code/short CoT) already gesture at this.

- **K=30 resampling at T=1 with error-type taxonomy** — for each problem, sample 30 responses, classify into A/B1/B2/C1/C2/D/E1/E2 by frequency-of-correct + answer-distinctness.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day H100 for K=30 × 500 problems on Qwen-1.5B (we currently run K=8 in P11).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — F-7 D-bucket is currently defined at K=8; their richer taxonomy could subdivide D-bucket into D-pathological vs D-noisy.

- **Probe-as-selector across resamples** — score each of K resamples with the trained probe, pick argmax probe score, evaluate accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on cached resamples once they exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of refutation #4.

- **Per-error-type one-vs-rest probing (Table 2)** — train a separate binary classifier per error type using the same hidden-state pool.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU after K=30 generation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we already pay the K=30 cost — gives per-bucket internal signatures for free.

## Approaches & framings

- **Token-location matters as much as layer.** Reframes correctness probing as a 2-D search (layer × token-position), not 1-D (layer only). Direct intersection with F-2: we currently describe "L19 DoM" without specifying *which* token. The paper would say we should also report the optimal token.
- **Multifaceted truthfulness (no universal direction).** Reformulates the "is there a truth direction" question as "how many skill-specific directions are there, and how do they overlap?" Intersects F-3 (we already see prefill⊥final within MATH-500); generalizes it to N-direction across N skills.
- **Error-types as compressed behavioral fingerprints.** Resamples + categorical taxonomy (A–E) are a cheap proxy for the model's "knows the answer / sometimes / never" modal landscape. Intersects F-7 D-bucket: provides a finer 8-way partition that could reveal whether D-bucket is one type or a mixture.
- **Internal-external alignment as a measurable quantity.** Frames the gap between probe-correct and generation-correct as a *quantity to optimize* (selector accuracy vs. greedy accuracy). Intersects F-8 selective-prediction: our gating framing collapses to their selector framing when K → 1.
- **Probing under unrestricted long-form generation.** Most prior probing literature uses few-shot or single-token settings. They explicitly run unrestricted generation, like our P11 1024-tok setup. Intersects F-1/F-5: validates the 1024-tok regime as the right setting for probing.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models tested: Mistral-7b, Mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2, Llama3-8b, Llama3-8b-instruct (all 7–8B). They do *not* test Qwen-2.5; we'd be the first to check their findings on Qwen.
- Probing classifier: linear logistic regression on a single (layer, token) activation; layer chosen by validation set per dataset and token type. They do not use OOF cross-validation; they use a held-out test set per dataset.
- Exact-answer-token extraction: instruct-LLM in few-shot setting, *not* a heuristic. They acknowledge this is a noisy step. For MATH this is easier (the boxed expression).
- Resampling: K=30, T=1, greedy=False. They note K=30 was chosen because correctness plateaus at that point (Appendix D).
- Code: https://github.com/technion-cs-nlp/LLMsKnow (already in our migrated `repo_url` field).
- Generalization heatmap subtracts logit-min-exact, *not* logit-mean — important methodological choice; they argue logit-min-exact is the best logit baseline.
- Error type definitions are quantitative thresholds on (n_distinct_answers, freq_correct, freq_top_incorrect) — reproducible from any K=30 resample bundle.

## 2410.02707 — LLMs Know More Than They Show: On the Intrinsic Representation of LLM Hallucinations (Orgad, Toker, Gekhman, Reichart, Szpektor, Kotek, Belinkov; ICLR 2025)

**Relevance:** This paper is the most direct methodological challenge to F-2 in our literature. It establishes three pillars: (1) truthfulness signal concentrates in the *exact answer tokens* (not prefill, not last-generated, not final-prompt), boosting probe AUROC by 10–20 points; (2) probes do *not* generalize across tasks beyond what the logit-min-exact baseline already captures, refuting universal-truthfulness claims; (3) probes can be used as *selectors* across resamples, lifting accuracy 30–40 points in error types where the model's external preference is weak. Tested on Mistral-7b, Mistral-7b-instruct, Llama3-8b, Llama3-8b-instruct across 10 datasets; we'd be the first to check on Qwen-2.5.

**Key claim we tested:** "Probing the *last exact answer token* yields a stronger correctness signal than probing prefill or final-generated tokens, but this signal does not generalize across tasks beyond what output logits already provide."

**Our result:** **TO TEST** (HIGH priority). Currently, F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) is the closest match in our findings registry; we have not run a LEAT probe on Qwen-1.5B + MATH-500.
