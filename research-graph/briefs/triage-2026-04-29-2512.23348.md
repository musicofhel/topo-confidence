## Refutations

No genuine refutations identified after careful read of the full PDF (~25 minutes) — paper is off-topic / purely foundational. Recommend **CITED ONLY** status.

Specific reasons no refutation can be constructed:

1. The paper makes **zero quantitative empirical claims**. §5.4 states explicitly: "These results are presented descriptively and without further interpretation. No claims are made regarding semantic interpretation of features or comparative performance." There is no number in the paper that could refute a number in F-1…F-10.
2. The paper's framework explicitly **disclaims geometric shape recovery**: §3.3 Remark — "Unlike Vietoris–Rips or Čech constructions, the simplicial complexes arising from our method are not designed to recover the ambient geometric shape of the data… Persistent homology is used here as a multiscale descriptor of how dense regions interact and merge across thresholds, rather than as a tool for geometric shape reconstruction." F-10 (PH at Gaussian null) is a *shape-recovery* statement on V-R PH; the FTS construction is solving a different problem and so cannot corroborate or contradict F-10 by direct application.
3. The only stability theorem proved (Theorem 8, d_B ≤ ε under ε-perturbation) applies to the **basic** density model — the very model the author admits "rapidly loses higher-order information" and "collapses to contractible structures" (§3.3, §5 intro). The actually-useful density-guided variant has **no** stability proof. So even the methodological-variant pitch from the prior admission note evaporates: we cannot inherit a stability guarantee for the construction we'd want to run.
4. Datasets are COIL-20 (1,440 grayscale object images) and Paul15 (~2,700 hematopoietic single cells). Neither overlaps the MATH-500 / 1024-tok / Qwen-2.5 residual-stream regime, and the paper provides no Betti numbers, AUROC, or accuracy comparisons — only "well-defined filtrations are produced."

## Methodologies extracted

- **Functorial finite-topology pipeline (X, d) → T_i → poset P_i → order complex Δ(P_i)** — Build a filtration of finite topologies on a fixed point set X via continuous identity maps (no inclusions of complexes required), then pass to T_0-quotient posets and order complexes for homology.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2 days CPU using their public repo (https://github.com/cg-istanbul/fts-persistent-homology) on cached Stage 2 NPZs from P11.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — the paper itself disclaims geometric shape recovery, and our F-10 question is precisely about shape; running this on residual streams would not produce a result interpretable as evidence for or against F-10.

- **Anchor-neighborhood density-guided generator augmentation** — Pick a fixed candidate set A = {A_1, …, A_k} of "anchor neighborhoods" once from the metric data, filter by per-scale density threshold τ_i to obtain A_i ⊆ A, then augment with secondary sets B_i to mediate transitions between scales without introducing new density-driven features. Topology T_i is generated by G_i = A_i ∪ B_i. The construction is "faithful to anchor structure" (every minimal nonempty intersection of anchors in A_i appears in the minimal basis of T_i).
  *Replication cost*: medium — need to instantiate "anchor neighborhood" choice for high-dim residual streams; not obvious how to pick A on 28-layer × 1024-tok × 1536-dim data. Author's COIL-20 / Paul15 examples don't generalize cleanly.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — the high-dim adaptation is a research project of its own and our F-10 baseline already used the standard PH instrument.

- **Beat-point removal at the poset level** — Standard poset homotopy reduction; preserves the order complex up to homotopy, so persistent invariants are unchanged (Theorem 2).
  *Replication cost*: trivial (combinatorial trick).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — only relevant if we were already running the FTS pipeline.

## Approaches & framings

- **Metric → topology → poset → simplicial complex pipeline (separating metric data from homological analysis)**: a four-step factorization that lets one apply combinatorial poset reductions before simplicialization. This is a clean reframing of how PH is built but does not intersect any F-N / H-N framing — F-1…F-10 are statements about Qwen residual-stream geometry, not about PH-construction abstractions.
- **PH as a density-interaction descriptor rather than a shape descriptor**: §3.3 / §5.5 recasts persistent homology as "how dense regions interact and merge across thresholds." This *is* an interesting framing, conceptually adjacent to D-bucket (F-7) being a collective-density signature, but the paper neither operationalizes the connection nor runs a comparable experiment.

- **Paul15** — ~2,700 hematopoietic single cells, public benchmark dataset for trajectory inference. Applicable? **no** — single-cell biology, not residual-stream geometry.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Public repository: https://github.com/cg-istanbul/fts-persistent-homology (Selçuk Kayacan, 2026).
- Homology computed only on the **2-skeleton** of the order complex (sufficient for H_0 and H_1; no claim about higher dimensions).
- Pipeline outputs: per-scale topology, T_0-poset, order complex, persistence barcodes (visual + machine-readable), per-step Betti numbers, anchor counts, coverage statistics.
- The exact construction of the secondary B_i sets is "deferred to the implementation" (§5.1 Remark) — i.e., not specified in the paper text. To replicate one must read the repo.
- The basic density model is "computationally infeasible in practice, as the number of such dense subsets grows exponentially with |X|" (§5.1) — only the anchor-augmented variant is tractable.
