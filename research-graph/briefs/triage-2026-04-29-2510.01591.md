# Triage brief — 2510.01591 — CLUE: Non-parametric Verification from Experience via Hidden-State Clustering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01591
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **CLUE refutes F-2's specific "L19 is the peak layer" claim**
   - Their Figure 4 shows the centroid distance `d^(ℓ) = ||V_succ^(ℓ) − V_fail^(ℓ)||₂` is **monotonically increasing** with depth and peaks in the *final* layers, across both 1.5B and 4B reasoning models. F-2 chose L19 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B has 28 layers, so L19 is mid-late, not the final layer) as the *peak* layer for prefill DoM AUROC.
   - **Mechanism**: if separability is monotone in depth, then the layer-AUROC curve we used to pick L19 should have actually peaked at L25–L27, not L19. Either our peak-picking is wrong, or correctness signal vs. centroid-distance signal disagree on layer.
   - **Falsifiable experiment**: re-sweep prefill DoM AUROC at every layer 0–27 on the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` activations. If the curve is monotone-up rather than peaked at L19, F-2's "peak at L19" specifier is contradicted.

2. **CLUE refutes F-9's "single-layer L19 DoM ≈ multi-feature trajectory"**
   - F-9 says CoE-60 (60 trajectory features) is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM. CLUE's full L×D activation-delta matrix achieves binary classification 80.9 / 85.2% on AIME24/25 with Nemotron-1.5B (a directly comparable scale to our Qwen-2.5-1.5B). Our F-2 single-layer L19 DoM hits AUROC 0.7731 — translating to ~70% binary classification.
   - **Mechanism**: if their full-layer delta beats single-layer DoM by ≥10 accuracy points on a comparable 1.5B model, then "all layers contain non-redundant signal" — directly contradicting F-9's redundancy claim.
   - **Falsifiable experiment**: replicate CLUE's centroid construction on our cached MATH-500 activations (we have all 28 layers cached at prefill + final-token positions for 1.5B), use 5-fold CV. Compare against L19-only DoM AUROC. If full-layer-delta ≥ 0.85 vs. L19-only 0.7731, F-9 is contradicted.

3. **CLUE refutes the "prefill > final-token" framing of F-2**
   - F-2 reports prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 > final-token L19 DoM AUROC 0.7186. CLUE finds the *delta* between two reasoning-block boundary positions — i.e., end-of-`</think>` minus start-of-`<think>` — is the strong signal. Their winning representation includes information from the post-reasoning state, contradicting "prefill is what matters most."
   - **Mechanism**: if `Δh = h(end) − h(start)` outperforms either endpoint alone, then framing prefill as the privileged signal is wrong — what matters is the displacement induced by reasoning, not where it starts.
   - **Falsifiable experiment**: on the same cached activations, compute `h(L19, last-prefill-tok) − h(L19, last-CoT-tok)` per problem, do DoM on this delta vector, measure OOF AUROC. If > 0.7731, prefill-as-privileged framing falls.

4. **CLUE's RL-vs-SFT finding refutes the universal applicability of F-2 / F-8**
   - CLUE Section 4.5: SFT models' self-reranking *barely matches majority@64* — their hidden states are NOT geometrically separable. RL-trained models develop clean correctness clusters. F-2 / F-8 were established on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct (SFT, no RL stage on the released checkpoint). If CLUE is right, our results should not have held.
   - **Mechanism**: either (a) Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct has more RL-derived structure than CLUE assumes (Qwen post-training pipeline includes DPO), or (b) our 0.7731 AUROC is an artifact of our specific MATH-500 distribution, or (c) CLUE's RL-vs-SFT dichotomy is too strong.
   - **Falsifiable experiment**: re-extract L19 prefill activations on a *pure* SFT model with no DPO/PPO stage (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base, or a Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct trace) and recompute DoM AUROC. If it drops to chance, F-2 generalizes only to RL-tuned models — a major scoping change. If it survives, CLUE's training-paradigm claim is the wrong frame.

5. **CLUE refutes H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probes)**
   - H-12 hypothesizes label-free (semantic entropy) probes can replace supervised DoM. CLUE shows that with just 10k+10k labeled trajectories, simple element-wise mean centroids crush both LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o) AND DeepConf confidence-based methods on weak models. CLUE is supervised-but-training-free — it needs labels but no gradient updates.
   - **Mechanism**: the "labels are too expensive, prefer label-free" premise of H-12 is empirically refuted: labels are cheap (rule-based math verifier) and supervised geometric clustering wins.
   - **Falsifiable experiment**: run semantic entropy probes on our P11 traces and compare AUROC to a CLUE-style supervised centroid AUROC on the same data. If centroid ≥ semantic entropy by ≥ 0.05 AUROC, H-12 should be downgraded to PARKED.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on 1.5B)**: CLUE binary classification accuracy 80.9% on Nemotron-1.5B AIME24 is the direct comparator. Their experience set is 10k+10k balanced; ours is 5-fold OOF on 500 problems × MATH-500. Headline anchor: CLUE 80.9% binary acc vs F-2's AUROC 0.7731 (≈70% binary acc) — factor-of-1.15 advantage from going single-layer→all-layers.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)**: CLUE's `Δh = h(end) − h(start)` *across* sequence positions is conceptually parallel to F-3's "the two endpoints carry distinct signal." CLUE then uses both to form a delta. F-3's orthogonality is preserved; CLUE adds a third frame (the displacement vector also carries signal).
- **F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature)**: CLUE labels by final-answer correctness only, so D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong) is invisible to them. But their robustness on TNR (filter incorrect attempts) connects to D-bucket detection.
- **F-8 (Selective prediction at coverage 0.5: 71.6% accuracy on answered, K=2.5 avg)**: CLUE's reranking is the directly comparable functional unit. CLUE top-maj@16 on AIME24 with Nemotron-1.5B = 70.0% (vs. majority@64 = 56.7%, +13.3 pts). Our F-8 71.6% on answered at coverage 0.5 sits in the same ballpark, but the test sets differ (MATH-500 vs. AIME-24).
- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null)**: CLUE doesn't do PH but their finding "geometry is separable in linear PCA" is consistent with F-10 — the separability lives in low-rank linear directions, not topological complexity.
- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering)**: CLUE's RL-vs-SFT finding is a trigger for H-1 — steering may only work on RL-tuned models where the geometry is already separable.
- **H-5 (prefill familiarity vs. decomposability)**: CLUE generalizes math AIME centroids to GPQA at top-maj@16 = 47.0% (Nemotron-1.5B, GPQA, +2.6 pts over majority). This argues against a strong domain-specific prefill-signal interpretation.
- **H-12 (label-free probes)**: see Refutation 5.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing)**: CLUE *is* a non-parametric C_exact verifier. P11-FE19 (the highest-ROI item in the queue) should now compare against a CLUE-style reference baseline, not just confidence baselines.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Activation-delta representation** — `Δh = h(end-of-</think>) − h(start-of-<think>)` ∈ ℝ^(L×D), full L×D matrix kept.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU (we already have prefill + final-token cached; would need to re-mark `<think>`/`</think>` boundaries — Qwen-2.5-Instruct doesn't emit those tokens by default, so adapt to "last-prompt-tok" → "last-generated-tok").
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cleanest way to test our own F-2 / F-9 claims via Refutations 2 and 3.

- **Layer-averaged Euclidean distance to centroids** — `d(A,B) = (1/L) Σ_ℓ ||A_ℓ − B_ℓ||₂`, classify by nearest of `V_succ`, `V_fail`. No training, no optimization.
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU (numpy means + distances on cached NPZ).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a 5-line script that's strictly more powerful than single-direction DoM if F-9 turns out to be wrong.

- **Layer-wise centroid-distance curve `d^(ℓ)`** — measure how separable correct vs. incorrect become as a function of depth.
  *Replication cost*: 10 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct refutation test for F-2's "L19 peak" specifier (Refutation 1).

- **Cross-model verification** — use centroids from model A's experience to verify model B's solutions. Specifically RL-trained-model-as-verifier on SFT-model outputs.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 (re-extract activations from a second model on same problems).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — interesting but expensive; only after intra-model replication.

- **PCA visualization of class-separability per layer** — Figure 1, Figure 4, projects deltas to 2D and shows clusters by color.
  *Replication cost*: 1 hour CPU (already have activations; just plot).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — visual sanity check for layer-wise separability story.

- **TPR/TNR breakdown** — report binary classification accuracy split by class to expose optimistic-bias of LLM judges (GPT-4o TNR drops to 34% on Polaris-4B AIME25).
  *Replication cost*: free — already breaks out cleanly from our existing AUROC arrays.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful framing for narrative docs comparing F-2 to LLM-judge baselines.

- **Train-on-AIME, test-on-GPQA OOD eval** — explicitly frame the math experience set as the training distribution and report OOD generalization.
  *Replication cost*: needs new GPQA generation traces (~$50 + few hours H100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — desirable but not free; pairs with H-2 (breathing on short CoT).

## Approaches & framings

- **"Activation delta as the unit of analysis"** — they argue what matters is the *transformation* induced by reasoning, not the absolute state. This is a useful reframing for our P11 narrative which currently leans on the *prefill* state alone (F-2). The delta frame would unify F-2 (prefill) and the orthogonal final-state signal (F-3) under one representation.
- **"RL → separable geometry, SFT → entangled"** — a training-paradigm scoping condition on hidden-state probing methods. Our F-2/F-8 results were obtained on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, which has DPO post-training; this paper raises the question of whether F-2 transfers to non-DPO SFT-only checkpoints.
- **"Supervised but training-free"** — aspirational efficiency: deterministic mean over labeled trajectories beats any gradient-trained probe. Argues against our reliance on logistic-regression DoM probes when a 5-line numpy script is competitive.
- **"Layer-averaged distance, not best-layer distance"** — methodological move that intentionally avoids per-layer peak-picking (no need for L19-vs-L23 sweeps if you just average all layers). Removes a hyperparameter.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Models**: Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B (RL), Polaris-4B (RL), DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (distillation/SFT), Qwen3-4B (SFT), DeepSeek-7B (SFT). Use HF "recommended inference setting" (temperature, system prompt).
- **Generation lengths**: 16k / 32k / 64k tokens — three settings ablated; conclusions stable across. (Our P11 1024-tok labels are well under this.)
- **Experience set construction**: for each problem in AIME+MATH, sample 32 unique solutions; rule-based verifier labels them; 10,000 correct + 10,000 incorrect randomly drawn for balanced centroids.
- **Test-time setup**: 64 candidate solutions per test problem.
- **Reasoning-block delimiters**: `<think>` and `</think>` literal tokens — only present in reasoning models that emit them. Adapting to Qwen-2.5-Instruct (which doesn't) requires picking analogous boundary tokens (e.g., end of prompt, end of generation, or a `\n\n`-terminated CoT).
- **Hidden-state extraction**: at the final token of `<think>` and final token of `</think>` — i.e., one token before and one token after the reasoning span.
- **No code released** in v1 of the paper (Tencent technical report; check arxiv v2 for code link).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2510.01591 — CLUE: Non-parametric Verification from Experience via Hidden-State Clustering (Liang et al., Tencent AI Lab, 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct same-pipeline competitor. CLUE uses an L×D activation-delta matrix `Δh = h(end-of-</think>) − h(start-of-<think>)` and classifies new traces by layer-averaged Euclidean distance to success/failure centroids built from labeled experience (10k+10k balanced). This is the same hidden-state-as-correctness-predictor problem we worked on (F-2 / F-8) but with three methodological shifts: (a) all-layers instead of single-layer L19, (b) sequence-position delta instead of prefill state, (c) supervised-but-training-free instead of logistic-regression DoM probe. Tested on Nemotron-1.5B / Polaris-4B / DeepSeek-7B / Qwen3-4B / DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B — Nemotron-1.5B AIME24 is the closest scale match to our 1.5B Qwen MATH-500 setup.

**Key claim we tested:** Hidden states encode correctness as a geometrically separable signature — and this signal is *stronger when read across all layers as a delta* than when read at any single position/layer. They also claim signal monotonically strengthens with layer depth, peaking in final layers (Figure 4).

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** P11-FE91 (CLUE replication on our cached 1.5B MATH-500 activations) and P11-FE92 (layer-AUROC monotonicity test) directly evaluate the two strongest CLUE claims against our F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 single-layer L19) baseline. Both are 30-min CPU jobs on already-cached NPZs. Until run, the F-9 (CoE-60 redundant) and F-2 ("L19 peak") specifiers are at risk.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE91, P11-FE92, P11-FE93, P11-FE94, H-71, H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** TO TEST.
