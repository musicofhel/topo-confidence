# Triage brief — 2506.24106 — On the Predictive Power of Representation Dispersion in Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.24106
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 is potentially redundant: a label-free scalar may match the supervised L19 prefill DoM AUROC of 0.7731.**
   Mechanism: the paper shows that average pairwise cosine distance of final-layer hidden states monotonically separates correct-from-incorrect slices on five different multiple-choice datasets (Tables 3–8: e.g. on MMLU 1B, mean dispersion drifts from 0.0923 at 0% correct → 0.1171 at 100% correct, with SE ≈ 0.001), with calibrated MAE 1.4–2.2% (Table 5). No probe, no labels. If we re-run the controlled-slice protocol on our `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` 1.5B run with L19 prefill activations, batch dispersion may yield correct-vs-incorrect ROC ≥ 0.7731 with zero training. That would refute the implicit claim that the DoM *direction* is doing the work — what's doing it would be the scalar spread.
   Falsifiable test: bin our 500 MATH-500 problems by 10% accuracy strata using K=8 self-consistency, compute mean pairwise cosine distance of L19 prefill activations per bin, and check monotonicity with non-overlapping SE bars at neighboring bins. Compare bin-level AUC against probe-DoM bin-level AUC.

2. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) may be a probe artifact rather than two genuine signals.**
   Mechanism: the paper shows in Figure 5 that the dispersion-vs-perplexity correlation strengthens monotonically from layer 1 to layer 17, and that *both* within-cluster and between-cluster distances grow with training (Figure 7). If batch dispersion is the underlying correctness-carrying scalar at every deep layer, then per-batch dispersion at prefill-token L19 and per-batch dispersion at final-token L19 should be *strongly correlated across problems* — even though the probe-fit DoM directions are nearly orthogonal. The orthogonality would then be an under-determined-by-data property of the supervised probe, not evidence of two distinct geometric signals. F-3 currently relies on the directional cosine to argue independence; a high scalar-correlation would weaken that argument.
   Falsifiable test: from `scratch/pathway10_temporal_and_verifier_results.json`, recompute per-problem batch-dispersion at L19 for the prefill window vs a final-token window (each over a sliding mini-batch of correct/incorrect peers). If those two scalars correlate at r > 0.5 across problems, F-3's "geometrically unrelated" claim is too strong — at the scalar level, prefill and final tokens may be carrying the same signal.

3. **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null) overclaims geometric vacuity.**
   Mechanism: F-10 says persistent-homology summaries of residual-stream point clouds are statistically indistinguishable from a matched-covariance Gaussian, and concludes that "trained geometry doesn't help." But the paper shows that a much simpler 1st-moment scalar (mean pairwise cosine distance) *does* separate correct from incorrect slices at the same layer, on the same architecture family. PH integrates over scale; the cosine-distance mean is a single-scale scalar. A Gaussian null at the same dimension would have flat dispersion across slice-fraction; the paper shows non-flat (~30% relative change between 0% and 100% slices on Llama-3B). So either (a) the residual-stream geometry is non-Gaussian in a way that's invisible to PH but visible to dispersion, partially overturning F-10's "geometry vacuous" framing, or (b) the paper's signal is confounded by length / answer-token presence and the same confound would show up if we replicated their protocol on our PH-null streams.
   Falsifiable test: take the exact NPZ point clouds that produced the F-10 Gaussian-null result (`pathway8_layerwise/*/cloud_*.npz`), compute the paper's batch-pairwise-cosine-distance per problem, and bin by 10% correctness. Either dispersion is monotonic with correctness (refines F-10 → "PH null but mean-distance informative") or it is not (replicates F-10, mildly weakens the paper's universality claim).

4. **H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket) competes with a vastly simpler scalar.**
   Mechanism: the paper's Section 3.1 setup ranks unlabeled examples by hardness using only mean pairwise cosine distance — no kernel-density estimator, no manifold method, no per-question feature engineering. If D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong; the pathological-correctness bucket from F-7) appears as a *low-dispersion outlier slice* under the paper's protocol, the density-estimation machinery proposed for H-7 is over-engineered for the task. The deeper refutation is that D-bucket may not have a "distinctive geometric signature" but rather just a *quantitative* low-dispersion signal sitting on the same monotonic curve as A/B/C buckets.
   Falsifiable test: extract per-problem L19 activations for the 36 D-bucket problems, compute their pairwise cosine distance, and check if it lies on the same monotonic curve as A/B/C against K=8-derived correctness. If yes, F-7's "distinctive collective signature" reduces to "lower dispersion."

5. **The "push-away" training objective is what F-1 / F-5 (breathing) is geometrically diagnosing — collapse refutation.**
   Mechanism: Table 13 (Squeeze ablation) shows that artificially *minimizing* batch dispersion during training causes Δppl up to +27.5 at step 1000. This is direct causal evidence that low dispersion → worse predictive performance. F-1 (universal dimensional breathing) and F-5 (content-dependent breathing) describe an *increase* in dimensionality during prefill+early generation that the paper would interpret as the model spreading representations to enable confident predictions. If breathing is the runtime signature of dispersion, then breathing amplitude should track per-problem batch dispersion within a single forward pass. If they are uncorrelated, F-1's universality is preserved but its causal role is undermined: breathing would be a side-effect of attention dynamics, not the dispersion-driven mechanism the paper would predict.
   Falsifiable test: from `pathway11_h100` cached temporal PR curves, compute per-token batch dispersion (across the 500 MATH-500 problems) at the same time positions where PR peaks. Check correlation between per-problem peak-PR token and per-problem peak-dispersion token.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Average pairwise cosine distance (representation dispersion)** — for a set of N hidden vectors {E_i}, compute D = (1 / C(N,2)) · Σ_{i<j} (1 − E_i·E_j / (‖E_i‖‖E_j‖)). Equation (1) in the paper.
  *Replication cost*: ~milliseconds on CPU per batch of 100 vectors.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in scalar to test against L19 prefill activations on our 500-problem MATH-500 cache.

- **Dispersion gap G = within(T) + between(T, T̄)** — uses *only* the rows of the output projection W_o (the unembedding matrix). T = digit tokens for math, code keywords for code; T̄ = a reference common-vocabulary set. No forward pass.
  *Replication cost*: seconds on CPU; just reads the unembedding for Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Distill-Qwen-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a checkpoint-quality scalar across our model zoo (1.5B, 7B, Distill-Qwen variants we use in P11) without GPU.

- **Calibration-by-dispersion regression** — bin a small labeled subsample by accuracy, fit isotonic / polynomial / random-forest regressor mapping batch-dispersion → accuracy, predict on unseen data. Reported MAE 1.4–2.2% on MMLU.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would give a calibrated MATH-500 selective-prediction baseline competitor to F-8 (71.6% at coverage 0.5).

- **kNN-LM layer selection by dispersion** — at each candidate sub-layer (attention output vs FFN output), compute D over ~100 chunks; pick the layer with highest D as the kNN-LM key.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — already have layer-wise activations cached.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: probably no; we don't run kNN-LM. But the within-block attn-vs-FFN dispersion comparison is interesting for our P10 layer-direction work.

- **Push-away auxiliary loss** — L_total = L_CE + λ · (−D_avg) where D_avg is mean batch-pairwise cosine distance. λ swept ∈ {0.001, …, 0.5}; LR ∈ {5e-4, 7e-4, 1e-3}. GPT2-small from scratch, 500–1000 training steps.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2 GPU-days for a small LM training run; A100 budget.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't pretrain. But the *gradient direction* of the push-away term is informative for steering experiments (H-1).

- **Squeeze (clusterize) ablation** — auxiliary loss = +D_avg (sign-flipped); shows Δppl up to +27.5. A direct causal control for the dispersion claim.
  *Replication cost*: same as push-away.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for a fresh training run, but the ablation logic translates: a scalar steering vector that *reduces* prefill dispersion should hurt MATH-500 K=1 accuracy if dispersion is causal.

- **Vendi Score as dispersion alternative** — exponential of Shannon entropy of normalized eigenvalues of the kernel matrix (Friedman & Dieng 2023, arxiv 2210.02410). Paper reports Vendi tracks the same trends as cosine-distance dispersion.
  *Replication cost*: minutes on CPU per batch.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a kernel-spectrum diversity scalar is one step from our participation-ratio trajectory analyses; maps directly onto Pathway 1 / Pathway 11 PR curves.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Breadth-of-embedding-space" framing**: the paper reframes prediction quality as a *geometric breadth* property of the hidden-state cloud. Intersects F-10 (PH null) directly: both papers ask "what does the geometry tell us?" with opposite headline answers.

- **Label-free correctness signal via 1st-moment scalars**: the paper makes the strong claim that you don't need probes at all — a single scalar over hidden states suffices. Intersects F-2 / F-8 / H-12: our pipeline currently leans on probe-fit directions, while this paper says scalar suffices.

- **Output-embedding-only model selection**: skips forward passes entirely and reads only W_o. A *training-free, data-free* scoring of checkpoints. Novel framing relative to anything in our literature; intersects H-8 (DoM-direction stability across checkpoints) by offering a competing checkpoint-quality scalar.

- **Within-cluster / between-cluster decomposition** (Fig 7): both grow with training. This is a refutation-oriented framing for our "cluster geometry" arguments — even semantically similar contexts spread out as the model improves, not just dissimilar ones.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Public code: https://github.com/yanhong-lbh/rep_dispersion (CC-BY 4.0).
- Sample size for reliable dispersion estimate: N ∈ {10, 50, 100} chunks; moving 10 → 100 alters mean by ≤ 1.5% and *never* changes layer ranking → ~5,000 tokens suffice for a layer-level estimate (a few milliseconds).
- Sequence-level: 100,000 segments × 512 tokens for population-level perplexity-vs-dispersion plots.
- Last-token vs sequence-level: same monotonic trend at context lengths 16 / 64 / 128 / 256 tokens (Figure 4) — robust to context length.
- Layer-wise: in LLaMA-3.2-1B (16 layers), correlation r reaches its strongest value at the deepest layer (layer 17); shallower layers are nearly uncorrelated.
- Push-away λ in {0.001, …, 0.5}; cross-domain task often picks small λ ≈ 0.01–0.02 (not the largest); larger λ destabilizes training.
- Squeeze ablation uses λ = 0.1 with sign flipped → +27.5 ppl at LR 1e-3, step 1000.
- Output-embedding (unembedding) row distances: all on CPU, no GPU. Strong cross-checkpoint Spearman > 0.95 with MATH accuracy.
- Vendi Score replicates dispersion trends (App A.5) — alternative scalar if cosine distance ever fails.
