# Triage brief — 2512.16030 — Do Large Language Models Know What They Don't Know? KalshiBench: Evaluating Epistemic Calibration via Prediction Markets

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 / F-8 may be in-distribution artifacts** — KalshiBench's temporal filter
   ensures every question resolves *after* every model's training cutoff, removing
   memorization from the calibration signal. Under this filter, four of five
   frontier models post **negative** Brier Skill Scores (worse than predicting the
   base rate), and only Claude eeks out BSS=0.057. F-2 reports prefill L19 DoM
   AUROC 0.7731 on MATH-500, which is largely *in-training* (MATH dates from 2021
   and is widely web-scraped). **Mechanism**: if the DoM probe rides on memorized
   problem familiarity rather than truly-internal uncertainty, then on temporally-
   clean OOD content the DoM signal collapses toward chance. **Falsifiable test**:
   take ~300 Kalshi-style post-cutoff binary questions (paper's HF release), have
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B answer + extract prefill L19 activations, fit DoM on a held-out
   fold of these OOD items, measure AUROC. If AUROC ≪ 0.7731 (e.g. < 0.6), F-2's
   generalization claim collapses to "MATH-500-specific memorization probe."

2. **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling) is directly
   contradicted** — GPT-5.2-XHigh with extended reasoning (~2.07M output tokens;
   26× Claude's) posts the **worst** calibration of all five models: ECE=0.395,
   BSS=-0.799, MCE=0.622. Crucially its *accuracy* (65.3%) is comparable to the
   non-reasoning models. So extended reasoning improves nothing while making
   uncertainty estimates much worse. **Mechanism**: the paper hypothesizes
   confirmation bias in long reasoning chains — the model generates arguments
   *for* its initial guess, ratcheting confidence without updating. H-19 routes
   verification compute to *uncertain* problems on the assumption that more
   compute helps; if more reasoning produces miscalibrated overconfidence, the
   routing signal itself becomes corrupted (uncertain problems get verified into
   spurious certainty). **Falsifiable test**: implement the H-19 verification
   routing on MATH-500, then compute ECE and BSS *separately* on the verified
   subset vs. the K=1 subset. If verified-subset calibration is worse than K=1
   despite better accuracy, H-19 must be re-framed as accuracy-only and cannot
   support abstention.

3. **F-2's "correctness predictor" framing conflates accuracy with calibration**
   — KalshiBench's headline is that calibration and accuracy are *largely
   decoupled*: similar accuracies (64-69%) span 3× ECE range (0.120-0.395). F-2
   reports AUROC 0.7731, which is a ranking metric — it says high-DoM cases are
   more often correct than low-DoM cases, but says nothing about whether the
   raw DoM score (after sigmoid) matches actual accuracy in each decile.
   **Mechanism**: AUROC is invariant to monotonic re-scaling; ECE is not. F-2
   could have AUROC 0.7731 and yet have high-DoM cases at "95% prefill-DoM
   confidence" be correct only 60% of the time — exactly the failure mode
   KalshiBench documents for verbalized confidence. **Falsifiable test**: convert
   prefill-DoM scores to probabilities via Platt scaling on a held-out fold,
   compute ECE on the test fold of the cached MATH-500 1024-tok extractions. If
   ECE > 0.1, F-2 needs the "correctness predictor" framing weakened to
   "correctness-ranking probe; calibration requires post-hoc fitting."

4. **H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probes for refusal) is contraindicated by
   broken expressed-confidence channel** — Tian et al. 2023 (cited by Nel) show
   verbalized confidence diverges from token probabilities. KalshiBench shows
   that even with the explicit instruction "Be calibrated: if you're 70%
   confident, you should be correct about 70% of the time," all five frontier
   models still systematically overshoot. **Mechanism**: semantic-entropy probes
   compute entropy over the model's *output distribution*, which is the same
   pathway that produces broken verbalized confidence — both inherit the RLHF
   pressure toward confident-sounding responses without proper uncertainty.
   **Falsifiable test**: on the KalshiBench HF release, compare semantic-entropy
   probes (Farquhar 2024 / SEPs from 2406.15927) against KalshiBench's
   verbalized-confidence ECE. If SEP-ECE > 0.20 on the same questions, H-12's
   bet on label-free output-side signals must yield to internal hidden-state
   probes (DoM, D²HScore).

5. **F-8 selective-prediction's high-confidence regime may be unreliable** —
   F-8 reports 71.6% accuracy at coverage 0.5 (K=2.5 avg). KalshiBench shows
   that in the 90-100% verbalized-confidence bin, all five models are wrong
   15-32% of the time. The selective-prediction promise is precisely that
   high-confidence cases are reliably correct; if the analogous regime in
   prefill-DoM space (top-decile DoM) shows similar miscalibration, F-8's
   deployment claim fails. **Mechanism**: F-8 was evaluated at coverage 0.5
   (median DoM threshold) — performance at extreme deciles (top 10% DoM) was
   not the headline. **Falsifiable test**: bin Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill-DoM scores
   on the cached 1024-tok MATH-500 set into deciles, compute per-decile
   accuracy and confidence-vs-accuracy gap. If the top-decile gap exceeds 15%,
   F-8 deployment claims must be restricted to mid-coverage regimes.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC=0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500, OOF 5-fold)
  — KalshiBench's frontier-model accuracies (64.3%-69.3%) and Claude's ECE=0.120
  set a benchmark for what "well-calibrated frontier model" looks like. Our
  1.5B model's prefill probe is a *ranking* signal in the same space, but its
  calibration has not been measured against the KalshiBench yardstick.
- **F-8** (selective-prediction 71.6% accuracy at coverage 0.5) — directly
  comparable to KalshiBench's "Performance in the 90-100% confidence bin" table
  (Claude: 94.6% conf → 70.0% acc, gap +24.6%). F-8 should be re-evaluated at
  the equivalent extreme-decile regime.
- **F-9** (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — KalshiBench's
  accuracy/calibration decoupling suggests CoE features may carry a *separate*
  calibration signal that single-layer DoM lacks. The redundancy claim was
  measured on AUROC, not ECE.
- **H-12** (semantic-entropy probes label-free for refusal) — directly affected
  by the KalshiBench finding that the expressed-confidence channel is broken.
- **H-19** (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched
  compute) — directly contradicted by GPT-5.2-XHigh's reasoning-degrades-
  calibration result.
- **H-5** (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — if
  models cannot distinguish memorizable from temporally-OOD content (KalshiBench
  shows uniform overconfidence on post-cutoff Q&A), then "familiarity" as a
  prefill signal is in question. The decomposability prong is unaffected.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Verbalized confidence elicitation via structured XML prompt** — system
  prompt instructs `<think>...</think><answer>yes|no</answer><confidence>0-100</confidence>`
  format, with explicit "Be calibrated" instruction. *Replication cost*: trivial
  (prompt-only). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — adapting to MATH-500 yes/no
  paraphrase or to verifier-style "is this answer correct? yes|no, confidence
  X" lets us compare verbalized vs prefill-DoM head-to-head.
- **Expected Calibration Error with 10 equal-width bins** — standard Naeini 2015
  binning. *Replication cost*: 10 lines of numpy on cached probability arrays.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we have not measured ECE for prefill-DoM
  scores. Should be a 1-day add to validate_claims.py.
- **Brier Skill Score against climatology baseline** — `BSS = 1 - BS / (ȳ(1-ȳ))`
  where ȳ is class base rate. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — gives a "did we beat constant prediction" sanity check that
  AUROC alone hides.
- **Maximum Calibration Error (MCE)** — worst-case bin gap. *Replication cost*:
  trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful for high-confidence regime.
- **Overconfidence Rate at threshold τ** — `OCR@τ = |{p_i > τ ∧ ŷ_i ≠ y_i}| /
  |{p_i > τ}|`. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes —
  exactly the deployment-relevant metric for F-8.
- **Reliability diagram (10-bin)** — confidence vs accuracy per bin. *Replication
  cost*: 30min plotting. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a missing visualization
  for F-2 / F-8.
- **Temporal filtering by knowledge cutoff** — restrict eval to questions
  resolving after `max_m t_cutoff(m)`. *Replication cost*: dataset-curation, not
  applicable to math benchmarks but conceptually important.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: indirectly — informs how to construct OOD
  cross-checks for F-2.
- **Per-category ECE breakdown** — partition test set by category, compute
  per-category metrics. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*:
  partial — MATH-500 has 7 subjects (algebra, geometry, etc.); per-subject
  ECE/AUROC for prefill-DoM is missing from F-2 and would be a useful
  H-5 follow-up.

## Approaches & framings

- **Calibration as a distinct capability decoupled from accuracy** — the
  paper's central conceptual move. Cuts directly into F-2/F-8/F-9 framings,
  which equate AUROC with "correctness prediction" and elide the calibration
  layer.
- **Temporal filtering as a contamination-control protocol** — "if model
  cutoff < event resolution, memorization is bounded." Generalizes our
  "1024-tok vs 256-tok" deconfounding move from Phase 6.5: any benchmark with
  knowable retrieval pathways can be flipped from "test of capability" to
  "test of memorization" by ignoring temporal structure.
- **Reasoning paradox: more thinking ≠ better uncertainty** — extended
  reasoning chains amplify confirmation rather than evidence-updating. Recasts
  H-19 (and the Pathway 11 "stage marker" framing) — staged reasoning may
  improve accuracy while *destroying* calibration, so we cannot evaluate
  reasoning-trace quality on accuracy alone.
- **Climatology / base-rate baseline as a forecasting sanity check** — most
  models post negative BSS. The implicit baseline for any of our "X predicts
  correctness" claims should be "constant predict-correct-rate" — we have
  never explicitly compared F-2 / F-8 against the trivial constant predictor
  in BSS terms.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **KalshiBench v2** — 1,531 cleaned + deduplicated Kalshi prediction-market
  binary questions (Sep 2021 - Nov 2025), 300-question evaluation sample
  (seed=42) restricted to post-Oct-2025 resolution. 13 categories, 40% yes
  rate. *License*: HF dataset under 2084Collective; *Accessibility*: HF
  (https://huggingface.co/datasets/2084Collective/kalshibench-v2). Code:
  github.com/2084collective/kalshibench. *Applicable?* yes — directly usable
  as an OOD calibration probe for our hidden-state pipeline. We can ask Qwen-
  2.5-1.5B these questions, extract prefill L19 activations, and test whether
  prefill-DoM transfers from MATH-500.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- System prompt template uses XML-tagged sections (`<think>`, `<answer>`,
  `<confidence>`) — useful for our own verbalized-confidence experiments.
- Temperature 0.7 for standard models, 1.0 with extended reasoning for
  XHigh. Provider-recommended.
- Deduplication strategy: limit to 2 questions per `series_ticker` to preserve
  diversity while reducing redundancy in recurring markets.
- ECE bin width 0.1 (10 equal bins).
- Random seed 42 for the 300-question sample.
- Cost tracking via tokens × provider rates — Claude: $11.63 / 224K tokens;
  GPT-5.2-XHigh: $30.32 / 2.07M tokens.

  prefill-DoM transfers from math to forecasting. ~4h H100.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2512.16030 — Do Large Language Models Know What They Don't Know? KalshiBench (Nel, 2025)

**Relevance:** Introduces KalshiBench, a temporally-filtered benchmark of 300
prediction-market questions resolving after every evaluated model's training
cutoff. Headline: all five frontier models (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2-XHigh,
DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B-Thinking, Kimi-K2) are systematically overconfident
(ECE 0.120-0.395, MCE 0.246-0.630), and 4 of 5 post negative Brier Skill
Scores against the base-rate baseline. Most consequential finding for our
program: accuracy and calibration are decoupled (3× ECE range at similar
accuracy), and reasoning enhancements degrade calibration (GPT-5.2-XHigh
worst at 26× output tokens of Claude). This forces a critical re-examination
of F-2 (prefill-DoM AUROC=0.7731 measures *ranking*, not calibration),
F-8 (selective-prediction at coverage 0.5 may not extend to top-decile DoM),
H-19 (verification routing), and H-12 (semantic-entropy probes).

**Key claim we tested:** Frontier LLMs are uniformly overconfident in their
expressed-probability channel; more reasoning makes calibration worse, not
better; calibration and accuracy are decoupled.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** We have not measured ECE/BSS for prefill-DoM
scores on MATH-500, nor tested whether DoM transfers to a temporally-clean
OOD set like KalshiBench. Triage proposes P11-FE70 (calibration audit of
cached MATH-500 DoM scores) and P11-FE71 (KalshiBench transfer test) as
the immediate cross-checks.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE70, P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, H-72,
H-73; intersects existing P11-FE19 (H-19 verification routing) for the
calibration-audit follow-up.

**Status:** TO TEST
