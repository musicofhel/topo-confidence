# Triage brief — 2512.07667 — Depth-Wise Activation Steering for Honest Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07667
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (privileged single-layer L19 for correctness) — refuted if a Gaussian depth schedule beats L19-only.** Goral et al. show that on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B/14B, *single-layer* steering is dominated by a Gaussian-over-depth schedule even at equal energy budget; on two Qwen models the single-layer intervention is actively *worse than no steering*. Mechanism: per-layer contrastive PCA directions `d_l` form a coherent cross-depth family, so a smooth weighting `α_l` aggregates information that any single layer misses. F-2 claims L19 is the privileged correctness layer, with `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046` taken as evidence that two specific layers carry the signal. Falsifiable test: extract last-non-pad-token activations at *all 28* Qwen-2.5-1.5B layers on the same correct/incorrect contrastive pairs that produced the 0.7731 AUROC, compute `d_l = PCA1(Δa_l)`, and evaluate a Gaussian-weighted multi-layer score `s(x) = Σ_l α_l ⟨a_l(x), d_l⟩` on the held-out fold. If `(μ, σ)` grid search produces an OOF AUROC > 0.7731 by ≥ 0.02, F-2's single-layer privileged-layer claim is overturned and F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) collapses with it.

2. **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token DoM, cos = 0.046) — refuted if `d_l` forms a smooth depth family.** F-3 frames the prefill and final-token DoM directions as orthogonal, implying two qualitatively distinct geometric regimes. Goral et al.'s Fig. 1 method assumes a *continuous* family of per-layer directions: their PCA-1 vector at each layer is a smooth function of depth, and the Gaussian schedule peaks in the *mid-to-late* zone (e.g. ~30% depth for an 8B-32-layer model) without a discontinuity at any single layer. Mechanism: orthogonality between two specific layers is consistent with continuous rotation along depth — neighbours can be near-parallel even when far-apart layers are orthogonal. Falsifiable test: compute pairwise `cos(d_l, d_{l'})` for all 28×28 layer pairs of Qwen-2.5-1.5B using the same correct/incorrect contrast as F-2. If the matrix shows a smooth gradient (high near-diagonal, decaying with `|l−l'|`) rather than two clusters, F-3's "two orthogonal geometries" framing is incomplete; the 0.046 cosine is one entry in a continuous family, not evidence of two regimes.

3. **H-1 (per-position L19 DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — refuted if single-layer steering backfires the way it did on Qwen-2.5-7B/14B.** Goral et al. report that *single-layer* steering on Qwen-2.5-7B drops MASK honesty 27.0 → 24.4 and on Qwen-2.5-14B drops 23.4 → 20.9, while a Gaussian schedule lifts both to ≥ 30. Qwen-2.5-1.5B is the same family architecture, just smaller. Mechanism: a point-mass edit at the residual stream over-concentrates the perturbation in one layer, producing downstream cascades the model cannot absorb; smoothing the same energy budget across depth preserves the intervention's information content while reducing per-layer perturbation. Falsifiable test: run H-1's planned per-position L19 DoM addition `a' = a + α·d_19` on MATH-500 K=1 generation for Qwen-2.5-1.5B at α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, measure accuracy delta vs no-steering baseline (48.6%); separately run the Gaussian-schedule variant `a'_l = a_l + α_l·d_l` at matched total norm. If the single-layer variant produces no improvement or accuracy drop while Gaussian lifts accuracy, H-1 must be reformulated as a *depth-schedule* problem and the L19-privileged framing is wrong for *interventions* even if it survives for *probing*.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective geometric signature) — refuted if a multi-layer Gaussian probe widens the D vs A AUROC gap.** F-7 attributes a distinctive geometric signature to the D-bucket. If that signature is genuinely *layer-localised* at L19, smoothing across depth should weaken the probe (averaging in noise). Goral et al. show the opposite for honesty: smoothed multi-layer evidence beats single-layer evidence even for tasks where one layer was previously thought sufficient. Mechanism: D-bucket separability may be carried by mid-late depth as a continuous band, not a single layer. Falsifiable test: compute per-layer D vs A contrastive direction on cached pathway 11 H100 NPZs, build a Gaussian-weighted score, and compare D-vs-A AUROC against single-layer L19. If multi-layer score AUROC ≥ single-layer L19 + 0.03, F-7's "collective signature" is a multi-layer band that the L19-only frame undersells.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-layer contrastive PCA-1 steering vectors** — for each block ℓ, build `Δa_ℓ = a_ℓ(x⁺) − a_ℓ(x⁻)` over n contrastive pairs, take PCA-1 as `d_ℓ`. *Replication cost*: 30min CPU for 28 layers on existing Qwen-2.5-1.5B contrastive activation cache. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly implements the F-2/F-9 refutation test; we already have correct/incorrect labels and per-layer activations from pathway 11.
- **Gaussian depth schedule `α_ℓ = exp(−(ℓ−μ)²/(2σ²))`** — analytic two-parameter weighting of per-layer interventions, fixed-energy budget. *Replication cost*: trivial (closed form). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — replaces the H-1 grid search "which single layer?" with a 2D `(μ, σ)` grid.
- **Equal-budget allocation ablations** — compare uniform / random / box-filter / Gaussian at matched ‖α‖. *Replication cost*: 1h on cached H100 NPZs. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — isolates "shape" vs "total magnitude" cleanly, bears directly on H-1.
- **Last-non-pad-token activation extraction for contrastive pairs** — extract at last token of input prompt, not final-generation token. *Replication cost*: already done in pathway 11. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already in our pipeline as the prefill construction; F-2 uses essentially this pattern.
- **Hyperparameter validation split (25% reserved)** — paper reserves 25% of MASK for `(layer, α)` and `(μ, σ)` grids; reports on remaining 75%. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — current OOF 5-fold is similar in spirit; would adopt a held-out-by-problem split to avoid leakage.
- **External-LLM judge for output classification** — gpt-oss-20B at temp 1.0 maps generations to discrete labels using prompts from Ren et al. 2025 (MASK). *Replication cost*: not relevant to MATH-500 (we have ground-truth answers). *We'd plausibly run this*: no — math has objective labels.
- **LoRRA-style LoRA fine-tune comparison** — LoRA adapter on the same honest/dishonest contrastive targets used to form steering vectors. *Replication cost*: H100 day, custom dataset. *We'd plausibly run this*: no — out of scope and our F-2/F-9 work is explicitly post-hoc/test-time.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Steering is a depth-allocation problem"** — reframes activation steering from "which layer + what scalar?" to "what is the budget distribution `α(ℓ)` over depth?". Intersects H-1 directly: H-1's current scalar-α formulation should be upgraded to a 2-parameter (μ, σ) Gaussian schedule before the experiment is run.
- **Honesty ≠ knowledge** — paper uses MASK to decouple "knowing the answer" from "reporting the answer truthfully under pressure". Orthogonal to our project's MATH-500 K=1 framing (where the question *is* whether the model knows). Worth noting in PERSPECTIVES if we ever discuss why our setup intentionally collapses these two axes.
- **"Single-layer edits are degenerate"** — paper claims point-mass edits are a special case of a richer schedule. If true for steering, the same critique may apply to single-layer *probing* — directly motivates the F-2/F-9 multi-layer probe refutation.
- **"Smooth shape > flat or random shape, at fixed budget"** — implies the shape of depth allocation carries information independent of total magnitude. If we extend this to probing: a Gaussian-weighted ensemble of per-layer directions should beat both a single layer and a flat-weighted ensemble.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Steering direction is computed at the **last non-padding token** of the prompt (not final-generation token). Matches our prefill construction.
- Direction orientation: `d_ℓ` is sign-flipped to keep `(1/m)Σ⟨Δa_ℓ^(j), d_ℓ⟩ > 0` on the validation set. Trivial but easy to forget when the PCA gives an unsigned axis.
- Gaussian schedule centred at `μ = L/2` by default, `σ` swept; the paper finds optimal peak in mid-to-late depth (not at exactly L/2).
- Code: `https://github.com/marysia/gaussian-activation-steering` (mentioned in paper footnote 2). Worth pulling if we run the multi-layer probe to ensure the PCA orientation convention matches.
- Excludes the embedding layer in per-block enumeration.
- Best single-layer baseline is grid-searched on `(layer, α)` per model — for LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers) the best single-layer is layer 10, α = 2 — that's ~31% depth, NOT the 67% depth that L19/28-layer Qwen-2.5-1.5B corresponds to. This depth-fraction discrepancy is the single sharpest pointer that "best probing layer" and "best steering layer" may not coincide.

## 2512.07667 — Depth-Wise Activation Steering for Honest Language Models (Góral, Winkels, Basart; 2026)

**Relevance:** Direct methodological challenger to single-layer steering. Builds a per-layer contrastive PCA-1 direction `d_l` and an analytic Gaussian schedule `α_l` over depth, then shows that on seven open-weight LLMs (LLaMA, Qwen, Mistral) the Gaussian schedule beats single-layer steering at equal budget on the MASK honesty benchmark. **Key claim we tested:** "Gaussian depth schedule beats single-layer at fixed energy budget" — extended to our setting, this predicts that a multi-layer Gaussian-weighted probe beats single-layer L19 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B correctness AUROC.
