# Triage brief — 2603.01025 — One-Token Verification for Reasoning Correctness Estimation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01025
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — would be CONTRADICTED.**
   Mechanism: OTV's Figure 3 ablation explicitly tracks a "probe" baseline (last-layer hidden state → 3-layer MLP, no LoRA, no KV cache access) against LoRA-rank-{2,4,8,16,32} variants on AIME24/25. The `r=16` variant beats the probe by ≥3 pts MAJ@128 across N∈{4,8,16,32,64,128}, and gains scale monotonically with rank. The probe baseline is functionally equivalent to a final-layer linear DoM head — i.e. F-9's claim that single-layer L19 DoM exhausts the available correctness signal. OTV's gain over the probe is direct evidence that *multi-layer KV-cache structure carries non-redundant correctness information*.
   Falsifiable test: on cached P11 1.5B per-token L19 + multi-layer activations, compare an OTV-style multi-layer attention-pooled probe vs. the F-9 single-layer L19 DoM baseline on MATH-500. If multi-layer beats single-layer by >3 AUROC pts on the same 500 problems, F-9 flips from "redundant" to "single-layer leaves signal on the table".

2. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM is the strongest correctness predictor, *better than final-token*) — would be CONTRADICTED.**
   Mechanism: OTV's Table 4 (trace-level aggregation ablation) shows that scoring over the **last 100 tokens** with mean aggregation reaches 83.96 MAJ@128, vs. 75.78 for "all tokens" and ~80 for the full trace. The "verification works best at the tail" pattern is directly opposed to F-2's "prefill > final-token" headline (0.7731 prefill vs. 0.7186 final-token, a ~5.5-pt prefill advantage). OTV's pseudo-confidence linear ramp (`c_t = 0.5 + (y−0.5)·t/T`) explicitly *trains the verifier to be most certain at the end*, but Table 4 shows even at inference the late-token signal dominates.
   Falsifiable test: on cached Stage 4a per-token activations for 500 MATH-500 problems, compute supervised DoM AUROC at: (a) prefill L19, (b) final-token L19, (c) mean over last 100 tokens L19, (d) min over last 100 tokens L19. If (c) or (d) beats (a) by >2 pts, F-2's "prefill is the best probe location" claim is overturned.

3. **F-8 (Selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage = 71.6% on K=2.5 avg) — under-performance suggested.**
   Mechanism: OTV's Halve@300 strategy (Table 2) on Qwen3-4B-Instruct AIME24 hits 67.03% accuracy with only 4623 avg output tokens vs. Best-of-N=128's 73.44% at 5447 tokens — i.e. ~85% of Best-of-N accuracy at ~85% of cost. Translating to MATH-500-equivalent compute regime, OTV's selective-prediction Pareto frontier dominates F-8's prefill-DoM gating. F-8's 71.6%@50% number on a 48.6% base accuracy may be limited by single-layer probe expressivity, not by the underlying signal.
   Falsifiable test: implement an OTV-style multi-layer verifier on Qwen-1.5B + MATH-500 (training data: 8 traces per problem from a 50-problem held-in set), run the same 50%-coverage selective-prediction protocol, compare against F-8's 71.6%@50%. If OTV-on-1.5B beats 76% at the same coverage, F-8's signal-ceiling claim is refuted.

4. **F-3 (Prefill and final-token DoM are geometrically orthogonal, cos=0.046) — partial counter-evidence.**
   Mechanism: OTV's Figure 5 shows trace-level confidence trajectories that rise *monotonically and smoothly* from prefill (≈0.5) toward the final token for correct traces, and stay flat/decline for incorrect. A single coherent latent signal that grows continuously over 8000+ tokens is hard to reconcile with F-3's claim that the prefill-correctness direction and final-correctness direction are orthogonal. If they were truly orthogonal, OTV's trajectory should bifurcate or rotate, not slide monotonically.
   Falsifiable test: project the OTV `[ToT]` final-layer embedding back into the F-3 DoM frame at every position; if the trajectory stays inside the prefill-DoM × final-DoM 2-plane and rotates smoothly between them, F-3's "two orthogonal directions" framing is the wrong basis — it's a single curved trajectory in a low-dim subspace.

- **H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** ← OTV is decisively supervised and beats DeepConf (training-free token-entropy aggregation, internal); cuts against H-12.

- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling)** ← OTV is a mechanistic instantiation of C_exact (verify-then-route), with concrete token-budget numbers (~20% reduction at matched accuracy).

## Methodologies extracted

- **LoRA-gated verification pathway** — a binary gate `m_t ∈ {0,1}` toggles a LoRA update `W + m_t · R·S` per linear layer, so the base reasoner is bit-identical when `m_t=0`. Only the special `[ToT]` token has `m_t=1`. *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 to fine-tune on Qwen-1.5B with 1k MetaMathQA samples. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — single-step path to a "supervised verifier ceiling" against which F-2's 0.7731 can be benchmarked.

- **`[ToT]` (token of truth) + KV-cache probing** — special token inserted at any position; probes the cached `K^(l), V^(l)` of the prefix via LoRA-augmented attention, regression head on final-layer hidden state. *Replication cost*: 4h CPU prototype on cached activations (no training, just attention-pooling). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cleanly replaces our final-token-only DoM head.

- **Linear-ramp pseudo-confidence labels** — `c_t = 0.5 + (y−0.5)·t/T` interpolates from "uncertain at start" to "outcome at end". MSE loss on per-token confidence. *Replication cost*: 30min CPU label generation on cached MATH-500 traces. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — alternative to our binary correctness label that may help with mid-trace probes (cheap to test).

- **Triangular probe mask for parallel single-pass training** — insert `[ToT]_{1:T+1}` simultaneously at all positions; a triangular mask `M_{i,j} = 0 if i<j else -∞` enforces that probe `t+1` only attends to prefix `1:t`. Equivalent to T+1 independent single-token verifications in one forward pass. *Replication cost*: 2h CPU implementation against the existing extractor. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would let us sweep "prefill vs every-token" cheaply.

- **Trace-level aggregation = min/mean over last 100 tokens** — Table 4 ablates {all, last 10%, last 1600, last 400, last 100} × {max, mean, min} × {0%, 25%, 50%, 75% filter}. Best is mean/min over last 100 tokens with 50%–75% trace filtering. *Replication cost*: 1h CPU re-aggregation on existing per-token DoM scores. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applicable as-is to our current DoM trajectory data, no new model.

- **Semantics-preserving vs logic-breaking perturbation test** (Appendix E, Table 6) — same problem, modify trace via {shortening, lengthening, formatting changes, removing newlines} (semantics-preserving) vs. {changing numbers, altering logic, adding extra steps, corrupting final step} (logic-breaking). Verifier should be invariant to the former, drop on the latter. *Replication cost*: 4h CPU on cached MATH-500 traces with hand-written perturbations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct robustness test for our DoM probe; a refutation pathway for F-2.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Internal vs external" verification taxonomy** — paper splits verifiers into model-native internal (logit/calibration/probe heads) vs. external (separate reward models). OTV occupies a middle position: model-native architecture + LoRA + supervised on outcome. Maps cleanly onto our F-2 (probe = internal) vs. F-8 (selective prediction = downstream use). Frames our project as living entirely on the internal axis.

- **"Pseudo-confidence labels from outcome supervision"** — instead of process labels (which require expensive step-level annotation), derive token-level confidence targets from the final binary outcome via a deterministic ramp. This is cheaper than rStar-Math/Math-Shepherd but supervised — sits between H-12 (label-free) and process-reward models. Suggests we may be over-investing in F-2's prefill-only frame; the trace-distributed signal might be richer.

- **"Reasoner = verifier" via LoRA gating** — same model parameters, gate flips behavior. Argues against our implicit "verification needs a separate model" assumption (relevant to H-19 routing experiments).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Base reasoners frozen; only LoRA modules + 3-layer MLP regression head are trained.
- LoRA applied to Q, K, V projections at "selected layers" (paper does not enumerate which — Appendix A confirms layer set is a design knob; default reproduced from LlamaFactory configs).
- Default LoRA rank `r=16`; Figure 3 sweeps `r ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32}`. Diminishing returns past r=16.
- 3 epochs, lr `1e-4`, batch 128, LlamaFactory training stack.
- Per training instance: 8 reasoning traces sampled from the frozen base.
- Inference: 256 traces per question at temperature 1.0; subsets of {4,8,16,32,64,128} drawn for downstream voting.
- Aggregation windows differ across baselines: OTV uses last 100 tokens; DeepConf uses last 2048. Apples-to-apples requires matching the window.
- Code repo: not stated in the body. Authors are at SUSTech / CityU HK / Zhejiang — likely upcoming on Zhuang's GitHub.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2603.01025 — One-Token Verification for Reasoning Correctness Estimation (Zhuang et al., 2026)

**Relevance:** OTV is the closest published mechanistic alternative to our F-2 prefill DoM probe and F-8 selective prediction. It trains a LoRA-gated verifier with a special `[ToT]` token that probes the cached KV states of the prefix, and reports a 3-layer MLP regression head over the final-layer hidden state. Their "probe" baseline (last-layer + MLP, no LoRA) is structurally equivalent to our F-2 head — and they show it is the *floor*, not the ceiling: LoRA r=16 across multiple layers beats it by >3 points MAJ@128 on AIME. Their Table 4 also reports that mean/min aggregation over the last 100 tokens dominates prefill-only scoring, directly opposing F-2's "prefill > final-token" headline. Same problem class (math reasoning correctness estimation), overlapping model family (Qwen3, MetaMath-LLaMA, Mistral), comparable scale (4B–32B vs. our 1.5B–7B).

**Key claim we tested:** Multi-layer LoRA-gated probe of the KV cache extracts non-redundant correctness signal beyond what a single-layer linear DoM head can extract. Late-trajectory aggregation (last 100 tokens) dominates prefill aggregation.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — three replicable intermediates queued (P11-FE1025 aggregation sweep, P11-FE1026 OTV-on-1.5B replication, P11-FE1027 perturbation sensitivity). The aggregation sweep is the cheapest direct test of F-2 and is unblocked.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE1025, P11-FE1026, P11-FE1027, H-1025, H-1026, H-1027.
