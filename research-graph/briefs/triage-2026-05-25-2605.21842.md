# Triage brief — 2605.21842 — Energy-Gated Attention: Spectral Salience as an Inductive Bias for Transformer Attention

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21842
Triaged: 2026-05-25

## Refutations

**R1 — Refutes F-2 (L19 prefill DoM as correctness predictor) if the "dominant spectral mode" learned by EGA diverges from DoM.**
The paper claims a single learned linear projection discovers the "dominant spectral mode" of the embedding field — essentially the first principal component of spectral energy (§3.2, §6). Our F-2 already established cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216, treating DoM as approximately the supervised version of the covariance PC1. If the EGA energy projection direction, trained on an attention-gating objective rather than correctness supervision, discovers a spectral-energy-dominant direction that is *orthogonal* to both DoM and PC1, it would mean the correctness-predictive direction is not the dominant spectral mode of the residual stream — it is task-specific rather than a fundamental property of the representation geometry. This would weaken the "DoM ≈ PC1 captures the dominant variance axis" interpretation of F-2. Falsifiable by: computing the EGA energy projection on Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 prefill activations and measuring cosine similarity to DoM and PC1.

**R2 — Refutes F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal) if spectral energy redistribution explains away breathing as an artifact of non-uniform token importance.**
The paper frames token-level information density through spectral energy: ~36% of tokens are "coherent structures" (high-energy content words) and ~64% are "turbulent background" (function words, filler). If the dimensional expansion/contraction we call "breathing" (F-1) is explained entirely by the mixture of high-energy and low-energy tokens at each position — i.e., "breathing" is just the spectral energy landscape fluctuating as context builds up, not a genuine geometric phase transition — then F-1's universality claim becomes a restatement of unequal token information density, which is trivial. Falsifiable by: partitioning L19 prefill tokens by EGA-style energy score, computing participation ratio separately within high-energy and low-energy subpopulations, and checking whether breathing persists within each subpopulation or only appears in the mixture.

**R3 — Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer DoM) if multi-scale spectral decomposition of trajectory features recovers signal that single-layer DoM misses.**
The paper's ablation shows EGA-C (causal convolution with 4 filter scales) matches EGA-1 at +0.100 vs +0.103, and learned scale weights converge to near-uniform [0.226, 0.253, 0.260, 0.260] across scales. They argue this proves multi-scale structure exists but a single projection captures it. However, CoE-60 trajectory features (F-9) *are* a multi-scale representation (60 layers × time), and we declared them redundant with single-layer DoM. If a wavelet/multi-scale spectral decomposition of CoE-60 trajectories — rather than simple concatenation — extracts non-redundant signal components at specific temporal scales that single-layer DoM cannot access, this would refute F-9. Falsifiable by: applying CWT or DWT to the 60-layer CoE trajectory per problem and checking whether wavelet-coefficient features lift AUROC above 0.7731.

**R4 — Refutes H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence) if spectral energy gating provides a simpler, better-grounded formalism than the Sharpness Dimension framing.**
If the spectral energy framework (Wiener-Khinchin, POD criterion) subsumes the Sharpness Dimension construct referenced in H-4 — i.e., if the EGA energy gate at inference time captures the same information as SD with less theoretical overhead — then H-4's proposed formal correspondence becomes moot. The energy framework would be the simpler, more direct formalization. Falsifiable by: computing both SD and EGA energy scores on L19 activations and measuring mutual information.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core finding is that a single learned linear projection (the energy direction) captures the dominant spectral mode of embeddings. This is structurally identical to our finding that DoM ≈ PC1 (cos = 0.9216) is a single dominant direction predicting correctness. The difference: their projection is learned for attention gating, ours is learned for correctness supervision. Whether these converge to the same direction is an empirical question with direct implications for F-2's mechanistic interpretation.

- **F-10 (PH null — topology adds nothing beyond covariance):** The paper explicitly uses POD/PCA-style spectral energy ordering as its theoretical backbone, with no topological machinery. Their success with a purely covariance-based approach reinforces F-10's conclusion that the useful geometric structure in residual streams is captured by covariance/spectral methods, not by persistent homology.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature):** The τ ≈ 0.35 threshold identifies ~36% of tokens as "coherent structures." If applied to our L19 prefill activations, this token-level energy classification could provide an alternative view of D-bucket membership — are the problems in D-bucket (uncertain difficulty) those with ambiguous spectral energy profiles, where the coherent/background boundary is blurry?

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6%):** The energy gate produces a per-token "salience" score that suppresses ~64% of tokens. If adapted to a per-problem aggregation, this spectral-energy-based salience could serve as an alternative abstention criterion — compare its selective-prediction curve against the DoM-based one that yields 71.6% at 50% coverage.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes familiarity vs decomposability):** The paper's "coherent structure" / "turbulent background" distinction at the token level maps onto a question about what the prefill signal actually encodes. If high-energy tokens correspond to "familiar" subexpressions and low-energy tokens to "novel" structure, the energy profile of a problem's prefill could encode H-5's familiarity dimension.

- **Cov-spectrum AUROC 0.7928 (FE881):** The paper's spectral-energy framework provides a principled theoretical grounding for why log-eigenvalues of the covariance matrix predict correctness: they are literally the spectral energy distribution of the residual stream. The cov-spectrum probe is computing the energy spectrum that EGA's theory says should be informative.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Spectral energy projection (EGA-1 gate)** — Single learned linear projection w_proj^T x_j estimates spectrally-weighted energy of each token embedding. Z-normalized, sigmoid-thresholded at τ to produce a 0–1 gate.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on cached NPZs (just a linear projection + normalization on 500×1536 matrix).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — trivially applicable to cached L19 prefill activations as a per-token importance weighting.

- **POD energy-ordering criterion** — Retain modes whose energy exceeds threshold, suppress the rest. Applied as smooth sigmoid gate rather than hard cutoff.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (threshold sweep on PCA eigenvalues we already have).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already have the PCA decomposition; the POD criterion is just a principled way to select the eigenvalue cutoff.

- **Morlet CWT scalogram of embeddings** — Continuous wavelet transform across embedding dimensions, visualizing multi-scale spectral energy distribution of token sequences.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU with PyWavelets.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — apply to cached L19 activations to visualize multi-scale energy structure across MATH-500 problems.

- **Wavelet family ablation (fixed Daubechies db2/db4 vs learned)** — Systematic comparison of fixed-structure vs data-adaptive energy estimators. Finding: learned always beats fixed; db2 > db4 > Morlet among fixed.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU to run db2/db4/Morlet projections on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap and directly tests whether structured spectral bases add anything for our correctness-prediction task (not just attention gating).

- **Z-normalization stability analysis** — Track convergence of threshold τ across random seeds to identify stable "linguistic constants."
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (compute energy projection, z-normalize, find empirical threshold on cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — check if a stable τ emerges in our correctness/incorrectness context.

## Approaches & framings

- **Coherent structures in embedding space (turbulence analogy):** Tokens are analogized to positions in a turbulent flow field — some carry disproportionate "energy" (information) and govern "transport" (attention flow). This reframes residual stream geometry from a static PCA view to a dynamic energy-transport view. Intersects our F-1 breathing finding: breathing could be the periodic emergence and dissolution of coherent structures.

- **Bottom-up vs top-down attention (neuroscience framing):** Standard attention = top-down (query selects what matches). EGA = bottom-up (salience selects what matters independently of query). This dual-process framing could apply to our prefill vs final-token orthogonality (F-3): prefill DoM might capture bottom-up salience (content-driven, query-independent), while final-token DoM captures top-down task-directed attention.

- **Spectral energy as token importance weighting for aggregation:** Rather than uniform mean pooling across tokens, weight by spectral energy. This directly connects to the admission note's suggestion about spectral-energy-weighted mean pooling for DoM AUROC.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- EGA adds only d+2 parameters per attention head (d for w_proj, one each for τ and α).
- Z-normalization before sigmoid is essential — without it, raw energy values vary across layers making τ layer-dependent.
- Gate renormalization (step 4 of Algorithm 1) preserves sum-to-one attention weights — prevents the gate from scaling down output magnitude.
- Code: https://github.com/AthanasiosZeris/energy-gated-attention
- α converges to ~2.2 (sigmoid sharpness), τ converges to ~0.35, regardless of initialization in [-0.5, 0.5] and [1.0, 5.0] respectively.
- Causality constraint satisfied trivially for per-position projections; causal left-only padding needed only for convolutional variants.
