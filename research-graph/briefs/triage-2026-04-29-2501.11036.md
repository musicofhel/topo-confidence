# Triage brief — 2501.11036 — LF-Steering: Latent Feature Activation Steering for Enhancing Semantic Consistency in Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11036
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **H-1 (per-position raw DoM steering on MATH-500) may be ceiling-limited by polysemanticity.**
   Mechanism: LF-Steering's Table 3 directly compares "neuron-level" steering (their equivalent of our raw L19 DoM-direction injection — ours is exactly the kind of layer-wide hidden-state direction they identify as polysemantic) against "feature-level" steering (SAE-disentangled features). On RobustBOOLQ the gap is 16% accuracy (50.5 vs 66.4) and the std-dev gap is 5.7 (9.07 vs 3.39). If the same polysemanticity holds for L19 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B, the H-1 steering experiment could land at a small-or-null effect simply because the L19 hidden state encodes many non-correctness features that all rotate together when we inject `α·w`. Falsifiable: run H-1 with raw DoM steering AND with an SAE-disentangled "DoM-feature" steering on Qwen-1.5B MATH-500; expect feature-level effect size to dominate and raw-direction to fail or barely budge accuracy.

2. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) might be an artifact of polysemantic mixing rather than a genuine geometric fact.**
   Mechanism: prefill and final-token L19 hidden states almost certainly encode different mixes of polysemantic features (instruction-following, syntax, partial-answer formation, ...). The raw mean-difference direction (DoM) absorbs all of those. Two raw DoMs computed at different positions can be nearly orthogonal even if the underlying *correctness*-related features are mostly shared. Under SAE decomposition the "key features" responsible for correctness may overlap heavily across prefill and final token, making cos(prefill, final) at the feature level much greater than 0.046. Falsifiable: train a TopK SAE on Qwen-1.5B L19 (or use a public Qwen SAE), encode prefill and final-token activations from cached MATH-500 NPZs, identify the per-position correctness-relevant feature index sets via the LF-Steering thresholding recipe, compute Jaccard or cosine on the shared-feature mass; predict ≥0.3 overlap (vs 0.046 raw cosine).

3. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 → "best correctness predictor") may be substantially beatable by SAE-feature classification at the same layer.**
   Mechanism: F-2 takes the *single* DoM direction as the L19 correctness probe. LF-Steering's premise is that any single layer-wide direction collides with polysemantic features and leaves signal on the table. Specifically: if the SAE decomposes L19 into 65,536 sparse features and ~10–100 of them carry correctness signal disentangled from style/topic/length, then a sparse-feature logistic probe should beat the raw DoM probe. Falsifiable: train (or load a pretrained) Qwen-1.5B SAE on L19 hidden states, run the LF-Steering "key features" locator on (correct, incorrect) MATH-500 pairs from cached H100 NPZs, fit a logistic probe on the feature mask, score 5-fold OOF AUROC vs 0.7731. If feature-level AUROC ≥ 0.83, F-2 should be reframed as "raw L19 DoM is the best *raw-direction* predictor" rather than the best L19 predictor.

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) is broader than the LF-Steering authors' own caveat: they explicitly note "addressing this issue at a single layer may offer only a partial solution."**
   Mechanism: LF-Steering localizes everything to top-1 layer and acknowledges this as a limitation; their conclusion flags multi-layer feature circuits as future work (Marks 2024 sparse feature circuits). If multi-layer SAE features substantially outperform single-layer SAE features for correctness on MATH-500, F-9's "single-layer DoM captures all signal CoE-60 captures" claim could be a low-bar threshold that masks a richer multi-layer feature graph. Falsifiable: compare single-layer (L19) vs multi-layer (e.g., L17–L24 union) SAE-feature correctness probes on cached Qwen-1.5B activations; if multi-layer feature AUROC ≥ single-layer + 0.03 with non-overlapping feature sets, F-9 needs a "single layer is sufficient *for raw directions*" qualifier.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering on MATH-500)** ↔ LF-Steering is exactly the right method-comparison cousin. Their `α=20`, `t=0.10` setup is a free reference point for our α-sweep on `{-4,-2,-1,0,1,2,4}` and tells us our α range is probably an order of magnitude too small for *raw* steering. Numerical anchor: their feature-level steering moved RobustBOOLQ accuracy by +20.0 points from a 46.4% baseline. Comparable to "lift MATH-500 K=1 from 48.6% (1.5B) baseline" magnitude H-1 hopes for.
- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** ↔ LF-Steering's "Top-1 transformer layer locating" via per-layer concatenated-pair classifier (Yang et al. 2024 method) is a methodologically distinct way to identify which layer carries the signal. They find layers 17–32 in Llama2-7B (mid-to-final). For 1.5B/28-layer Qwen, layer 19 sits in the same mid-to-final band — corroborating, not contradicting, F-2's choice of L19. METHOD_DIFFERS edge.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ LF-Steering operates only on last-token feature representations to locate "key features." That they get strong steering effects from a last-token-only signal is *consistent* with our observation that final-token L19 DoM also carries 0.7186 AUROC, but it makes no statement about prefill. Their success at last-token does not refute F-3, but the polysemanticity argument (Refutation 2 above) does.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** ↔ LF-Steering's interpretation table (Table 2) shows manually-named features like "looking for answer" (15780) and "location-related questions" (26247). If D-bucket has a collective signature, an SAE decomposition might localize it to a small feature subset interpretable as "problem-class characteristic," potentially extending F-7 with mechanistic feature-level evidence.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** ↔ LF-Steering rejects the head-level granularity (their SCS baseline) in favor of feature-level, demonstrating feature-level outperforms head-level by ≥4–9% accuracy on every dataset. If we run H-13 we should plan a feature-level extension *immediately*, since head-level alone is now a weak comparison.

## Methodologies extracted

- **TopK SAE pretraining (Gao et al. 2024 method, applied here)** — train a TopK sparse autoencoder mapping 4096-dim hidden states → 65,536-dim feature space, k=192 retained, on 850K open-domain RedPajama subset, 32K steps, lr 2e-4. *Replication cost*: H100-week for Qwen-1.5B/7B from scratch; **hours** if a pre-trained Qwen SAE (e.g., Goodfire's Ember or community SAE-lens checkpoints) can be reused. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — but only via a pre-trained checkpoint; from-scratch SAE training is out-of-budget for a single triage refutation.
- **Top-1 transformer layer locating via per-layer classifier (Yang et al. 2024)** — for each layer, train a binary classifier on concatenated `[h(m), h(n)]` from semantically-equivalent prompt pairs `(m, n)` with consistency label `l`; pick layer with highest test accuracy. *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached activations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — as a methodological alternative to our PR-by-layer / DoM-AUROC-by-layer search; cheap sanity check that L19 is the right layer for *correctness* under this classifier-driven definition of "the right layer."
- **Key feature locating via average feature difference + threshold** — feed (correct, rephrased-incorrect) contrastive pairs `(u, v)` through pretrained SAE, take last-token features `z(u), z(v)`, compute mean `|z(u) - z(v)|` over the 500-pair dataset, threshold at `t = 0.10` to get key feature index set `I`. *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached activations + a Qwen SAE checkpoint. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the smallest concrete cross-check for Refutation 3.
- **Feature-level activation steering at inference** — encode hidden state through SAE, for each `i ∈ I` with `z_i(q) ≠ 0`, set `z_i(q) ← z_i(q) + α · g_i` where `g_i` is the per-feature average difference, decode back through SAE decoder, replace the layer's hidden state. `α = 20`. *Replication cost*: H100-day for full MATH-500 sweep. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly competes with H-1's raw DoM steering; would strengthen H-1 to "DoM steering on disentangled features moves MATH-500 accuracy."
- **Out-of-domain locality testing** — measure whether steering hurts unrelated tasks (AG News, IMDB, CNN/Daily Mail, XSum). LF-Steering shows their method is OOD-neutral while ActAdd hurts AG News by 2.8 points. *Replication cost*: 2h H100. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required regularizer for any H-1 "DoM steering moves accuracy" claim; without it we cannot rule out cross-domain damage.
- **Hyperparameter sweep on `t` and `α`** — `t ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30}`, `α ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50}`. They report peak at `t=0.10, α=20`, with extreme α=50 degrading. *Replication cost*: included in the sweep above. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — informs our H-1 α range, which is currently `{-4,-2,-1,0,1,2,4}` and may be 5–10× too small.

## Approaches & framings

- **Polysemanticity as the explanation for raw-direction probes' ceiling.** Reframes our F-2 as "single L19 direction is the best *single direction*, not the best L19 signal." Useful as a counterfactual frame for narrative writeup and for any future P11 ceiling discussion.
- **"Grey-box" vs "black-box" steering taxonomy.** Useful framing language if Pathway 11 is ever written up as a paper — situates DoM probing/steering as a grey-box, in contrast to SFT-based correctness alignment.
- **Coarse-to-fine localization** (layer-first → feature-within-layer). Cleanly nests with our pathway-by-layer investigation; if SAE features are tractable for Qwen, "feature-within-L19" becomes a natural P11 sub-pathway.
- **Locality (OOD-neutrality) as a steering-method regularizer.** The paper makes a strong case that locality should be reported alongside any steering result. Recommends a tightening of H-1's success criterion from "moves MATH-500 accuracy" to "moves MATH-500 accuracy with bounded OOD degradation" before any steering claim is promoted to F-status.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- TopK SAE: 4096→65536, k=192. RedPajama 850K subset, 32K steps, lr 2e-4, batch 1/GPU on 8×V100 32GB. Total ≈4 GPU-days.
- Steering hyperparameters: feature-difference threshold `t = 0.10`, steering strength `α = 20`. α range tested: [5, 50], peak at 20, degrades by α=50.
- Layer-locating dataset: 500 (m, n, l) instances from Yang et al. 2024, 4:1 train:test split. Per-layer linear classifier on concatenated hidden states.
- Key feature locator: `g_i = (1/|D_f|) Σ |z_i(u) - z_i(v)|`, `I = {i : g_i > t}`. Steering bias `b_i = g_i` for `i ∈ I` and `z_i(q) ≠ 0`.
- Feature interpretation done manually post-hoc on top-ranked feature IDs (Table 2).
- No code link in the paper. SCS baseline code: github.com/nrimsky/CAA references, but LF-Steering itself has no public repo flagged in the manuscript.
