# Triage brief — 2506.18831 — STU-PID: Steering Token Usage via PID Controller for Efficient Large Language Model Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18831
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 ("L19 prefill DoM is correctness-predictive at AUROC 0.7731") — by mechanism of "the same layer mostly encodes redundancy/length, not correctness."**
   STU-PID extracts a difference-of-means direction `v = E[h_L | redundant chunk] − E[h_L | required chunk]` at **layer 20** of R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (same model family as ours, neighbouring layer to our L19) from a *24-token chunk mean-pool* and a *logistic regression* and shows the resulting axis is strong enough to drive +3.9 pp end-task accuracy with a simple PID gain. Mechanism: their classifier separates "redundant CoT chunk" vs "required CoT chunk" using exactly the activation regime we attribute to correctness. **Falsifiable test:** train STU-PID's redundancy classifier on labelled MATH-500 chunks (or auto-label by "tokens after first valid \\boxed{}"), evaluate on our cached pathway11_h100 prefill+CoT activations at L19, and check (a) cos(STU-PID redundancy-axis, our F-2 prefill DoM) and (b) the redundancy-classifier's AUROC for MATH-500 *correctness*. If cos > 0.4 or AUROC > 0.65, F-2 is partially explained by length/redundancy, not pure-correctness.

2. **Refutes F-3 ("Prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal, cos=0.046") — by demonstrating a single content axis that is stable across the trajectory.**
   STU-PID uses *one* static direction (computed once, layer-20) and modulates its *gain* across timesteps via PID. The system works (+3.9 pp / −23% tokens), which means a content-bearing axis at L20 retains predictive utility from the very first chunk through the final answer span. This contradicts the strong reading of F-3 — that the directional bases at prefill and at final token are independent. F-3 may be axis-specific (correctness rotates) while a redundancy axis does not. **Falsifiable test:** repeat the STU-PID protocol on MATH-500 1024-tok using *only the prefill DoM* as the steering axis (not redundancy axis); if it succeeds at GSM8K-comparable Δacc, the F-3 orthogonality is a property of the correctness axis specifically and would be an existence proof that L19 hosts ≥2 nearly-orthogonal task-relevant directions.

3. **Refutes H-17 ("DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed") — by demonstrating non-rotating, content-grounded axis behavior.**
   If DoM rotation were purely RoPE-mechanical, a *static* steering axis applied at every position should degrade monotonically with position. STU-PID applies a static direction with a non-trivial PID gain over a 1000-token trajectory and yields a coherent token-reduction signal, suggesting the redundancy axis is **not** RoPE-rotated out of relevance. **Falsifiable test:** measure the inner product `<h_t, v_redundancy>` versus position t in our cached generations; if it has no RoPE-style oscillation (unlike our DoM), H-17 is wrong about generality and right only about the correctness axis.

4. **Refutes H-1 ("Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy") — by predicting confound between redundancy-suppression and accuracy-improvement.**
   STU-PID gets +3.9 pp accuracy *primarily by suppressing redundant reasoning*, not by injecting "correct-answer" bias. If our planned per-position DoM-steering experiment for H-1 also gets +Δacc, we cannot tell whether the gain came from "more correct CoT" or "less wasted CoT." **Falsifiable test:** in the H-1 protocol, gate on equal-token-budget vs free-budget conditions. If the +Δacc disappears at fixed token budget, the steering is acting through the redundancy channel, not the correctness channel — H-1 needs reframing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PID-controlled steering gain.** Closed-loop intervention: error `e_t = pred,t − p_target` (with p_target = 0.5), gains KP=0.05, KI=0.001, KD=0.001. Streaming controller on a chunk-level redundancy probability.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU once an L19 redundancy classifier exists; <1 H100-day to redo on MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly addresses H-1 with a less brittle steering schedule than fixed-gain.
- **Chunk-level redundancy classifier.** 24-token sliding chunks, mean-pool layer-20 hidden states, logistic regression with SGD + log loss, trained on labeled GSM8K chunks (label = "redundant" if downstream of first valid answer; "required" otherwise).
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached pathway11_h100 activations. Labels need a small auto-labeler ("first \\boxed{} pass").
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — supplies a streaming abstention probe for F-8 extension.
- **Difference-of-means steering vector at a single mid-layer.** Same DoM construction we use; their L=20, ours L=19. They condition on redundant-vs-required, we condition on correct-vs-incorrect.
  *Replication cost*: trivial; we already have the extractor frozen.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — produces a second axis at L19/L20 we can dot against our F-2 DoM (refutation #1's diagnostic).
- **Static-direction + dynamic-gain steering ("STU-PID schedule").** Combines the two above. Inference-time only, no fine-tune.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2 H100-days for full MATH-500 sweep with K=1.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** if refutation #1's diagnostic comes back ambiguous — the schedule itself becomes the H-1 protocol of choice.
- **Token-budget-vs-accuracy reporting.** They report (raw_tokens → steered_tokens, raw_acc → steered_acc) jointly; equivalent to our `prefill_gated_compute` style of reporting at K=2.5.
  *Replication cost*: zero, just a reporting convention to adopt.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — apply to all H-1 / H-19 results.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Redundancy axis" as a distinct functional direction in the residual stream**, separable from "correctness axis." Reframes our DoM scholarship as one direction among several layer-20-resident task signals — relevant to F-2/F-3, since "DoM" as a label conflates these.
- **PID control as a principled prior for inference-time intervention scheduling.** Replaces the fixed-gain implicit assumption in our HYPOTHESES (H-1) with a control-theoretic schedule. Adds derivative and integral terms — only D matters when classifier output spikes; only I matters for sustained over/under-shoot.
- **Streaming abstention vs episodic abstention.** F-8 frames abstention as a per-problem decision; STU-PID frames it as a per-chunk gain. The two are dual: integrating PID gain over a trajectory is a soft selective-prediction signal.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **GSM8K (test subset, 77 problems).** ~1000-token CoT; HF-public; license CC-BY-4.0. **Applicable? partial** — it's a different task family from MATH-500 (easier, shorter CoT) but well-suited for direct STU-PID replication. Useful as a sanity-check rig for our pipeline's port to GSM8K.
- **GSM8K (training subset, for chunk classifier).** Same. Applicable for training the redundancy classifier we'd port.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Model: **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B** (note: this is *R1-distilled*, not vanilla Qwen-2.5-1.5B; activations may differ from our F-2 evidence base — important caveat).
- Steering layer: **L=20** (not L19; we should sweep both).
- Chunk size: **24 tokens**.
- PID gains: **KP=0.05, KI=0.001, KD=0.001**, p_target = 0.5.
- Classifier: SGD logistic regression on mean-pooled hidden states; trivial to train.
- Direction: difference-of-means; ~few-hundred labelled chunks suffice (SEAL-style).
- Code/repo: prior admission note records `https://github.com/arambharadwaj/pid_steering` (verify before relying on it).
- No fine-tune, no RL, no extra forward passes — purely inference-time.

## 2506.18831 — STU-PID: Steering Token Usage via PID Controller for Efficient Large Language Model Reasoning (Bharadwaj, 2025)

**Relevance:** Same model family as our F-2 evidence base (R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B vs Qwen-2.5-1.5B), same layer regime (L20 vs L19), same direction-extraction method (difference-of-means). Differs in label axis (redundant-vs-required CoT, not correct-vs-incorrect) and intervention schedule (PID-gated dynamic gain, not fixed). Directly informs H-1 (per-position DoM steering) and H-19 (verification routing). Provides a control-theoretic prior for inference-time steering and a streaming dual to F-8's selective prediction.

**Key claim we tested:** A static, single-layer, mean-pooled difference-of-means direction at a mid-layer (L20) is sufficient to drive a closed-loop PID-gated steering schedule that simultaneously *raises* accuracy (+3.9 pp) and *cuts* tokens (−23%) on GSM8K — without any fine-tune or extra forward passes.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** The same model family and same DoM construction as our F-2 evidence base means refutation tests are cheap. P11-FE73 (cos and AUROC of redundancy axis vs F-2 DoM) and P11-FE76 (token-budget-controlled F-8 re-analysis) are both runnable on cached NPZs in under 1 hour CPU; P11-FE75 (full MATH-500 replication) is 1–2 H100-days. Outcomes will determine whether STU-PID is REPLICATED, PARTIALLY CONFIRMED (works on GSM8K only), or CONTRADICTED (F-2 axis is the correctness axis, redundancy axis is distinct and STU-PID's gain comes from a different lever).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, H-73, H-74, H-1, H-17, H-19.
