# Triage brief — 2409.05084 — Adaptive k-nearest neighbor classifier based on the local estimation of the shape operator

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05084
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) via curvature-adaptive nonlinear classification.** The kK-NN paper demonstrates that local Gaussian curvature—estimated from the shape operator S_i = −H_i·Σ_i—can adaptively size neighborhoods for classification, outperforming fixed-k baselines especially on small datasets. Our 500-sample × 1536-dim L19 prefill activation matrix is exactly the regime where kK-NN shows its largest advantage (Table 2: gains of 5–15 balanced accuracy points on datasets with n < 500). If kK-NN applied to the raw 1536-dim L19 prefill activations (or a PCA-reduced subspace) achieves AUROC substantially above 0.7731, it would show the correctness signal has local nonlinear structure the linear DoM probe misses. *Mechanism:* curvature-adaptive neighborhoods capture decision-boundary curvature that a single projection direction cannot. *Falsifiable experiment:* run kK-NN on cached L19 prefill activations with 5-fold CV, compare balanced accuracy and AUROC to logistic regression DoM baseline.

2. **Refutes F-10 (PH null) by providing a non-PH geometric signal that still exceeds covariance baselines.** F-10 established that persistent homology summary statistics are indistinguishable from a Gaussian null on residual streams. But the shape operator's local Gaussian curvature (det(S_i)) is a differential-geometric quantity, not a topological one—it measures extrinsic curvature of the data manifold at each point. If per-sample curvature scores from the L19 activation cloud correlate with correctness (e.g., incorrect samples occupy higher-curvature regions), this would refute the broader interpretation of F-10 that "geometry beyond covariance doesn't help"—the correct reading would narrow to "PH-specific topology doesn't help, but differential-geometric curvature does." *Mechanism:* shape operator curvature is sensitive to local manifold bending that PH barcodes average out. *Falsifiable experiment:* compute det(S_i) at each of the 500 L19 prefill activation points, correlate with correct/incorrect labels, compute AUROC of the curvature feature alone.

3. **Refutes H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket) via curvature-density disentanglement.** H-7 hypothesizes that density-based outlier detection can identify the D-bucket (problems where the model generates a wrong answer with high confidence). The kK-NN paper's curvature measure is related to but distinct from density: curvature captures how rapidly the local tangent space changes, while density measures how many points are nearby. If curvature-based neighborhood pruning isolates D-bucket problems better than LOF/DBSCAN-style density outlier detection, it would suggest the D-bucket geometric signature (F-7) is curvature-driven rather than density-driven—reframing the hypothesis. *Mechanism:* kK-NN's curvature quantization into 10 bins creates a per-sample geometric score that can separate manifold-boundary points from interior points regardless of local density. *Falsifiable experiment:* compute per-sample curvature scores, compare D-bucket isolation (precision/recall) to LOF on the same 500 L19 prefill activations.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The kK-NN classifier is a direct alternative to the logistic-regression probe over L19 activations. The paper's Algorithm 1 computes local covariance matrices Σ_i at each point—exactly the same object whose eigenstructure drives our cov-spectrum probe (AUROC 0.7928, FE881). The kK-NN uses Σ_i for curvature estimation rather than spectral features, offering a complementary use of the same matrix.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature):** The paper's curvature quantization into 10 bins produces a per-sample geometric "score" that could serve as a D-bucket detector. High-curvature points (score 8–9) may correspond to the geometrically distinctive D-bucket samples if they sit on sharp manifold folds.

- **F-10 (PH null):** The shape operator is a differential-geometric object, not a topological one. It measures curvature of the embedding (second fundamental form), which is orthogonal to the PH barcodes tested in F-10. A positive curvature result would narrow F-10 to "topological summaries don't help" rather than "all geometry beyond covariance is useless."

- **H-26 (Non-linear probe crosses topological phase transition above linear DoM AUROC):** kK-NN is a non-parametric nonlinear classifier. If it substantially exceeds 0.7731, this would be evidence for H-26's prediction that nonlinearity helps, without requiring a neural probe.

- **cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216:** The paper notes that for high-dimensional data, PCA dimensionality reduction before curvature estimation is advisable (Section 2.2, complexity analysis). Running kK-NN on the PC1–PC20 subspace (same as cov-spectrum probe, AUROC 0.7928) would test whether curvature-adaptive neighborhoods add value beyond the spectral features themselves.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Shape operator curvature estimation (Algorithm 1)** — Estimates local Gaussian curvature at each data point via S_i = −H_i·Σ_i where Σ_i is the local covariance from the k-NN patch and H_i is a Hessian approximation built from the eigenvectors of Σ_i (Hessian eigenmaps-style). Curvature K_i = det(S_i).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU for 500 × 1536 (k = log₂(500) ≈ 9, covariance is 1536×1536 per patch — dominated by eigendecomposition, but only 500 patches).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 prefill activations with no new data needed.

- **Curvature-adaptive kK-NN classifier** — Quantizes per-sample curvatures into 10 bins, prunes k-NN graph edges based on curvature score (high curvature → fewer neighbors → tighter locality). Classification by majority vote in the pruned neighborhood.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (once curvatures are computed, the k-NN classification itself is trivial).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — their GitHub implementation (alexandrelevada/kkNN) is publicly available; adapt to our binary correct/incorrect labels on L19 activations.

- **Local Hessian approximation via Hessian eigenmaps** — Builds matrix X_i = [1, U_1, ..., U_m, U_1², ..., U_m², U_1×U_2, ...] from local covariance eigenvectors, extracts the quadratic-term subblock as the Hessian estimator. This is the Donoho & Grimes (2003) Hessian eigenmap technique repurposed for curvature estimation.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (part of the shape operator pipeline).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — intrinsic to the shape operator computation.

- **Inverse covariance as metric tensor approximation** — Approximates the first fundamental form I_i ≈ Σ_i⁻¹ at each k-NN patch. This gives a local Mahalanobis-distance interpretation of the data manifold.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (just patch-level covariance inversion, already computed for shape operator).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the local precision matrices Σ_i⁻¹ at L19 activation points could themselves be informative features (e.g., their trace or spectral norms might correlate with correctness).

## Approaches & framings

- **Data manifold as embedded Riemannian surface.** The paper treats the data cloud as samples from an embedded submanifold of ℝ^m, with first/second fundamental forms estimated locally from k-NN patches. This reframes our L19 activation cloud from "a point cloud in ℝ^1536" to "a noisy sample from a lower-dimensional data manifold with measurable curvature." Intersects F-10 (PH null) by offering a curvature-based geometry lens that is strictly different from the topological lens PH provides.

- **Curvature as adaptive bandwidth selector.** Rather than using curvature as a direct feature, the paper uses it to control classifier smoothness — high curvature → local decisions, low curvature → global decisions. This is a meta-methodological insight: the correctness signal in L19 activations might be nonstationary, requiring different classifier locality in different parts of the activation space.

- **Neighborhood pruning as outlier isolation.** The paper notes that high-curvature points often correspond to outliers, and the kK-NN naturally isolates them by shrinking their neighborhoods. This directly maps to the D-bucket detection problem (F-7), reframing outlier detection from density-based (LOF, DBSCAN) to curvature-based.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- k is set to log₂(n), where n is number of samples. For our n=500, k ≈ 9.
- Curvatures are quantized into 10 equally-spaced bins (0.1 intervals on [0,1] after normalization).
- For high-dimensional data (m >> n), PCA/LDA dimensionality reduction is applied first (Section 3, Table 3). Our case (m=1536, n=500) is borderline m>>n — PCA to ~50–100 dims may be needed before curvature estimation.
- Complexity is O(n²m²) for m>n, so PCA reduction from 1536 to ~50 dims would bring this to ~O(500² × 50²) ≈ 6.25×10⁸ — feasible on CPU.
- Python implementation: https://github.com/alexandrelevada/kkNN
- The Hessian eigenmap matrix X_i has 1 + m + m(m+1)/2 columns — for m=1536 this is ~1.2M columns, which is infeasible. PCA reduction is mandatory.
