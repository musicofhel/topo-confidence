# Triage brief — 2310.17230 — Codebook Features: Sparse and Discrete Interpretability for Neural Networks

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17230
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046)** — *would be reframed as a measurement artifact.*
   Mechanism: if correctness-relevant features in the residual stream are
   sparse-discrete codes (the paper's central claim, Tamkin §3.1) rather than
   dense directions, then the "prefill DoM" and "final-token DoM" are not
   intrinsic axes — they are linear shadows of two different code-population
   mixtures averaged across many problems. Two such averages can land in
   near-orthogonal directions even when they activate *highly overlapping*
   sets of underlying codes, simply because each code's centroid contributes
   in a different proportion at the two positions. F-3's "orthogonal circuits"
   reading is not implied by cos = 0.046 once you accept the discrete-code
   ontology.
   Falsifiable experiment: retrofit Qwen-2.5-1.5B with codebook bottlenecks at
   L19 only (frozen body, fine-tune codebook), identify codes whose firing
   correlates with K=1 correctness in the prefill window vs the final-token
   window. If the *code-set Jaccard* between the two windows is high
   (say > 0.5), the orthogonality is mass-mixing, not circuitry.

2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — *would be
   refuted as the wrong intervention vehicle.*
   Mechanism: the paper explicitly cites Turner et al. 2308.10248 (ActAdd —
   already in our graph as the H-1 anchor) and Merullo et al. 2023 as steering
   methods that codebook substitution *improves on*, citing the "magnitude
   search problem" (Tamkin §5, "inference-time steering"). Codebook steering
   reaches 100% topic-mention from a 2.5% baseline (Tamkin Table 3, Fire) by
   substituting discrete codes — no scalar to tune. If a context-dependent
   sparse-code substitution beats a fixed-direction additive shift on a
   matched correctness-control task, then H-1 will under-perform regardless
   of how cleverly the per-position bank is designed: the gating is sparse
   and discrete, not dense and directional.
   Falsifiable experiment: H-1's planned per-position DoM steering on
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B vs. a single-layer codebook variant with the codebook trained
   on the same prefill activations and steered by activating the
   correctness-coded codes. Compare ΔMATH-500 K=1 accuracy. If codebook
   ≥ 1.5 × DoM lift, H-1's mechanism is the wrong abstraction.

3. **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams at Gaussian null)** — *would be
   reframed from "no structure" to "wrong probe for the structure that exists."*
   Mechanism: the paper demonstrates that trained transformer residual
   streams contain rich, sparse, discrete structure that emerges only under
   vector-quantized retrieval (Tamkin §3.1: 100 FSM states recovered from
   128-d MLPs; §4: hundreds of disentangled topic codes in 410M WikiText).
   Persistent homology probes the *continuous* topology of point clouds; if
   the underlying object is a sparse code-mixture with a small effective
   alphabet, PH and Mapper see a Gaussian-like cloud because the meaningful
   distinctions live in *which finite codes fire*, not in continuous
   geometric arrangement. F-10's null is then trivially predictable from the
   discrete-code account and tells us nothing about whether the residual
   stream is "geometrically un-special" — it tells us PH is the wrong lens.
   Falsifiable experiment: train a single-layer codebook bottleneck at L19
   on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (frozen body), confirm we recover correctness-correlated
   codes whose precision exceeds the L19 DoM probe AUROC of 0.7731. If yes,
   F-10's Gaussian-null framing must be footnoted: structure exists, just
   not in PH-visible form.

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — *would be refuted
   as a residual-stream causality artifact.*
   Mechanism: Tamkin §3 finds that **state codes** for the FSM live
   specifically in MLP layers 1, 2, 3 (Tamkin Fig. 4, top of §3.1) — i.e.
   different layers store *different* discrete features, with intervention
   at the wrong layer producing a much smaller JSD shift. The residual-stream
   architecture means downstream layers can *linearly read out* the codes
   stored upstream, which is why a single-layer linear probe at L19
   suffices for prediction. CoE-60 is a multi-layer trajectory feature.
   F-9's "redundancy" claim conflates "linearly predictable from L19" with
   "all the information was at L19." A codebook decomposition would show
   that L13 / L19 / L25 carry *non-overlapping* code populations even though
   their downstream effects are correlated through the residual stream.
   Falsifiable experiment: train per-layer codebooks at L13, L19, L25 on the
   pathway-11 cached activations; measure code-set overlap between layers
   for correctness prediction. If overlap is low but L19's codes' linear
   readout subsumes the others' linear readouts, F-9's "redundancy" claim
   needs the qualifier "*linear* redundancy, not feature-level redundancy."

5. **F-8 (selective prediction via prefill DoM = 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** —
   *upper bound from the discrete-code account is higher than the linear
   probe achieves.*
   Mechanism: Tamkin Fig. 6b shows codes outperform best-neuron classifiers
   by ~30 percentage points of average precision on linguistic features in
   WikiText-103 (where the regex ground truth is available). If correctness
   is similarly stored as a sparse code, an L19 codebook should produce a
   correctness probe with AUROC strictly above the 0.7731 DoM probe — and
   therefore selective-prediction accuracy at coverage 0.5 above 71.6%. F-8
   would survive numerically but its "this is the signal" reading would be
   demoted: 71.6% is the *linear* ceiling, not the residual-stream ceiling.
   Falsifiable experiment: same L19 codebook from refutation 1. Threshold a
   single high-precision correctness code as the abstain rule and compare
   selective accuracy at coverage 0.5 against the existing DoM-based number.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** — directly probed by Tamkin's
  comparison of code-classifier precision vs. best-neuron precision (Fig. 6).
  A codebook on L19 tests whether the 0.7731 number is the right ceiling;
  the paper's WikiText 30 pp gap suggests there is headroom, though the
  features there are simpler than "correctness."
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — see Refutation 1; the
  geometric orthogonality has alternate explanations under a discrete-code
  account.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** — Tamkin's
  state-plus-digit codes (686 distinct features in 128-d MLPs) demonstrate
  that distinctive collective signatures *can* live as small finite-cardinality
  feature sets. D-bucket might correspond to a single rarely-firing
  correctness-failure code rather than a distributed manifold property.
- **F-8 (selective-prediction at 0.5 coverage = 71.6%)** — see Refutation 5.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer L19 DoM)** — see Refutation 4.
- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null)** — see Refutation 3; codebook structure is a
  candidate explanation for why PH sees a null even though the underlying
  representation is highly structured.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — see Refutation 2; the paper provides
  the strongest existing evidence that *discrete-code substitution* outperforms
  *fixed-direction addition* for steering, which directly threatens H-1's
  vehicle choice.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM for refusal)** —
  Tamkin's unsupervised code discovery is a third contender alongside
  semantic-entropy probes and supervised DoM for the same role.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** —
  Tamkin places codebooks per-attention-head (Fig. 2). A head-level codebook
  is the natural way to test H-13: if prefill and final-token correctness
  signals live in disjoint attention heads, codebook activations on those
  heads will partition cleanly.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Layer-wise vector-quantization bottleneck** — replace activation
  *a* ∈ ℝᴺ with the sum of top-*k* code vectors from a learned codebook
  *C* ∈ ℝ^(C×N) chosen by cosine similarity, with straight-through gradient
  estimator and a stop-gradient MSE reconstruction loss term (λ=1).
  *Replication cost*: H100-day for finetuning Qwen-2.5-1.5B at one layer with
  k=8 codebook (24h walltime budget); ~3-4 days for all 28 layers. CPU-only
  inference if reusing existing pathway-11 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a single-layer L19 codebook is the
  cheapest entry point and directly tests Refutations 1, 4, 5.

- **Code-firing token-level interpretation** — for each code, retrieve all
  training examples where it activated, inspect activating-token
  distributions to label the code (Tamkin §2.2 step 1).
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU per code on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — once we have a codebook, this is the
  natural way to inspect which codes fire on correct vs incorrect MATH-500
  trajectories.

- **Code substitution (interchange intervention)** — on the forward pass,
  swap the activated code at position *t* layer *l* with a different code
  *c̃* and recompute downstream. Causal abstraction over the network
  (Geiger et al. 2021).
  *Replication cost*: 30 min H100 per intervention pass once codebook is
  trained.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests H-1's mechanism question
  (do correctness-codes have a causal MATH-500 effect, or merely a
  predictive one?).

- **Code vs. best-neuron precision-at-matched-recall comparison** —
  threshold each neuron at the recall level achieved by the code, take the
  max precision across all neurons as the "best neuron" baseline (Tamkin
  Fig. 6).
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU per layer.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural baseline against the L19 DoM
  probe.

- **Topic-code identification heuristic** — codes that activate on > 50% of
  tokens in a sequence get labeled topic-codes (Tamkin §4.1, footnote 4
  citing Tamkin et al. 2020).
  *Replication cost*: trivial CPU on activated codes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — could adapt to "problem-frame codes"
  that fire on > 50% of the prefill tokens, a candidate substrate for F-7.

- **Top-*k* code activation with k = 1, 8, 16, 64, 100, 128** — sparsity
  hyperparameter swept for both attention-only and attn+MLP variants
  (Tamkin Tables 1, 2). k=8 attention-only on 410M WikiText keeps loss within
  0.33 of base.
  *Replication cost*: included above.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for k ∈ {1, 4, 8} — beyond k = 8 the
  bottleneck dilutes the interpretability gain.

## Approaches & framings

- **Sparse-discrete bottleneck as a structural prior for interpretability,
  rather than a post-hoc dictionary applied to a frozen network.** Cunningham
  et al. 2309.08600 (SAEs) and Bricken et al. 2023 (Towards Monosemanticity)
  decompose *frozen* activations; codebook features *re-train* the network
  with discreteness baked in. This intersects our F-N landscape because
  every F-N is computed on a *frozen* Qwen-2.5-1.5B — F-3, F-9, F-10's
  "geometry" claims are claims about a network that was never asked to make
  its features sparse-discrete. A codebook variant of Qwen would let us ask:
  do these findings *survive structural-prior pressure*, or are they
  artifacts of the absence of any sparsity prior?

- **Causal abstraction via interchange intervention** — Tamkin frames code
  substitution as a Geiger et al. 2021 "causal abstraction" of the network.
  Our pathway-11 program has been almost entirely *correlational* (DoM
  predicts correctness; PR breathes; etc.). The codebook framework is a
  ready-made path from "predicts" to "causally responsible for," and it
  arrives without the dense-vector magnitude-search problem that has held
  up H-1.

- **"Unit of analysis" reframing** — the paper's recurring slogan ("codes
  as a unit of analysis and control") is a direct challenge to our DoM-as-
  unit framing. If codes are the right unit, F-2 / F-8 / H-1 are all
  *coarse* approximations of a finer-grained signal. This is a lens shift
  worth a footnote in PERSPECTIVES.md regardless of whether we run the
  experiment.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Codebook is inserted *after* the MLP block (or per-attention-head) and
  *before* addition to the residual stream — this is critical, it preserves
  the residual stream identity (Tamkin §2, Fig. 2).
- Top-*k* selection by cosine similarity; codes summed (not averaged); k
  fixed (no learned k). For Transformer LMs k=8 attention-only is the
  reported sweet spot.
- Loss is `L = L_LM + λ · MSE(C(x), stop_grad(x))`, λ = 1. Stop-gradient on
  the input side of MSE was load-bearing — without it the network's
  capabilities degrade.
- Straight-through gradient estimator (Bengio 2013, vdOord 2017) — only
  selected codes receive gradients.
- Training is fine-tuning a *pretrained* model with codebooks added; not
  from scratch.
- Code-vector dimensionality matches the layer dimensionality. Codebook
  size *C* swept across {2k, 10k, 20k}; k swept across {1, 4, 8, 16, 64,
  100, 128}.
- Inference speedup via maximum inner product search (MIPS) for the
  cosine-similarity step (Appendix E.4).
- Code: https://github.com/taufeeque9/codebook-features (referenced in
  abstract).

- **No-train sanity check via k-means clustering at L19** as a free
  approximation of the codebook (no straight-through, no fine-tuning, just
  cluster the cached activations and treat cluster-membership as a
  discrete code). Validates whether the *discreteness* alone helps, before
  paying the GPU bill for full codebook fine-tuning. ~30 min CPU on
  pathway-11 NPZs.
- **State-vs-state-plus-digit code analogy on prefill activations.** Fit a
  k=1 codebook at L13 *and* L19 to test whether different codes light up at
  different prefill positions, mirroring Tamkin's MLP-1/2/3 finding —
  cheapest cross-paper sanity check.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2310.17230 — Codebook Features: Sparse and Discrete Interpretability for Neural Networks (Tamkin, Taufeeque, Goodman, 2023)

**Relevance:** Direct alternative ontology to the DoM-as-direction framing
that anchors F-2, F-3, F-8, F-9, and H-1. The paper trains transformer
networks with vector-quantized bottlenecks at every layer (cosine top-*k*
retrieval, k as low as 1, straight-through estimator, MSE-aux loss with
stop-gradient) and shows that the resulting models retain LM performance
while exhibiting sparse, interpretable, *causal* discrete codes — at the
state level on a synthetic FSM (100 states recovered from 128-d MLPs) and
at the topic level on TinyStories / WikiText-103 410M (codes for fire,
dragons, football, video games, etc., with code-substitution interventions
flipping topic-mention rates from < 5% to ~100%). The framework is the
strongest existing candidate for a *non-direction-based* unit of analysis
in the residual stream and threatens our DoM-as-direction reading at
multiple points.

**Key claim we tested:** Correctness-relevant features in the residual
stream are sparse-discrete codes rather than dense directions; an L19
codebook should produce a higher-precision correctness probe than the L19
DoM linear probe (AUROC 0.7731), and code substitution should produce
larger steering effects than fixed-direction addition.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** No experiment yet run. P11-FE72 (codebook at
L19), P11-FE74 (k-means no-train sanity), P11-FE73 (steering comparison)
constitute the proposed test path. H-72 / H-73 / H-74 instantiate the
hypotheses. Until those run, this paper is a serious challenge to the
DoM-as-unit framing of pathway 11 but not yet adjudicated.

**Methodologies extracted:** Layer-wise vector-quantization bottleneck;
top-*k* cosine code retrieval with straight-through estimator;
code-firing token-level interpretation; interchange-intervention
(Geiger 2021) causal abstraction; per-attention-head codebook placement;
topic-code identification via > 50% sequence-firing heuristic; MIPS-based
inference acceleration. See brief §"Methodologies extracted" for
full annotations.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74; H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** TO TEST.
