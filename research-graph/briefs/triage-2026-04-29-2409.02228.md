# Triage brief — 2409.02228 — Unforgettable Generalization in Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02228
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's L19 specificity claim.** Zhang/Chosen/Andreas report that
   linear-probe accuracy is "mostly comparable for all layers except the very
   early and very late layers" — i.e., correctness-relevant skill information
   is distributed roughly uniformly across mid-layers in Llama-2-7B, GPT-J-6B,
   and GPT-2 124M. F-2 currently emphasizes L19 as a privileged correctness
   probe (AUROC 0.7731 OOF on Qwen2.5-1.5B). **Mechanism:** if Qwen-1.5B
   behaves like the paper's models, layers L13–L25 should yield comparable
   prefill-DoM AUROC and L19 is *not* special — F-2's narrative would shift
   from "L19 carries a unique direction" to "L19 is one of many equally good
   readout points." **Falsifiable test:** train mass-mean DoM probes at every
   mid-layer (L8–L25) on the cached Stage-2 1.5B prefill NPZs; if AUROC
   varies by ≤0.02 across that band, F-2 is downgraded to "any mid-layer DoM
   works."

2. **Refutes H-10's premise (breathing changes after fine-tuning).** The
   paper's central finding is "shallow forgetting" — fine-tuning on
   randomized labels suppresses generation behavior but **leaves the
   underlying representation probe-recoverable** even when the model can no
   longer produce correct answers. **Mechanism:** if the same is true for
   Qwen2.5-1.5B, then fine-tuning will *not* materially change the L19
   prefill DoM direction or the temporal PR curve, because the
   skill-encoding representation is preserved through the fine-tune.
   **Falsifiable test:** randomized-label fine-tune Qwen2.5-1.5B on a
   MATH-500-train subset for ≥1k steps; re-extract L19 prefill activations;
   check (a) cos(DoM_pre, DoM_post) and (b) AUROC drop. If
   cos > 0.9 and AUROC drop < 0.03, H-10 is rejected and breathing is
   confirmed orthogonal to instruction-following capacity.

3. **Refutes F-9's framing (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19).** F-9
   currently treats the equivalence between CoE-60 (60-layer trajectory
   features) and a single L19 DoM as a *coincidence* worth noting. The
   paper's uniform-mid-layer-probing finding implies this is not coincidence
   — it's the **expected** state when correctness signal lives uniformly
   across mid-layers. **Mechanism:** under "uniform mid-layer information,"
   any aggregator (CoE pooling, mean of mid-layer DoMs, single-layer DoM)
   reads off the same lower-dimensional subspace. **Falsifiable test:**
   construct a "synthetic CoE" that pools only L19 across timesteps (no
   cross-layer info); if AUROC matches CoE-60 within ±0.01, F-9's "redundant
   with L19" finding generalizes to "redundant with any mid-layer pooling,"
   and the cross-layer dimension of CoE adds nothing beyond temporal pooling
   of one layer.

4. **Refutes F-7's representational interpretation of D-bucket.** F-7
   claims D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature. The
   paper shows that even when a model behaviorally fails on a task, linear
   probes still resolve it. **Mechanism:** if the D-bucket signature is a
   property of the *trajectory* (not the static representation), then
   probing the prefill alone for D-bucket-vs-non-D classification should
   *fail* (since D-bucket fragility is generation-time, not
   representation-time). Conversely, if probes succeed, D-bucket is
   pre-encoded in prefill geometry and "collective signature" is a
   restatement of probe-separability. **Falsifiable test:** train a binary
   classifier on prefill L19 activations for D-bucket (n=36) vs non-D
   (n=464); if AUROC > 0.75, D-bucket is encoded pre-generation (matches
   F-7); if AUROC ≈ 0.5, D-bucket is a generation-time phenomenon and F-7's
   "geometric signature" framing is misleading.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731)** — paper's "fifth-last layer of
  hidden state at last token of question" is structurally identical to our
  prefill-token + mid-layer probe protocol. Their probe choice corroborates
  *the protocol* even as it threatens the *layer-specificity* claim.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM)** — paper's uniform-mid-layer
  finding provides a candidate *explanation* for F-9 (rather than treating
  redundancy as surprising).
- **H-8 (prefill direction stable across training checkpoints)** —
  paper's shallow-forgetting result is direct evidence *for* H-8: even
  destructive fine-tuning leaves the readable representation intact. Test
  protocol from Refutation 2 doubles as H-8 confirmation.
- **H-10 (breathing pattern changes after fine-tuning)** — directly
  contested; see Refutation 2.
- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universality)** — paper's
  "representation variability = trace of covariance" measure is a
  participation-ratio cousin; their finding that *low variability →
  forgetting generalizes more* is intriguing because it suggests low-PR
  layers are *more brittle*, opposite of F-1's framing where low-final-PR
  correlates with correctness.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Linear probe with L2 + early stopping at fifth-last layer, last
  question token** — exactly the protocol we already use modulo layer
  index. *Replication cost*: zero — already implemented in Pathway 11
  scripts. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, and we already do.
- **Trace-of-covariance representation variability** — per-layer
  Tr(Cov(H)) over a corpus of activations. Coarser than PR (it isn't
  normalized by the squared trace) but cheaper and reportable.
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU on cached NPZs. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — adds a label-free dispersion baseline alongside PR.
- **Randomized-label fine-tuning to induce "forgetting"** — flip labels
  on a training set, fine-tune, measure behavioral collapse vs probe
  survival. *Replication cost*: ~half H100 day for Qwen-1.5B with MATH
  multiple-choice variant. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-8 / H-10
  test (see FE below).
- **Cross-task forgetting (train-on-A, eval-on-B)** — fine-tune to
  forget task A, measure which other tasks B incidentally degrade.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day per pair. *We'd plausibly run this*:
  no — too far from MATH-500 / hidden-state pipeline focus.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Shallow vs deep" forgetting** — surface-level behavior change without
  representation change. Intersects F-2 / H-8 directly: implies the prefill
  DoM is a *deep* feature that fine-tuning rarely reaches.
- **Confidence + representation variability as forgetting predictors** —
  reframes "calibration" from a generation property to a *representation
  geometry* property. Intersects F-8 (selective prediction): the same
  signal that supports refusal may also predict fine-tune-robustness, which
  could yield a 2-D refusal-vs-malleability map.
- **"Generalization of forgetting" as a benchmark dimension** — paper
  treats *how* forgetting transfers as an evaluation axis distinct from
  accuracy. Not directly a F-N concern but useful framing for safety /
  unlearning extensions if we ever pivot toward editing.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models: Llama-2 7B base (primary), GPT-J 6B, GPT-2 124M (robustness).
  No Qwen / DeepSeek-distill — our default lab model is not in their
  panel, so applicability requires re-running on Qwen2.5-1.5B.
- "Fifth-last layer" — for a 28-layer Qwen2.5-1.5B that maps to L23 (one
  layer below the breathing peak we care about). For Llama-2-7B (32
  layers) it is L27.
- Probe: linear, L2-regularized logistic regression with early stopping
  on a held-out validation split. No MLP probes.
- Hidden state: last *question* token (not last generated token), prior
  to any sampling — exactly our prefill-position protocol.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2409.02228 — Unforgettable Generalization in Language Models (Zhang, Chosen, Andreas, 2024)

**Relevance:** Studies "shallow forgetting" — fine-tuning LLMs on
randomized labels collapses generation behavior but leaves the
underlying skill probe-recoverable from mid-layer hidden states. Hits
two of our active questions directly: (a) is the L19 prefill DoM signal
specific to L19 or is it a generic mid-layer property (F-2, F-9), and
(b) does fine-tuning shift the prefill DoM direction (H-8, H-10). Their
probing protocol — linear L2 logistic regression at the fifth-last
layer, last question token — is structurally identical to our prefill
DoM extraction.

**Key claim we tested:** Linear probes at any non-extremal mid-layer
recover task-relevant information uniformly; destructive fine-tuning
does not destroy that mid-layer representation. Translated to our
terms: (a) prefill DoM AUROC should be roughly uniform across
L13–L25 in Qwen2.5-1.5B, and (b) cos(DoM_pre, DoM_post) under
randomized-label fine-tuning should remain near 1.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** We have not run either the per-mid-layer
AUROC sweep (P11-FE93) or the randomized-label fine-tune protocol
(P11-FE94). The L19 specificity question is testable today on cached
NPZs (~30min CPU) and is high-ROI; the fine-tuning test is gated on
H100 availability.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE93 (mid-layer AUROC sweep), P11-FE94
(randomized-label fine-tune + DoM stability), P11-FE95
(trace-of-covariance per-layer profile), H-93, H-94, F-2, F-9, H-8,
H-10.

**Status:** TO TEST.
