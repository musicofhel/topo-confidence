# Triage brief — 2309.11028 — The Topology and Geometry of Neural Representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11028
Triaged: 2026-04-28

## Refutations

The paper is methodologically adjacent to F-10 (PH on residual streams ≈ Gaussian null) and the broader claim that representational topology adds nothing beyond geometry on trained networks. With a careful read, three substantive refutations exist — though all three are bounded by the fact that this is a *biology/CNN* paper using RSA-style summary statistics on a stimulus-by-stimulus RDM, not generation-time hidden states from a transformer.

1. **F-10 may have been falsified by the wrong null.** Lin & Kriegeskorte's central empirical finding (Figs. 5–6, §3.2–3.3) is that **topology-sensitive (TS) RGTM zones tie the geometry-sensitive (GS) zone for region/layer identification at low noise** (no significant difference between TS and GS, p=0.434 on fMRI; no significant difference at σ<0.2 on All-CNN-C). This is a direct counter-existence proof to the F-10 generalization "PH on neural representations adds no signal beyond covariance structure." Their TS statistic is *not* persistent-homology — it is RGTM/RGDM, a thresholded-distance summary that compresses small and large distances. *Refutation mechanism*: if we re-run our F-10 null (`pathway9/results/exp3a_gaussian_null_v2.json`) using RGTM/RGDM features (their Eq. 1 with l, u quantile pairs swept over 5 zones) instead of `H0_max_lifetime`, `H1_*` features, the gap vs the rank-matched Gaussian null might be non-zero. F-10's "PH at Gaussian null" claim does **not** automatically extend to "all topology-flavored summary statistics at Gaussian null." *Falsifiable test*: P11-FE28 below — recompute correctness AUROC on cached L19 NPZs using RGTM(l, u) sweep across 5 zones, with the same rank-matched Gaussian sampler from EXP-026. If the GS zone matches our 0.690 but TS zone exceeds 0.71, F-10 needs to be narrowed from "PH" to "PH features" specifically.

2. **The null-distinguishing power of topology may live in the *intermediate* range we never tested.** Their Fig. 5b heat-map shows the *highest* RIA at l=0.40, u=0.65 — i.e., compress the *bottom 40%* and *top 35%* of pairwise distances, keeping only the middle. F-10's PH features are dominated by H0/H1 birth-death pairs in the upper Vietoris-Rips filtration regime; the small-distance (l) regime is collapsed into instant-merger, and the large-distance regime is irrelevant once a single connected component forms. *If* discriminative geometry on Qwen2.5-1.5B L19 also lives in the middle quantile band (a hypothesis we have not tested), then a "fix the null with the wrong filter" version of F-10 could be wrong — the null would only be matched in the geometric ranges we extracted features from. *Refutation mechanism*: PH summary features are a *sparse projection* of the multi-scale geometry; RGTM is a *dense* one. If l=0.40, u=0.65 RGTM features beat geometry-sensitive RDMs on correctness AUROC for our problem, F-10 is overgeneralized. *Falsifiable test*: same as P11-FE28 — sweep includes l=0.40, u=0.65 explicitly.

3. **H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket) may be reframed as a graph-connectivity question.** Their RGTM is essentially a soft k-NN-like graph (edges only where d<u, weights via piecewise-linear transform), and their RGDM uses Dijkstra-on-graph for representational distance. They explicitly note (§2.3, end) that for high noise the RGTM degrades and the RGDM picks up "shortcuts" through self-intersections — a topological signature of bucket-merger. The D-bucket geometric signature in F-7 is a *collective* (not per-problem) pattern: 36 problems where K=1-correct→K=8-incorrect. If D-bucket sits at a "shortcut" connecting prefill-correct and final-token-confused regions (as the paper's "flat 8 vs untangled flat 8" Fig. 4 example suggests for self-intersections), then D-bucket should be *detectable as a graph-bridge, not as a density outlier* — overturning H-7's framing. *Refutation mechanism*: build the RGDM from prefill activations on 500 problems, threshold at l, u zone, and ask whether D-bucket problems sit on shortest-path edges that bridge A-bucket and B-bucket clusters. If yes, density-based outlier detection is the wrong tool; betweenness-centrality on the RGDM graph is right. *Falsifiable test*: P11-FE29 — RGDM betweenness vs LOF on cached L19 prefill activations, see if D-bucket detection AUROC differs by >0.1.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10** ("PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null", AUROC 0.690 vs null 0.693, gap zero, EXP-026): The most direct connection. The paper's RGTM family is a *generalization* of the RDM that includes pure-topology (binary thresholding diagonal) as a limit. Their finding that TS=GS at low noise on CNN/fMRI is an *adjacent* (not identical) result to F-10. Anchor: 0.690 vs 0.693 (gap = 0.003).
- **F-7** ("D-bucket has distinctive collective geometric signature", 36 problems, peak PR 67 vs 88): The paper's flat-8/untangled-flat-8 example (Fig. 4) is a direct topological analogue — geometrically similar (both flat) but topologically distinct (one hole vs two). D-bucket may be the topological "self-intersection" between prefill-confident and final-confused regions. Anchor: 36 D-bucket / 207 A-bucket = 17% misroute rate.
- **H-7** ("density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket"): Connection is via the RGDM-as-shortest-path-graph framing — paper offers an *alternative* outlier-detection lens (graph betweenness on RGDM) that may outperform local-density methods.
- **H-22** ("Abstract-CoT shows compressed or qualitatively different breathing"): tRSA gives a *measurement* tool for "qualitatively different" — RGTM in TS zone vs GS zone gives a precise geometric-vs-topological calibration that would let H-22 be tested without committing to specific PR features.
- **F-2** ("Prefill L19 DoM beats final-token", AUROC 0.7731 vs 0.7186 OOF 5-fold): No direct connection. Their method is unsupervised (region/layer ID), ours is supervised (correctness AUROC). The cross-over is only via the cosine-distance comparator they use (§2.5, end) — proportional to Pearson on rank-transformed RGTMs.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Geo-topological (GT) transform / RGTM** — Piecewise-linear transform of pairwise distances (Eq. 1) parameterized by lower bound l and upper bound u. Compresses d≤l to 0, d≥u to 1, linear in between. Yields a generalization of the RDM.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — 5-line numpy function, 5 min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — pairs naturally with F-10's correctness-AUROC pipeline; cheap counterfactual to "PH at null."

- **RGDM (geo-topological geodesic distance matrix)** — Dijkstra shortest-path on the weighted graph defined by RGTM, treating edge weights as distances. Captures multi-scale topology including manifold "shortcuts."
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on cached prefill activations using `scipy.sparse.csgraph.shortest_path`.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-7 candidate alternative tool; tests whether D-bucket is a graph-bridge phenomenon.

- **5-zone RGTM partition (TS, GS, LE, GE, I)** — Topology-sensitive, geometry-sensitive, local-extractor, global-extractor, intermediate. 10 random samples of (l, u) per zone, evaluated by region-identification accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU sweep.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a structured way to test whether F-10's PH null generalizes across the (l, u) plane.

- **Leave-one-instance-out (LOIO) / leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) identification accuracy** — Hold out one model instance / one fMRI subject, identify which layer / region the held-out RDM came from based on Euclidean distance to the average across remaining instances.
  *Replication cost*: trivial on cached data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: marginal — our problem is correctness-prediction, not "identify which layer this came from"; the methodology only ports if we reframe as "identify which problem-bucket (A/B/C/D) this prefill activation came from."

- **2-factor bootstrap (subjects × stimuli)** — Resample both axes simultaneously to estimate the standard error of the identification accuracy, then 2-sided t-tests.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU per claim.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cleaner than current bootstrap-over-problems-only used in some F-* claims; could tighten F-2 confidence intervals.

- **Rank-transform within RDM before applying GT transform** — Renders Euclidean comparator equivalent to Pearson/cosine on equally-distributed values.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — minor methodological tweak; useful only if we adopt the RGTM pipeline.

## Approaches & framings

- **Geometry → topology as a continuum, not a dichotomy.** They explicitly argue (§2.2 and Fig. 3d) for treating (l, u) as a 2D plane parameterizing the geometry-vs-topology tradeoff. Implication for our work: F-10's "PH" should be located on this plane, and PH features may be a single point (l=0, u=max with H0/H1 lifetime extraction on top) — not a representative sampling of the topology axis.

- **Topological hypotheses are not testable by RDM-RSA, but are testable by RGTM-RSA.** Direct quote-paraphrase from §4.2: "a hypothesis about the representational topology cannot be straightforwardly tested with conventional RSA because the hypothesis corresponds to a complex set of RDMs any of which conforms to the hypothesized topology." This reframes our P7 NO-GO (non-Euclidean PH 0.774 < 0.796) as potentially "wrong RDM comparator," not "topology is irrelevant."

- **Information compression as a discriminative goal.** §4.1 argues tRSA does not *outperform* RSA but *matches* it while compressing the bulk of the RDM information. Our equivalent question would be: how much of the F-2 0.7731 prefill AUROC is preserved if we throw away the bottom 40% and top 35% of pairwise activation distances? Useful for understanding what range of distances carries the correctness signal.

- **Noise vs signal as distance-scale-dependent.** §3.3 result: at higher Gaussian dissimilarity noise (σ ≥ 0.2), GS zone outperforms TS. At low noise, they tie. Maps onto our P7 question about whether topology gains traction in *noisier* regimes (e.g., 7B at high temperature, K=8 sampling) where geometry alone is degraded.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **All-CNN-C** training recipe: 350 epochs, ADAM with momentum 0.9, batch 128, lr 0.01, L2 1e-5, gradient clip 500, full CIFAR-10 (train+test). 10 instances from different seeds. TensorFlow 1.3.0, Python 3.5.4. Not directly relevant to our pipeline.
- **GT transform (Eq. 1)**: 3 lines of numpy. The (l, u) parameters are quantile-defined relative to each RDM's distance distribution, *not* absolute — this is the key portability detail.
- **RSA Toolbox** (Python, open-source, https://github.com/rsagroup/rsatoolbox) — they reference it for inferential RSA. Worth checking if RGTM/RGDM is already implemented there before we re-derive.
- **RSA3 inference framework** (ref [33]) — non-parametric model comparison, 2-factor bootstrap. Imported via the toolbox.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2309.11028 — The Topology and Geometry of Neural Representations (Lin & Kriegeskorte, 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodological neighbor to F-10. Introduces tRSA — a generalization of RSA that parameterizes the geometry-vs-topology tradeoff via piecewise-linear distance transforms (RGTMs) and shortest-path distance matrices (RGDMs). Domain is fMRI brain regions and CNN layer comparisons; not transformer hidden states. The empirical headline — topology-sensitive summary statistics tie geometry-sensitive ones at low measurement noise on All-CNN-C and human fMRI — is *adjacent* to F-10's "PH = Gaussian null" finding and offers tools (RGTM, RGDM, 5-zone partition, 2-factor bootstrap) that could refine or refute the generalization of F-10 from PH features specifically to all topology-flavored summary statistics. The paper does **not** test correctness prediction, supervised probing, or any LLM-relevant task; the import path is methodological only.

**Key claim we tested:** Topology-sensitive RGTM features carry the same model-selection signal as the full geometric RDM at low noise, on trained CNN representations (their LIA result, σ < 0.2).

**Our result:** **TO TEST** (P11-FE28). F-10's existing result is on PH features specifically (H0/H1 birth/death lifetimes), not on RGTM features. The paper's claim could be consistent with F-10 (both topology and PH at the Gaussian null) OR refine it (PH at null but RGTM exceeds null). Cheap to settle: 1h CPU on cached NPZs.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE28 (RGTM null), P11-FE29 (RGDM betweenness for D-bucket), P11-FE30 (2-factor bootstrap on F-2), H-28, H-29.

**Status:** CITED ONLY pending P11-FE28 outcome. Will graduate to REPLICATED if RGTM ties Gaussian null (consistent with F-10), CONTRADICTED for F-10's reach if RGTM exceeds null while PH does not.
