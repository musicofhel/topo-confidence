# Triage brief — 2402.10528 — Can We Verify Step by Step for Incorrect Answer Detection?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10528
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 71.6% acc / 50% coverage, K=2.5 avg) is *compute-matched dominated* by black-box NLI on rationales.**
   - *Mechanism*: PDS aggregates Pairwise Process Supervision Score (`min_j max_i (ent − con)`) using a SummaC zero-shot NLI model across K=5 sampled chains. It uses **no model internals**. On HotpotQA from GPT-4 it gains +5.10% F1 / +3.01% AUC-PR over the answer-vote baseline (ADS) without touching residual streams. F-8's pitch is that prefill-DoM (a single forward pass) *cancels* the need for K-sample compute. If PDS at matched-K (K=5) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B/MATH-500 beats prefill-DoM at K=1 by a margin larger than DoM beats ADS, then F-8's compute-savings story collapses: the question becomes "is one extra forward pass worth losing the dominant signal?" instead of "DoM beats sampling-based methods".
   - *Test*: run their PDS pipeline (SummaC + their thresholds H=0 / H=0.4) on the K=5 samples we already cached for `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`, plot a risk-coverage curve, compare to F-8's selective curve at coverage 0.5 directly.

2. **F-9 ("CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM") is too narrow a redundancy claim.**
   - *Mechanism*: PDS quantifies cross-chain *content* agreement — a signal that is **orthogonal in modality** to both CoE-60 (per-token cosine trajectory) and prefill-DoM (single-layer activation projection). If a logistic stack {DoM ⊕ residualized PDS} retains lift over DoM alone, then F-9's "single-layer DoM captures everything CoE adds" is correct but **understates how much signal exists** — there is a third axis (textual/semantic agreement across samples) that neither captures.
   - *Test*: orthogonalize PDS against L19 DoM via OLS residualization on the 1.5B 1024-tok set; if the residual still scores > 0.6 AUROC for correctness, F-9 needs the qualifier "redundant *within hidden-state features*; PDS is a separate axis".

3. **F-4 ("asymmetric collapse: correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at final token") may be a content-agreement confound.**
   - *Mechanism*: Correct chains produce more semantically consistent rationales (PDS posits this and shows it works as a falsehood detector). Final-token participation ratio drops as the model converges on a single answer subspace. If correct vs incorrect groups differ in PDS by more than they differ in collapse, the geometric collapse asymmetry could be a **downstream consequence** of content agreement rather than an independent geometric fact. F-4 already has a "collective vs per-problem" caveat in the F-N body — PDS gives a sharper handle: per-problem, the cross-chain entailment minus contradiction score is a content-only proxy for the same effect.
   - *Test*: per-problem on our 500 MATH-500 questions, compute (i) cross-chain PDS over K=5, (ii) final-token L19 PR — test partial correlation. If `PR_correct − PR_incorrect | PDS` shrinks toward zero when PDS is regressed out, F-4's asymmetric-collapse signal is mostly a re-expression of content agreement.

4. **H-12 ("semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM for refusal") needs a finer-grained sibling.**
   - *Mechanism*: PDS is a sentence-level NLI signal aggregated across K chains. SEPs measure entropy over semantic clusters of answers only. PDS strictly subsumes SEP by also examining the *rationale*. If PDS beats SEPs and DoM head-to-head on Qwen-2.5-1.5B/MATH-500, H-12's framing should narrow to "label-free *answer-level* entropy can match supervised DoM" while a separate hypothesis owns the rationale-NLI direction.
   - *Test*: three-way head-to-head on the cached K=5 samples — DoM (supervised), SEP (cluster entropy), PDS (NLI agreement) — at K=5 matched compute. If PDS > {SEP, DoM} at AUROC and at risk-coverage, H-12 is too narrow.

Direct numerical anchor: 0.7731 (prefill-DoM AUROC) vs whatever PDS gets on the same 500 MATH-500 questions.
- **F-8** (selective prediction at 71.6% acc / 50% coverage, K=2.5 avg): PDS is the strongest *known* matched-compute baseline for F-8. The 50% coverage point in F-8 implicitly assumes K=2.5 average — PDS uses K=5 fixed, so a fair comparison must align coverage and average sample count.
- **F-9** (CoE-60 redundant with DoM): PDS is the third-axis baseline that distinguishes "redundant in hidden states" from "redundant overall".
- **H-6** (CoE-60 vs single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels): regardless of which wins, PDS needs to be in the comparison.
- **H-12** (SEPs replace supervised DoM): PDS = NLI-extension of SEPs.

- **F-7** (D-bucket has distinctive collective signature): if PDS distinguishes D-bucket as low PDS (ambivalent) vs A-bucket (high PDS), then PDS recovers the bucket signal without needing geometric tools — relevant counterfactual for F-7's "geometry is required" pitch.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PPSS (Pairwise Process Supervision Score)** — NLI-based asymmetric pair score: `min_j max_i (ent(a_i, b_j) − con(a_i, b_j))` over sentence pairs of two rationales, using a frozen zero-shot SummaC NLI head on top of an off-the-shelf NLI model (DeBERTa-class).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU once K=5 rationales are in hand for MATH-500 / 1.5B (we have these in `pathway11_h100/`). NLI model is HF-hostable, ≤500M params.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is the cheapest matched-compute baseline against F-2/F-8 and we already have all the inputs.

- **PSS (Process Supervision Score)** — average of PPSS across all ordered pairs of K rationales. Pure aggregation of PPSS.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once PPSS is computed (O(K²) sentence-pair NLI).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — same pipeline as above.

- **PDS (Process Discernibility Score)** — `(normalized_ADS + PSS) / 2`, where `normalized_ADS = 2(ADS − n/2)/n` rescales the answer-agreement count into the same (−1, 1) range as PSS. Threshold-based classifier, H=0 for free-form / H=0.4 for discriminative.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached samples.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — see above.

- **PDS variants for ablation** — PDS-ADS (PSS only, no answer-vote term), PDS w/o ans (NLI on rationales only, drop answer tokens from sentences), PDS-avg (replace `min_j` with `avg_j`), PDS-HaloCheck / PDS-SelfCheckNLI (swap NLI backbone). Their main ablation finding is that **min-aggregation across rationale pairs is critical** — average aggregation loses ~5 F1 points.
  *Replication cost*: same single pipeline with switches.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the min-vs-avg ablation is informative for our future selective-prediction work since it tells us whether "any single contradiction kills credence" beats "average entailment".

- **Verify-and-Edit + PDS** — selective post-editing: when PDS < H, retrieve passages and rewrite the rationale; when PDS ≥ H, leave alone. Improves HotpotQA / 2WikiMultihop end-task accuracy by ~2.9% under near-perfect retrieval (ground-truth supporting docs as retriever output).
  *Replication cost*: H100 day if we want to do generation post-editing; 0 if we only want to read off the routing decision.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not for math (no analogous retriever) — but the **PDS-as-router** idea is reusable for compute-routing experiments (analog to H-19's C_exact verification routing).

- **Per-LLM trigger-word extraction for "the answer is" parsing** — they tabulate dataset×LLM-specific trigger phrases for answer extraction (Table 9). A reminder that majority-vote pipelines have a non-trivial extraction step and that mismatched extraction rules produce false-disagreement signals.
  *Replication cost*: zero (config table only).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: noted as a gotcha for any K-sample work we do — our Qwen 1.5B / 7B answer-extraction needs the same care, and we should sanity-check that ADS / PDS evaluation does not fail on extraction noise.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Answer-checking is impoverished; rationale-checking is the real signal"** — reframes self-consistency (Wang et al. 2203.11171) as a minimal-information baseline. This frame complements our project's frame of "hidden-state geometry beats answer voting" — both are claims that the signal is richer than ADS, but they live in disjoint modalities (text NLI vs residual stream).
- **"Minimum across pairs, maximum across sentences within a pair"** — aggregation philosophy: any single contradiction in any pair is treated as decisive. Inspires a possibly-different reading of F-7's D-bucket ("any single anomalous problem in the bucket is decisive for the geometric signature"). This is a methodological framing we could borrow when writing aggregations over per-problem features.
- **"Threshold should be task-agnostic"** — they explicitly argue for not tuning H per task because held-out data is often unavailable in deployment. Our F-8 selective-prediction story has a coverage-tuning step that this paper would frame as a deployment liability.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- NLI backbone: SummaC zero-shot — GitHub: `tingofurro/summac`, HF model: `tals/albert-xlarge-vitaminc-mnli` (DeBERTa-class also works). Inference is sentence-pair classification → softmax over {entailment, neutral, contradiction}; PDS uses `P(ent) − P(contradiction)` per pair.
- Sentence segmentation: not specified explicitly in the methods text — presumably NLTK / spaCy sentence split per rationale. Worth checking the repo for the canonical splitter.
- K=5, temperature=0.7, n=5 for all generations. For Gemini Pro: topK=40, topP=0.95 (their explicit setting).
- Threshold H: 0 for free-form, 0.4 for discriminative tasks (yes/no, MCQ). Held constant across LLMs / datasets within a task type.
- HotpotQA & 2WikiMultihop "from GPT-3.5" subsets are **replaced** with GPT-4 because GPT-3.5 refused too many open-domain questions — a generation-pipeline gotcha for any K-sample work that includes deployment-tuned RLHF models.
- Code & data: https://github.com/XinXU-USTC/R2PE.git (we can pull their NLI pipeline directly).

## 2402.10528 — Can We Verify Step by Step for Incorrect Answer Detection? (Xu, Diao, Yang, Wang, IJCAI 2025; arXiv Feb 2024)

**Relevance:** Introduces the **R2PE benchmark** (45 subsets across 8 datasets × 6 LLMs, ~38.3K labeled instances) and the **Process Discernibility Score (PDS)**, a black-box K=5 selective-prediction signal that aggregates pairwise NLI entailment-minus-contradiction across CoT rationales using SummaC. PDS reports +5.10% F1 / +3.01% AUC-PR over the answer-vote baseline (ADS) and outperforms SelfCheckGPT and HaloCheck. Methodologically orthogonal to our project: text-level cross-chain NLI vs hidden-state geometry. The strongest matched-compute baseline against F-2 / F-8's selective-prediction story we have not yet run.

**Key claim we tested:** That cross-chain rationale agreement (NLI-based, K=5) is a stronger correctness signal than answer-only majority voting and stronger than prior text-only hallucination detectors (SelfCheckGPT, HaloCheck) — applied to selective answer rejection.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** Direct head-to-head against F-2 (prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) and F-8 (selective acc 71.6% at coverage 0.5) is queued as P11-FE75 (HIGH, ROI=9, ~30–60min CPU). Outcomes: (i) PDS > DoM at K=5 → F-8 needs to be reframed as a K=1 advantage; (ii) DoM > PDS at K=5 → F-8 strengthens substantially; (iii) PDS adds residual variance over DoM → F-9 needs the qualifier "redundant within hidden-state features".

**Related experiments:** P11-FE75, P11-FE76, P11-FE77, P11-FE78, P11-FE79; H-78, H-79, H-80, H-81.
