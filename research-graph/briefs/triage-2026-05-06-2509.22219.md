# Triage brief — 2509.22219 — Interpretable Discovery of One-parameter Subgroups: A Modular Framework for Elliptical, Hyperbolic, and Parabolic Symmetries

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22219
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — orbit-invariant representation could expose DoM as crude linearization.** The Hγ-Net framework discovers one-parameter subgroup symmetries and constructs orbit-separating invariant representations that are provably complete. If the correct/incorrect separation in the 500×1536 L19 prefill cloud is governed by a one-parameter subgroup action (e.g., correct activations lie on one orbit and incorrect on another), then the invariant representation under that subgroup would be a richer, geometrically correct predictor than a linear direction. If `invRep(x)` yields AUROC > 0.7731, the DoM direction is exposed as a projection artifact of a symmetry-structured space, and F-2's "single direction" characterization is the wrong frame. **Falsifiable experiment:** fit Hγ-Net (elliptic regime, n=1536 or PCA-reduced) on cached L19 prefill activations with correctness as target; compare invariant-rep AUROC to 0.7731.

2. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046, "structural not positional") — the subgroup type could reveal the orthogonality is a specific Lie-algebraic consequence, not a generic structural claim.** Applying Hγ-Net to learn the transformation between prefill and final-token DoM representations (treating the token-position progression as a group action on hidden states) could reveal the transformation's Lie-algebraic regime. If it discovers a hyperbolic (boost) or parabolic (shear) generator rather than an elliptic (rotational) one, this would refute the implicit assumption behind H-17 (RoPE-mechanical rotation) and recharacterize the orthogonality: cos≈0 under a hyperbolic boost means something fundamentally different from cos≈0 under a rotation. F-3's "structural" label would need to specify which Lie-algebraic structure. **Falsifiable experiment:** extract paired (prefill, final-token) activation vectors, fit Hγ-Net with each regime assumption, compare residuals to determine which regime best describes the prefill→final transformation.

3. **F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal across architectures and scales) — if breathing is a specific one-parameter subgroup action, "universal" may be regime-dependent.** The framework's canonical classification into elliptical/hyperbolic/parabolic regimes provides a finer-grained geometric vocabulary than "participation ratio changes." If breathing at L19 follows a hyperbolic (squeeze) subgroup while at other layers it follows an elliptic (rotation) subgroup, then "universality" of breathing is superficial — the underlying geometric mechanism differs by layer. This would refute the universality claim by showing it conflates geometrically distinct phenomena. **Falsifiable experiment:** for each layer l in {1, 5, 10, 15, 19, 24, 28}, fit Hγ-Net to the correct→incorrect transformation on cached per-layer activations and test whether the discovered regime type is consistent or varies.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's invariant representation is orbit-separating by construction (Proposition 3.6) — if correct/incorrect activations are related by a continuous symmetry, `invRep` would capture the full orbit structure that DoM linearizes. The AUROC 0.7731 ceiling could reflect exactly the information loss from linear projection of a curved orbit.

- **F-3 (cos=0.046):** The paper classifies one-parameter transformations into three regimes. The prefill→final-token relationship (cos=0.046) could be characterized as: (a) elliptic with specific rotation angle near π/2, (b) hyperbolic with large boost parameter, or (c) parabolic with large shear. Each has different implications for H-17 (RoPE-mechanical rotation hypothesis).

- **F-10 (PH-at-null):** The paper discovers continuous symmetries that PH cannot detect — orbit structure under Lie subgroups is smooth and one-dimensional, exactly the kind of geometry that Vietoris-Rips persistent homology with summary statistics would miss. A non-trivial subgroup discovery would not refute F-10 directly but would provide an alternative geometric characterization of what structure the activations actually have, strengthening H-35 (F-10's PH-null is summary-statistic-bound, not topology-bound).

- **H-36 (prefill/final-token DoM coordinate-orthogonality is rotation-only, not topology-different):** The Hγ-Net framework could directly test this by fitting elliptic, hyperbolic, and parabolic regimes and comparing reconstruction error. If elliptic regime fits best, H-36 is supported; if hyperbolic or parabolic fits better, H-36 is refuted.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder):** If the correct/incorrect separation is governed by a hyperbolic (squeeze) subgroup, the asymmetric collapse would have a clean Lie-algebraic explanation: correct activations lie in the contracting direction of a hyperbolic generator.

- **Cov-spectrum probe (AUROC 0.7928):** The covariance spectrum's eigenvalues encode second-order orbit structure. The paper's invariant representations are nonlinear and capture the full orbit geometry — they could explain why the cov-spectrum probe (0.7928) exceeds the linear DoM probe (0.7731): the eigenvalue structure partially captures orbit curvature that the linear direction misses.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Hγ-Net invariant representation layer** — learnable, parameterized layer that maps inputs to orbit-separating invariant representations under a one-parameter subgroup. Decomposes input via a learnable orientation matrix A into 2D blocks, performs gauge-fixing on the first block, applies inverse transformation to remaining blocks.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h CPU implementation + 30min training on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to the 500×1536 L19 prefill matrix. Would need PCA reduction to manageable dimensionality first (768 2D blocks for 1536 dims is already the paper's framework).

- **Canonical regime classification (elliptic/hyperbolic/parabolic)** — data-driven determination of which geometric regime best describes a transformation by fitting each regime separately and comparing prediction error / generator recovery.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU (fit three regimes, compare residuals).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — apply to correct→incorrect mean-shift transformation in L19 to classify the separation geometry.

- **Generator recovery via cosine similarity** — the paper evaluates symmetry discovery quality by computing cosine similarity between learned and ground-truth Lie algebra generators. Useful as a diagnostic for stability of discovered structure.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (once generator is learned).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — use as stability metric for any subgroup we discover on cached activations.

- **Invariance error metric** — E_{h,x}[‖f(x) − f(h·x)‖²] measures how well a function respects the discovered symmetry. Could be applied to test whether our DoM probe is approximately invariant to some hidden symmetry.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — compute invariance error of the DoM linear probe under the discovered subgroup to quantify how much signal the linear probe loses.

## Approaches & framings

- **Regime-conditioned symmetry discovery:** Fixing the geometric regime a priori (elliptic/hyperbolic/parabolic) and learning only the subgroup parameters within that regime. This avoids the identifiability issues of jointly inferring both regime and parameters. Intersects F-3/H-36: we could condition on each regime and let model selection determine which best describes prefill→final.

- **Canonicalize–process–restore principle (Section F.5):** Factor any equivariant function as: canonicalize input to orbit representative, apply unconstrained function, restore equivariance by inverse group element. This framing could recharacterize the DoM probe as a canonicalization step followed by a simple threshold — making explicit what information the canonicalization preserves vs discards.

- **Orbit-separating completeness as a design criterion:** The paper's key guarantee is that invariant representations separate orbits (Proposition 3.6). This is a stronger guarantee than PCA or linear probes provide. If applied to our activation data, it would test whether the AUROC ceiling (0.7928 for cov-spectrum) is due to incomplete orbit separation in the feature space.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Regime classification of the correct→incorrect mean-shift direction:** Decompose the 1536-dim correct-mean minus incorrect-mean vector (our DoM) into 768 2D blocks. For each candidate orientation matrix A (start with PCA eigenvectors), compute the skew-symmetric, symmetric-traceless, and nilpotent parts of the generator relating the two class centroids. The dominant part's type (skew-sym = elliptic, symmetric = hyperbolic, nilpotent = parabolic) classifies the regime. Uses cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` NPZ data. ~20min CPU implementation.

- **Invariance error of DoM probe under candidate one-parameter subgroups:** Using cached L19 prefill activations, generate synthetic group-transformed samples by exponentiating candidate generators (e.g., PCA-derived rotation in the PC1–PC2 plane), evaluate DoM probe on original vs transformed inputs, compute invariance error. Tests whether the DoM probe is already approximately invariant to a natural symmetry. ~15min CPU.
