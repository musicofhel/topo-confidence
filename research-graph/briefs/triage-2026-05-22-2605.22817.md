# Triage brief — 2605.22817 — Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22817
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) may be a GRPO mode-collapse artifact, not an intrinsic geometric property.** VPO demonstrates that GRPO concentrates policy mass onto a narrow set of high-probability responses (§1, §2), producing "near-duplicates" as k grows. If the L19 prefill DoM direction's predictive power depends on GRPO having already committed the model to a single solution strategy per problem, then a VPO-trained Qwen-2.5-1.5B (which deliberately maintains diverse solution strategies) could show a degraded or rotated DoM direction — not because correctness prediction is impossible, but because the geometry of a multi-strategy policy is fundamentally different from a collapsed one. **Test:** Extract L19 prefill activations from a VPO-trained Qwen checkpoint on MATH-500, refit the DoM direction, and compare AUROC and cos(DoM_VPO, DoM_GRPO). If AUROC drops below 0.70 or cos < 0.5, the DoM finding is training-objective-dependent rather than architecture-intrinsic. This directly tests H-707.

2. **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy) could fail under VPO because the model deliberately preserves uncertainty.** VPO trains models to *not* commit to a single answer — the whole point is maintaining reward-diverse candidate sets. Selective prediction via DoM works by thresholding on a confidence proxy; if VPO-trained models show uniformly high dimensional uncertainty (because they maintain multiple strategies simultaneously), the DoM threshold loses discriminative power. The gap between confident-correct and confident-wrong narrows. **Test:** Run the F-8 selective-prediction pipeline on VPO vs GRPO checkpoints: if VPO's DoM-based coverage-accuracy curve is flat (no selective benefit), F-8's mechanism is entangled with GRPO's mode collapse rather than with problem-level difficulty. This directly tests H-282 and H-682.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature) may be caused by GRPO's gradient void at low pass rates, not by intrinsic problem structure.** VPO's Fig. 6 shows that GRPO's reward-space diversity collapses to near-zero during training — all rollouts converge to the same reward vector. Problems with low pass rates under GRPO (the D-bucket) get no positive gradient signal, so their activations freeze into a distinctive cluster. VPO, by sampling Dirichlet-weighted scalarizations, gives partial-credit signal even to problems that fail the aggregate scalar — which should dissolve the D-bucket signature. **Test:** Compute the D-bucket membership on MATH-500 under VPO-trained Qwen, measure the geometric signature from F-7, compare to GRPO. If D-bucket shrinks or loses its distinctive covariance structure, the signature is GRPO-induced. This directly tests H-708.

4. **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder) could reverse under VPO.** Under GRPO, correct solutions concentrate on a single strategy (low entropy), producing the observed collapse. Under VPO, correct solutions are explicitly trained to span multiple strategies (high reward-space diversity), which could *expand* rather than collapse the representation at the final token. If correct-trajectory representations under VPO show *less* collapse than incorrect ones (reversing F-4), the asymmetric collapse is a training-objective artifact. **Test:** Compare L19 final-token effective dimensionality for correct vs incorrect trajectories on MATH-500 under VPO vs GRPO checkpoints.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** VPO trains on Qwen models (1.7B, 4B, 7B-Coder, 8B) — the 1.5B and 7B sizes bracket our experimental regime. The paper's core finding that GRPO produces "near-duplicates" (diversity → 0.003 on Maze, Table 2) while VPO maintains diversity directly challenges whether the DoM direction is stable across RL objectives. cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 suggests DoM ≈ PC1 of the activation covariance; VPO would change that covariance by distributing mass across the Pareto front rather than collapsing it.

- **F-8 (71.6% at 50% coverage):** VPO's explicit separation of exploration (training) from exploitation (search) is philosophically opposed to the selective-prediction paradigm, which uses a single-pass confidence score to decide whether to answer. Under VPO, the right strategy is to *always* generate K candidates and let search select — making F-8's coverage threshold obsolete as a deployment mechanism. H-851 (width-scaling via DoM selection) is directly testable: does DoM-ranked best-of-K selection beat random selection more on GRPO than on VPO pools?

- **F-7 (D-bucket signature):** The reward collinearity diagnostic in Table 9 and App. F provides a principled way to characterize the reward landscape. The on-policy ρ̄ values (0.03–0.95 across domains) map onto how much reward-space diversity the model maintains. This is analogous to asking whether D-bucket problems have a different ρ̄ structure than easy problems.

- **H-56/H-59 (K-sample consistency vs DoM):** VPO directly maximizes pass@k diversity, so K-sample consistency under VPO would be a much harder baseline for DoM to beat. If DoM selection still outperforms consistency-rate on a VPO-trained pool, that strengthens F-2; if not, it suggests DoM is mainly useful on mode-collapsed distributions.

- **H-57/H-60 (D-bucket vanishes at K=40 majority vote):** VPO's diversity means fewer K are needed for the majority-vote frontier to stabilize — D-bucket shrinkage should happen at lower K under VPO than under GRPO.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Stochastic Dirichlet scalarization** — Sample w ~ Dir(1) over d reward dimensions each training step; evaluate set-level reward as E_w[max_{y∈S} w⊤r(x,y)]. Replaces fixed scalar advantage.
  *Replication cost*: Not directly applicable (we don't do RL training). However, the Dirichlet weighting scheme over reward components could be applied as an analytical tool to our cached features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have RL training infrastructure for Qwen.

- **Multi-answer chain generation** — Train model to emit m=3 candidates per rollout in a single autoregressive chain, separated by delimiter tokens. Each candidate attends to prior ones.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day(s) for training; inference is cheap.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires RL training loop.

- **Reward-space diversity metric** — Average pairwise L1 distance between reward vectors of K completions. Measures spread in reward space (not token space). A pool with diversity → 0 has mode-collapsed.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached data if we have multiple completions per problem.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we could compute this on our K-sample data if we have multiple MATH-500 completions scored by correctness sub-components.

- **On-policy reward collinearity diagnostic (ρ̄)** — Flatten (N_responses × D_reward_dims), compute D×D Pearson correlation matrix, average off-diagonal entries. Predicts when diversity-preserving training helps.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applicable to any feature matrix. We could compute ρ̄ of our cov-spectrum features or DoM-decomposed scores across problems.

## Approaches & framings

- **Exploration–exploitation separation:** Training should produce diverse candidate pools; search at test time handles exploitation. This frames our DoM-based selective prediction (F-8) as a *single-pass exploitation* strategy that could be outperformed by K-sample search with a diversity-preserving policy. Intersects H-851 (width-scaling).

- **Reward diversity vs surface diversity:** VPO distinguishes between token-level surface variation (noise) and reward-space coverage (structured diversity). This maps onto our distinction between geometrically distinct solutions vs cosmetically different CoT traces. Intersects F-9 (CoE redundancy) — CoE-60 trajectory features may capture surface diversity rather than reward diversity.

- **Pareto front coverage as training objective:** Instead of optimizing a single scalar, cover the Pareto front of multi-objective rewards. This reframes the question of what DoM actually measures: if DoM = PC1 of the covariance, and PC1 captures the axis of maximum variance in a mode-collapsed distribution, then DoM is measuring the *scalar collapse axis* rather than a semantically meaningful correctness signal. Intersects F-2.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- VPO is implemented on top of veRL (Sheng et al. 2024) with GRPO backbone; only the advantage estimator changes.
- Training: FSDP1 with bf16 mixed precision, fp32 parameter dtype, AdamW lr=1e-6, constant schedule, no warmup, weight decay 0.01, gradient clip 1.0, KL coefficient 1e-3 (k3 estimator), PPO-clip ε=0.2, dual-clip c=3.0, ppo_epochs=1.
- Rollout sampling: vLLM at temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, top-k -1. Eval: temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95 or 1.0 depending on domain.
- K=8 scalarization weights shared across G rollouts in each group; ~48 GPU-hours per run on 4×H100.
- Multi-answer uses m=3 candidates per chain with delimiter token separation.
- All code built on veRL framework.
