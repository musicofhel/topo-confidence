# Triage brief — 2405.07987 — The Platonic Representation Hypothesis

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

### 1. PRH-derived cross-model kernel alignment may subsume F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)

**Mechanism**: Huh et al. demonstrate that mutual k-NN alignment between an LLM
and a vision model (DINOv2) is a *positive* predictor of GSM8K accuracy,
plotted as an "emergence-esque trend" (their Figure 4 right). They explicitly
include math reasoning in their downstream-correctness story. Our F-2 isolates
a single direction at L19 prefill that gives 0.7731 AUROC on MATH-500. If PRH
is correct, then a *modality-agnostic* per-problem alignment score
(mutual k-NN between Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill embeddings on a MATH-500 prompt and
DINOv2 embeddings on a paired image, or simply Qwen-1.5B ↔ Qwen-7B alignment
on the same prompt) should also predict per-problem correctness — possibly
*better* than the supervised L19 DoM, since alignment is label-free.

**Falsifiable test**: Compute mutual k-NN(k=10) between Qwen-1.5B L19 prefill
activations and Qwen-7B L19 prefill activations on the 500 MATH-500 prompts,
using the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZs. Compute a
per-problem alignment score (the fraction of mutual neighbors among the
problem's k-NN set in each model). Test whether per-problem alignment
correlates with per-problem correctness (Spearman ρ, AUROC of alignment-score
threshold). If AUROC ≥ 0.7731, F-2's "single direction" framing collapses to
a special case of platonic-kernel alignment. If AUROC ≪ 0.7731, PRH's
correctness-predicts-alignment story does not transfer to per-problem
prediction at the prefill token, only to population-level model competence.

### 2. PRH "last token shows no alignment signal" directly contradicts F-2's choice of probe location

**Mechanism**: Appendix C.2 reports — verbatim — "Simply taking the last token
did not show any strong alignment signal. We also experimented with prompting
the language model and taking the last token representation. ... Using
prompting showed similar trends to average pooling but had slightly lower
alignment scores." Their working choice is **average pooling each layer to a
single token**. Our F-2 / F-8 pipeline reads a *single* last-prompt-token
hidden state at L19 and finds it carries the strongest correctness signal.
The two findings are in tension: if last-token L19 is information-rich for
correctness (our claim), it should also align across scales (PRH's
prediction); if it does not align (PRH's observation), correctness signal
must come from a within-model basis that is *orthogonal* to the platonic
kernel — which would be a deeper claim than "F-2 just measures alignment."

**Falsifiable test**: On the cached MATH-500 activations, compute mutual k-NN
alignment between Qwen-1.5B and Qwen-7B at L19 in three configurations:
(a) last prompt token (the F-2 probe location), (b) average-pooled prompt
tokens (PRH-style), (c) the post-answer-newline marker H-18 looks at.
Predict: PRH says (b) > (a). Our F-2 logic predicts (a) is the most
correctness-discriminative. If alignment (b) ≫ (a) but DoM AUROC (a) ≫ (b),
then the F-2 signal lives in a within-model subspace that is *not* the
platonic kernel — strengthens F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality) into a
per-direction claim about what is and isn't shared across models.

### 3. PRH bijective-observation assumption refutes F-1 universality framing if it survives only for vision/text and not within-modality scale pairs

**Mechanism**: Section 4.1 explicitly grounds the convergence proof on
*bijective, deterministic* observation functions. Section 6 acknowledges that
this fails for lossy/stochastic projections, and the empirical alignment
ceiling they report is ≈0.16 (mutual k-NN, max=1.0) — i.e. only 16% of
nearest neighbors agree even between the *most aligned* vision-language pair
they study. F-1 ("dimensional breathing is universal across transformer
architectures and scales") implicitly extends a PRH-style universality claim
to the inference-time *trajectory*, not just to static representations. PRH's
own data is a refutation candidate: if even strongly bijective same-modality
pairs (Qwen-1.5B ↔ Qwen-7B, both English text on identical prompts) show
mutual k-NN alignment ≪ 1.0 at L19 prefill, then F-1's universality is
a population-statistical claim about *amplitude* of dimensional change, not
about the geometry of the change.

**Falsifiable test**: Compute mutual k-NN(k=10) and CKNNA between
Qwen-1.5B and Qwen-7B residuals at every layer on the same 500 MATH-500
prompts. If even at the best-aligning layer pair the score is < 0.30, this
quantifies how much breathing universality (F-1) is content-locked vs.
geometry-locked, and confirms that "all transformers breathe" is a statement
about per-token effective rank trajectories, not about hidden-state
neighborhoods.

### 4. PRH "alignment increases with scale and competence" predicts a monotone pattern that breathing F-5/F-6 explicitly violates

**Mechanism**: Their Figure 3 / Section 2.2 claim is that **larger** and
**more competent** models align *more*. Our F-6 reports a 7B-vs-1.5B
inversion: the 7B model has *more concentrated* incorrect-group prefill
geometry, i.e. the more competent model has a *narrower*, not wider,
prefill kernel. If PRH's monotone-with-scale claim were correct on
MATH-500, the 7B mutual k-NN to a third reference model (e.g. Llama-3-8B)
should strictly exceed the 1.5B's, and the gap should track the K=1 accuracy
gap (48.6% → 73.2%, +24.6 pp). F-6's incorrect-group concentration suggests
that at the per-bucket level the 7B kernel may actually be *less* aligned
on incorrect items because it has resolved them into a tighter subset.

**Falsifiable test**: Bin MATH-500 problems into the 7B-correct / 7B-incorrect
groups using `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`. Within each
bin, compute mutual k-NN alignment of (Qwen-1.5B, Qwen-7B) at L19 prefill.
If the alignment is *higher* on the 7B-correct bin and *lower* on the
7B-incorrect bin, F-6's PR inversion has a clean PRH-language reframing as
"the larger model becomes more aligned to a third platonic kernel only on
the items it solves." If alignment is monotone in 7B accuracy on either bin,
PRH's coarse claim transfers to per-problem level.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Mutual k-NN alignment metric** — fraction of overlap in k-nearest-neighbor
  sets between two models on a paired dataset; symmetric, batched, k=10 is
  default; the family of CKNNA generalizes between this and CKA. Eqn. 18 in
  Appendix A.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on 500-sample MATH-500 prefill cache.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct probe of F-1, F-2, F-6.

- **CKNNA (Centered Kernel Nearest-Neighbor Alignment)** — Centered Kernel
  Alignment restricted to top-k mutual neighbors; recovers CKA at k=|X| and
  mutual k-NN at small k.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 hour CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a continuous knob between local
  (k=10) and global (k=1024) alignment, useful for distinguishing "neighbors
  agree" from "global geometry agrees."

- **BrainScore-style max-over-layer-pairs alignment** — vision uses class
  token of each layer, language averages each layer to a single token, then
  pairwise alignment across all layer pairs is computed and the **max** is
  reported. This reduces ambiguity about which layer to compare.
  *Replication cost*: ~2 hours CPU on cached residuals (28 layers × 28 layers
  for Qwen-1.5B vs Qwen-7B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap, and directly tests the claim that
  L19 is the privileged layer (do other layer pairs show stronger alignment?).

- **L2 normalization + 95th-percentile feature truncation** before alignment
  — hides emergent outlier dimensions (Dettmers et al., 2022).
  *Replication cost*: trivial preprocessing.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be a flag in any alignment helper.

- **Caption density variation (5/10/20/30 words via LLaMA3-8B-Instruct
  summary)** — tests sensitivity of alignment to information content of the
  conditioning text.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day GPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — orthogonal to MATH-500 correctness.

- **Color cooccurrence PMI sanity check** — recovers a CIELAB-like color
  layout from text-only or image-only PMI on quantized colors.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 hour CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — purely conceptual, no transfer to F-N.

- **Average pooling each layer to a single token (LLM)** — preferred over
  last-token extraction in their hands. Our pipeline does the opposite.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — recompute from cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes (as control for refutation #2 above).

## Approaches & framings

- **Representation-as-kernel**: characterize a representation purely by the
  similarity structure it induces on a dataset. Aligns with our temporal
  effective-rank framing (F-1) and CoE-60 (F-9), both of which are kernel
  summaries of the residual stream — and reframes them as alignment-to-truth
  proxies rather than as standalone confidence signals.
- **PMI as the convergent kernel** (Eqn. 6): contrastive learners with NCE
  objectives converge to a representation whose dot-product approximates
  pointwise mutual information; bijective observation functions preserve this
  across modalities. Implies a label-free "ground truth" the L19 DoM can be
  scored against.
- **Multitask / Capacity / Simplicity-Bias triple** as the three drivers of
  convergence. Reframes F-1's universality as "all sufficiently large
  next-token predictors satisfy the same constraints, hence breathe similarly."
- **Anna Karenina principle for representations**: "all strong models are
  alike, each weak model is weak in its own way." Direct rhetorical bridge
  to F-7 (D-bucket) — D-bucket may be the residue of "weak" trajectories
  that haven't converged to the platonic kernel.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Public repo: github.com/minyoungg/platonic-rep — likely contains a clean
  implementation of mutual k-NN, CKNNA, and the BrainScore-max layer-pair
  loop. Worth vendoring before reimplementing.
- Default hyperparameters: k=10, batch size = 1000–1024, l2-normalize,
  95th-percentile clip on outlier dimensions.
- They use the **class token** for ViTs (not patch-mean) and **mean pool**
  for LLMs (not last token); both are layer-wise.
- "Maximum over pairwise layer alignments" is the headline number — they
  do not pre-commit to a layer.
- Cross-modal alignment is computed *with paired data only* (image + its
  caption); for our use we substitute "same prompt seen by two models."
- Bias-correction note: HSIC has a sample-size bias; they use Unbiased CKA
  in some panels.

## 2405.07987 — The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024)

**Relevance:** Reframes F-1 (cross-architecture breathing universality) as a
specific case of representation convergence under multitask scaling, model
capacity, and simplicity bias. Provides the canonical kernel-alignment
metric (mutual k-NN, k=10) and the BrainScore-style max-over-layer-pairs
protocol that lets us test whether F-2's L19 prefill DoM is a within-model
projection of a cross-model platonic kernel, or a model-specific direction
orthogonal to it. Their GSM8K alignment-vs-accuracy emergence trend (Figure
4) is the closest existing evidence that representation alignment predicts
math-problem correctness, which is the exact prediction problem F-2/F-8
address on MATH-500.

**Key claim we tested:** Cross-model kernel alignment (mutual k-NN) increases
with scale and competence; it predicts downstream math performance (GSM8K)
in a non-linear, "emergence-esque" trend.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — Triggers FE80 (per-problem alignment as
label-free correctness probe), FE81 (layer-pair scan), FE82 (within-bin
alignment to test F-6 reframing), FE83 (cross-arch generalization, cached
subset of H-20). All four are cheap (≤3h CPU) and run on existing
`pathway11_h100` caches; results will determine whether F-2's supervised
DoM is a special case of the platonic kernel or genuinely model-specific.
Two specific tensions to resolve: (a) PRH says last-token alignment is
weak but average-pool is strong; F-2 picks last-token as the strongest
correctness probe. (b) PRH says scaling tightens alignment; F-6 reports
a 7B PR inversion driven by incorrect-group concentration.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE80, P11-FE81, P11-FE82, P11-FE83, H-20,
H-90, H-91, H-92.
