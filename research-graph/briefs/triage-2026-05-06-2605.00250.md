# Triage brief — 2605.00250 — Information-Geometric Adaptive Sampling for Graph Diffusion

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00250
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

No genuine refutations identified after careful read — paper is likely off-topic or purely non-overlapping. Recommend weak CITED ONLY status.

**Rationale:** This paper is about adaptive step-size control for *graph diffusion generative models* (molecular/social network generation). It derives a Fisher-Rao metric on the statistical manifold of *diffusion transition kernels* to adaptively schedule sampling steps. The "Fisher-Rao" and "information geometry" keywords triggered the admission filter, but the paper's domain (graph generation sampling schedules) has no mechanistic overlap with our findings about *residual-stream geometry predicting LLM correctness*.

Specific reasons ≥3 refutations cannot be constructed:

1. **No LLM hidden states involved.** The paper operates on graph adjacency/node-feature spaces during diffusion generation, not on transformer residual streams. There is no probe, no hidden-state extraction, no correctness signal.

2. **Different use of "Fisher-Rao."** The Fisher-Rao metric here measures sensitivity of Gaussian transition kernels to drift perturbations (Eq. 8: I(f_t) = dt/g²_t · I). This is a generative-process diagnostic, not an analysis of representation geometry. It cannot refute F-1 (breathing), F-2 (DoM AUROC), or F-10 (PH-null) because it measures a fundamentally different object.

3. **No correctness/confidence signal.** The paper has no concept analogous to correct vs. incorrect answers, selective prediction, or confidence calibration. It cannot challenge F-8 (selective-prediction coverage) or any H-N hypothesis about what the prefill signal encodes.

4. **No layer-wise analysis of trained models.** The "manifold" in this paper is the space of transition distributions parameterized by drift vectors during reverse-SDE sampling — not the manifold of hidden states across layers. The "adaptive sampling" is about *time-step scheduling during generation*, not about probing or steering.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

**Extremely tenuous connections only:**

- **H-544 (dual/information-geometric coordinates):** The admission note flagged this. However, H-544 concerns applying information geometry *to the residual-stream activation manifold* (e.g., treating the population of hidden states as a statistical manifold and computing Fisher-Rao distances between them). This paper applies information geometry to the *transition kernel manifold of a diffusion sampler*. These are different objects. The connection is purely terminological — both use Fisher-Rao metrics, but on different spaces.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing):** One could stretch to say that the paper's notion of "informational speed varies across the trajectory" is analogous to breathing (dimension changes across layers). But the analogy is superficial: breathing measures participation ratio of activations, while DVS measures drift-field norm divided by noise scale along a generative process.

No numerical anchors from the headlines table (AUROC 0.7731, cos 0.046, etc.) can be meaningfully connected to this paper's results (FCD scores on QM9 molecular generation).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Drift Variation Score (DVS)** — Computes ‖f(x_k, t_k) − f(x_{k-1}, t_{k-1})‖² / g²_{t_k} as a local sensitivity indicator for adaptive step sizing.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (the formula itself is trivial to compute on any sequence of vectors).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — it measures sensitivity of a diffusion drift field between time steps, not a quantity naturally applicable to a fixed set of 500 cached activation vectors. Our activations are not sequential samples from a diffusion process.

- **Equal arc-length parametrization on Fisher-Rao statistical manifold** — Reparametrize a curve on a statistical manifold so each discrete step covers equal informational distance.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU if applied to any parametric curve.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires a parametric family of distributions evolving along some axis. Our L19 activations don't form a parametric curve in the relevant sense.

- **EMA-smoothed variation state with power-law step scaling** — Exponential moving average (α=0.2) of local variation with β=0.5 square-root damping for adaptive control.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — engineering detail for diffusion sampling, not applicable to our static cached activations.

## Approaches & framings

- **Treating sequential numerical outputs as a parametric curve on a Riemannian statistical manifold.** If we ever model the *layer-by-layer* evolution of hidden states as a parametric curve (which H-544 gestures toward), the mathematical framework in §3.1–3.3 provides a clean formalization. But this is a "if we ever need it" connection — no immediate intersection with current F-N/H-N framings.

- **Constant informational speed as an optimality criterion.** The idea that a well-designed process should maintain equal information-distance per step is potentially relevant to thinking about why L19 is special (maybe it's where informational speed peaks in the layer-wise trajectory). But this would require a completely different operationalization than anything in this paper.

## Proposed FutureExperiments

### Tier 1 — Cheap local (5min–2hr, CPU only, ROI 5–10)

The core DVS formula (‖Δf‖²/g²) is specific to diffusion sampling. However, the broader *idea* — measuring how fast a representation changes along some axis and using that as a diagnostic — could be loosely mapped to our layer-axis data. Specifically, if we had per-layer activations (layers 0–27), we could compute a "layer-wise drift variation" measuring how fast the hidden state changes between adjacent layers, analogous to DVS along the time axis. But we only cache L19 activations in our standard NPZ pipeline, not all-layer activations. The all-layer data would require new extraction (H100 time).

Given that this paper is off-topic and provides no method directly applicable to cached single-layer activations, no Tier 1 experiment is warranted.

No experiment warranted. The connection between graph diffusion sampling schedules and residual-stream correctness prediction is too tenuous to justify H100 time.
