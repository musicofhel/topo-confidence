# Triage brief — 2506.18167 — Understanding Reasoning in Thinking Language Models via Steering Vectors

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18167
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Decomposable-behaviors refutation of F-2's "single direction" framing.**
   F-2 says a single L19 prefill DoM direction predicts correctness at AUROC
   0.7731. Venhoff et al. show that DeepSeek-R1-Distill thinking models encode
   ≥4 *separately-mediated* linear directions for distinct reasoning behaviors
   (uncertainty estimation, example testing, backtracking, knowledge addition),
   each with its own causally-peaked layer (their Qwen-1.5B: L17 / L17 / L18
   for these three behaviors via attribution patching). If our "prefill DoM" is
   actually a *projection* of one of these behavior axes (most likely
   uncertainty-estimation), then a 4-vector behavior-decomposed predictor
   should beat 0.7731 on the same 500 problems.
   **Falsifiable test**: extract their four behavior DoM vectors on
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B (after running their GPT-4o annotation on our cached K=1 CoTs)
   and stack them as a 4-feature logistic. If joint AUROC > 0.79 OOF, F-2 is
   under-decomposed.

2. **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token DoM, cos 0.046) reduces to a
   behavior-decomposition artifact.**
   F-3 treats the prefill/final orthogonality as evidence that the model
   computes "expected difficulty" at prefill and "answer-confidence" at final
   token. Venhoff et al. provide a competing read: prefill activations are
   dominated by the uncertainty/backtracking-anticipation axis (high in
   thinking models per their Figure 2: ~9% backtracking, ~24% uncertainty in
   thinking vs 1–4% / 4–6% in base models), while final-token activations are
   dominated by deduction-completion. Under this read, cos(prefill, final) =
   0.046 is just cos(uncertainty-direction, deduction-direction), which their
   Appendix C shows is naturally low.
   **Falsifiable test**: project our prefill L19 DoM onto their
   uncertainty-estimation steering vector. If |cos| > 0.5, F-3 is a labelling
   artifact, not a statement about temporal computation.

3. **H-1 per-position DoM steering may be the wrong steering scheme entirely.**
   H-1 proposes per-position steering with the *prefill DoM* extracted from
   correct-vs-incorrect contrasts. Venhoff et al.'s evidence is that effective
   steering on thinking models requires (a) extracting from
   *behavior-annotated* contrasts, not correctness contrasts, (b) applying at
   *behavior-specific* token positions identified by GPT-4o annotation, and
   (c) normalizing to per-layer mean-activation magnitude. If H-1 fails (no
   accuracy lift) but their behavior-vector scheme produces measurable
   behavior shift on the same model, the failure mode is a wrong contrast
   construction, not a wrong layer. This refutes the implicit H-1 assumption
   that *correctness contrast → causal direction*.
   **Falsifiable test**: run H-1 with two contrast variants — (a) our
   correctness DoM, (b) their backtracking/uncertainty DoM. Compare K=1 MATH-500
   accuracy delta under +/- 1.0σ steering for both.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket has *collective* signature, not per-problem) is challenged
   by per-token behavior steering.**
   F-7's headline claim is that D-bucket membership (K=1 right, K=8 wrong) has
   no per-problem geometric signature; it only emerges in a collective
   density. Venhoff et al. localize backtracking to a single residual-stream
   direction at L17 (Qwen-1.5B) that is causally manipulable per-token via
   attribution patching. If D-bucket problems are pathological because the
   model *fails to backtrack* on them, then per-token "backtracking direction
   activation" should be a per-problem signature.
   **Falsifiable test**: compute per-token backtracking-axis projection on
   D-bucket vs A-bucket K=1 generations on cached P11 NPZs. If D-bucket has
   significantly lower backtracking-direction activation, F-7's collective-
   only mechanism is wrong.

5. **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics) may collapse to
   "breathing is uncertainty-behavior dependent".**
   F-5 frames the temporal participation-ratio "breathing" as content-driven.
   Venhoff et al.'s Figure 2 shows R1-distill models emit ~9% backtracking
   sentences vs ~1% in base models — a 9× difference in a behavior whose
   linear direction lives at the same middle layers where breathing peaks.
   If breathing amplitude tracks per-window backtracking-direction
   activation, F-5 needs a finer specification: "behavior-mediated" rather
   than "content-mediated".
   **Falsifiable test**: align per-token breathing PR on cached P11 7B/1.5B
   trajectories with per-token annotation labels (GPT-4o pipeline on our
   CoTs). If PR peaks 1:1 with backtracking/uncertainty token windows, F-5
   needs revision.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, 1.5B)** ↔ their Qwen-1.5B selected
  layer L17 for backtracking/uncertainty steering. Two layers off ours, on a
  different (R1-distilled) Qwen-1.5B — the proximity is suggestive that our
  L19 prefill direction is partially the same direction Venhoff et al.
  isolate as "uncertainty-estimation".
- **F-3 (cos(prefill DoM, final DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ their Appendix C cosine
  heatmap showing most behavior pairs are near-orthogonal (low |cos|) — the
  same kind of orthogonality their paper attributes to *behavior separation*,
  not temporal separation. Direct alternative explanation for our F-3 number.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** ↔ their selected
  layers span L17–L18 (different layers per behavior) on Qwen-1.5B; suggests
  CoE-60's multi-layer integration may be capturing per-behavior layer
  specialization that single-layer L19 misses. We previously saw 0.811 vs
  L19's redundancy; gap may reopen if behaviors are layer-specialized.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** ↔ direct
  method-template paper. Their public code at
  github.com/cvenhoff/steering-thinking-llms gives the steering harness we'd
  want as a starting point (greedy decoding, 1000 max tokens, normalization
  to mean-activation magnitude).
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** ↔
  their attribution-patching localization to specific middle layers is the
  shallower-than-head-level version of H-13. Not a substitute, but evidence
  that the localization machinery works on this model family.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed)** ↔
  if behaviors live at distinct middle layers, the apparent "rotation" we see
  may be a sequence of behavior-direction crossings rather than a smooth
  RoPE-driven rotation. Their per-behavior layer assignments would let us
  decompose the rotation into discrete behavior transitions.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Difference-of-Means with sequence-position-averaged activations** —
  per-behavior DoM where the positive set's activation is averaged over a
  10-token window preceding and including the annotated behavior sequence.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU per behavior given cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — exact recipe for per-behavior DoM
  extraction we'd need for H-1 variant comparison.

- **Attribution patching for layer localization** — KL-divergence-on-
  next-token gradient evaluated against candidate steering vector at each
  layer; pick layer with peak |∂L/∂a · u|.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 per behavior (gradient-based, all 28 layers).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would replace our hand-picked L19 with a
  data-driven layer choice for the correctness DoM.

- **Steering-vector magnitude normalization to layer mean activation** —
  rescale u to ||ā_overall_ℓ|| before adding to residual stream; ensures
  comparable steering strength across layers and behaviors.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-1 implementation almost certainly needs
  this normalization to produce measurable accuracy deltas.

- **GPT-4o annotation pipeline (6-label scheme)** — initialization,
  deduction, adding-knowledge, example-testing, uncertainty-estimation,
  backtracking. Prompt template in Appendix A; output format
  ["label"]…["end-section"].
  *Replication cost*: ~$5–10 in API costs for our 500 cached P11 CoTs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap and gives per-token behavior labels
  we don't have.

- **Embedding/unembedding cosine filter** — exclude early layers whose
  steering vectors have high max-cos with embedding rows (token-specific,
  not behavior-specific).
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful sanity check for our L19 DoM
  (should have low embedding-cos if it's behavioral, not lexical).

- **Per-behavior cosine-similarity heatmap** — pairwise cos between behavior
  steering vectors at their causally-relevant layers; tests whether
  behaviors share a direction.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly diagnoses the F-3 alternative
  explanation.

- **Behavioral-fraction differential** (their Figure 2 method) — annotate
  reasoning chains, compute per-category sentence fraction, compare thinking
  vs base models on identical prompts.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU + GPT-4o annotation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a direct R1-distill vs Qwen2.5
  comparison we don't have.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning as decomposable into separately-mediated linear directions.**
  Reframes our "single L19 prefill DoM ⇒ correctness" story as a
  potentially aggregated projection of multiple behavior axes. Intersects
  F-2 directly: opens the question of whether correctness predictions can be
  improved by stacking 4–6 behavior probes vs one correctness probe.

- **Causally-peaked-layer model.** Each behavior has *one* dominant layer
  (KL peak in attribution patching), not a distributed signature. Intersects
  F-9 directly — would predict per-behavior layers different from L19, which
  CoE-60 captures and single-layer DoM misses.

- **Thinking-model behavior taxonomy as the unit of analysis.** Frames
  reasoning models in terms of micro-behavior fractions (initialization
  16%, deduction 50%, uncertainty 24%, backtracking 9%, etc.) rather than
  global statistics like accuracy or PR. Could replace our pure
  correctness-contrast experiments with behavior-contrast experiments.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Greedy decoding, 1000 max tokens per response. (Our P11 H100 uses
  1024-tok labels — directly comparable.)
- Selected layers per model from attribution patching:
  Llama-8B = L12 (≈37% depth), Qwen-1.5B = L17–L18 (≈60% depth, our L19 is
  ≈68%), Qwen-14B = L24–L29 (≈50–60% depth). All sit in the middle third
  of the network — consistent with our "middle layer" prior.
- Public code: https://github.com/cvenhoff/steering-thinking-llms.
- Annotation token-position scope: 10 tokens preceding the annotated
  sequence + the annotated sequence itself ("decision point + execution").
- Normalize each candidate vector to ||ā_overall_ℓ|| magnitude before
  attribution patching to avoid layer-norm-scale bias.
- Steering-strength scan reports α = ±1.0 implicit (no scan documented in
  paper); be careful when replicating.
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill Qwen-1.5B is a *different* base than our
  Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct — distillation target is R1's CoT, not direct math
  finetuning. Cross-base comparison is possible but interpretation must
  separate "thinking vs not-thinking" from "1.5B base differences".

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2506.18167 — Understanding Reasoning in Thinking Language Models via Steering Vectors (Venhoff, Arcuschin, Torr, Conmy, Nanda — ICLR 2025 Workshop)

**Relevance:** Direct method-template paper for H-1 and a structural challenge
to F-2/F-3. Demonstrates that thinking-model reasoning behaviors (uncertainty,
backtracking, example-testing, knowledge-addition) live at separate
middle-layer linear directions, extractable via behavior-contrast
Difference-of-Means and localizable via attribution patching. Their
Qwen-1.5B selected layers (L17–L18) are within two layers of our hand-picked
L19, raising the live possibility that our F-2 prefill DoM is a projection
of one of their behavior axes (most likely uncertainty-estimation). Public
code at github.com/cvenhoff/steering-thinking-llms gives an off-the-shelf
steering harness for H-1 replication.

**Key claim we tested:** Reasoning behaviors in thinking models are mediated
by separate, largely-orthogonal linear directions in the residual stream,
each localizable to a single middle layer via attribution patching.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — H-41 / H-42 / H-43 / P11-FE71–74 set up the
direct comparison. Their decomposed-direction framing is a serious
alternative explanation for F-2's "single direction" headline and F-3's
prefill/final orthogonality. Until the GPT-4o annotation pipeline is run on
our cached CoTs (P11-FE71), we cannot tell whether our 0.7731 AUROC is the
uncertainty axis, a 4-behavior aggregate, or genuinely a separate
correctness direction.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74; H-41,
H-42, H-43; F-2, F-3, F-7, F-9; H-1, H-13, H-17.
