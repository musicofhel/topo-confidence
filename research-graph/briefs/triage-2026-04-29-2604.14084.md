# Triage brief — 2604.14084 — TIP: Token Importance in On-Policy Distillation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14084
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-7 ("D-bucket signature is collective, not per-problem").**
   *Mechanism*: TIP claims that the most corrective tokens are individually
   identifiable as a "low-entropy + high teacher–student divergence" quadrant
   (Q3) — i.e. *per-token*, not collective. If overconfident-wrong is detectable
   token-by-token from cheap statistics, F-7's claim that the D-bucket has only
   a *collective* (not per-problem) geometric signature is overstated.
   *Falsifiable*: compute per-token student entropy and (if 7B teacher logits
   are available) per-token KL on the 500 cached MATH-500 1.5B traces. Bin
   problems by per-trace density of low-entropy tokens. If D-bucket problems
   have significantly higher Q3-token density than A/B/C-bucket
   (Mann–Whitney p < 0.01), F-7's "collective only" framing is overturned.

2. **Refutes F-2 ("prefill L19 DoM is *best* correctness predictor, AUROC 0.7731").**
   *Mechanism*: TIP shows that ≤10% of tokens (the Q3 quadrant) carry enough
   signal to match all-token training. Implies a token-level signal exists
   whose *aggregate* (e.g. mean student entropy across the generation)
   integrates more correctness information than any single residual-stream
   direction at prefill. Token-level uncertainty captures dynamic collapse
   during generation; prefill cannot.
   *Falsifiable*: compute mean per-token entropy across the generation for
   each MATH-500 trace, score AUROC against the 1024-tok correctness label.
   If AUROC > 0.7731, F-2's "prefill is best" claim must be revised to
   "prefill is best *among static features*."

3. **Refutes H-12 ("semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM").**
   *Mechanism*: TIP explicitly demonstrates that entropy alone misses the
   low-entropy/high-divergence quadrant — exactly the overconfident-wrong
   region (D-bucket analog). If we replace DoM with SEP we will reproduce
   that failure mode. So H-12's "replace" framing is too strong; entropy is
   *structurally incomplete*.
   *Falsifiable*: compute SEP-style entropy and DoM scores on the 500 traces,
   stratify by bucket. If SEP and DoM correlate ≥0.6 on A/B/C buckets but
   diverge on D-bucket (SEP fails to flag D-bucket while DoM does), H-12
   must be downgraded from "replace" to "complement, with D-bucket gap."

4. **Refutes F-8 ("selective prediction at 50% coverage → 71.6% accuracy via prefill DoM").**
   *Mechanism*: TIP's 50%-retention entropy frontier matches all-token
   training, suggesting that a label-free entropy threshold gating at 0.5
   coverage may match the supervised DoM probe's 71.6%. If yes, the
   *supervision* in F-8 is unnecessary — making the headline "selective
   prediction works *via prefill DoM*" misleading; the work is being done
   by entropy.
   *Falsifiable*: rank the 500 traces by mean per-token entropy, gate the
   top half, compute answered-set accuracy. If ≥ 71.6%, F-8's mechanism
   attribution must shift from "DoM-driven" to "entropy-driven, DoM
   coincident."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — TIP's entropy-only 50% retention
  works because per-token student entropy is itself a strong correctness
  proxy. Direct comparator: mean entropy AUROC vs 0.7731.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — TIP's Q3 quadrant
  (low-entropy + high-divergence) is the per-token analog of our
  overconfident-wrong D-bucket. They claim per-token identifiability;
  F-7 claims only collective identifiability. Direct contradiction at the
  unit-of-analysis level.
- **F-8 (selective prediction 71.6% @ 0.5 coverage)** — TIP's 50%-retention
  frontier matches all-token training in distillation; structurally identical
  to our 50% coverage selective-prediction frontier. Direct comparator:
  entropy-gated answered accuracy vs 71.6%.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)** — TIP suggests an even
  cheaper aggregate (mean per-token entropy) may be redundant with both —
  i.e. tightens F-9's redundancy claim into a three-way redundancy.
- **H-12 (SEP as label-free DoM replacement)** — TIP's "entropy is incomplete"
  result is *exactly* the boundary condition for H-12. Constrains H-12 from
  "replace" to "replace except on overconfident-wrong cases."
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing)** — TIP's "Q3 tokens carry corrective
  signal" suggests verification routing should be *token-level* (Q3-density
  trigger), not problem-level. Refines H-19's gating granularity.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-token entropy of student logits** — standard softmax entropy at each
  generation position; aggregate across the trace.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU if logits cached; 4h H100 if we need to
  recompute. We currently cache hidden states (NPZ), not logits, so this
  may require a small re-extract.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct comparator to F-2 prefill DoM AUROC.

- **Token-level teacher–student KL divergence** — KL between teacher (7B) and
  student (1.5B) softmax distributions at aligned token positions during
  on-policy student rollouts.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day — needs teacher forced-decode on student
  rollouts (token-position alignment is non-trivial because rollouts are
  student-driven).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for a subset (e.g. 50 problems) — gives the
  second axis to test the Q3 quadrant hypothesis directly.

- **Two-axis quadrant binning (entropy ≷ median × divergence ≷ median)** —
  partition tokens into four quadrants; the Q3 quadrant (low entropy, high
  divergence) is the corrective signal locus.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU once both per-token signals are computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests "D-bucket = high-Q3-density"
  hypothesis (refutation #1, #3 above).

- **Type-aware token sampling for training** — selective backprop on Q3
  tokens only.
  *Replication cost*: H100 + custom training loop.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we're not doing distillation; this is
  training-side, our regime is inference-side analysis.

## Approaches & framings

- **Two-axis decomposition (entropy × divergence)** — reframes "informative
  token" from a 1D uncertainty score to a 2D quadrant taxonomy. Maps onto
  our four-bucket A/B/C/D *problem* taxonomy at the token level: D-bucket
  ≈ Q3 (low entropy + wrong = high divergence). Reusable framing for
  per-token diagnosis of overconfident failure.
- **"Entropy is useful but structurally incomplete"** — formal acknowledgment
  that single-axis uncertainty fails to flag the overconfident-wrong region.
  Directly constrains the SEP/SEH family of label-free correctness probes
  (H-12).

## 2604.14084 — TIP: Token Importance in On-Policy Distillation (Xu, Sang, Zhou, He, Wang, Geramifard, 2026)

**Relevance:** Direct intersection with F-2 / F-7 / F-8. TIP introduces a
two-axis token taxonomy (student entropy × teacher–student divergence) and
shows that the low-entropy + high-divergence quadrant (Q3, "overconfident
wrong") carries dense corrective signal — a per-token analog of our D-bucket
finding (F-7). Their 50%-retention entropy frontier mirrors our 50%-coverage
selective-prediction frontier (F-8). They use Qwen2.5 + MATH-500, so their
signals are directly replicable on our cached 1.5B P11 traces.

**Key claim we tested:** A label-free per-token statistic (student entropy,
optionally combined with teacher–student KL) carries enough correctness signal
to match supervised probes — *except* on the overconfident-wrong (D-bucket / Q3)
region, where entropy alone fails.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — three FEs (P11-FE74/75/76) read directly off
cached MATH-500 1.5B data. Pre-registered: if mean per-token entropy AUROC
> 0.7731, F-2 leadership narrows; if Q3-density discriminates D-bucket, F-7's
"collective only" claim is overturned.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, H-72, H-73; constrains
H-12 (SEP replacement scope), refines H-19 (verification routing granularity).

**Status:** TO TEST
