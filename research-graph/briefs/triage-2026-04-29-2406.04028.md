# Triage brief — 2406.04028 — Contrastive Sparse Autoencoders for Interpreting Planning of Chess-Playing Agents

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04028
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos=0.046) by reframing as common-vs-differentiating decomposition.**
   *Mechanism*: Poupart's CSAE splits a hidden-state pair `[h(s_0); h(s_t)]` into `c`-features (common to both endpoints) and `d`-features (differentiating). Their Table 1 + clustering analysis shows the two feature sets carry uncorrelated structure even when extracted from the same hidden states (max-per-cluster Pearson averages ~0.1). Applied to our `[h(prefill); h(final)]` pair, the same decomposition would predict that the *raw* cosine `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046` is dominated by orthogonal `c`-features (context-dependent reconstruction) while the underlying `d`-features (correctness-differentiating) may be highly co-aligned. F-3 would then be a measurement artefact of pooling shared and contrastive subspaces.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Train a CSAE on cached P11 H100 hidden-state pairs `[h(prefill); h(final)]` for the 500 MATH-500 1.5B problems (correct vs incorrect = pair sign). Measure cos(d⁺_prefill_DoM, d⁻_final_DoM) restricted to `d`-features. If this exceeds, say, 0.4 while raw residual cos is 0.046, F-3 is refuted as written.

2. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) by showing trajectory-level contrastive features carry information single-state probes cannot reach.**
   *Mechanism*: Poupart trains the contrastive linear probe (eq. 9) on *concatenated* root-and-trajectory hidden states; their Table 1 shows F1=0.578 with the joint feature set vs lower per-component scores. The CoE-60 vs L19-DoM comparison in F-9 was conducted on independent per-layer features, never on a contrastively-decomposed pair. If the CSAE-style contrastive probe on `[prefill; mid-token]` or `[prefill; PANL]` pairs exceeds the L19-DoM AUROC of 0.7731 by ≥0.02, then F-9's "single-layer DoM is sufficient" claim is too strong — trajectory contrast adds signal that the single-vector projection misses.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Build a contrastive probe over (`h(prefill_L19)`, `h(L19 @ token-50)`) pairs for 500 MATH-500 problems; compare AUROC to 0.7731 baseline. Cost: 1h CPU on cached NPZs.

3. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective signature) if hierarchical clustering of d-features finds NO D-bucket cluster.**
   *Mechanism*: Poupart's Section 4.3 shows that agglomerative clustering of contrastive d-features yields a stable taxonomy of strategic concepts (their Fig. 8/9). Apply the same recipe to a CSAE trained on (`h(prefill_correct)`, `h(prefill_incorrect)`) pairs from 500 MATH-500 problems: if the resulting `d`-feature taxonomy fails to assign D-bucket problems (K=1 right, K=8 wrong, the 36-problem pathological set in F-7) to a coherent cluster, F-7's claim of a "distinctive collective signature" is undermined — the bucket would just be a tail of an otherwise smooth distribution.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Cluster d-features from a CSAE trained on cached P11 hidden states. Compute Adjusted Rand Index between cluster labels and D-bucket membership. ARI ≤ 0.1 refutes F-7.

4. **Refutes F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC=0.7731 as the canonical correctness direction) if a CSAE-style joint probe exceeds it by ≥0.02.**
   *Mechanism*: Poupart's central methodological claim is that single-state probing leaves planning-relevant information on the table. Our F-2 explicitly uses single-state (prefill-only or final-only) DoM. If joint contrastive probing on (`h(prefill); h(post-answer-newline)`) yields AUROC ≥ 0.79, F-2 should be reframed as "best *single-state* predictor" rather than "best correctness direction available."
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Use cached P11 H100 PANL activations + prefill activations; concat and fit a 2x-dim logistic probe with l1 regularization (poor-man's CSAE). Compare to F-2's 0.7731 5-fold OOF.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: Poupart's contrastive linear probe (eq. 9) is methodologically a joint-state extension of our single-state probe. Headline anchor: 0.7731. A CSAE-flavoured contrastive probe is a direct upgrade path.

- **F-3 (cos=0.046 between prefill and final-token DoM)**: The c/d decomposition directly reframes whether the orthogonality is geometric (real subspaces) or partitioning (common + differentiating components folded into a single vector). Headline anchor: 0.046.

- **F-7 (D-bucket pathological 36/500)**: Poupart's clustering taxonomy provides a literal recipe for testing whether D-bucket is a "feature cluster" or a tail.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)**: CSAE introduces a way to extract trajectory-level signal that single-layer L19 may not capture; the redundancy claim becomes contingent on which probe class one allows.

- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free)**: CSAE itself is unsupervised in feature discovery (only the optimality probe in eq. 9 needs labels). Provides an alternative label-free comparator alongside SEPs.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)**: The c/d partition is a different attribution lens (feature-level rather than head-level) — runs alongside H-13 as an independent decomposition.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive Sparse Autoencoder (CSAE)** — SAE with the dictionary partitioned into `[c; d]` features via tensor concatenation; trained with three losses: standard SAE reconstruction (eq. 5), a contrastive penalty pulling c-features together while pushing d-features apart (eq. 8 — `||c⁺−c⁻||₁ + ||d⁺⊙d⁻||₁`), and an auxiliary linear-probe BCE on d-features (eq. 9).
  *Replication cost*: 4-8h H100 for a small CSAE on cached MATH-500 prefill+final activations (much smaller than chess data volume).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly answers refutations 1, 2, 4 above and the refutation pile is explicitly load-bearing for F-2/F-3/F-9.

- **Joint hidden-state probing** — concatenate `h(s_0)` (root/prefill) with `h(s_t)` (trajectory step) before probing; F1=0.578 for joint vs ~0.54 for c-only or d-only (Table 1).
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached P11 H100 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheapest possible test of refutation 4 before committing to a full CSAE.

- **Activation-maximisation feature interpretation** — for each dictionary feature, retrieve the top-k samples that activate it most and inspect them qualitatively (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 4).
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU for a fitted SAE/CSAE.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a practical interpretability pass on whatever features F-2-style probes pick up.

- **Sanity checks via dead/overactive feature counts and entropy** — count features with activation frequency `F<0.1%` (dead) or `F>10%` (overactive); compute activation-distribution entropy `H(A_s)`, `H(A_t)` (Table 1).
  *Replication cost*: ~10 min CPU after fitting.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required to interpret a CSAE on our data.

- **Hierarchical-clustering feature taxonomy** — agglomerative clustering of features after NMF + t-SNE; produces a dendrogram for human exploration (Sec. 4.3).
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for refutation 3 (D-bucket cluster test).

- **Cross-cluster vs intra-cluster cosine similarity comparison of decoder weights** — diagnostic showing whether feature clusters reflect representational structure or only activation patterns (Fig. 8b).
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we fit a CSAE.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Single hidden state is insufficient for planning interpretability."** — the paper's core thesis. Reframes our F-2/F-8 in terms of single-state probes that may be missing trajectory-contrastive information; supports H-13's intuition that the relevant signal may live in cross-position structure.

- **Common (c) vs differentiating (d) feature partition.** — a generic decomposition that applies wherever you have paired hidden states (correct/incorrect, prefill/final, K=1/K=8). Directly gives a new framing for F-3 (orthogonality as partition artefact) and F-9 (redundancy contingent on probe class).

- **"Dynamical concepts" via rollouts** (Schut et al. 2023, extended here) — analogous to our breathing/temporal trajectory analysis (F-1) but framed at the optimal-vs-suboptimal trajectory level. Provides a chess-side parallel for our generation-time PR curves.

- **Frugal automated taxonomy via hierarchical clustering** (rather than LLM-based or causal-circuit interpretability). Useful framing for keeping a human in the loop on F-2/F-7 features.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/Xmaster6y/lczero-planning — LcZero-specific, but CSAE training code is the relevant artefact.
- Architecture: `f = ReLU(W_e h + b_e)`; `ĥ = W_d f + b_d`; with `f = [c; d]` partition implemented via tensor concatenation (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 3a).
- Three loss terms (must all be enabled): `L_SAE` (eq. 5, standard reconstruction + l1) on full `f`, *plus* a dedicated `L_SAE` on `c`-features alone reconstructing `h(s_0)`, *plus* `L_contrast` (eq. 8) coupling c⁺/c⁻ and d⁺/d⁻, *plus* `L±` (eq. 9, BCE optimality probe). Omitting any of the four reportedly degrades quality (their Table 1 + appendix B.2).
- Sanity check tells: dead `c`-features outnumber dead `d`-features (153 vs 0); overactive `d`-features outnumber overactive `c`-features (119 vs 58). Use these as health metrics during training.
- Linear probe on c-features outperforms d-features for optimality classification (Table 1 P column 0.541 vs 0.575 — actually d slightly better, but c carries trajectory-look-up-table behaviour; expect similar weirdness on transformer streams).
- Visualization: NMF → t-SNE → agglomerative cluster (Fig. 5); use `sklearn`'s `AgglomerativeClustering` with default Ward linkage.
- Per-feature pair-similarity metric: Pearson correlation of activation frequencies + count of overlapping top-k activated samples (Fig. 4b).
- Known failure mode (Sec. 5.1 "Limitations"): "blinking" — features develop different facets for symmetry-related inputs (white/black boards in chess). Analogous risk on MATH-500: features may have different facets for "answer present" vs "answer pending" prefixes.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Joint-probe concat baseline (cheapest CSAE-flavoured test).** Use cached P11 H100 prefill + final-token L19 NPZs for Qwen2.5-1.5B (`pathway11_h100/` from STATE.md). Concatenate the two activations per problem, fit a 5-fold OOF logistic probe with l2, compare AUROC against 0.7731 (F-2). If joint AUROC > 0.7731 + 0.01, refutation 4 has weight. Cost: 30 min CPU. No new data needed.
- **D-feature cosine on (prefill, final) pair.** Train a small SAE (no contrastive loss) on `[h(prefill); h(final)]` concatenated streams, then enforce a c/d split post-hoc by partitioning the dictionary into the half of features that reconstruct best from prefill alone (call them c) vs the rest (d). Recompute `cos(d_prefill_DoM, d_final_DoM)`. Refutes F-3 if cos rises sharply. Cost: 4h H100 for SAE fit + 30 min CPU. Cached prefill + final NPZs only.
- **D-bucket cluster-overlap test.** Fit any SAE (or use raw L19 prefill activations + agglomerative clustering with k=20). Compute ARI(cluster_labels, D-bucket_membership) on the 36 D-bucket vs 207 A-bucket problems (F-7). Cost: 30 min CPU. No GPU needed, no new data.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2406.04028 — Contrastive Sparse Autoencoders for Interpreting Planning of Chess-Playing Agents (Poupart, 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodologically adjacent. The paper introduces Contrastive Sparse Autoencoders (CSAE) — SAEs whose dictionary is partitioned into common (c) and differentiating (d) features when trained on paired hidden states from optimal vs suboptimal chess rollouts. The c/d partition idea is directly transposable from (root, trajectory) chess pairs to (prefill, final) MATH-500 pairs, and it provides a concrete reframing of three of our findings: F-3 (orthogonality may be a partition artefact), F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy is contingent on probe class), and F-7 (D-bucket "distinctive signature" can be tested as a cluster). The chess-domain experiments themselves don't transfer (ResNet+SE backbone, board-plane inputs), but the contrastive-loss formulation (eq. 8), the joint-probe approach (eq. 9), and the hierarchical-feature-taxonomy recipe (Sec. 4.3) all do.

**Key claim we tested:** Single-state hidden-state probing leaves trajectory-contrastive information on the table; a contrastively-decomposed paired-state probe captures planning-relevant concepts that single-state probes cannot.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — three concrete tests proposed (P11-FE4028 cheap joint-probe baseline, P11-FE4029 full CSAE on prefill+final, P11-FE4030 D-bucket cluster overlap). The joint-probe baseline (P11-FE4028, 30 min CPU) is the gating experiment: if joint AUROC ≤ 0.7731, the rest of the CSAE arc is not worth pursuing on MATH-500.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE4028, P11-FE4029, P11-FE4030, H-4028, H-4029, H-4030.

**Status:** TO TEST
