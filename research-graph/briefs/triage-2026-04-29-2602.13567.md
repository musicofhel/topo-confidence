# Triage brief — 2602.13567 — DistillLens: Symmetric Knowledge Distillation Through Logit Lens

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13567
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "L19 is uniquely privileged" framing.** F-2 says prefill L19
   DoM hits AUROC 0.7731, materially better than other layers. DistillLens's
   ablation (Table 4) finds that adding a *single* logit-lens layer at the
   midpoint already captures +3.06 R-L of the +3.56 R-L total gain achievable
   with 5 interleaved layers — i.e. 86% of the multi-layer benefit is captured
   by one mid-depth probe, but the remaining ~14% comes from layers *spread
   across all depths*. If trajectory-spread information is non-trivially
   complementary in distillation, the same may hold for correctness probing,
   and our L19-only AUROC may be a local optimum rather than a true depth
   privilege. **Mechanism**: layer-wise distributions carry distinct
   uncertainty signal that aggregates super-additively under symmetric
   divergence — same aggregation should work for probes.
   **Falsifiable test**: compute multi-layer JSD-of-logit-lens features for
   correctness prediction on cached Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill activations across
   all 28 layers; if AUROC > 0.7731 by ≥ 0.01 (i.e. matches DistillLens's 0.5
   R-L scaling), F-2's "single-layer dominance" framing weakens to
   "single-layer is a useful summary".

2. **Refutes F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046) at the distribution
   level.** F-3 asserts geometric orthogonality between prefill and final-token
   DoM directions. DistillLens (Algorithm 1, §3.2) projects hidden states from
   *both* early and final layers to the same vocabulary simplex via logit lens
   and minimizes pairwise JSD across all of them — and reports monotonic
   improvement (Table 1: +3.92 R-L on GPT-2-120M). If projected prefill and
   final distributions were genuinely orthogonal in any meaningful sense, JSD
   between paired layers would be ~ log 2 (saturated), and the gradient signal
   from intermediate layers would not consistently transfer. The paper sees
   smooth improvement with depth coverage, suggesting prefill and final
   distributions in vocab space are aligned even when their per-class DoM
   directions are not. **Mechanism**: the DoM cos=0.046 may capture only the
   "correct-vs-incorrect" decision axis in residual space, not the broader
   distributional structure that logit-lens projects. **Falsifiable test**:
   on our cached Qwen2.5-1.5B activations, compute logit-lens projections at
   prefill (L19) and final-token (L19), then compute mean per-problem JSD; if
   < 0.3 (vs ceiling log 2 ≈ 0.693), F-3's orthogonality is a probe-direction
   artifact, not a distributional fact.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) under
   correctness-prediction-via-distillation.** F-9 says CoE-60's 28-layer
   trajectory features are redundant with single-layer DoM. DistillLens's
   Table 5 shows that *interleaved* layer selection (avg R-L 25.8) beats
   first-5 (24.9) and last-5 (25.2) — i.e. even within fixed-budget layer
   selection, *which* layers matter. This contradicts F-9's strict-redundancy
   claim: if all-layer trajectory info collapsed to single-layer info, layer
   selection pattern would be irrelevant. **Mechanism**: interleaved depth
   spans the "evolving thought process" (the paper's framing); first-K and
   last-K each miss complementary information from the other half.
   **Falsifiable test**: rerun CoE-60 vs single-L19 DoM AUROC on Qwen2.5-1.5B
   MATH-500, but with three CoE variants — first-14-layers, last-14-layers,
   interleaved-14. If interleaved beats single-layer DoM and beats both
   contiguous variants by > 0.005 AUROC, F-9 needs to relax to "single-layer
   captures most variance, but layer-pattern aggregation adds residual signal".

4. **Refutes the implicit H-11 framing (big-to-small distillation transfers
   probe quality).** H-11 proposes distilling 7B "self-prediction" into 1.5B.
   DistillLens (Table 1, Llama row) successfully distills Llama-7B → TinyLlama-
   1.1B with +1.66 avg R-L over standard KD — but it distills *generation
   quality*, not probe scores. Their objective (JSD on logit-lens projections)
   does not preserve any specific probe direction; it preserves vocab-space
   distribution. This means: the canonical distillation recipe people would
   reach for to "transfer 7B's correctness signal to 1.5B" probably does
   *not* transfer F-2's prefill-L19-DoM AUROC. **Mechanism**: probe directions
   are linear functionals of residual stream coordinates, but the unembedding
   matrix WU is many-to-one — distinct residual configurations produce
   identical projected distributions, so JSD-distillation is invariant to
   the very directions a DoM probe reads. **Falsifiable test**: take the
   public DistillLens-trained TinyLlama-1.1B checkpoint
   (github.com/manishdhakal/DistillLens), extract prefill-L mid-depth
   activations on MATH-500, train a DoM probe, measure AUROC. If AUROC ≤
   standard-KD baseline + 0.01, distillation does NOT inherit probe-quality
   and H-11 needs an objective that explicitly preserves DoM directions
   (not just logit-lens-projected distributions).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Logit lens projection** — `p^(l)(y|x) = softmax(WU · h^(l))` for any
  intermediate layer l, using the model's own (frozen) unembedding matrix.
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU per cached NPZ (just a matmul).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct way to compare F-2 layer ranking
  against vocab-space JSD ranking.
- **Symmetric Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) loss on layer pairs** —
  `L_JSD(p, q) = 0.5 [KL(p‖m) + KL(q‖m)]` where m = 0.5(p + q). Bounded,
  symmetric, dual-sided penalty (paper's §3.1.2 proves linear hallucination
  penalty + bounded missed-recall ceiling).
  *Replication cost*: 1 hr CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful as a label-free divergence-based
  probe alternative to DoM.
- **Uniform interleaved layer mapping** — `l' = round(l × LT/LS)` selects
  proportional-depth layer pairs, peaks at 5 layers in their setup.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for any cross-model probe alignment work.
- **Jeffreys Divergence (JD) variant** — `L_JD = KL(p‖q) + KL(q‖p)`,
  symmetric alternative to JSD. Marginally outperforms JSD on some benchmarks
  (Table 3, GPT-2-340M).
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe as a probe-loss ablation.
- **Adaptive KL (AKL, Wu et al. 2024)** — hybrid that balances FKL/RKL
  dynamically. Used as a strong baseline in the paper.
  *Replication cost*: 1 hr to implement.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: low priority.
- **MiniLLM hybrid integration** — DistillLens layered on top of on-policy
  MiniLLM training, gives the strongest reported result.
  *Replication cost*: requires full distillation training run, ~ days H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — distillation training is out of scope for
  topo-confidence; we use frozen models.
- **Exposure-bias measurement curve** — generation-length-binned exposure
  bias (Figure 3, Appendix B.3); plots accuracy degradation as a function
  of generated sequence length.
  *Replication cost*: 1 hr on cached MATH-500 generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — could relate to length-band PR control
  in H-15.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Evolving thought process across depth"** — frames hidden-state
  trajectories as iterative belief refinement. Direct echo of P10 v2 framing
  (residual-stream state evolves through layers); supports the depth-as-time
  view that breathing (F-1) lives in.
- **"Intermediate layers as uncertainty information conduits"** — frames
  high-entropy mid-layer distributions as load-bearing for final prediction,
  not noise to be discarded. Crosscuts F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature):
  if D-bucket problems suffer from collapsed mid-layer entropy, that would
  be a mechanistic explanation rather than a descriptive geometric tag.
- **Symmetric vs asymmetric divergence as a design axis** — paper explicitly
  decomposes FKL ("mean-seeking, pull-up"), RKL ("mode-seeking, push-down"),
  and symmetric (JSD/JD, dual-sided) in terms of optimization landscape.
  Useful frame for any future probe-loss design where we currently default
  to logistic regression.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Models used**: GPT-2-XL (1.5B) → GPT-2-medium (340M), GPT-2-base (120M);
  Llama-7B → TinyLlama-1.1B. All intra-family.
- **Layer mapping (GPT-2-120M ← GPT-2-1.5B)**: student {2,4,6,8,10} →
  teacher {8,16,24,32,40}.
- **Layer mapping (GPT-2-340M ← GPT-2-1.5B)**: student {4,8,12,16,20} →
  teacher {8,16,24,32,40}.
- **Layer mapping (TinyLlama-1.1B ← Llama-7B)**: student {4,7,11,15,18} →
  teacher {5,10,16,21,26}.
- **Optimal K = 5 layers** (interleaved); 6 layers exceeds 40GB A100 memory.
- **λ = 1.0** for intermediate loss weight (ablated in Appendix B.2).
- **Task loss = Reverse KL (RKL)** — favors instruction-following per Gu et
  al. 2024.
- **Hardware**: 4× A100 (40GB), BF16 mixed precision, max seq length 512.
- **Exposure-bias formula** in Appendix B.3.
- **Code**: https://github.com/manishdhakal/DistillLens

## Replicable intermediates

- **Logit-lens prefill-vs-final JSD on cached Qwen2.5-1.5B P11 NPZs.**
  We have prefill (L19) and final-token (L19) hidden states cached from
  pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute. Apply Qwen's tied embedding matrix
  as WU, project both to vocab simplex, compute per-problem JSD. If JSD is
  small, F-3's orthogonality is a DoM-direction artifact. ~ 1 hr CPU.
- **Multi-layer DoM ensemble vs single-layer L19 DoM.** Train a 5-layer
  interleaved DoM probe on cached Qwen2.5-1.5B activations (e.g. layers
  {4,9,14,19,24}); compare 5-fold OOF AUROC against single-L19 0.7731.
  ~ 30 min CPU.
- **Layer-pattern ablation (first-5 vs last-5 vs interleaved) for DoM.**
  Direct port of paper's Table 5 ablation to correctness probing. ~ 1 hr
  CPU on cached activations.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2602.13567 — DistillLens: Symmetric Knowledge Distillation Through Logit Lens (Dhakal, Jinadu, Budathoki, Sunderraman, Ding, 2026)

**Relevance:** Method-level adjacency. DistillLens uses the same building
block (logit-lens projection of intermediate hidden states) we would use
to convert F-2's per-class DoM directions into full vocab-space
distributions. Its multi-layer-aggregation finding (Table 4: 86% of gain
from single mid-layer, 14% residual from 5-interleaved) and layer-pattern
finding (Table 5: interleaved > first/last at fixed budget) directly
challenge F-2's "L19 is privileged" and F-9's "CoE-60 redundant with L19"
framings. Paper does generation-quality distillation, not correctness
probing, so its methodology transfers but its results don't directly
replicate or contradict ours.

**Key claim we tested (proposed FE69, FE71):** Multi-layer interleaved
aggregation of logit-lens distributions strictly improves over single
mid-depth. In our terms: multi-layer DoM ensemble > single-L19 DoM AUROC.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** Pending FE69-FE72. Current default expectation:
single-layer captures most of the variance (consistent with paper's 86%
single-mid-layer), but interleaved aggregation will add ≥ 0.01 AUROC.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE69 (multi-layer DoM ensemble), P11-FE70
(prefill-vs-final vocab JSD), P11-FE71 (layer-pattern ablation), P11-FE72
(public-checkpoint probe-quality test for H-11), H-72, H-73.

**Status:** TO TEST
