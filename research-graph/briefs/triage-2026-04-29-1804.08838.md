# Triage brief — 1804.08838 — Measuring the Intrinsic Dimension of Objective Landscapes

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a privileged direction) is weakened if random k-dim subspaces of L19 activations match its AUROC.**
   *Mechanism*: The paper's central methodological move is replacing a single carefully-chosen direction with a *random* d-dim subspace and showing that solutions exist there once d crosses a low threshold (e.g. d_int90 = 750 / D = 199,210 ≈ 0.4% for MNIST FC). By analogy, our prefill L19 supervised DoM is a *single* direction in the 1536-dim residual stream — the "direct" method in their terminology. If MATH-500 correctness has low effective intrinsic dimension *as a function of L19 prefill activations*, then a random k-dim projection of L19 (k = 5, 50, 500) followed by logistic regression should approach the supervised DoM's AUROC=0.7731. If random k=50 already reaches AUROC ≥ 0.74, F-2's "this direction is special" framing collapses to "any sufficiently large random sketch of L19 contains the correctness signal."
   *Falsifying experiment*: Project the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` L19 prefill activations into Gaussian random k-dim subspaces for k ∈ {1, 5, 50, 500, 1536}; fit logistic regression with 5-fold OOF; compare AUROC against the supervised DoM baseline (0.7731). 20 min CPU.

2. **F-7 / H-9 (per-problem D-bucket difficulty signature) is refuted if the signature is model-specific rather than dataset-determined.**
   *Mechanism*: The paper's robustness finding is that d_int90 varies by only 1.33× while D varies by 24.1× across MNIST FC architectures — task difficulty is a property of the *task*, not the model size. F-7 claims D-bucket has a "distinctive collective geometric signature" and H-9 claims breathing amplitude predicts difficulty; both are framed as per-input properties of MATH-500 problems. The dataset-determined-difficulty thesis predicts D-bucket / breathing amplitude rankings should be largely *the same* across models that have enough capacity to reach the d_int90 threshold (1.5B and 7B both clear MATH-500). If 7B's per-problem breathing amplitudes do *not* correlate with 1.5B's (Spearman r < 0.3), then "collective geometric signature" is a model-specific artifact, not a property of MATH-500 problems.
   *Falsifying experiment*: Compute per-problem prefill PR (and L19 DoM score) on 1.5B and 7B for the same 500 MATH problems from `pathway11_h100`; rank-correlate. If r < 0.3, F-7 / H-9 are refuted.

3. **H-1 (per-position fixed-vector DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is refuted if steering effects only emerge in a low-dim *manifold* (k > 1), not along a single direction.**
   *Mechanism*: Inverted pendulum d_int90 = 4 — four parameters span the entire policy manifold. By analogy, even if MATH-500 correctness has low intrinsic dimension as a function of activations, the relevant subspace is unlikely to be exactly 1-D. EXP-028 already showed E1 fixed-vector steering fails because the direction rotates during decoding (Pathway 10 v1). The paper's framework predicts: there's some k > 1 such that random k-dim subspace steering succeeds while k=1 single-direction steering fails. Specifically, if k ≈ 5–50 random-subspace steering produces non-trivial MATH-500 K=1 deltas but per-position single-direction steering does not, H-1's "per-position bank of single directions" mechanism is wrong — the steering manifold is multidimensional.
   *Falsifying experiment*: At L19, sample a random k-dim subspace (k ∈ {1, 10, 50}); sweep steering magnitude; generate K=1 MATH-500 with steered activations on Qwen-2.5-1.5B; measure accuracy delta. If k=10 lifts accuracy by ≥ 1pp but k=1 does not (and the k=1 result already exists from H-1 attempts), H-1's single-direction framing fails.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across architectures and scales)** — Paper's d_int90 robustness across MNIST FC depths/widths (1.33× variation over 24.1× D variation) is a *parameter-space* analog of F-1's *representation-space* universality. Both say "scaling D adds redundancy, not difficulty." But d_int90 is measured per-task on training, while breathing PR is measured per-input at inference; the analogy is loose, not load-bearing. Anchor: prefill L19 PR universality across 1.5B (D≈1.5×10⁹) and 7B (D≈7×10⁹).
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** — Paper provides the natural null: random k-dim subspaces of L19 activations should approach this AUROC as k grows. No prior result rules this out.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Paper's framing "extra parameters add to s, not to difficulty" is the parameter-space cousin of F-9's "extra layers add to redundancy, not to predictive power." Same intuition, different domain.
- **F-10 (PH on trained streams = Gaussian null)** — Paper repeatedly flags that high-dim landscapes look simpler than low-dim intuition predicts; supports the prior that geometric structure on residual streams is weaker than expected. Confirming, not refuting.
- **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS)** — Paper measures dimensions of *objective landscapes*, the same object the EoS literature studies. Direct conceptual link: d_int90 is a static landscape measure; EoS sharpness is a dynamic measure of the same landscape. H-4 should cite this paper as the most-cited prior work on landscape dimension measurement.
- **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts difficulty)** — Paper makes the dataset-level analog: d_int90 measures task difficulty. H-9 needs to claim per-problem variation, which the paper does *not* support — it gives a single number per dataset.
- **H-16 (Marchenko-Pastur bias correction preserves breathing magnitude)** — The paper's whole methodology depends on PR/dimensionality estimates that are noisy at small d; H-16's MP correction (Chun et al. 2509.26560) directly addresses what this paper handles via large-scale empirics + bootstrap.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Random subspace training** — θ^(D) = θ_0^(D) + P · θ^(d) with P a frozen D×d random projection matrix (columns unit-normalized), θ^(d) initialized to zero, trained via SGD/Adam in ℝ^d. The intrinsic dimension d_int90 is the smallest d achieving 90% of direct-baseline performance.
  *Replication cost*: not directly applicable — we don't fine-tune. But the projection trick is portable to *probing*: project cached activations into random k-dim subspaces, fit a logistic regression there. 20 min CPU on existing P11 cache.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly enables Refutation #1's experiment.

- **Sparse random projection (Li, Hastie, Church 2006)** — sparse Bernoulli ±1 entries with sparsity ~1/√D for fast P · θ^(d) with sub-quadratic memory. Used by the paper for CIFAR-10 scale.
  *Replication cost*: small CPU; library available (`scipy.sparse`).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if Refutation #1 scales — random projections of L19 activations across all 500 MATH-500 problems × 5 folds = ~7,500 projections, manageable with sparse P.

- **Fastfood transform (Le, Sarlós, Smola 2013)** — O(D log D) dense-equivalent random projection via Hadamard + diagonal Gaussian + permutation + diagonal Rademacher. Used for ImageNet-scale D.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU + library lookup; not standard scipy.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — our D ≤ 1536 (residual stream) is small enough that dense projections suffice.

- **d_int90 sweep protocol** — geometric sweep of d, oversample around threshold, fit threshold-crossing dim with bootstrap CI.
  *Replication cost*: minimal once Refutation #1 framework exists.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — for our probing analog, sweep k = 1, 5, 50, 500 and find the AUROC-90%-of-supervised threshold.

- **Memorization probe via shuffled labels** — d_int90 on shuffled-label MNIST jumps from 750 → 190,000 (3.8 floats per memorized example). Directly distinguishes generalization from memorization.
  *Replication cost*: would require building a per-example label-shuffle of MATH-500, fitting an in-context probe; mismatched to our pipeline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we already have F-9 and H-12 covering "is the signal real or memorized?"

- **Inductive-bias probe via shuffled inputs** — d_int90 on pixel-shuffled MNIST: FC unchanged at 750 (permutation-invariant), LeNet jumps 290 → 1400. Reveals when an architecture's inductive bias is irrelevant to the data.
  *Replication cost*: not applicable — Transformer position embeddings make this an apples-to-oranges experiment for LLMs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

## Approaches & framings

- **MDL-as-d_int90 framing** — d_int90 gives a constructive, computable upper bound on the minimum description length of a *solution*, not the dataset. New conceptual move: solutions can be cheaper than networks. Intersects F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM) — both say "the cheap representation suffices." Intersects H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM) — both say "the supervised direction is one realization of a low-d signal."

- **Solution-set codimension framing**: D = d_int + s, where s is the dim of the solution manifold and d_int is its codim. This decomposes "model capacity" into "capacity needed (d_int)" and "redundancy (s)." Reusable lens: in our probing setup, define d_probe = smallest k at which random-subspace probes hit our supervised AUROC; the "redundancy" of L19 is then 1536 − d_probe.

- **Cross-domain difficulty comparison via d_int90** — Inverted pendulum (d_int=4) ≪ MNIST FC (750) ≪ CIFAR conv (2,900) ≪ ImageNet (>500k). Suggests our breathing/PR signals could in principle be calibrated against d_int by training a small probe and asking: at what k does the probe reach a target AUROC for MATH-500 vs simpler tasks (e.g., GSM8K, HumanEval)? Intersects H-2 (breathing on short CoT) and H-3 (breathing on code generation).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Initialize θ^(d) to zero so that θ^(D) starts at θ_0^(D); preserves benefit of standard init (Glorot, He).
- Columns of P normalized to unit length; use approximate orthogonality of high-dim Gaussian vectors instead of explicit Gram-Schmidt.
- For dense P at modest D: directly store as a single matrix (fits in memory at D ≤ ~10⁶).
- For larger D: sparse Achlioptas-style ±1/0 entries (Li, Hastie, Church 2006) with sparsity √D — preserves Johnson-Lindenstrauss with much lower compute.
- For very large D (ImageNet-scale): Fastfood transform — O(D log D) memory and time.
- Threshold heuristic: 90% of direct baseline accuracy; oversample d near the crossing point; use bootstrap for CI on d_int90.
- Optimizer choice matters: SGD/SGD+momentum are rotation-invariant (rotation of P leaves trajectory unchanged); Adam/RMSProp are not — landscape "shape" depends on basis. Implication for our random-projection probe: use plain logistic regression (rotation-invariant), not Adam-trained nonlinear probes, when comparing across random subspaces.

Code link: official Uber implementation referenced in paper, not URL'd here (need to check `github.com/uber-research/intrinsic-dimension`).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 1804.08838 — Measuring the Intrinsic Dimension of Objective Landscapes (Li, Farkhoor, Liu, Yosinski, ICLR 2018)

**Relevance:** Foundational work on measuring task / landscape difficulty via the dimensionality of the *solution manifold* in parameter space. Their method (random subspace training: θ = θ_0 + P θ^(d), measure d_int90 = smallest d achieving 90% of baseline accuracy) is the canonical reference for "few degrees of freedom suffice." For our project, the methodology — *not the data* — ports directly to the probing problem: replace supervised L19 DoM with random k-dim subspace probes of L19 activations, find d_probe, and compare to the 1536-dim full-rank supervised baseline.

**Key claim we tested:** Their headline robustness finding (d_int90 varies by 1.33× across MNIST FC architectures while D varies by 24.1×) predicts that for a given task, difficulty is largely model-independent. By analogy, MATH-500 per-problem difficulty signals (F-7 D-bucket, H-9 breathing amplitude) should agree across 1.5B and 7B if they are dataset-determined.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — neither H-123 (random-subspace probing) nor H-124 (cross-model rank correlation) has been run yet. The paper's d_int sweep methodology is directly portable to our cached residual-stream activations and gives a principled MDL-style upper bound on the L19 correctness signal.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72 (random subspace probing), P11-FE73 (cross-model rank correlation), P10-FE56 (low-d steering), H-123, H-124, H-125. Conceptually adjacent: H-4 (sharpness dim / EoS), H-16 (MP correction), F-9 (CoE redundancy).

**Status:** TO TEST (HIGH priority for H-123 and H-124; MEDIUM for H-125).
