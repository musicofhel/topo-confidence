# Triage brief — 2603.14923 — Directional Routing in Transformers

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14923
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Author: Kevin Taylor (single-author, kevin.taylor1924@gmail.com)
Submitted: 16 Mar 2026 (cs.LG, preprint under review)
Scale tested: 433M routed vs 417M baseline (12L × 12H × d_model=1536 × d_head=128) + 26M ablation
Headline gist: K=4 unit-norm direction vectors per head + a 4-layer mean-pooled MLP router that produces sigmoid weights `r_{h,k}` ∈ [0,1]; output is `o'_h = o_h − Σ_k r_{h,k}(o_h·d_{h,k}) d_{h,k}` (additive direction-pruning at the head output, not a residual-stream gate).

## Refutations

1. **Within-head learned directions are NOT orthogonal — vs F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046).**
   The paper measures pairwise angles between the 4 learned directions inside each head and finds an average of **75.9° (vs 89.4° for random vectors in 128D)**, with 83% of pairs within 20° of orthogonal — i.e. learned directions in trained transformers come out *near-orthogonal but consistently tighter than random*. Our F-3 reports cos=0.046 (≈87.4°) and frames it as evidence of two independent computational axes. **Mechanism**: if learned directions in trained transformers systematically pack at 75-87° rather than 90°, our cos=0.046 may simply be inside that natural superposition tolerance, not evidence of independent circuits. **Falsifiable**: extract per-head DoM vectors at L19 from cached prefill+final activations, compute the empirical angle distribution between any two random direction pairs in head-space; if the median is ≤87°, F-3's "orthogonal" framing collapses into "within-superposition-tolerance."

2. **Coordination, not heads, carries the load — vs H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits).**
   Their Table 3: knocking out the three identified induction heads (L6H1, L6H7, L3H4) keeps **92.5%** of induction accuracy (98.6% of normal); turning routing OFF entirely → **0.0%**. For factual recall, knocking out the primary mover head (L10H11) actually *increases* P(Paris) to 1.22× normal, while routing-off averages 0.0000× normal. **Mechanism**: in a model where coordination dominates, head-level attribution finds nothing meaningful — the signal lives in the modulator, not the heads being modulated. **Falsifiable for H-13**: run head ablation (zero W_O column-blocks for individual heads at L19) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B during prefill; if AUROC for correctness changes by ≤2 pp under any single-head removal but drops sharply under simultaneous multi-head removal, H-13's per-head circuit hypothesis collapses into coordination.

3. **Strong perplexity signal that fails to transfer to MC accuracy — vs F-8 (selective-prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%).**
   Routing reduces perplexity 31-56% across code/math/prose/factual (Table 5) but the routed model **loses 6 of 7 multiple-choice benchmarks** with average ΔAccuracy = −1.2% (Appendix A, Table 6). Discussion §8 explains: "Routing is a better *decoder* of the same underlying knowledge, not a source of new knowledge. Perplexity rewards confidence on tokens the model already partially knows; multiple-choice tests whether the model knows the answer at all." **Mechanism for F-8**: our DoM AUROC=0.7731 is also a "confidence on partially-known answers" signal; selective prediction at 71.6% may be capped by the same ceiling — held-out generation tasks where the model doesn't already partially know would show no lift. **Falsifiable**: extend the prefill-DoM selective-prediction protocol to a benchmark Qwen-2.5-1.5B has weaker prior knowledge of (e.g. fresh AIME-2024 problems or held-out MATH-500 with paraphrased questions); if coverage-50 accuracy drops to baseline K=2.5 of ~48.6%, F-8 generalizes only on familiar distribution.

4. **CKA between routed and baseline = 0.95-0.99 at every layer — vs F-1/F-2 (L19 contains a distinct correctness signal).**
   The paper claims routing "does not change what the model represents, only how those representations are processed" (§7 ablations + §8 discussion), backed by CKA staying 0.95-0.99 at *every* layer despite a 31-56% PPL gain. **Mechanism**: if our L19 prefill DoM is, like the routing mechanism, a *readout* of pre-existing knowledge rather than a distinct feature axis, then suppressing along DoM during generation should not change L19 representations (high CKA) yet still hurt accuracy. **Falsifiable**: run paired Qwen-2.5-1.5B inference on MATH-500 with vs without per-step L19 DoM-suppression steering; compute layer-wise CKA between activations. CKA ≥ 0.95 + accuracy drop ≥ 5pp would refute the "DoM is a distinct feature" framing of F-2.

5. **Most critical layer is the LEAST cross-domain-varying one — vs F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective signature).**
   Layer 9 has routing variance = 0.00055 (127× lower than L0=0.070) yet disabling its routing causes the worst single-layer perplexity hit: **+42.6 PPL** (Table 4) — more than double L8 (+15.3). L9 directions target syntactic features (punctuation, articles, conjunctions, discourse markers); L0 directions are domain-adaptive. **Mechanism for F-7**: we have implicitly searched for D-bucket signatures at the layer with the *most* variance (L19 in 28L Qwen). If criticality and variance are anti-correlated as the paper finds, the D-bucket signature should be measurable at low-variance layers (likely Qwen L23-L26, near the LM head). **Falsifiable**: re-run D-bucket vs A-bucket discrimination AUROC at each of Qwen-2.5-1.5B's 28 layers; if AUROC peaks at a layer with *lowest* cross-bucket activation variance, F-7's "distinctive collective signature" needs a different geometric reading.

6. **Mean-pooled (sequence-level) routing beats per-token routing — vs H-1 (per-position DoM steering).**
   At the 26M ablation scale, per-token routing produced PPL 9.46 vs **7.95 for mean-pooled** (+19% worse with finer granularity). The paper attributes this to optimization difficulty at small scale, but the result holds. **Mechanism for H-1**: the per-position DoM steering bank we plan to test could underperform a single sequence-pooled DoM injection at our 1.5B scale, especially without per-position labels. **Falsifiable**: H-1's experiment design should run a paired control with mean-pooled DoM (one steering vector per sequence) alongside the per-position bank.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Vocabulary projection of attention-output directions** — embed direction `d_{h,k} ∈ R^{d_h}` in full d_model (zero-pad outside the head's slice), project through `W_out`, then dot with `W_LM^T` to get vocab-sized logits; top-10 tokens label the direction. Causally validated: overriding routing weight for the 25 article-encoding directions shifts P(article-as-next-token) by −6.2% on n=50 held-out sequences.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (we have model + W_LM cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — we would project our L19 prefill DoM through Qwen's W_LM to see what tokens it encodes. Cheap, and gives a content/syntactic label for the direction we've been using as a black-box AUROC vector.

- **Cross-domain routing-weight variance as specialization metric** — for each (head, direction), measure variance of `r_{h,k}` across math/code/prose/factual inputs; high variance = domain-adaptive, low = fixed pruning.
  *Replication cost*: not directly applicable (we don't have a learned routing module). But the analog — variance of L19 DoM-projection across MATH-500 problem domains — is ~30 min CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes**, as a check on H-5 (prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability).

- **Within-head pairwise direction-angle measurement** — among K=4 learned directions per head, compute pairwise angles; compare to angles of K random vectors in d_h dimensions. Detects superposition.
  *Replication cost*: requires per-head DoM extraction (we currently have per-layer pooled). ~3h CPU + cache reorganization.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly tests F-3's orthogonality framing.

- **Marginal logit attribution by direct subtraction** — for each component class (token embed, raw heads, routing delta, MLPs), set component to zero, recompute, take ΔLogit. Non-additive due to LayerNorm but interpretable as relative magnitude.
  *Replication cost*: cheap on a single example, ~10min CPU. Already what we do for F-2 OOF eval if reframed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — apply to MATH-500 final-token logit decomposition to see where the +/− answer-token contribution lives.

- **Routing-OFF / routing-NEUTRAL inference-time intervention** — set `w=0` (suppress everything) or `w=0.5` (uniform half-suppression) at inference; observe perplexity / circuit collapse. Distinct from training-from-scratch ablation.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once a steering hook is in place — standard P10 v2 steering program territory.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — the analog for us is "DoM-suppress at L19 during generation" (essentially H-1's negative control).

- **Cross-domain routing-weight swap** — replace router weights for code-domain inputs with prose-domain weights at a chosen layer; measure ΔPPL. Tests whether layer-level routing is domain-fungible.
  *Replication cost*: not directly applicable; analog would be "use 7B's prefill DoM for 1.5B inference" which we partially do already.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — we have no routing module to swap; the analog is already implicit in P11 H100 cross-model experiment.

- **PCA + nearest-centroid domain classification of routing vectors** — at a chosen layer, run PCA on routing weights across input domains; check if 4-domain separation is clean.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min CPU on cached L19 activations projected through DoM.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes**, as a cheap check on H-5.

## Approaches & framings

- **Routing as denoising / decoding rather than feature creation.** Discussion §8: "Routing is a better decoder of the same underlying knowledge, not a source of new knowledge." Reframes directional intervention as removing cross-domain interference rather than computing new features. **Intersects F-1/F-2/F-8**: our DoM may be a decoder of pre-existing geometric structure rather than a distinct correctness feature. This is actually a stronger framing for the H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probe) program — if signal is decoder-side, label-free probes that read the same underlying structure should match supervised AUROC.

- **Two-regime architecture: domain-adaptive early layers + syntactic-pruning late layers, emerging without architectural pressure.** Without auxiliary loss, the model self-organizes into specialist layers. **Intersects F-1/F-7**: our pathway-level analysis has not asked whether breathing has *layer-specialized* roles (early-vs-late). Suggests testing whether early-layer breathing is content-adaptive while late-layer breathing is task-invariant.

- **Within-head superposition tolerance ≠ true orthogonality** (75.9° vs 89.4° random). Reframes "are these directions independent" as a question about statistical separation under the model's effective rank. **Intersects F-3**: our cos=0.046 is decision-grade only relative to a baseline angle distribution we have not measured.

- **Coordinator vs components: when explicit coordination exists, head-level attribution is empty.** **Intersects H-13**: our planned head-level circuit decomposition assumes the standard transformer has implicit (residual-stream) coordination that head-level KO can still uncover; this paper suggests it may not.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 433M param model: 12 layers, 12 heads, d_model=1536, d_head=128, K=4 directions per head → **576 total directions**, 73,728 direction params.
- Router: 4-layer MLP shared across all heads in a layer, takes mean-pooled sequence representation, produces `r ∈ [0,1]^{H×K}`. Sigmoid temperature **T=5.0** to push toward binary decisions. **One** routing decision per sequence (mean-pool bottleneck).
- 12 router MLPs total = 16.1M params (3.7%); +74K direction = 16.2M params total (3.9% overhead, 0.02% FLOPs).
- Training: AdamW + cosine LR, **20K steps**, **2.5B tokens**, GPT-2 tokenizer. **No auxiliary routing loss, no load-balancing, no special init.** Single seed (no variance reported — flagged as a paper limitation).
- Wall-clock penalty: 3,651 → 3,210 tok/s at seq_len=1024 (13.7% slower) due to mean-pool requiring full-sequence forward pass before routing weights are known.
- 6K-step convergence speedup: routed model hits baseline final PPL 1.3× faster, then continues improving — possible inductive-bias gain.
- Pythia-410M cited as directional reference at 120× more training data — we are not in their efficiency-claim regime.
- No code release announced in the paper text we read; single-author preprint.

## 2603.14923 — Directional Routing in Transformers (Taylor, 2026)

**Relevance:** Single-author preprint introducing K=4 unit-norm direction vectors per attention head plus a 4-layer mean-pooled MLP router that produces sigmoid suppression weights, at 3.9% parameter cost on a 433M model. The paper's central interpretive claim — "routing is a decoder of pre-existing knowledge, not a feature" (CKA 0.95-0.99 with baseline at every layer despite 31-56% PPL drop) — is a direct stress-test of our F-2 framing of the L19 prefill DoM as a distinct correctness feature. Three other findings carry refutational weight: (i) within-head learned-direction angles average 75.9° (not 90°), placing our F-3 cos=0.046 inside a natural superposition tolerance, (ii) routing's strong perplexity gain fails to transfer to multiple-choice accuracy, predicting that F-8's selective-prediction may plateau on unfamiliar distributions, and (iii) the most critical layer is the *lowest-variance* one (L9, +42.6 PPL when knocked out, variance 127× below L0), inverting our implicit search direction for F-7. The architectural mechanism itself — sequence-pooled directional suppression — is also a P10-style steering analog we have not yet built.

**Key claim we tested:** "Directional intervention at attention output dominates raw head outputs in determining which token wins, and the directional structure is interpretable as a decoder of pre-existing knowledge rather than a new feature axis."

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** The decoder-vs-feature claim is directly testable on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B P11 activations (P10-FE91 / H-91).
