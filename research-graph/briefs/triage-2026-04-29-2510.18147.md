# Triage brief — 2510.18147 — LLMs Encode How Difficult Problems Are

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18147
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos=0.046) by alternative interpretation.**
   Mechanism: Lugoloobi & Russell train probes at *post-instruction* token positions ranging from -16 to -1, and report that for AMC math the "best" probe layer/position varies widely (e.g. layer 38 / pos -1 for DeepSeek-Llama-70B; layer 16 / pos -1 for Llama-3.1-8B). For mathematical datasets the optimal token positions distribute across the last 16 prompt tokens rather than concentrating at the final query token. If a single linearly-decoded *difficulty* axis is recoverable across this entire post-instruction span, then our "prefill ⊥ final-token" claim could be a labeling artifact (binary correctness vs continuous difficulty) rather than a true geometric fact about the residual stream — i.e. one direction explains both, decomposed differently by the two label schemes.
   Falsifiable test: Re-extract the AMC linear-probe direction from cached Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B prefill NPZs at L19 (and at the layer this paper finds optimal for the 1.5B-Math variant), then compute cos similarity against (a) our prefill DoM and (b) our final-token DoM. If their probe direction has high cos with *both* of our directions, F-3's orthogonality claim is rescued by labeling difference; if it has high cos with only one, F-3 holds. Either outcome falsifies a candidate interpretation.

2. **Refutes the polarity assumption in H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy via "correct" direction).**
   Mechanism: Their Figure 4 shows that steering Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with α=−3 along the *difficulty* direction (toward "easier") raises Pass@1 across all difficulty bins, while α=+3 (toward "harder") collapses it via repetition / max-length hallucination. If the *effective* steerable axis is "treat-as-easy ↔ treat-as-hard" rather than "answer-correctly ↔ answer-incorrectly," our supervised correctness DoM may have been steering on a less-effective derivative axis. Critically, ρ=0.88 difficulty decoding from prefill is a stronger linear signal than our 0.7731 correctness AUROC at L19 — so the "right" direction for intervention may not be the one we extracted.
   Falsifiable test: Head-to-head steering on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B at MATH-500. Compare (a) our prefill correctness DoM at α∈{-3,-2,-1,0,+1,+2,+3} vs (b) their AMC difficulty-probe direction at the same α grid, holding everything else fixed. If their direction beats ours by > 2 pp on K=1 accuracy, H-1 needs reframing as "treat-as-easy steering" not "DoM steering."

3. **Refutes F-2's "L19 is special" framing for Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B.**
   Mechanism: Table 4 reports the Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B AMC probe at ρ=0.824 — they don't publish the specific (layer, position) tuple for this model, but their general claim is that the *best* layer is mid-stack and varies across model families. Our F-2 narrative privileges L19 specifically; if their best AMC probe on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B falls at a different layer (e.g. L14 or L23) and that layer also outperforms L19 on our binary-correctness task, the L19 claim becomes a local optimum rather than a privileged residual-stream depth.
   Falsifiable test: Sweep our prefill correctness probe AUROC across all 28 layers of Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on cached MATH-500 NPZs. If any layer ≠ L19 beats L19 by > 1.5 pp AUROC, F-2's "L19 specifically" wording requires a downgrade to "L19 is one of several effective layers."

4. **Refutes F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics).**
   Mechanism: They find that human-perceived difficulty is linearly decodable from *post-instruction* activations *before generation begins* — i.e. the model's per-problem "difficulty embedding" is fixed at prefill time and is not produced by the autoregressive computation. If a substantial fraction of our breathing-amplitude variance across problems is explained by this static prefill scalar, then "content-dependence" is being driven primarily by problem-difficulty signal trickling through layers, not by content-specific computational expansion. This collapses F-5 toward an AR-mechanics + difficulty-encoding mixture.
   Falsifiable test: Per-problem regress our breathing magnitude (peak − floor PR across the temporal curve) against their AMC difficulty probe score. If R² > 0.5, F-5's "content-dependent computation" claim weakens — breathing amplitude is mostly a difficulty thermometer. If R² < 0.2, F-5 is robust.

5. **Refutes the "DoM is the relevant correctness signal" framing of F-2 + F-8 jointly.**
   Mechanism: They demonstrate via GRPO checkpoint tracking (Figure 6, 7) that the *human-difficulty* probe strengthens as Pass@1 improves (β=+6.66, p<0.001), while the *LLM-derived* difficulty probe degrades (β=−0.63, p=0.022). Our DoM is a binary correctness probe — i.e. it sits in the *LLM-derived* family, not the human-grounded family. If GRPO systematically erodes LLM-derived correctness signals while strengthening human-grounded difficulty signals, then F-8's selective-prediction-via-DoM result (71.6% at coverage 0.5) may be brittle to any future RL post-training of the base model — and the right deployable signal is human-difficulty steering, not correctness DoM.
   Falsifiable test: If we ever fine-tune Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with GRPO on MATH-train (Dr.GRPO recipe — they show 64.7 → 76.2 Pass@1 in 43 steps on a single A100), re-evaluate F-8's selective prediction at every checkpoint. If 71.6% degrades by > 5 pp by peak-Pass@1 step, F-8 is RL-fragile and the human-difficulty probe is the durable correctness signal.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731).** They report Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B AMC probe ρ=0.824 from post-instruction prefill activations — same model, same regime. Their Spearman ρ on a continuous IRT difficulty target is not directly comparable to our binary correctness AUROC, but it sits on the *same residual-stream signal class*. Strong corroboration of "prefill encodes problem-level structure on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B."
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046).** See Refutation #1 — their probe positions span -16 to -1 *post-instruction*, complicating our "prefill vs final" cleavage.
- **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective geometric signature).** Their finding that Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with α=−3 difficulty steering improves accuracy *across all difficulty bins* (not just hard) suggests a uniform "treat-as-easier" intervention. D-bucket (peak PR 67/88 from headlines) might be the natural target — these are the problems where the prefill difficulty probe says "hard" but the underlying answer is reachable. Connection: D-bucket may be exactly the population that benefits most from α=−3 steering.
- **F-8 (selective prediction 71.6% acc at coverage 0.5).** See Refutation #5. Their GRPO tracking (Figures 6, 7) gives β=+6.66 for human-difficulty / Pass@1 vs β=−0.63 for LLM-difficulty / Pass@1 — directly relevant to whether F-8 survives RL post-training.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** They actually run this experiment on the same model with a difficulty direction. α=−3 yields > 0 accuracy gain across all bins. This is the closest existing replication of H-1 in the literature. See Refutation #2 for the reframing.
- **H-5 (prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability).** Their ρ=0.88 IRT-difficulty result is the cleanest evidence yet that prefill encodes a continuous "difficulty" scalar — a plausible operationalization of "familiarity vs decomposability." H-5 should be reformulated as "prefill encodes IRT-style difficulty (≈ human-perceived familiarity)" with their probe direction as the candidate readout.
- **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts problem difficulty).** Their cross-model power-law (1 − ρ = C·N^(−α), α=0.045 on AMC, α=0.066 on Codeforces) gives a quantitative prior for what "difficulty encoding strength" should look like — useful for calibrating H-9.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute).** They find α=−3 steering (zero extra compute) yields gains comparable to per-step refinement — suggesting "treat-as-easy" routing might compete with C_exact. Adjacent but not overlapping.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Linear probe over post-instruction tokens at every layer × position grid** — extract activations at all post-instruction token positions (last K tokens of prompt before generation), every layer; train a linear regression probe at each (layer, pos); report best. *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU on cached Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B prefill NPZs (we have these). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives layer-position heatmap of correctness signal, generalizes our F-2's single (L19, prefill) anchor.
- **Power-law model-size scaling fit** — fit `1 - ρ_CV = C · N^(−α)` in log space on best-probe-per-model, regressing log(1 − perf) on log(N), report α and R²_log. *Replication cost*: 4h CPU once we have probes from a 5+ model panel. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if we expand beyond the Qwen-2.5 family.
- **Steering via fitted probe coefficients with α grid [−3, +3]** — use the linear probe weight vector as the steering direction (no contrastive pair needed); apply at the probed (layer, pos) during generation; sweep α. *Replication cost*: 2h H100 per model on MATH-500 K=1. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of H-1, and head-to-head against our supervised correctness DoM (Refutation #2).
- **GRPO-checkpoint probe tracking** — train a probe at every saved GRPO checkpoint, plot probe-strength vs training-step alongside Pass@1, then residualize on training step and regress probe vs Pass@1. *Replication cost*: 1d A100 (Dr.GRPO recipe: 64.7 → 76.2 Pass@1 in 43 steps with batch 256, lr 1e-5, single A100 / 80GB) + ~2h CPU per checkpoint for probe training. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly addresses Refutation #5 (RL-fragility of F-8).
- **IRT (Item Response Theory) difficulty calibration** — uses Easy2HardBench's IRT-fit difficulty scores from competitive-leaderboard human attempts as ground truth. *Replication cost*: zero — labels already published. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — bolt onto MATH-500 by mapping problems to AMC IRT scores where overlap exists.
- **5-fold CV with Spearman rank correlation** — different from our usual AUROC/binary regime, suited to continuous IRT targets. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, when extending to continuous-target probes.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Difficulty as a linear direction" reframes correctness probing.** Our project frames correctness as binary; their reframing is that difficulty (a continuous, problem-level scalar) is the *upstream* representation, and per-problem correctness is downstream. This is a non-trivial inversion — our DoM may be a binary thresholding of their continuous direction.
- **"Human-grounded vs model-grounded" labels are not interchangeable.** Their AMC (human IRT) and GSM8K (LLM-derived) probes diverge by ρ ≈ 0.3, and during GRPO they evolve in opposite directions. This argues that any future "self-supervised" correctness signal we build (e.g. semantic-entropy probes from H-12) should be benchmarked against a human-grounded reference, not assumed to be substitutable.
- **GRPO as a probe-selectivity microscope.** Tracking probe strength across RL checkpoints reveals which signals RL preserves vs erodes. This is a generic recipe that applies to any of our probes (DoM, CoE, breathing magnitude, asymmetric collapse) — every claim we make about a base-model signal should arguably carry an "RL-fragility" footnote.
- **Steering polarity as a falsifiability tool.** They find α=−3 helps and α=+3 hurts asymmetrically; this asymmetry is itself diagnostic (rules out trivial "any perturbation helps" interpretations). We should adopt α-grid asymmetry as a sanity check for any future H-1 steering experiments.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Easy2HardBench / E2H-AMC** — 500 sampled questions from training set; IRT difficulty from AMC competition human-attempt logs; HF-accessible (Ding et al. 2024, arxiv 2409.18433). Applicable? **yes** — we can map MATH-500 ↔ AMC problems where they overlap and inherit IRT scores; or train a probe on E2H-AMC and zero-shot evaluate on MATH-500.
- **E2H-GSM8K** — 500 questions; LLM-derived difficulty from Open LLM Leaderboard accuracy. HF-accessible. Applicable? **yes, as a contrast** — useful as the "noisy automated label" arm for replicating their human vs LLM divergence on our 1.5B-Math.
- **E2H-Codeforces** — coding analog with human leaderboard difficulty. HF-accessible. Applicable? **no** for our current MATH-only pipeline; **yes** if H-3 (code-generation breathing) is ever activated.
- **MATH-500** — already in our pipeline; they use it as their downstream Pass@1 benchmark.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Model's chat template applied before activation extraction** — they explicitly note "format the input with the model's chat template and collect activations from every token after the final prompt token." If our prefill NPZs were extracted *without* the chat template, we'd be probing a different distribution.
- **Probe direction = linear regression coefficient vector** — no contrastive pair, no class-mean difference. Just the fitted ridge/OLS weights, reused as a residual-stream direction at generation time.
- **Steering applied at the probed (layer, position)** — same layer and same token offset where the probe was fit. We currently apply our DoM only at L19 prefill — they apply at the optimal position per model (e.g. -1, -2, -4 from prompt end).
- **Dr.GRPO recipe** — Verl library, single A100-80GB, batch 256, 64 grad-accum steps, lr 1e-5 (raised from default 1e-6), MATH train split filtered to difficulty ≥ 3 (8,890 questions), checkpoint every step, peak Pass@1 76.2 at step 43/67. Cleaner / cheaper than the published Dr.GRPO baseline (Liu 2025).
- **Math-Verify** — they verify MATH-500 answers with the HuggingFace `math-verify` library (greedy decoding, max_new_tokens=3000). We currently use a custom verifier in P11; worth comparing.
- **Code release**: paper says "We release probe code and evaluation scripts to facilitate replication" — no URL in the body or references; check OpenReview / first-author Github once published.

## 2510.18147 — LLMs Encode How Difficult Problems Are (Lugoloobi & Russell, 2025)

**Relevance:** Most directly relevant external paper to F-2 / H-1 found to date. Same model family (probes 60 LLMs including Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B), same regime (post-instruction prefill activations), same downstream (MATH-500 Pass@1 with the same Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base). They train linear probes on continuous IRT human-difficulty labels (AMC ρ=0.88) and on LLM-derived difficulty (GSM8K ρ=0.58); steer Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with the difficulty direction (α=−3 improves all difficulty bins, α=+3 collapses); and track probe strength across GRPO checkpoints (human-difficulty β=+6.66 with Pass@1, LLM-difficulty β=−0.63).
