# Triage brief — 2509.24988 — Generalized Correctness Models: Learning Calibrated and Model-Agnostic Correctness Predictors from Historical Patterns

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24988
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the "self-knowledge" framing implicit in F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731).** RQ1 of the paper establishes empirically that an LLM has no privileged access to its own correctness: training Qwen2.5-7B to predict Llama3.1-8B is as good as Llama predicting itself; training Llama on Qwen is as good as Qwen on itself (e.g., Llama-self vs Qwen-on-Llama: 69.35% vs 69.0% MMLU). F-2 currently presents the prefill L19 direction as a property *of the generating model's residual stream*. If the GCM's central claim transfers, then a *text-only* CM (no hidden states, no probe, no L19 access) trained on Qwen-2.5-1.5B's MATH-500 history should match F-2's 0.7731 AUROC. Mechanism: the predictive content of the L19 prefill is a downstream readout of question + response surface features, not a signal that requires same-model parameters. Falsifiable experiment: fine-tune Qwen3-8B as a text-only CM on Qwen-2.5-1.5B's 500 MATH-500 (q, r, ĉ) tuples (243/500 K=1 correct from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`), evaluate AUROC on a held-out 25%, compare head-to-head with the L19 DoM 0.7731 OOF number. **Refutation fires** if text-only AUROC ≥ 0.77.

2. **Refutes F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy K=2.5 avg) as a "best-available" abstention method.** The GCM achieves 30.0% more coverage than Llama-3-70B's P(True) and 10.8% more coverage than a same-model SCM at the 5% risk threshold, with AURC strictly below both (Fig. 4). F-8's selective-prediction figures are reported only at one operating point (50% coverage) and only against a same-model probe baseline. Mechanism: a multi-model-trained CM monetizes correctness *patterns* across models that any single-model probe cannot see; AURC is the standard scalar comparison and our reports skip it. Falsifiable: compute AURC for the prefill DoM on the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` selective-prediction sweep, then train a Qwen3-8B GCM-style head on the same 1.5B history and compare AURC. **Refutation fires** if any GCM-style baseline beats the prefill DoM AURC.

3. **Refutes H-12 ("semantic-entropy probes label-free replace supervised DoM") and weakens F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy claim).** The paper's RQ2B ablation shows that *answer phrasing* (the surface form of the response) and *world knowledge* of the CM are the two largest contributors to correctness prediction — bigger than model-identity, conditioning on the query, or any hidden-state feature. Mechanism: if the predictive juice is in the response *text*, then any probe of hidden states (DoM, CoE-60, semantic-entropy probes) is a noisy proxy for what a text-only judge would extract directly. H-12 specifically positions SEPs as a *label-free* upgrade path; this paper says even with labels, the bottleneck is text features, not internal state. Falsifiable: ablate the prefill-DoM input space — train a CM that sees only `(q, r̂)` (Answer-only) or `(q, r̂, r)` (Full text) on MATH-500, compare AUROC against L19 DoM. **Refutation fires** if Answer-only ≥ 0.7186 (final-token DoM) or Full-text ≥ 0.7731 (prefill DoM).

4. **Refutes the cross-scale framing implicit in F-2 / F-6 ("7B is the stronger verifier" caveat).** The paper finds GCMs trained on Qwen3-8B beat *Llama-3-70B's self-emitted P(True)* (Acc 0.822 / AUROC 0.849 vs Acc 0.798 / AUROC 0.584). Self-prediction is *not* a function of model scale — a smaller model trained on aggregate history beats a much larger model's self-prediction. F-6's narrative treats prefill PR inversion at 7B as evidence about what the larger model "knows about itself"; this paper says larger-model self-knowledge is largely illusory. Falsifiable: take Qwen-2.5-7B's MATH-500 K=1 history (366/500 from results.json), train a Qwen3-8B GCM on it, evaluate it against Qwen-2.5-7B's own P(True) on a held-out split. **Refutation fires** if the external GCM beats 7B P(True) on AUROC by ≥ 0.05 — i.e., 7B's self-prediction was scale-irrelevant.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 1.5B; final-token 0.7186) — the GCM achieves AUROC 0.890 on Llama3.1-8B MMLU and 0.905 on TriviaQA-Llama3.1-8B (Tables 2–3). These are not directly comparable to MATH-500/1.5B but pose a strong **upper bound** on what label-free + history-aware approaches can buy. The 0.890 vs 0.7731 gap is 0.117 AUROC units; if even half of that survives the MMLU→MATH transition, F-2's prefill probe is dominated by a text-only history-trained CM.
- **F-8** (selective prediction 71.6% acc at coverage 0.5, K=2.5 avg) — direct competitor. The paper reports "30.0% more coverage at 5% risk vs Llama-3-70B P(True), 10.8% vs SCM" (Section 3.5). Our 71.6% number sits at the 50% coverage point; the GCM's risk-coverage curve shape is what we don't have.
- **F-3** (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, orthogonality) — *not* directly addressed. The paper treats CMs as black boxes over text and never opens up the hidden-state directions that produce the P(True) score. F-3 remains untouched by this paper.
- **H-5** (prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — RQ2B's "world knowledge" finding is consistent with H-5's "familiarity" interpretation: the CM's parametric knowledge of the answer (= familiarity) is the largest single contributor to correctness prediction.
- **H-11** (Big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B) — generalized by this paper. Distillation-of-self is one specific GCM training; the paper shows this is dominated by training on multiple-model histories.
- **H-12** (Semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM) — see Refutation 3 above. The paper offers a different label-free path (in-context learning with k=5 retrieval) that the brief should benchmark against SEPs as candidate replacements for DoM.
- **H-19** (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute) — overlaps with the paper's selective-prediction story. The GCM with posthoc spline calibration is in effect a label-aware routing signal; H-19's PANL-equivalent verification activation should be evaluated head-to-head with a text-only GCM signal.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Specific Correctness Model (SCM)** — LoRA rank 32, batch size 16, 1 epoch, ~10k examples, Qwen3-8B base, finetune as a P(True) classifier on a single target model's history. *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100 per target model. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct competitor to F-2's L19 DoM as an MATH-500 correctness predictor.
- **Generalized Correctness Model (GCM)** — same recipe but train on the concatenation of correctness datasets from 8 different LLMs. *Replication cost*: 4–8h H100 once if we collect MATH-500 K=1 histories for ≥4 models. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — refutation experiment for "self-knowledge" framing of F-2.
- **Answerless / Answer-only / Full ablation framework** — three CMs trained on `P(c|q)`, `P(c|q, r̂)`, `P(c|q, r̂, r)` to attribute predictive contribution to query, answer, and full response. *Replication cost*: 3× the SCM cost. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — clean attribution test for whether MATH-500 prefill DoM is doing more than reading the question.
- **P(True) extraction prompt** — verbatim "Please respond just 'yes' or 'no' in lowercase if [Model Name] will respond correctly to Model Prompt:" with logit of token "yes" as confidence. *Replication cost*: zero training; ~1h CPU/GPU eval per (target × dataset). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — easy zero-training baseline we don't currently have for MATH-500.
- **k=5 semantic-ICL retrieval via Chroma vector DB** — embed the train split of `(q, r, r̂, c)` tuples, retrieve top-5 by similarity at eval time, inject as context, elicit verbalized confidence. *Replication cost*: 30min CPU once history is collected. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — high inference cost per query but cleanly compares to F-2 without finetuning.
- **Spline calibration as posthoc layer** — Lucena 2018 spline calibration applied on top of the CM logits using as little as 5% of target dataset. *Replication cost*: minutes CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to the existing prefill-DoM scores to drop ECE without retraining.
- **Specialized batch-size selection for ECE ≤ 0.03** — appendix C of the paper claims an "optimal batch size" yields well-calibrated CMs out of the box with vanilla cross-entropy. *Replication cost*: minimal once SCM pipeline exists. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if SCM/GCM gets stood up.

## Approaches & framings

- **Self-knowledge ≠ correctness prediction.** The paper reframes correctness prediction as *historical pattern recognition*, not introspection. This is a frontal challenge to the implicit framing in P10 and F-2 that "the residual stream knows things about itself" — if the paper is right, the L19 prefill direction is a generic question-answer-readability axis, not a self-aware confidence axis. Intersects F-2 / F-3 / H-5.
- **CM as a probe of *what training data is sufficient for correctness prediction*.** By systematically ablating conditioning variables, the paper turns the CM from a black-box scoring system into a controlled lens on which features carry correctness information. Useful template for ablating MATH-500 prefill features beyond "DoM works".
- **Forecasting analogy with explicit history.** Frames calibration as a Brier-style forecasting problem and identifies "history" as the missing component vs human forecasters. Suggests our F-8 selective-prediction story is incomplete without an explicit history-of-predictions module.

- **MMLU** — 57 subject MCQ benchmark. ~14k questions, public HF. Applicable? **partial** — not our reasoning regime (1024-tok labels, MATH-500), but a clean cross-task generalization target.
- **TriviaQA** — short-form QA, ~95k questions, public HF. Applicable? **no** — too far from MATH-500 reasoning.

License: presumably released with code at https://github.com/The-Inscrutable-X/CalibratedModelAgnosticCorrectness. Applicable? **yes** — drop-in training material for a cross-task GCM experiment if we want to see whether their MMLU-trained GCM transfers to MATH-500 directly.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- LoRA rank 32, batch size 16, 1 epoch, ~10k examples → calibrated CM out of the box.
- Average MMLU response length: 198 tokens. Their prompts are dramatically shorter than our 1024-tok regime.
- ECE evaluated with both standard and RMSCE (Hendrycks 2019 adaptive binning) — RMSCE is the more robust metric we should adopt.
- Spline calibration (Lucena 2018) outperforms the alternatives in their appendix H.
- Code repo: https://github.com/The-Inscrutable-X/CalibratedModelAgnosticCorrectness — pull this for the SCM/GCM training scripts and the prompt templates.
- Compute footprint: GCM eval at 0.125s/example, 7.3 minutes for 3511 MMLU samples on Qwen3-8B; ICL adds 20× to that (2.6s/example).
- They use a Qwen3-8B reasoning model as base; reasoning models with `<think>` blocks are out-of-distribution — the GCM trained on non-reasoning histories still beats Qwen3-32B reasoning model's P(True), which is interesting for our 1.5B/7B non-reasoning baseline analysis.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Cheap text-only baseline**: take `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`, extract per-problem (question, K=1 response, K=1 correctness), prompt Qwen3-8B with the paper's verbatim P(True) template, compute AUROC. **Cost: 1h H100 once Qwen3-8B is loaded.** This is the *zero-training* GCM-equivalent number to compare against F-2's 0.7731. - **Cross-model self-vs-cross test on cached MATH-500**: prompt Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Qwen-2.5-7B each to predict the other's correctness on MATH-500 with the P(True) template; compare to self-prediction. Replicates the paper's RQ1 in our setting in <2h H100.
- **Spline-calibrate the existing prefill-DoM scores**: take the OOF prefill-DoM probe outputs from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`, fit Lucena 2018 spline calibration on a 5% holdout, recompute ECE/RMSCE on the rest. Cost: 5min CPU. Direct replicable check on whether DoM ECE drops without changing AUROC.

## 2509.24988 — Generalized Correctness Models: Learning Calibrated and Model-Agnostic Correctness Predictors from Historical Patterns (Xiao, Patil, Lee, Stengel-Eskin, Bansal, 2025)

**Relevance:** A frontal challenge to the implicit "self-knowledge" framing of our prefill L19 DoM (F-2). The paper establishes via RQ1 that an LLM has no privileged access to its own correctness — Qwen2.5-7B predicting Llama3.1-8B is as good as Llama predicting itself. RQ2A then trains a Generalized Correctness Model (Qwen3-8B finetuned on 8 model histories) that beats both Specific Correctness Models trained on each target and the self-emitted P(True) of much larger models (Llama-3-70B). The result challenges our framing in two ways: (a) the predictive content of L19 prefill may be a generic question-answer-readability axis rather than an internal-self signal; (b) our F-8 selective-prediction story is operating-point-bound and underexplored against history-aware baselines.

**Key claim we tested:** Their RQ1 says self-knowledge does not exist for purposes of correctness prediction; their downstream story says GCMs beat single-model probes on selective prediction.

The paper's headline numbers (Acc 0.820 / ECE 0.023 / AUROC 0.890 on Llama3.1-8B MMLU; AURC 0.069 vs Llama-3-70B P(True) 0.108) are on MMLU/TriviaQA short-form, not our 1024-tok MATH-500 reasoning. Whether RQ1's no-self-knowledge result transfers to long-CoT reasoning is the empirical question.
