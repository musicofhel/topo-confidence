# Triage brief — 2401.10474 — LDReg: Local Dimensionality Regularized Self-Supervised Learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10474
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Hanxun Huang, Ricardo J. G. B. Campello, Sarah Erfani, Xingjun Ma, Michael E. Houle, James Bailey (ICLR 2024).

## Refutations

1. **Global PR can hide local collapse — threatens F-1 universality framing.** F-1 reports a universal participation-ratio (PR) breathing curve across 4 transformer scales — prefill PR ~20, peak PR 60–115, final-token PR 2–18 — treating PR as a *global* covariance dimensionality. Huang et al. demonstrate (Fig. 2c–d, Tab. 1) that representations can have high effective rank globally while collapsing locally: BYOL has effective-rank 583.8 (highest of all SSL baselines) but mean LID 15.9 (lowest). **Mechanism:** breathing-as-PR may be measuring a global statistic that averages over heterogeneous local manifolds, so two models with identical PR-breathing curves could have entirely different local geometries — i.e., F-1's "universality" claim could be a global-statistic artifact. **Falsifiable test:** estimate per-token LID (Method-of-Moments, k=64) on cached P11 H100 1024-tok prefill / mid-generation / final residuals for Qwen-2.5-1.5B vs 7B. If geometric-mean LID curves diverge across scales while PR curves align, F-1's universality reduces to a coincidence at a coarser statistic.

2. **Geometric-mean aggregation could erase the asymmetric-collapse direction in F-4.** F-4 / the headlines table report `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046` and final-token PR 2–18 for correct vs. incorrect trajectories aggregated as arithmetic means across the 500-problem MATH set. Huang et al.'s Theorem 4 (Appendix C.5) proves that *asymptotic Fisher-Rao distance + geometric mean* is theoretically preferred over arithmetic mean for aggregating intrinsic-dimensionality measures, with their Corollary 2.1 tying log-LID arithmetic mean to a Fisher-Rao distance from uniform. **Mechanism:** if our final-token PR distribution is heavy-tailed (a few problems with collapse PR 2 dragging the mean), the arithmetic correct-vs-incorrect gap could vanish or invert under geometric aggregation. **Falsifiable test:** re-aggregate F-4's final-token PR comparison for correct vs. incorrect using geometric mean of log-PR (or log-LID with k=64). If the asymmetric-collapse gap shrinks below 1σ, the F-4 "correct trajectories collapse harder" framing is an aggregation artifact.

3. **F-7's per-problem dissolution is exactly Huang et al.'s local-vs-global signature — and may be a replication, not novelty.** F-7 is built on a sharp gap: D-bucket group prefill PR = 14.49 (lowest of any bucket) while per-problem k=20 NN local PR = 12.84 (indistinguishable from other buckets, A=12.66, B=12.80, C=12.97). Huang et al.'s headline observation (§1, Fig. 1c) is precisely this *high global D, low local D* pattern, with theoretical machinery (Fisher-Rao on LID distributions) for measuring it. **Mechanism:** F-7's "distinctive collective signature" claim asserts a real geometric phenomenon, but if D-bucket's group-vs-local gap is just instance-level local dimensional collapse measured at group scale, then it is a known SSL phenomenon, not a novel D-bucket-specific finding — and the right framing is *low LID at the bucket members*, not "collective signature." **Falsifiable test:** compute geometric-mean LID per bucket (k=64 NN within bucket, then within full 500-problem set) on cached prefill activations. If D-bucket has lower geometric-mean LID than A/B/C buckets while group-PR ordering reproduces, F-7 is reframed as bucket-level local-LID collapse and loses the "collective vs. per-problem" distinction.

4. **Single-direction DoM probes (F-2) are exactly the failure mode LDReg addresses.** F-2 reports prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731 — a single direction extracted as the difference of class means in residual space. Huang et al. argue that representations with high *global* effective rank can still have many points whose local neighborhoods collapse to LID 1, and that pushing LID upward improves downstream linear-probe accuracy by 0.1–0.7 pts on ResNet-50/SimCLR variants. **Mechanism:** if some MATH-500 problems sit in low-LID neighborhoods of the L19 residual space, the correct/incorrect signal at those points may not be linearly separable along a single global DoM — so a per-cluster (or LID-conditioned) DoM probe could beat 0.7731. **Falsifiable test:** estimate per-problem prefill LID at L19 using k=64 NN; split MATH-500 into high-LID vs. low-LID halves; train separate DoMs per half and compare AUROC vs. global DoM. If the per-half DoMs jointly outperform the global DoM by ≥1 pt AUROC, the F-2 single-direction story is incomplete and a local-LID-conditioned probe is justified.

## Methodologies extracted

- **LID via Method of Moments (Amsaleg et al. 2018)** — for each anchor `z_i`, compute the k nearest-neighbor distances within the batch; LID*_i = -μ_k / (μ_k - w_k), where w_k is the distance to the k-th NN and μ_k is the mean of all k NN distances. Paper uses k = 64. *Replication cost*: 20–60min CPU on cached P11 NPZs (k-NN dominates). *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — drops directly onto our prefill / final-token activations and is the cheapest way to test refutations 1–4.
- **Asymptotic Fisher-Rao distance between LID distributions** — Huang et al.'s Theorem 1, eq. for comparing two CDFs of local distance distributions in the asymptotic small-radius limit. Reduces to a closed-form expression in LID*_F. *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU once LID per-problem is computed. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** if refutation 1 fires — it is the principled distance for comparing two models' local geometry.
- **LDReg loss term** — `L = L_SSL - β · (1/N) · Σ ln(LID*_i)` (L1) or `L = L_SSL - β · (1/N) · Σ (ln LID*_i)²` (L2). Paper uses β = 0.005–0.01 for ResNet-50 SSL. *Replication cost*: not directly applicable (we don't train); but the *loss* could become a regularizer on probe heads or on activation-patching counterfactuals. *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** in the short term — we are not training models. Worth keeping as reference if H-1 (DoM steering) is ever extended to learned interventions.
- **Effective rank (Roy & Vetterli 2007)** as a global-D baseline — entropy of normalized singular values of the representation matrix. Paper uses it for Tab. 1 "Effective Rank" column. *Replication cost*: 5min CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — it is a well-defined alternative to PR that is comparable across models and avoids the small-sample bias H-16 targets.
- **Geometric-mean aggregation of log-LID across batch** — Theorem 2 / Corollary 2.1. Practical recipe: report `exp(mean(log LID*_i))` instead of `mean(LID*_i)`. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — drop-in change to F-1 / F-4 reporting that is theoretically motivated and one line of code.

## Approaches & framings

- **Local intrinsic dimensionality as a per-sample statistic** — reframes "dimensional collapse" from a global property of the covariance matrix to a per-anchor property of nearest-neighbor distance distributions. For our work, this is the missing language for F-7's collective/per-problem dichotomy and for H-7's density-based outlier framing. Suggests an experiment family: "are correctness signals encoded in *local* geometry that a global probe smears?"
- **Fisher-Rao on a statistical manifold of distance CDFs** — instead of comparing scalar dimensionality numbers, compare distributions of local distance CDFs. Useful framing for cross-model comparison (F-1) where we currently compare scalar PR curves; a Fisher-Rao distance between two models' LID distributions is a much stronger universality test.
- **"Log-scale and geometric mean" as the natural arithmetic of LID** — small but consequential reformulation: any future plot we make of dimensionality should be on log axes with geometric-mean error bars. Affects how we'd write up F-1 / F-4 in a paper.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2401.10474 — LDReg: Local Dimensionality Regularized Self-Supervised Learning (Huang et al., ICLR 2024)

**Relevance:** LDReg formalizes a phenomenon we have stumbled on empirically in F-7 (D-bucket has a group-PR signature that "dissolves" at per-problem k=20 NN scale): representations can have high *global* dimensionality while collapsing *locally*. Their machinery — per-anchor LID via Method of Moments, asymptotic Fisher-Rao distance between LID distributions, and Fréchet/geometric-mean aggregation — gives us (a) a more principled statistic than PR for F-1's universality claim and F-4's asymmetric-collapse gap, (b) a theoretical home for F-7's group-vs-per-problem split, and (c) a per-anchor measure that could refine F-2's single-direction DoM probe. Their LDReg loss itself does not transfer (we are not training SSL models) but the *measurement* tools are drop-in on cached residual-stream activations.

**Key claim we tested:** representations with high global effective rank (or PR) can still exhibit low *local* intrinsic dimensionality on a per-anchor basis, and aggregating dimensionality measures with arithmetic mean is theoretically inferior to geometric mean / Fisher-Rao distance.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — we have not yet run LID estimation on cached P11 activations. Three FutureExperiments (P11-FE74 D-bucket LID, P11-FE75 geometric-mean F-4, P11-FE76 LID-conditioned DoM, P11-FE77 cross-model LID universality) operationalize the test. Each is CPU-only on already-cached NPZs.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, P11-FE77, H-69, H-70, H-71
