# Triage brief — 2604.11962 — The Linear Centroids Hypothesis: How Deep Network Features Represent Data

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11962
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731) may be a "plausibility" signal, not a "correctness" signal — same failure mode Walker et al. demonstrate for Marks & Tegmark mass-mean probes.**
   *Mechanism*: F-2 uses a mass-mean (difference-of-means) probe on **latent activations** at L19 — the exact construction Walker et al. test in §4.3. Their Llama-3.1-8B Fig 7 shows that latent-activation mass-mean probes trained on the `likely` dataset capture *plausibility under the model's logit distribution*, not *truthfulness*; centroid-based mass-mean probes generalize to truth datasets, latent-based ones do not. Translated to our setting: the L19 prefill DoM may be picking up "this prompt is in-distribution / model has high prior" rather than "this trajectory will be correct," and AUROC=0.7731 reflects in-distribution familiarity rather than correctness prediction.
   *Falsifiable test*: extract centroids at L19 (μ = J_L19_MLP(h)^T · 1) for the same 500 MATH-500 prefill positions, re-train the OOF 5-fold mass-mean probe on centroids, compare AUROC. Stronger test: train probe on MATH-500 prefill DoM (latent vs centroid), evaluate cross-task transfer to a non-math benchmark. If centroid-DoM transfers but latent-DoM does not, the latent-DoM signal is the LCH-predicted "spurious extracted feature."

2. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, "orthogonal directions") may be an artifact of the latent-activation substrate, not a real geometric fact about the correctness expert.**
   *Mechanism*: LCH §3.2 + Theorem 3.4 argues that features correspond to *centroid* directions of *aligned local experts*, not arbitrary directions in latent space. If the same underlying "correctness expert" governs both prefill and final-token positions but operates from different latent regions, latent-activation DoM directions can be near-orthogonal while the *centroid* DoM directions are aligned (both are row-sums of Jacobians describing the same functional behavior).
   *Falsifiable test*: compute centroids at L19 for prefill last token vs final answer token across MATH-500 (500 problems × 2 positions). Compute centroid-DoM for correct vs incorrect at each position. If cos(prefill_centroid_DoM, final_centroid_DoM) >> 0.046 (e.g. > 0.5), F-3's orthogonality is a substrate artifact and §10.1 of PROJECT_RECORD's "two distinct circuits" interpretation needs revision.

3. **F-10 (PH on residual streams indistinguishable from Gaussian null) may be a substrate problem, not a true topological null.**
   *Mechanism*: Walker et al. Fig 3 (left panel) explicitly shows that latent activations form linear directions *even when the feature is spurious* — i.e., linear/topological structure in latent space is not faithful to the network's computation. PH on residuals is by construction a measurement on latent activations. Centroids, by Theorem 3.4, capture functional alignment of local experts, and the toy-network result in Fig 9 shows centroids migrate and *develop structure* during training that latent activations do not show. Thus a PH null on residuals is consistent with non-trivial PH on centroids.
   *Falsifiable test*: re-run the EXP-029 PH pipeline against centroid clouds (J_L17→L21^T · 1 per problem, same n=500 cloud size, same Gudhi parameters). Compare H_0/H_1 lifetimes and Betti curves to (a) the original residual-stream null, (b) the Gaussian null. If centroid PH deviates from Gaussian null at any layer, F-10's "PH = Gaussian" claim collapses to "PH on the wrong substrate."

Their finding that centroid-based mass-mean probes generalize across truth datasets while latent-based ones overfit to "plausibility" is the most consequential single result for our project. Predicts that on Qwen-1.5B, swapping latent activations for centroids at L19 should either (a) raise AUROC (centroids cleaner) or (b) keep AUROC similar but improve transfer. - **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — both are latent-activation features. LCH predicts that centroid-CoE-60 vs centroid-L19-DoM might *not* be redundant because they describe different sub-component experts (across layers vs at one layer).

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Walker Eq (2) + Fig 6 is a *drop-in better tool* for this hypothesis. ## Methodologies extracted

- **Centroid extraction via Jacobian row-sum (Prop 2.2)** — μ_x^(ℓ1→ℓ2) = J_x(f^(ℓ1→ℓ2))^T · 1. For any differentiable sub-component, computable as a single backward pass with output_grad = 1.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached residuals if we re-run forward + backward for L19 MLP across 500 problems × prefill + final token positions. ~2h H100 to extend across L0–L27.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — central refutation tool for F-2/F-3/F-10.

- **Centroid mass-mean probe (centroid version of Marks & Tegmark)** — replace latent activations with centroids in the probe construction.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once centroids are extracted (5min CPU, sklearn LR with 5-fold OOF).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct head-to-head with F-2.

- **Centroid-based neuron attribution (Eq 2)** — s_N^(i,ℓ) = mean_x ‖μ_x^f − μ_x^f(i,ℓ)‖_2 / ‖μ_x^f‖_2 where f^(i,ℓ) is f with neuron i of layer ℓ ablated.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 per layer of interest (1024 forward+backward pairs to ablate each MLP neuron at L19 on n problems). For Qwen-1.5B L19 MLP (~8960 neurons), 1h H100 / problem-batch.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — supersedes ablation-only approaches for H-13 head-level attribution.

- **Local centroid as saliency map** — average centroid over a neighborhood B_ε(x), analogous to SmoothGrad. For text/transformer it's the local centroid of the embedding-to-output Jacobian averaged over a small Gaussian ball in the embedding.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU for visualization on 10 illustrative problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** for now — saliency is not a load-bearing question for our project; would only run if writing up a paper.

- **TopK SAE on centroids** — train a TopK sparse autoencoder on the centroid stream rather than the latent stream.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 to train an SAE on Qwen-1.5B L19 centroids over a sizable corpus.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — out of scope for our correctness-prediction focus, but flagged as a future option for label-free probing (H-12 extension).

- **Effective-dimension along input interpolation between class anchors** — interpolate between two centroids and measure effective rank of the centroid neighborhood. Their MNIST result shows greater dim drop between more semantically distinct classes.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached residuals (after centroid extraction).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — could be a sharper instrument for F-1 (dimensional breathing) since the breathing curve is currently measured on residuals.

## Approaches & framings

- **LRH vs LCH distinction** — features as latent-activation directions ("extracted features, possibly spurious") vs as centroid directions ("extracted *and* utilized"). Reframes our entire DoM analysis from "what direction do correct-vs-incorrect prefills span in residual space" to "what direction do their *local experts* span." Directly intersects F-2/F-3/F-7/F-9.
- **Mechanistic-by-construction interpretability** — centroids tie features to the network's computation via the Jacobian, not post hoc. Reframes "the prefill DoM is an indirect signal of correctness" to "the prefill local-expert direction *is* the correctness-extraction operation, by construction" — if it survives the substrate swap.
- **Local-expert / power-diagram framing of the network** — Voronoi-like tiling of input space, each tile has a centroid that is the *summary of its expert*. Provides a geometric vocabulary for "what does the model compute on this input" that is sharper than "what is its hidden state." Useful narrative scaffolding for the project's eventual writeup.
- **Hierarchical centroid analysis** — centroids of sub-components (input → ℓ vs input → final layer) yield coarser/finer features. Suggests a layerwise breathing analysis on centroids that could refine F-1 (currently only on residual streams).
- **Spurious-feature critique of LRH** — directly bears on H-12 (semantic entropy / label-free probing) and on the broader question of whether any of our F-N findings might survive on centroids but evaporate on activations (or vice versa).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/ThomasWalker1/LinearCentroidsHypothesis — has Jacobian-vector-product centroid extraction and probe code; check if Llama-3.1-8B mass-mean probe code is reusable for Qwen-2.5.
- Centroid via JVP: in PyTorch, `torch.autograd.grad(outputs=f_out, inputs=h, grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(f_out))[0]` gives J^T · 1 in one backward pass. Critical for cost: do not materialize the full Jacobian.
- Compute overhead: 10–15% over latent extraction for SAE/probe pipelines (Table 1). Llama-3.1-8B probe pipeline scaled ~5× (2329s vs 470s) because their centroid is full-model input-to-layer, not single-layer; for our L19-only experiment we should use the *layer-local* Jacobian (cheap).
- Local centroid (saliency): SmoothGrad-style averaging over Gaussian neighborhood, σ chosen as a fraction of the input norm. ε normalization important for stable attribution percentile (Fig 15).
- Their toy network setup uses ReLU + GELU softening (Balestriero & Baraniuk 2018c) — GELU centroids are stable approximations of ReLU centroids; relevant for transformer MLPs which use GELU/SwiGLU.
- For circuit discovery (Eq 2) they sample 256 points in B_ε(x) with ε = 0.25 × ‖μ‖. Robustness checks in Fig 15 show low variance; finite-sample percentile is stable.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.11962 — The Linear Centroids Hypothesis: How Deep Network Features Represent Data (Walker, Humayun, Balestriero, Baraniuk, 2026)

**Relevance:** Highly relevant. Walker et al. argue that the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) — the foundation of every probe-on-latent-activations technique we use, including F-2's L19 prefill DoM — identifies "spurious features" because latent activations form linear directions even when the feature is not utilized in computation. They propose the Linear Centroids Hypothesis (LCH): replace latent activations with **centroids** (Jacobian-row-sums describing the local expert at each input region), and feature discovery becomes mechanistic by construction. They demonstrate the LRH→LCH improvement on (a) sparse-autoencoder feature dictionaries (DINOv2/v3), (b) mass-mean truth probes on Llama-3.1-8B — which is *the exact probe family used in F-2* — and (c) GPT2-Large circuit discovery via a centroid-perturbation attribution metric (Eq 2).

**Key claim we tested:** Mass-mean probes constructed on centroids generalize across truth datasets while mass-mean probes on latent activations capture "plausibility under the model's logit distribution" rather than truthfulness (their Llama-3.1-8B Fig 7).

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — The LCH→LRH gap on Llama-3.1-8B mass-mean probes directly threatens the interpretation of F-2's 0.7731 AUROC as "correctness prediction." Our F-2 probe is constructed identically (mass-mean on latent activations). The refutation test is concrete and cheap (P11-FE71): extract centroids at L19 on Qwen-1.5B, retrain the OOF probe, compare AUROC. If centroids win, F-2 is partially a plausibility signal and the selective-prediction pipeline (F-8) should be rebuilt; if latent wins, F-2 is robust to the substrate swap.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71 (centroid-DoM probe — HIGH ROI), P11-FE72 (centroid-orthogonality test for F-3), P10-FE54 (centroid-PH refutation for F-10), P11-FE73 (centroid attribution for H-13), H-71 / H-72 / H-73 / H-74.
