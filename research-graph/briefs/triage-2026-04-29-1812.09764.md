# Triage brief — 1812.09764 — Neural Persistence: A Complexity Measure for Deep Neural Networks Using Algebraic Topology

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09764
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the framing of F-10 as "PH cannot recover non-trivial signal from trained networks."**
   F-10 says persistent homology computed on residual-stream activation point clouds sits at a
   permuted-Gaussian null. Rieck et al. compute PH on a *different* object — a stratified
   weight graph filtered by `|w| / w_max` in non-ascending order — and report a
   *clearly bimodal* distribution that distinguishes trained networks from divergent /
   Gaussian-init / uniform-init networks (Figure 2; Figure A.1 in the appendix), with a
   second-mode separation that is large compared to within-class variance. Mechanism: the
   weight-graph filtration sees structure that activation-cloud filtrations destroy because
   activations average over many tokens / inputs and erase the heavy-tailed weight
   anisotropy. *Falsifiable test*: implement Algorithm 1 from the paper on Qwen-2.5-1.5B's
   per-layer MLP `W_up` / `W_down` matrices, compare NP at L19 against random-Gaussian
   reshuffles of the same matrix, and check whether the gap exceeds the per-seed variance
   reported in Figure 2. If yes, F-10's null cannot be promoted to the universal claim
   "PH is uninformative for transformers" — only the narrower "PH on activation point
   clouds is uninformative."

2. **Refutes the implicit premise behind F-2 / F-8 that "correctness signal must live in
   activations."** The paper explicitly programmatically rejects "interrogating networks
   with input data" (§1, last paragraph) and demonstrates that a *purely structural*,
   weight-only measure tracks generalization on Fashion-MNIST / MNIST / CIFAR-10 / IMDB
   well enough to replace held-out validation loss as an early-stopping signal (Figure 4;
   Table in §4.2 — barycentre at (−0.53, −0.08) for Fashion-MNIST, 0.08% accuracy loss
   while stopping half an epoch earlier). Mechanism: if structural complexity is sufficient
   for generalization tracking, then the prefill L19 DoM (F-2, AUROC 0.7731) might be a
   proxy for static head-circuit *connectivity strength* rather than an active prefill
   computation. *Falsifiable test*: train two checkpoints of Qwen-2.5-1.5B that differ
   by ~1% on MATH-500 (e.g., LoRA adapters with different ranks), compute
   per-layer normalized NP, and check whether ∆NP at L19 alone correlates with ∆MATH-500
   accuracy as well as ∆DoM-AUROC. If so, F-2's status as a *correctness predictor* is
   confounded with weight-graph complexity that any post-training method recovers.

3. **Refutes H-4's "breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS" exclusivity claim.**
   H-4 frames breathing and Sharpness Dimension as the two candidate post-training
   structural-complexity measures. NP is a *third* candidate (older, p-norm of
   0-dim PH on the weight-graph filtration) that operates on a different object (weights,
   not activations). Mechanism: if NP and Sharpness Dimension agree on the same
   ordering of checkpoints by generalization but breathing disagrees (or vice versa),
   the breathing-EoS bridge that H-4 hypothesizes is local to dynamics-of-activation
   measures and *does not* link to the weight-space structural complexity family
   (NP, Bianchini-Scarselli Betti bounds, Guss-Salakhutdinov decision-region complexity).
   *Falsifiable test*: compute NP and Sharpness-Dim on the same Qwen-2.5-1.5B vs
   Qwen-2.5-7B comparison used in P11; check rank-correlation. If `corr(NP, SharpDim)
   > corr(NP, breathing)`, H-4 must be qualified to "activation-side structural
   measures" only, and a parallel weight-side family exists.

4. **Refutes any future use of F-7 (D-bucket collective signature) as a *per-prompt
   geometric* claim.** Rieck et al. emphasize that NP is per-network (frozen weights)
   and explicitly rejects per-input characterization as the wrong abstraction layer
   (§1, abstract). The paper's success at separating regimes via weight-graph PH while
   F-10 finds activation-cloud PH at null pushes the interpretation of F-7's collective
   signature toward "we are observing a collective fingerprint of routing through a small
   number of high-NP edges" rather than "the bucket has a per-prompt topological
   signature." Mechanism: if D-bucket (36 problems) consistently routes through a low
   set of L19 MLP weight-graph paths with above-mean NP contribution, F-7 is recast as a
   weight-side fingerprint, not a representation-side one. *Falsifiable test*: compute
   per-edge NP contribution on Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 MLP, then forward each D-bucket and
   A-bucket prompt and measure path activation overlap with the high-NP-contribution
   edges. If D-bucket's path-overlap with high-NP edges exceeds A-bucket's by more than
   the random-routing baseline, F-7's "collective" framing must explicitly attribute to
   the weight graph, not the activation cloud.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10 (PH on residual streams at Gaussian null)** — direct counterpoint. F-10's
  baseline test was activation-side; this paper's positive result is weight-side. Both
  can coexist; the joint reading sharpens F-10 to "*activation-cloud* PH at null"
  rather than "PH at null."
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731 OOF 5-fold on 1.5B)** — adversarial alternative
  framing. If a weight-only measure (NP per layer) can predict checkpoint-level
  generalization without activation interrogation, the prefill DoM's predictive
  power should be checked against a no-input static baseline (e.g., L19's contribution
  to mean normalized NP across the network).
- **F-1 (universal dimensional breathing across architectures)** — orthogonal. Breathing
  is per-token activation-side; NP is per-network weight-side. Both being "universal"
  means there are at least two independent universals to reconcile.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — see refutation 4 above; potential reframing.
- **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dim / EoS)** — see refutation 3 above; weight-side family.
- **H-8 (prefill direction stable across checkpoints)** — methodological alignment.
  NP-per-checkpoint can be used as a complementary stability check; if NP rank-orders
  checkpoints the same way prefill DoM does, H-8 gets independent corroboration.
- **H-16 (Marchenko-Pastur bias correction preserves breathing magnitude)** — methodological
  cousin. Both NP normalization (Theorem 1 / Theorem 2) and MP correction are
  finite-sample bias corrections for matrix-derived complexity measures; the
  normalization theorems here suggest a similar bound argument may exist for breathing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Stratified-graph weight filtration** — sort `|w|/w_max` in non-ascending order; build
  filtration where edges are added in this order, vertices present at start; persistence
  pairs are of form `(1, w'_i)` below the diagonal.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU per Qwen-1.5B layer (sort + union-find on ~100M edges
  per W matrix is `O(n log n)`; 28 layers → < 1 hour).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — small cost, directly tests refutation 1, and we
  have the weights cached.

- **Mean normalized neural persistence (Definition 4)** — per-layer NP normalized by the
  upper-bound from Theorem 1, averaged over layers; gives a single scalar per network.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once filtration is computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural roll-up for cross-checkpoint comparison.

- **Theorem 2 empirical bound proxy** — `||1 - w_max||_p ≤ NP(G_k) ≤ ||1 - w_min||_p`.
  Computable from sorted weight vectors without running PH at all; useful as a fast
  pre-filter / sanity check.
  *Replication cost*: <1 min CPU per layer (just sort + p-norm).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap proxy for layer-comparison sweeps.

- **NP-based early stopping (Algorithm 2)** — patience-style trigger on first-difference
  of NP across epochs; no validation set required.
  *Replication cost*: not applicable — we don't fine-tune from scratch.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — not relevant to inference-time analysis on a frozen
  pretrained model unless H-10 (post-fine-tune breathing change) becomes a project.

- **Random-init null comparison (Figure 2)** — compare trained-network NP against NP of
  Gaussian / uniform random matrices of the same shape.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cleanest first-pass test of refutation 1.

- **Clustering-coefficient sanity comparison (Appendix A.1)** — show that traditional
  graph-theoretic measures (clustering coefficient, shortest path) fail to separate
  trained from sabotaged networks while NP succeeds.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if FE-764 fires — defensive comparison against
  weight-norm-only baselines.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Don't interrogate with input data" framing.** Methodological commitment to
  weight-only structural measures; explicit rejection of feature-visualization,
  sensitivity, IB-theory, and weight-interaction-detection traditions because they
  all involve forward passes. This is the *opposite* commitment of our entire prefill
  / final-token DoM program (F-2, F-3, F-4) — a direct philosophical foil that lets
  us bound where activation-side methods can and cannot go.

- **Filtration-by-magnitude rather than filtration-by-distance.** Standard TDA
  filtrations on point clouds use distance; this paper uses weight magnitude on a
  fixed graph. Suggests a parallel filtration design for *activation* graphs:
  build a per-token graph weighted by attention magnitude or MLP-gate magnitude
  and filter analogously. Could revive the Pathway-7 non-Euclidean PH program
  with a different filtration choice (currently NO-GO at 0.774 < 0.796).

- **Bounded normalization for cross-architecture comparison (Theorem 1).** Provides a
  closed-form upper bound on NP per layer based on `(|V_k| × |V_{k+1}|)`; this lets
  comparable NP scores be defined across networks of different widths. Useful framing
  for any future "compare 1.5B vs 7B" structural-complexity claim — gives an analytic
  alternative to ad-hoc width normalization.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code released: https://github.com/BorgwardtLab/Neural-Persistence
- Architecture: `[650, 650]` fully connected, ReLU, Adam, hyperparameters tuned via
  cross-validation (Table A.1 in paper appendix).
- Default `p = 2` for the persistence-diagram p-norm; explicitly checks that p-norm of
  *all* weights does not give a workable early-stopping signal (paper §5 — fails to
  ever trigger), so NP captures something not visible in raw weight norm.
- Filtration uses **descending** weight order (large `|w|` first, small last); standard
  TDA convention is *ascending* — the paper notes this is an upper-level-set filtration,
  with persistence pairs below diagonal (after Bubenik 2015 and Cohen-Steiner 2009).
- Vertices are *all present at filtration start* — distinguishing this from typical
  Vietoris-Rips constructions; resulting persistence diagrams have entries of the form
  `(1, w'_i)`, equivalent to a maximum spanning tree / hierarchical clustering.
- Computational complexity: `O(n log n)` for sort + `O(n α(n))` for union-find PH.
- Theorem 2 lets you compute upper / lower bounds on NP from just the top-n / bottom-n
  weights without running PH at all — useful for very wide layers.
- The framework extends to convolutional layers via a closed-form approximation
  (Section A.4 — but the early-stopping criterion fails there, by their own admission).

- **Compute NP-per-layer on Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Qwen-2.5-7B from the cached HF weights**
  (no NPZ needed; weights are public). Single CPU job, ~1 hour. Compare against
  Gaussian-resample null on the same matrix shapes. If trained-vs-null gap > 5σ at
  any layer, refutation 1 fires; if gap < 1σ everywhere, F-10 generalizes to weights.
  Script needed: `pathway11_h100/neural_persistence/compute_np.py` (new — ~80 LOC
  union-find + sort).
- **Theorem 2 bound check** — for each layer of Qwen-2.5-1.5B, compute the empirical
  bounds `||1 - w_max||_2` and `||1 - w_min||_2` from sorted weights only; plot vs
  layer index. If L19 sits at a local maximum of the bound, that's a weight-side
  echo of the activation-side L19 anomaly (F-2). 5 min CPU. No new code beyond
  numpy sort + p-norm.
- **NP-rank vs DoM-AUROC-rank scatter** — for the 28 layers of 1.5B, plot
  per-layer NP (x-axis) vs per-layer prefill-DoM AUROC (y-axis, from
  `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`). High Spearman ρ would
  support the "DoM is a downstream proxy of weight-graph NP" reading from
  refutation 2; near-zero ρ would falsify it cleanly. ~30 min CPU.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 1812.09764 — Neural Persistence: A Complexity Measure for Deep Neural Networks Using Algebraic Topology (Rieck, Togninalli, Bock, Moor, Horn, Gumbsch, Borgwardt, ICLR 2019)

**Relevance:** Direct counterpoint paper to F-10. Defines a topological complexity
measure ("neural persistence") computed from the *weight matrix* of a fully-connected
neural network via 0-dim persistent homology of a stratified graph filtered by
descending normalized weight magnitude. Reports a non-null signal that distinguishes
trained networks from random / divergent networks (Figure 2; Figure A.1) — direct
contrast to F-10's activation-side null. Methodologically philosophy-of-mind opposed
to the prefill-DoM program: explicitly rejects "interrogating networks with input
data." Datasets used (MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10/IMDB on small fully-connected
MLPs) do not transfer to MATH-500, but the *method* transfers to Qwen-2.5-1.5B
weight matrices at trivial cost.

**Key claim we tested:** "Persistent homology on a *weight-graph* filtration with
descending magnitude order separates trained networks from random / divergent
ones." If this generalizes from MNIST-MLPs to Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 MLP, then F-10's
null applies only to *activation-cloud distance-based PH*, not to PH-of-trained-LMs
in general.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Method is implementable from the public code at
github.com/BorgwardtLab/Neural-Persistence; weights are public; cost is ~1h CPU
total. Refutation tests are listed in P11-FE764 / P11-FE765 / P11-FE766 and
H-764 / H-765. Until we run them, the prudent reading is: the paper *does not*
contradict F-10 directly (different objects: weights vs activations) but *does*
forbid extending F-10 from its measured regime to a universal "PH-doesn't-work-for-
transformers" claim.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE764, P11-FE765, P11-FE766, H-764, H-765.

**Status:** TO TEST
