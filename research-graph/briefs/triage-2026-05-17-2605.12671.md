# Triage brief — 2605.12671 — All Circuits Lead to Rome: Rethinking Functional Anisotropy in Circuit and Sheaf Discovery for LLMs

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12671
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — uniqueness assumption.**
   The paper demonstrates that single tasks are supported by multiple structurally distinct circuits with <5% IoU overlap (Table 2: IOI 4.1%, BLiMP 5.1%). If applied to our correctness-prediction task, the DoM direction may be one of many equally-valid low-overlap directions that achieve comparable AUROC. The "single direction" framing may be an artifact of our training protocol (single logistic regression run) rather than a property of the model. **Falsifiable by:** Running OASR-style repulsive probing on L19 prefill activations — if a second direction achieves AUROC ≥0.75 with cos(dir1, dir2) < 0.2, F-2's "single direction" framing is weakened.

2. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — mechanism non-uniqueness.**
   The paper's "Distributive Dense Circuit Hypothesis" argues that in high-dimensional superposition, combinatorially many distinct edge subsets produce equivalent readouts. If the prefill DoM direction is merely one realization among many structurally-distinct mechanisms that all predict correctness, then attributing semantic content ("familiarity" vs "decomposability") to the direction is unfounded — it is one arbitrary projection of a non-unique manifold. **Falsifiable by:** Discovering 5+ orthogonal directions at L19 that each predict correctness at AUROC >0.70; if they exist, projecting semantic meaning onto any one is questionable.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — redundancy may be structural not functional.**
   The paper shows that multiple circuits with near-zero overlap can produce identical task behavior (their three-edge sheaf vs thousand-edge sheaf both achieve near-100% on IOI). By analogy, CoE-60 and L19 DoM may be structurally distinct mechanisms that happen to predict the same labels — their apparent "redundancy" (our F-9 claim) may instead be two representatives from a large equivalence class, and the *union* may contain information that neither alone captures. **Falsifiable by:** Training a probe on the residual of (L19 DoM prediction errors) using CoE-60 features — if this residual probe exceeds chance (AUROC >0.55), then CoE-60 carries non-redundant signal that our current "redundancy" framing misses.

4. **Refutes the interpretability of cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 (FE291) — CAST PC1 ≈ supervised DoM.**
   The paper argues that high cosine between two directions does not establish that the underlying mechanism is unique — multiple decompositions of the residual stream can yield high-cosine approximations to any given readout direction via combinatorial abundance of contributing edges. The PC1≈DoM alignment might be one of many possible alignments, and the apparent convergence may be a statistical inevitability in 1536 dimensions rather than evidence of a canonical mechanism. **Falsifiable by:** Sampling random 1536-d directions and measuring how frequently cos(random, PC1) > 0.9 — if the null rate is non-negligible, the alignment is less meaningful. (Quick check: for uniform random directions in 1536-d, E[|cos|] ≈ sqrt(2/(π·1536)) ≈ 0.020, so 0.9216 is clearly non-trivial. The real test is whether *trained* directions converge: train 20 probes from different random seeds and measure pairwise cos.)

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core finding — non-uniqueness of internal mechanisms — directly questions whether our single DoM direction is the *only* direction achieving this AUROC, or merely one point in a high-dimensional equivalence class of similarly-performing projections.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature):** If circuits are non-unique, the "collective" geometric signature of the D-bucket (incorrect problems) may itself decompose into multiple distinct sub-signatures depending on which circuit realization one adopts as the reference frame.

- **F-10 (PH adds negative signal beyond covariance):** The paper's theoretical framework ("Distributive Dense Circuit Hypothesis") provides an alternative explanation for F-10: if mechanisms are dense and distributed, topological summaries over a single realization's residuals would indeed appear null — the structure is not in any single subgraph but in the *multiplicity* of subgraphs.

- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy):** If multiple structurally-distinct circuits all support the correctness prediction, steering along DoM may only disable *one* of many pathways. The Hydra Effect (McGrath 2307.15771) suggests ablating one circuit may strengthen alternatives.

- **cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216:** The convergence of supervised (DoM) and unsupervised (PC1) directions is consistent with the paper's thesis if interpreted correctly: in high-dimensional spaces, multiple valid directions exist but the dominant variance direction (PC1) happens to align with the easiest-to-find supervised direction (DoM). The *uniqueness* of this alignment under perturbation is what matters.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Overlap-Aware Sheaf Repulsion (OASR)** — Augments circuit/sheaf discovery with a penalty term λ_o·L_overlap(R) that penalizes edges already selected in prior discovery runs, using Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation with straight-through estimators. Discovers multiple low-overlap circuits for the same task.
  *Replication cost*: 2-4hr GPU (GPT-2 Small scale); would need fresh forward passes + attention pattern extraction for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires full circuit-discovery infrastructure (edge scoring, computation graph construction) that we don't have, and operates at attention-head/MLP granularity rather than our residual-stream activation level.

- **Repulsive direction probing (our adaptation)** — Inspired by OASR, but applied to linear probes rather than circuits: train probe 1, then train probe 2 with a penalty on cos(w₁, w₂) being high, check whether probe 2 still achieves high AUROC.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (sklearn logistic regression on cached 500×1536 activations, run 5-10 times with orthogonality constraint).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether DoM is unique or one of many directions.

- **Mutual IoU as overlap metric** — Edge-set intersection over union, computed pairwise or as n-way mutual intersection over union across k discovered circuits.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (once we have multiple direction vectors, compute pairwise cosines as the direction-space analogue).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — trivial on cached probe weight vectors.

- **Ultra-sparse circuit isolation** — Finding the minimum edge set that maintains task accuracy, then testing whether removal of any single edge is fatal.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (find minimum feature subset from cached 1536-d activations that maintains AUROC >0.75, test single-feature ablations within that set).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — standard feature selection on cached activations.

## Approaches & framings

- **Functional Anisotropy Hypothesis (negated):** The assumption that LLM tasks map to unique internal mechanisms. The paper refutes this. Intersects our work: we implicitly assume DoM is "the" correctness direction — this framing challenges that.

- **Distributive Dense Circuit Hypothesis:** Task behavior emerges from dense, compositional regimes of competing partially-redundant mechanisms. Provides theoretical grounding for why F-10 (PH null) might be expected: topological summaries of any single realization miss the distributed structure.

- **Mechanism plurality as a feature, not a bug:** Multiple valid explanations should be reported together. Intersects our work: if we find 3+ orthogonal high-AUROC directions, the reporting should characterize the equivalence class, not pick one representative.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation for differentiable edge selection: temperature τ annealed during training
- Straight-through estimators for gradient flow through discrete selection
- λ_o (overlap penalty weight) tuned per task; higher values produce lower-overlap but potentially lower-fidelity circuits
- Code: https://github.com/TonyXiChen/OASR (GPT-2 Small, PyTorch)
- 20-sheaf discovery requires 20× the compute of single-circuit discovery
