# Triage brief — 2602.15293 — The Information Geometry of Softmax: Probing and Steering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15293
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) may be a Euclidean-coordinate artifact, not a true geometric finding.**
   *Mechanism*: Park et al. argue the natural geometry of representations that feed a softmax is information-geometric, not Euclidean. Probe directions are *dual* objects (linear functionals on context vectors), so a Euclidean cosine between two probes measured in primal coordinates can be a "type error" — meaningless for understanding what those directions do mechanistically. Two probes that look orthogonal in primal coordinates can be highly aligned in dual coordinates $\phi(\lambda) = \nabla A(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[\gamma \mid \lambda]$.
   *Falsifiable test*: Apply a logit-lens-style readout at L19 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B unembed), compute the dual transform of `prefill_DoM` and `final_DoM` from the L19 NPZ caches, then recompute the cosine. **Predicts** cos_dual > 0.3, which would re-frame F-3 as "orthogonal in primal coords, partially aligned in dual coords." If cos_dual stays below 0.1, F-3 is geometry-invariant and survives the critique.

2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering) will leak ≥20% probability mass off-target if it adds the probe direction in primal coordinates.**
   *Mechanism*: Their Figure 4 shows Euclidean steering — the literal "add probe direction at scale α" recipe — degrades counterfactual mass on concept-related token pairs by 20–40% even when target probability is driven to ~0.99. Off-target KL grows monotonically with α; rank disruption hits 0.3–0.5. Standard DoM steering (and Pathway 2 spherical steering, and the planned H-1 sweep) is exactly this Euclidean recipe.
   *Falsifiable test*: When H-1 / P10-FE1 runs, instrument it with three controls beyond MATH-500 accuracy: (a) per-position perplexity on a held-out C4 slice with the same intervention applied at L19, (b) topic-drift rate (does the model still answer the math question?), (c) relative KL between baseline-decoded and steered-decoded distributions at the answer token. If degradation tracks Figure 4's Euclidean curve (>20% mass leakage at α large enough to flip an answer), H-1 in its current form is doomed and we need the dual-steering reformulation. If degradation is mild (<5%), our DoM happens to live close to a dual coordinate already (interesting fact about residual streams).

3. **F-2's AUROC 0.7731 is *necessary but not sufficient* for the probe to be the right object for any intervention or routing decision.**
   *Mechanism*: Park et al. demonstrate that primal and dual mean-difference probes both separate concepts at near-perfect accuracy (their Figure 6) — i.e. probe accuracy is essentially invariant to which coordinate system the probe lives in. But intervention behavior is *not* invariant: only the dual-space probe steers cleanly. Implication: F-2's AUROC tells us prefill DoM correlates with correctness; it does *not* license the leap to H-1 (steering), H-19 (verification routing), or any causal claim about the direction.
   *Falsifiable test*: Recompute L19 DoM in dual coordinates (logit-lens projection through Qwen unembed → mean-difference of dual coords) on the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZs, evaluate AUROC. **Predicts** AUROC_dual ≈ AUROC_primal ≈ 0.77 (probe quality coordinate-invariant). If AUROC_dual is materially better (>0.81), F-2's framing under-counts the signal; if dramatically worse, the primal direction has captured something the dual coordinate destroys (signals F-2 is a non-information-geometric phenomenon and the paper doesn't apply).

4. **(Borderline) F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) may dissolve once both are reparameterized in dual coordinates.**
   *Mechanism*: CoE is an aggregate of per-layer Euclidean features; L19 DoM is one slice. In information geometry, these objects live in distinct cotangent spaces and only the dual-transformed versions are directly comparable. Their measured Euclidean redundancy could be a coincidence that disappears once both are projected to the natural geometry.
   *Falsifiable test*: Compute logit-lens dual coords at every layer, recompute CoE-dual, and the partial correlation between CoE-dual and L19-DoM-dual on correctness prediction. If R²_unique(CoE | DoM) jumps from ≈0 (current finding) to >0.05, F-9's redundancy claim is coordinate-conditional.

- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** — Their framework subsumes label-free probing: any probability distribution from a softmax is an element of the same statistical manifold; a label-free criterion that picks out a hyperplane in dual coords would generalize semantic-entropy to arbitrary concepts.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — The paper's argument that probe directions are dual (covectors) suggests the natural attribution target is the unembed/readout, not the residual-stream mean — relevant if H-13 ever needs a circuit-level decomposition.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Dual steering** — Move the *dual coordinate* $\phi(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[\gamma \mid \lambda]$ in the direction of the probe (which lives in dual / cotangent space), then map back to primal $\lambda$ via the inverse Legendre transform of the log-partition $A$. Implemented in their released GitHub repo. The mathematical recipe at the final softmax layer is essentially: solve for $\lambda$ such that $w_W^T \lambda = c$ minimizes $D_{KL}(P_{\lambda_0} \| P_\lambda)$.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU at the *output layer* using cached residuals + Qwen unembed; logit-lens extension to L19 needs ~4h additional. No new data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — necessary control for H-1 / P10-FE1 to disentangle "DoM is a real causal direction" from "we are pushing in primal coords and getting noise."

- **Counterfactual probability-mass metric** — Track the joint mass on concept-related token pairs (e.g. {`he`, `she`}, {`run`, `runs`}) under steering, not the marginal target probability. Catches off-target leakage that single-token marginals hide.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU once a steering experiment runs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in instrumentation for H-1.

- **Concept-factorizable assumption diagnostic** — Their Section 5.3 / Figure 7 shows the optimality result requires $P(W=1 \mid \lambda)$ to be constant along the steering hyperplane. They diagnose this empirically; when it fails, dual steering's advantage shrinks.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU per concept once probe is fit.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would tell us whether MATH correctness is a "concept-factorizable" property of L19 representations or a tangled one.

- **Mean-difference (DoM-equivalent) probe in dual space** — Use $\phi(\lambda)$-coordinates for the difference of class means rather than raw $\lambda$. Authors show this remains an effective separator (their Figure 6).
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct comparison to F-2's primal DoM.

## Approaches & framings

- **The "type error" framing**: a linear probe is a covector / linear functional, not a vector; you don't add a covector to a vector. Reframes a substantial chunk of activation-engineering literature as Euclidean-conflated. Direct intersection with F-3's geometric claim and the entire steering portion of Pathway 2 / 10.

- **Probability-distribution-first vs logit-difference-first**: Park et al. explicitly critique the prior "logit difference / causal inner product" line of work (their own earlier paper, 2310.06824) by noting "logit differences are not probabilities." This matters for our PAPER_INDEX entry on 2310.06824 (currently mass-mean-validating) — the same group is now arguing their old framework is incomplete.

- **Geometry should be derived from how the model uses representations, not from the representations alone**: Our F-1 (dimensional breathing) and F-10 (PH at Gaussian null) are computed in the raw residual stream. Park et al.'s framing implies the *behavior-relevant* geometry is the one induced by the unembed-defined statistical manifold — not the L2 metric in $\mathbb{R}^{1536}$.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- They operate at the **final softmax / unembedding layer only** — explicitly flagged as a limitation (their Section 5 / future work). Extending dual steering to intermediate layers (L19) requires a logit-lens approximation: $A(\lambda) \approx \log \sum_y \exp(W_U \lambda)_y$ with the model's true unembed $W_U$ as the readout proxy.
- **Primary LLM**: Gemma-3-4B; final-layer hidden states; unembed taken directly from softmax weight matrix.
- **Embedding extraction**: 10K sequences × first 256 tokens from C4. Not directly compatible with our 1024-tok regime.
- **Dual-coordinate computation**: $\phi(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[\gamma \mid \lambda] = \sum_y P(y \mid \lambda) \gamma_y = W_U^T \cdot \mathrm{softmax}(W_U \lambda)$. Cheap once forward pass is cached.
- **Theorem 3** is an exact equality, not an asymptotic — the dual-steering trajectory $\hat{\lambda}$ satisfies $\phi(\hat{\lambda}) = \phi(\lambda_0) + t w_W$ exactly under the concept-factorizable assumption.
- **Code**: GitHub release (URL not extracted from abstract page; check paper Appendix or arxiv source).

## Proposed HYPOTHESES.md additions

### H-72: Prefill and final-token DoM are aligned in dual (information-geometric) coordinates despite primal orthogonality
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** Park et al. 2602.15293 + F-3 (cos = 0.046 in primal coords)
**Test:** Compute dual transform $\phi(\lambda) = \mathrm{softmax}(W_U \lambda) W_U$ at L19 using Qwen-2.5-1.5B `lm_head.weight` as the readout. For each cached MATH-500 problem, compute prefill_DoM_dual and final_DoM_dual as differences of class means in dual coords. Recompute cosine. Predicts cos_dual > 0.3 (i.e., directions are aligned in the natural geometry; the 0.046 was a coordinate artifact). Run on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZs. ~3h CPU.
**Requires:** CPU only, cached NPZs, `lm_head.weight` from Qwen-2.5-1.5B (already in HF cache from Pathway 11 extraction).
**Would change:** If cos_dual > 0.3, F-3's "orthogonal directions" headline becomes "orthogonal in primal, aligned in dual" — major reframing of the prefill-vs-final-token story (they encode similar things, but the residual stream stores them in different primal directions). Strengthens information-geometry as the right lens. If cos_dual ≤ 0.1, F-3 is geometry-invariant and the primal-coords critique doesn't apply to our setup; weakens motivation to pursue Park et al.'s framing further.
**Blocks:** P10-FE53 (no point dual-steering if dual coords don't even shift the basic geometry).

### H-73: Dual-coordinate steering causes < 5% off-target perplexity inflation at the alpha that flips MATH-500 K=1 correctness
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** Park et al. 2602.15293 Figure 4 + H-1
**Test:** Run two-arm experiment per P10-FE53. For each arm (primal additive / dual), at the smallest alpha that flips ≥10% of cached "borderline" problems (those near the prefill DoM threshold), measure off-target perplexity on a held-out C4 slice. Predicts: primal arm shows 20–40% perplexity inflation (per their Figure 4); dual arm shows < 5%. If dual arm also shows >10% inflation, MATH correctness is probably not concept-factorizable (cross-ref H-71 / P11-FE73) and Park et al.'s framework is the wrong tool here. If primal arm shows < 5%, our DoM happens to coincide with a dual coordinate (interesting fact about residual streams; suggests a hidden information-geometric alignment in trained transformers worth a separate writeup).
**Requires:** H100 for steering generations (~1 day), CPU for off-target metrics (~4h). Same cache as H-1 / P10-FE1.
**Would change:** If primal degrades and dual is clean, H-1 in its current form is wrong-headed and we should prefer dual steering. If both degrade, Park et al.'s framework doesn't help us. If both are clean, the primal DoM was already a "lucky" coordinate.
**Blocks:** any H-1 follow-on that assumes naive primal steering is the right baseline.

## 2602.15293 — The Information Geometry of Softmax: Probing and Steering (Park, Nief, Choe, Veitch, 2026)

**Relevance:** Provides a theoretical framework arguing that the natural geometry of representations feeding a softmax is information-geometric, not Euclidean, and that linear probe directions are *dual* (covector) objects rather than vectors in the primal residual-stream space. Their core operational claim — Euclidean additive steering causes 20–40% off-target probability mass leakage even when target probability is driven near 1.0 — directly threatens the soundness of H-1 (per-position DoM steering) in its current formulation. Their geometric framing is also a stress test for F-2 (AUROC 0.7731) and F-3 (cos = 0.046), both of which are computed in primal residual-stream coordinates and may be coordinate-conditional.

**Key claim we tested:** "Primal-coordinate additive steering is a type error; the optimal intervention adds the probe direction in dual coordinates ($\phi(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[\gamma \mid \lambda]$), and only the dual recipe avoids off-target mass leakage. Probe accuracy is coordinate-invariant; intervention quality is not."

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Three pre-registered experiments queued: (i) P11-FE72 — coordinate-invariance audit of F-2 and F-3 (cheap, CPU-only, no H100 dependency); (ii) P11-FE73 — concept-factorizability diagnostic to determine whether MATH correctness is even the kind of property the Park et al. framework applies to; (iii) P10-FE53 — full dual-steering control arm for H-1 once H100 is available. Until those run, the relevant F-N framings (F-2, F-3, F-9) carry an unresolved coordinate-system caveat.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P10-FE53, H-72, H-73, H-1, F-2, F-3, F-9.

**Status:** TO TEST (HIGH priority — coordinate audit is cheap and gates several downstream items).
