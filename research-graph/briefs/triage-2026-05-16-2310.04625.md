# Triage brief — 2310.04625 — Copy Suppression: Comprehensively Understanding an Attention Head

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04625
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (L19 prefill DoM predicts correctness) if copy suppression heads at nearby layers are the causal driver.** The paper shows L10H7 in GPT-2 Small writes suppression vectors into the residual stream along unembedding directions, directly modulating the model's confidence on specific tokens. If Qwen-2.5-1.5B has analogous copy suppression heads near L19, our DoM direction might be capturing copy suppression *activity* (which correlates with difficulty/incorrectness because hard problems elicit more naive copying attempts) rather than a genuine "correctness geometry." Mechanism: DoM = shadow of copy suppression head output projections. Falsifiable by: ablating candidate copy-suppression heads in L16–L21 of Qwen-2.5-1.5B and measuring whether the DoM probe's AUROC collapses.

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality is structural) if copy suppression is position-dependent and rotates the suppression direction.** The paper shows copy suppression fires only when a token is *predicted* at the destination (Step 2 of their mechanism: QK circuit gates on logit lens prediction). During prefill, the predicted-token distribution is different from at the final token, meaning the active suppression direction rotates between positions. If orthogonality (cos=0.046) simply reflects that copy suppression operates in different subspaces at prefill vs. final, it's an epiphenomenon of head gating rather than a structural property of the geometry. Falsifiable by: projecting out the unembedding subspace of top-predicted tokens at each position and re-computing cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) — if it increases toward 1.0, the orthogonality was suppression-direction-driven.

3. **Refutes F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM) if self-repair makes the probe unreliable under intervention.** Section 4 of the paper shows that when model components are ablated, downstream heads compensate (39% of self-repair = copy suppression deactivation). A selective-prediction system that *acts* on the DoM signal (by abstaining) effectively removes the model's downstream processing, meaning self-repair circuits will activate and shift the residual stream geometry, potentially making the probe's confidence estimate invalid for the cases where it matters most (borderline problems). Falsifiable by: measuring whether selective-prediction accuracy degrades when we also condition on the magnitude of copy-suppression-like head outputs at L19 — if high-copy-suppression examples are disproportionately misclassified by the probe, self-repair contamination is real.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The copy suppression mechanism shows that individual heads write *signed* logit contributions along specific unembedding vectors in the residual stream. Our DoM direction at L19 might partially capture the net copy-suppression signal (negative = more suppression = harder problem = more likely incorrect). The 0.7731 AUROC could partly reflect copy suppression intensity rather than an abstract "correctness direction."

- **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent):** Copy suppression is content-dependent by construction — it fires only for tokens that appear earlier in context. This aligns with F-5's claim that breathing isn't a mechanical artifact. However, the copy suppression account would predict that breathing magnitude correlates with token repetition density in the prompt, not with problem difficulty per se.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM):** If copy suppression accumulates across layers (L10H7 → L11H10 in GPT-2 Small, with cascading deactivation), trajectory features (CoE-60) would naturally be redundant with a single late layer that sums up all prior copy suppression. This is consistent with but doesn't independently confirm F-9.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering):** Copy suppression's self-repair property (Section 4) is directly relevant to H-1. Steering by adding the DoM direction would be partially undone by downstream copy suppression heads that detect the resulting overconfident prediction and suppress it. This predicts H-1 will show diminishing returns — copy suppression is literally an anti-steering mechanism.

- **H-4 (breathing ↔ sharpness dimension):** The paper's Appendix D shows copy suppression heads are the primary entropy-increasing components. If "dimensional breathing" (F-1) is driven by entropy-modulating heads, then the sharpness/EoS correspondence (H-4) might be mediated by copy suppression circuits rather than being a geometric primitive.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Copy Suppression-Preserving Ablation (CSPA)** — Ablation that removes all functionality from a head *except* the mechanistically-understood copy suppression pathway (OV projection onto unembeddings + QK filtering for top-5% logit-lens-predicted tokens). Measures % of KL divergence preserved.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h GPU (requires forward passes through Qwen-2.5-1.5B with head-level interventions).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires fresh model inference, not applicable to cached activations.

- **OV circuit decomposition (W_U × W_OV × W_E)** — Decomposes what an attention head writes to the residual stream per source token, measuring whether diagonal elements (same-token suppression) dominate.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (just weight matrix multiplication, no forward pass needed if we have model weights).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could check whether Qwen-2.5-1.5B L16–L21 heads have OV circuits with negative diagonals (indicating copy suppression candidates near our L19 extraction layer).

- **QK circuit analysis (W_U × W_QK × W_E)** — Checks whether attention patterns are gated by the predicted-token matching the context token.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (weight matrix operations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — identifies which heads near L19 could be implementing copy suppression and thus influencing our DoM direction.

- **Overconfidence metric via calibration curve** — L2 inner product between model's calibration curve deviation and a perfectly-overconfident reference, decomposed per attention head.
  *Replication cost*: 1h GPU (requires forward passes with per-head ablations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — needs fresh inference.

- **Logit lens intermediate-prediction measurement** — Using the unembedding matrix to decode residual stream states at intermediate layers into token probabilities.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (just matrix multiply cached L19 activations × W_U, if we have W_U).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could decode our cached L19 prefill activations into token-prediction space and check whether DoM aligns with the "most-predicted-token" direction in unembedding space.

## Approaches & framings

- **Self-repair as mechanistic obstacle to intervention.** The copy suppression → self-repair loop means that any probe-based intervention (steering, gating) will be partially compensated by downstream circuits. This reframes H-1 (DoM steering) from "will it move accuracy?" to "can it overcome self-repair?"

- **Calibration as the primary function, not correctness prediction.** The paper frames copy suppression as improving *calibration* (reducing overconfidence) rather than improving accuracy. This reframes our DoM probe: it may be detecting calibration quality (well-calibrated → more likely correct) rather than some deeper "correctness representation."

- **Component-level completeness claims.** Their 76.9% "explained behavior" methodology (CSPA) offers a template for asking "how much of the DoM signal is explained by a specific mechanistic account?" — e.g., "how much of AUROC 0.7731 is explained by copy suppression head activity?"

## Implementation details worth capturing

- TransformerLens library used for all experiments (weight processing options enabled for simplified LayerNorm).
- "Effective embedding" = MLP0(W_E) rather than raw W_E, because subtracting W_E from the residual stream after MLP0 *decreases* loss in GPT-2 Small.
- CSPA QK filtering threshold: top 5% of logit-lens-predicted tokens kept, rest mean-ablated.
- OV projection: project result vectors onto unembedding of source token, keep only negative components.
- Anti-induction heads = negative induction score heads; strong correlation between copy-suppression score and negative induction score across architectures.
- Code: interactive web app at https://copy-suppression.streamlit.app/ (visualization only, not replication code).
