# Triage brief — 2605.16928 — Full Attention Strikes Back: Transferring Full Attention into Sparse within Hundred Training Steps

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16928
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) could be an artifact of retrieval-head concentration, not a genuine correctness signal.**
   The paper shows that only ~15% of attention heads are "retrieval heads" while the rest are "local heads" operating within a sliding window. If the L19 DoM direction is dominated by contributions from this small retrieval-head subset, then the AUROC 0.7731 correctness signal might be explained by whether the model successfully retrieved problem-relevant tokens — a retrieval success/failure signal rather than a geometric correctness predictor. Falsifiable: decompose L19 prefill activations by head contribution (using the model's MHA/GQA structure), compute DoM restricted to retrieval-head vs local-head subspaces, and compare AUROCs. If retrieval-head-only DoM matches or exceeds 0.7731 while local-head DoM is at chance, F-2's interpretation as "dimensional breathing" is better explained as retrieval-head gating.

2. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM) may break under head-type-aware decomposition.**
   RTPurbo demonstrates that attention heads have fundamentally different roles (retrieval vs local) that are stable across inputs. If CoE-60 trajectory features and L19 DoM are both dominated by retrieval-head signals, their apparent redundancy (F-9) could be an artifact of both picking up the same retrieval-head concentration pattern. However, if they capture different head-type mixtures — e.g., CoE-60 aggregates both retrieval and local dynamics while L19 DoM is retrieval-dominated — then head-type conditioning could break the redundancy. Falsifiable: partition CoE-60 trajectory features by head type, recompute the CoE-60 vs L19 DoM correlation, and check if the redundancy holds within each partition.

3. **F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) may reflect a retrieval-head vs local-head phase transition rather than a structural geometric property.**
   The paper's finding that retrieval heads concentrate in later layers while local heads dominate early/mid layers suggests a natural partition where prefill attention patterns (which engage retrieval heads for long-range lookup) produce a different activation geometry than final-token patterns (which may shift to local-head dominance as generation proceeds). The near-zero cosine between prefill and final DoM could be explained by this head-type phase transition rather than by an intrinsic "breathing" geometry. Falsifiable: for Qwen-2.5-1.5B, identify retrieval vs local heads at L19, project prefill and final DoM vectors into the retrieval-head subspace alone, and measure their cosine. If cos is substantially higher than 0.046 within the retrieval-head subspace, orthogonality is an artifact of mixing two functionally distinct head populations.

4. **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) could be reframed as retrieval success probability.**
   If the prefill DoM is driven by retrieval-head behavior, then what H-5 hypothesizes as "problem familiarity" is better understood as the model's ability to retrieve relevant parametric knowledge via its retrieval heads. The "decomposability" axis would correspond to how many retrieval heads activate (diffuse vs concentrated retrieval). This is testable: problems where retrieval heads show concentrated attention (few tokens, high attention mass) should correlate with high DoM and correct answers, while diffuse retrieval should correlate with low DoM and errors.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's retrieval-head identification method (needle-based calibration, retrieval score R_h) provides a direct tool to test whether the L19 prefill DoM direction aligns with the retrieval-head subspace. The 15% retrieval head ratio is a concrete anchor — if ~15% of Qwen-2.5-1.5B's heads at L19 are retrieval heads, their contribution to the 1536-d residual stream is a testable component of DoM.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal):** The paper's finding that head specialization is "input-agnostic" and "highly stable" across documents parallels F-1's universality claim. Both suggest the existence of stable internal structure independent of input content. However, the mechanisms are different: F-1 describes dimensional expansion/contraction, while RTPurbo describes functional head partitioning.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature):** The dynamic top-p vs fixed top-k analysis is directly relevant. D-bucket (problems near the decision boundary) may correspond to queries with intermediate retrieval patterns — neither concentrated nor diffuse — which the paper shows are exactly where fixed sparsification strategies fail.

- **H-64/H-525 (DoM concentrated in sparse head circuit):** The paper's retrieval-head identification methodology is directly applicable to testing whether DoM signal is concentrated in a sparse set of heads, which is exactly what these hypotheses predict.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse at final token):** The paper's observation that retrieval heads use full attention during prefill but can be sparsified during decode suggests that the "asymmetric collapse" may correspond to a transition from retrieval-head-dominated (prefill) to local-head-dominated (final token) processing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Needle-based retrieval head identification** — Insert identical needle spans at beginning and end of a long document; compute retrieval score R_h as mean attention mass from later to earlier needle across all query heads. Rank heads by R_h to partition into retrieval (top ~15%) and local sets.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (single forward pass with attention output on a long sequence, then score computation). For 1.5B model this is cheap.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests H-64/H-525 and informs F-2 interpretation. Needs `output_attentions=True` on a single long input.

- **Low-dimensional pre-RoPE projection for token scoring** — Train r=16 dimensional linear projections W^Q, W^K on pre-RoPE query/key representations to approximate full attention scores. KL divergence loss between full and projected attention distributions. ~600 steps, ~30M tokens.
  *Replication cost*: 2-4h H100 (training projections for 1.5B model).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — primarily an efficiency technique, not a probing/correctness methodology. Low ROI for our questions.

- **Dynamic top-p token selection** — Replace fixed top-k with query-dependent top-p (p=0.9) selection on projected attention scores. Uses histogram-based O(1) memory implementation.
  *Replication cost*: implementation effort ~2h, but the core idea is trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — inference efficiency optimization, not a correctness signal method.

- **Head-wise per-layer sparsity profiling** — Measure per-head active token counts across different context lengths and query types to reveal ">three orders of magnitude" variance between concentrated and diffuse heads.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2h H100 (forward passes with attention outputs across varied inputs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the variance characterization directly informs whether DoM is driven by a few high-variance heads.

## Approaches & framings

- **Retrieval vs local head dichotomy as fundamental architectural feature.** The paper frames head specialization not as a learned soft preference but as a stable, input-agnostic binary partition. This reframes our "dimensional breathing" as potentially arising from the interplay of two fundamentally different computation modes within the same layer, rather than from a unified geometric process.

- **Pre-RoPE subspace as the "true" semantic space.** The paper's key insight is that retrieval operates in a low-dimensional subspace of pre-RoPE representations (r=16 captures >90% recall). This is relevant because our DoM vectors are computed on post-RoPE residual streams. If the correctness signal lives in a low-dimensional pre-RoPE subspace, we may be capturing it inefficiently through the full 1536-d residual.

- **Query-dependent sparsity as an intrinsic model property.** The 5× variance in active token counts between concentrated vs diffuse queries suggests the model has an internal "confidence in retrieval" signal. This intersects with F-2: DoM might be reading out this retrieval confidence.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Retrieval head identification requires only a single long sequence with duplicated "needle" spans — minimal calibration cost.
- Head partitioning on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B: 210 of 1,536 query heads (13.7%) are retrieval heads, concentrated in the latter half of layers.
- Low-dim projection: r=16 is optimal (r=4 and r=32 tested; r=16 best sparsity/accuracy tradeoff per Table 12).
- The 85%/15% retrieval/local split is claimed to be stable across models in the Qwen3 family — potentially transferable to Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
- Stage 1 trains only ~860K parameters (projections); Stage 2 fine-tunes the full model at very low LR (3e-6).
- Code/weights: not explicitly linked in the paper; no public repo URL found.
