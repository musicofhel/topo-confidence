# Triage brief — 2405.12250 — Your Transformer is Secretly Linear

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12250
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 is the unique peak) vs. paper's "many decoder layers are 0.99 Procrustes-linear and prunable."**
   - Mechanism: If consecutive transformer-decoder layers are linked by a near-perfect linear map A (Procrustes ≈ 0.99) and individual block contributions have *consistently low output norm* (the paper's Figure 3 — the residual stream barely moves between layers), then a probe direction discovered at L19 should be retrievable at L18 and L20 by composing with A. That predicts a *plateau* of AUROC across the middle layers, not the sharp single-layer peak F-2 records (final-token L19 AUROC = 0.7186; prefill L19 AUROC = 0.7731 — a 5.5pt gap that can't be reconstructed by a near-linear remap if both positions evolve through the same near-linear stack).
   - Falsifiable by a concrete experiment: per-layer prefill DoM AUROC sweep on the existing P11 H100 NPZs across all 28 Qwen-2.5-1.5B layers. If L18, L19, L20 form a flat plateau (Δ ≤ 0.01 AUROC), the paper wins and F-2 must be restated as "L19 is one of many equivalent middle layers." If L19 is a sharp peak (Δ ≥ 0.03 to L18 and L20), the correctness-discriminative subspace lives in the *non-linear residual* of the 0.99-Procrustes fit, contradicting the paper's "linear blocks are prunable" framing for any subspace that carries task-relevant signal.

2. **F-1 / F-5 (dimensional breathing is universal *and* content-dependent) vs. paper's "block contributions have low norm; main stream linearity is dominated by the residual."**
   - Mechanism: The paper explicitly notes (§3.1, Figure 1) that linearity score *drops sharply* once you subtract the residual component — i.e. the apparent inter-layer "structure" we measure on the full residual stream is largely the slow-drift accumulated input embedding, not active per-layer reorganization. If true, the participation-ratio / dimensional-breathing curves used in F-1 (and the content-dependence in F-5) are at risk of being a Frobenius-norm artifact: PCA on a sequence of vectors that barely change between layers will trivially show "smooth breathing" without any layer doing real work.
   - Falsifiable: re-extract the breathing curve on Qwen-2.5-1.5B P11 NPZs after subtracting `residual_stream[L_{i-1}]` from `residual_stream[L_i]` (the paper's recipe — Figure 1 right-panel). Expected outcome under our F-1: residual-removed breathing is *still* content-dependent and still cross-model-universal. Expected outcome under paper's framing: residual-removed breathing collapses toward Marchenko-Pastur null (which would also fold cleanly into P11-FE16 / H-16). A null result here would reduce F-1 to "the residual stream drifts on a low-dim subspace" — true but weaker, and would force F-5 to be re-tested on the residual-removed signal.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) vs. paper's "feature triggering regime — sparse non-linearity spikes on a few tokens drive model behavior" (§3.1, Figure 9 — long-tailed L2 error distribution in OPT-1.3B).**
   - Mechanism: The paper proposes that *almost all* decoder layer-to-layer maps are linear, but a sparse minority of tokens carry high-non-linearity spikes that are causally important. If D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong — pathological correctness pattern) is the F-7 fingerprint of those rare-but-load-bearing non-linear events, then D-bucket prefill activations should sit in the long tail of layer-linearization L2 error, while A-bucket / B-bucket should sit near the mode. That would *unify* F-7's collective signature with the paper's "feature triggering" hypothesis. Conversely, if D-bucket prefill L2 error is statistically indistinguishable from A-bucket (Mann-Whitney p > 0.05), then either F-7's collective geometry isn't the same phenomenon the paper describes, or the paper's "feature triggering" framing doesn't apply to math-reasoning models — refuting one or both.
   - Falsifiable: fit per-layer linear A on the 1024-tok P11 prefill NPZs (matrix lstsq, 20min CPU); compute per-problem L2 residual at L18→L19 and L19→L20; compare D-bucket (n=36) vs A-bucket (n=207) distributions.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 at L19)** — Paper's 0.99 inter-layer Procrustes is *consistent* with F-3, not in tension: position-orthogonality at one layer is preserved through near-linear inter-layer maps, so the paper *predicts* that cos(prefill_DoM_Lk, final_DoM_Lk) should remain ≈ 0.046 across nearby layers k ∈ {17, 18, 19, 20, 21}. Cross-layer DoM-orthogonality robustness is a free side-experiment.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Strong corroboration. The paper supplies the *mechanism* for F-9's redundancy: if every consecutive-layer map is 0.99-Procrustes, then a 60-dim trajectory feature CoE-60 is at most 60 near-affine images of a single direction, which is exactly why a single L19 readout matches it. Connection should be promoted to a CORROBORATED_BY edge in the graph.
- **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null)** — Mechanism connection. Near-linear inter-layer maps preserve gross geometry; if input token embeddings are approximately Gaussian on a low-dim subspace, near-linear propagation through 28 layers preserves that — so PH being indistinguishable from a Gaussian null is the *expected* outcome of the paper's framing, not an independent finding. CORROBORATED_BY.
- **H-10 (breathing pattern changes after fine-tuning)** — Direct prediction collision: paper's Table 1 says fine-tuning *increases* layer linearity universally (positive Δ for all of {OPT-125M, OPT-1.3B, OPT-2.7B, Llama2-7B, GPT2 family}). Therefore breathing measured on a base model vs. its instruction-tuned variant should *decrease* in amplitude (more linear ⇒ less reorganization). Pre-registers a directional prediction for H-10.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed)** — Strong support, not refutation. If 99% of the layer→layer transformation is captured by a single linear A, then DoM rotation across layers is plausibly *identical* to RoPE's rotation matrix R(θ_layer) for the relevant subspace. H-17 becomes testable directly: compare the lstsq-fit A matrix at each layer to the local RoPE rotation; if cos-similarity ≥ 0.9, H-17 is supported by this paper.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Sharpens the test. Paper's "feature triggering regime" + sparse non-linearity spikes (§3.1) predicts that prefill-vs-final circuit difference should localize to a *small number of attention heads* whose outputs are the rare non-linear events; H-13 then narrows to ~3-10 heads rather than circuit-wide attribution.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Generalized Procrustes linearity score** — `linearity_score(X,Y) = 1 − min_A ||X̃A − Ỹ||²` over all linear A (not just orthogonal), with `X̃ = X/||X||_F`. Bounded in [0,1], scale-invariant.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached P11 NPZs (lstsq per layer pair).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly probes whether F-2's L19-uniqueness is a non-linearity peak, and gives layer-pair scores we can correlate with bucket membership for F-7.

- **Block output norm tracking** — measure `||block_i(x)|| / ||residual_i||` per layer per token (paper's Figure 3).
  *Replication cost*: minutes on cached P11 NPZs (we already have residual streams, just need to subtract).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required as the residual-removal control for refutation #2 and a free F-1 sanity check.

- **L2-error-by-token distribution after linear approximation** — fit best A per layer-pair, compute per-token L2 residual `||x_i A − y_i||`, look at the histogram tail (paper's Figure 9).
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the test for refutation #3, and operationalizes the paper's "feature triggering regime" against our D-bucket.

- **Sequential-linearity-ranked depth pruning** — rank layers by linearity_score, drop top-K most-linear in order, measure perplexity / downstream metric.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2h CPU per pruning level on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with cached MATH-500 eval; mostly inference time.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes (with caveats) — gives a direct test of whether F-2's signal survives layer pruning, but at the cost of running fresh generation.

- **Linear-replacement + layerwise MSE distillation** — replace pruned layer with learned linear A, train on TinyStories with MSE on layerwise embeddings as auxiliary loss.
  *Replication cost*: H100 days; needs new training run.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — not aligned with the project's read-only-extraction methodology and the cost is high relative to information yield.

- **Cosine / MSE inter-layer regularization at pretraining** — `L_cos = λ(1 − cos(emb_i, emb_{i−1}))` as auxiliary loss during pretraining.
  *Replication cost*: H100 weeks (full pretraining run).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — out of scope, we don't pretrain.

- **Linear probing across all layers** — train a linear classifier on each layer's embedding vs. label and read off per-layer accuracy curve (paper Figure 5).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already partially what F-2's per-layer AUROC sweep does; paper formalizes the protocol and gives a comparison point.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Residual stream dominates; blocks barely contribute"** — reframes the standard "deep transformer = many layers of computation" picture into "deep transformer = single near-linear track with sparse high-non-linearity spikes." Intersects F-1 (we may be measuring the residual stream's slow drift, not breathing-as-computation), F-9 (explains why single-layer = trajectory-features), and F-10 (Gaussian null on PH falls out naturally).
- **"Feature triggering regime"** — the paper's hypothesis that *rare* token positions carry the model's actual non-linearity load. This is a near-perfect mechanistic candidate for F-7 (D-bucket = pathological-but-causally-load-bearing problems) and for F-2's prefill-token specialness (the prefill last token is one such trigger).
- **"Pretraining decreases linearity, fine-tuning increases linearity"** (Figure 2 + Table 1) — predicts that base→instruct conversion *flattens* dimensional breathing. Directly applicable to H-10.
- **"Procrustes generalized to all linear maps as a similarity score"** — a methodologically clean way to compare residual-stream geometries that is bounded, scale-invariant, and weaker than orthogonal-Procrustes; useful for cross-model alignment work in H-20.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/AIRI-Institute/LLM-Microscope (paper's reference impl).
- Models tested: GPT2 (small/large/XL), OPT (125M/1.3B/2.7B), LLaMA2-7B, BLOOM, Mistral 150M/650M (their own pretraining).
- Linearity-score normalization: Frobenius `||·||_F` on centered embedding matrices `X, Y ∈ R^{n×d}`, then lstsq on the normalized pair.
- "Linearity profile" is layer-index (normalized to [0,1]) on x-axis vs linearity score on y-axis — middle layers are most linear in their plots.
- Block-output norm vs residual-stream norm ratio (Figure 3) is the diagnostic for whether observed inter-layer "linearity" is genuine layer linearity or just residual-stream slow-drift dominance — important confound to control for when comparing to F-1.
- Cosine regularization coefficient: λ=0.5 worked best for both 150M and 650M Mistral; λ=2.0 (MSE) destabilized.
- Reward-model fine-tuning showed the same linearity-increase trend more *stably* across seeds than NLI fine-tuning (Table 1).
- L2-error long tails: Figure 9 (OPT-1.3B) shows a small fraction of layers / tokens with much larger linear-approximation errors — the "spike" tokens that carry non-linearity.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2405.12250 — Your Transformer is Secretly Linear (Razzhigaev, Mikhalchuk, Goncharova, Gerasimenko, Oseledets, Dimitrov, Kuznetsov; AIRI/Skoltech/SberAI/HSE/MSU, 2024)

**Relevance:** Direct mechanistic context for F-1 (dimensional breathing universality), F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC = 0.7731), F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with single-layer DoM), and F-10 (PH at Gaussian null). The paper claims transformer-decoder layer-to-layer embedding maps have generalized-Procrustes similarity ≈ 0.99 across GPT, LLaMA, OPT, BLOOM and Mistral, with consistently low block-output norms relative to residual-stream norm. Two implications collide with our finding stack: (i) if true, F-2's L19 peak should be a plateau across the linear middle layers — testable via per-layer AUROC sweep; (ii) F-1's breathing signal may be dominated by slow residual-stream drift rather than active per-layer reorganization — testable by residual-removal control. The paper's "feature triggering regime" (Figure 9 long-tailed L2 error distribution) is also a candidate mechanism for F-7's D-bucket fragility.

**Key claim we tested:** Decoder-only transformers have near-perfectly linear inter-layer embedding maps (Procrustes 0.99) and consistently low per-block output norms; therefore middle layers are largely interchangeable / prunable, and the actual non-linear "computation" lives in a sparse minority of triggering tokens.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — the paper triggers five concrete cheap experiments (P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75). The Procrustes/linearity protocol is directly applicable to our cached P11 NPZs and the paper offers a clean mechanism for H-17 (RoPE-mechanical DoM rotation) and F-9 (CoE redundancy via near-affine inter-layer maps). Until the per-layer AUROC sweep and residual-removed breathing curve are run, we cannot say whether F-1 and F-2 are genuine non-linear features or measurement artifacts of the residual-stream slow drift the paper describes.

### Methodologies extracted

- Generalized Procrustes linearity score: `linearity_score(X,Y) = 1 − min_A ||X̃A − Ỹ||²` over all linear A on Frobenius-normalized embeddings; bounded [0,1], scale-invariant.
- Block output norm vs residual-stream norm tracking (Figure 3) as a diagnostic for whether observed inter-layer "linearity" is genuine or just residual-dominance.
- Per-token L2-error distribution after linear approximation (Figure 9) to identify rare non-linearity-spike tokens.
- Sequential-linearity-ranked depth pruning + linear-replacement + layerwise MSE distillation (depth-pruning recipe).
- Inter-layer cosine / MSE regularization at pretraining (loss terms `L_cos` and `L_MSE` with coefficient λ).

### Approaches & framings

- "Residual stream dominates; blocks barely contribute" — reframes deep transformers as a single near-linear track plus sparse non-linearity events.
- "Feature triggering regime" — rare specific tokens carry the model's actual non-linearity load; candidate mechanism for F-7 D-bucket fragility.
- Pretraining decreases linearity, fine-tuning increases linearity (Table 1) — directional prediction for H-10.
- Procrustes generalized to all linear maps (not just orthogonal) as a similarity score — methodologically clean, useful for cross-model alignment work in H-20.

### Datasets

- TinyStories, Tiny-textbooks, SuperGLUE (MultiRC, BoolQ, CB, COPA, RTE, WiC, ReCoRD, XStoryCloze), Anthropic-Helpful (RM), WikiText, ARC-easy. None directly applicable to MATH-500 / 1024-tok regime; ARC-easy marginally close.

### Implementation details

- Reference impl: https://github.com/AIRI-Institute/LLM-Microscope
- Models tested: GPT2 (small/large/XL), OPT-125M/1.3B/2.7B, LLaMA2-7B, BLOOM, Mistral 150M/650M (their own pretraining).
- Cosine regularization sweet spot: λ=0.5; MSE λ=2.0 destabilized training.
- Reward-model fine-tuning showed more seed-stable linearity-increase than NLI fine-tuning (Table 1).
