# Triage brief — 2606.01249 — Trust Region On-Policy Distillation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01249
Triaged: 2026-06-06

## Refutations

**R1: Against F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) — TrOPD-distilled checkpoints may shift or destroy the DoM direction, exposing F-2 as checkpoint-fragile rather than architecture-robust.**

TrOPD trains the student on trust-region-filtered tokens, explicitly reshaping the student's internal distribution to match teacher-verifiable regions. If a TrOPD-distilled Qwen-2.5-1.5B has a substantially different L19 DoM direction (low cosine similarity to the original) or altered AUROC, then F-2's 0.7731 is a property of the *specific DeepSeek-distilled checkpoint*, not a robust feature of the Qwen-2.5-1.5B architecture. The mechanism: trust-region filtering selectively suppresses gradient updates from tokens where teacher/student distributions diverge — exactly the tokens where incorrect reasoning may live — potentially erasing the geometric separation that DoM relies on.
- *Falsifiable by:* TrOPD-distill 7B→1.5B on MATH-500 prompts, re-extract L19 prefill, fit DoM, compare AUROC and cos(new_DoM, original_DoM). If cos < 0.3 and AUROC changes by >0.05, F-2 is checkpoint-specific.

**R2: Against F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics) — TrOPD's distributional mismatch metric implies breathing amplitude may track policy divergence, not content understanding.**

TrOPD's central insight is that the ratio πT(x)/πS(x) — the teacher-student probability agreement at each token — determines supervision reliability. If dimensional breathing amplitude across positions correlates with this ratio (large breathing at tokens where the student's policy diverges from the teacher's), then F-5's "content-dependent" label is misleading: breathing would instead reflect *how far the model's current policy strays from a stronger policy at that token*, a training-dynamics artifact rather than a content-encoding signal. The mechanism fires because TrOPD explicitly partitions tokens by this ratio and shows it matters for learning.
- *Falsifiable by:* For each MATH-500 problem, compute per-position log(π_7B / π_1.5B) at each token, correlate with per-position participation ratio from cached activations. If r > 0.5, breathing tracks policy mismatch, not content.

**R3: Against F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage yields 71.6% accuracy) — TrOPD's +3.34-point math gains via better distillation could render DoM-based selective prediction practically unnecessary.**

TrOPD lifts DeepSeek-Qwen2.5-1.5B from 28.64→38.54 on AIME 2024 and from 75.39→78.51 on AMC 2023 — substantial gains from training method alone. If applied to MATH-500, the baseline accuracy would rise from our 48.6%, shrinking the headroom that selective prediction exploits. At higher base accuracy, the coverage/accuracy tradeoff in F-8 becomes less compelling: a model that gets 55%+ right on 100% of problems may beat one that gets 71.6% right on only 50%. The mechanism: TrOPD directly improves the student's reasoning by stabilizing distillation, rather than routing around its failures.
- *Falsifiable by:* Run TrOPD-distilled 1.5B on MATH-500, measure raw accuracy. If accuracy ≥ 58%, the full-coverage accuracy exceeds the 50%-coverage selective accuracy of 71.6% × 0.5 = 35.8% effective, and F-8's practical value is diminished relative to simply training better.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** TrOPD uses the same Qwen-2.5-1.5B architecture as our student model. The distillation procedure fundamentally alters internal representations. The trust-region criterion (πT/πS) is a per-token confidence signal that may be orthogonal to, or competitive with, the geometric DoM signal. Whether a TrOPD-distilled checkpoint preserves or destroys the 0.7731 AUROC directly tests the robustness of F-2.

- **F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046):** TrOPD's off-policy guidance mechanism generates hybrid sequences: teacher-generated prefix + student-generated continuation. This directly probes whether the "prefill" geometry depends on *who generated the prefix*. If teacher-prefixed student continuations have L19 geometry closer to teacher's final-token geometry than student's, F-3's orthogonality is partially a *policy identity* effect rather than a structural phase transition.

- **H-11 (big-to-small 7B→1.5B DoM distillation):** TrOPD provides a concrete, state-of-the-art distillation recipe for Qwen-2.5-1.5B that outperforms vanilla OPD by +3.06 on math tasks. H-11's feasibility depends on whether distillation preserves the DoM signal — TrOPD is the best available candidate recipe to test this.

- **H-366 (distillation transfers correctness behavior but not the prefill DoM direction):** TrOPD's trust-region mechanism selectively suppresses gradients from distribution-mismatch tokens. If these are the same tokens that encode the DoM direction, H-366's prediction (behavior transfers, direction doesn't) becomes more likely. TrOPD provides the missing controlled distillation recipe that H-366 needs.

- **H-367 (prefill direction stability under fine-tuning):** TrOPD produces a third fine-tuning variant (beyond SFT and RevKD) to compare DoM directions across training regimes. A TrOPD checkpoint would extend H-367's pairwise cos(DoM_a, DoM_b) analysis.

- **F-8 (selective prediction, 71.6% at 50% coverage):** TrOPD's math gains (+3.06 avg) demonstrate that better distillation can directly raise accuracy. The practical value proposition of F-8's selective pipeline depends on TrOPD-class methods *not* being available — if they are, the simpler approach of training better may dominate.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Trust-region token partitioning via speculative-decoding acceptance probability** — For each student-generated token x, compute P_trust(x) = min(πT(x)/πS(x), 1); tokens with high acceptance are in the trust region, others are outliers. Apply reverse KL in trust region, forward KL in outlier region.
  *Replication cost*: Requires teacher+student forward passes on same sequences; ~2h H100 for MATH-500 at 1024 tokens.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have a distillation pipeline, and this is a training-time method.

- **Adaptive outlier estimation with top-k forward KL** — For outlier tokens (where πT(x)/πS(x) ≪ 1), compute FKL over teacher's top-k vocabulary instead of masking entirely. Recovers informative signal from distribution-mismatch regions without destabilizing gradients.
  *Replication cost*: Part of TrOPD training loop; ~1 day H100 for full distillation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training-time technique, not applicable to our inference-time analysis.

- **Off-policy guidance with cosine-annealed teacher prefix** — Student continues generation from teacher-generated prefixes; prefix length anneals from max to zero during training. Uses K1-estimated forward KL for the teacher-prefix portion.
  *Replication cost*: Part of TrOPD training loop; ~1 day H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training-time technique, but the *concept* of hybrid teacher-prefix/student-continuation is testable as an activation-extraction experiment for F-3.

- **K1 reverse-KL estimator for memory-efficient token-level distillation** — Estimates reverse KL using only the sampled token's probability ratio log(πS(x)/πT(x)), achieving O(n) memory instead of O(nk) for full-vocabulary KL.
  *Replication cost*: Trivial to implement if you have both model logits.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the K1 ratio log(π_7B(x)/π_1.5B(x)) per token is a cheap feature we could extract alongside hidden states in a future GPU pass to test whether it complements DoM.

## Approaches & framings

- **Supervision reliability as the bottleneck in on-policy learning.** TrOPD reframes distillation failure not as "student is too weak" but as "teacher's supervision is unreliable in student-explored regions." This intersects our F-2/H-11 framing: when we ask whether the 7B DoM direction transfers to 1.5B, TrOPD suggests the transfer quality depends on *where in distribution space the distillation happens*, not just on the final checkpoint.

- **Speculative-decoding acceptance as a trust criterion.** Using min(πT/πS, 1) as a Bernoulli acceptance probability draws a direct parallel to speculative decoding. For our work, this suggests a diagnostic: at inference time, tokens where the 7B model would "accept" the 1.5B's generation (πT ≥ πS) might be exactly the tokens where L19 DoM is most predictive — a cheap test of whether DoM's correctness signal aligns with cross-model agreement.

- **Three-region optimization (trust / outlier / off-policy).** Partitioning the token stream into three learning regimes by supervision quality. For our hidden-state analysis, this suggests an analogous partitioning: trust-region tokens might show different L19 geometry than outlier tokens, providing a finer-grained decomposition of F-5's content-dependence finding.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Student models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-SFT-1.7B (both ~1.5B params, same scale as our Qwen-2.5-1.5B).
- Teacher models: Skywork-OR1-Math-7B (single-domain), Skywork-OR1-7B (multi-domain), Qwen3-Nemotron-4B.
- Training: 200 steps, LR 5×10⁻⁶ fixed, batch size 128 prompts × 4 rollouts, max generation 8096 tokens.
- Top-k for FKL: k=64 vocabulary items from teacher distribution.
- Off-policy guidance coefficient: β=0.001 for imitation learning.
- Off-policy prefix length: cosine-annealed from max to 0 during training.
- Code: https://github.com/Xingrun-Xing2/TrOPD/tree/main (public).
- TrOPD FKL variant is best overall; TrOPD Mask and TrOPD Clip are ablation alternatives.
- Concurrent work AOPD (2605.06387) is orthogonal and combinable (+1.04 points when combined).

## 2606.01249 — Trust Region On-Policy Distillation (Xing, Wang, Gao, Li, Tang, 2026)

**Relevance:** TrOPD proposes a trust-region-based on-policy distillation framework for reasoning LLMs that partitions student-generated tokens by teacher-student distributional agreement. We have not tested this directly, but the trust-region concept maps to a testable question in our framework: does cross-model agreement predict correctness independently of DoM?
