# Triage brief — 2604.25783 — Subliminal Steering: Stronger Encoding of Hidden Signals

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25783
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

**R1 — F-2 (AUROC 0.7731) may be detecting a subliminal training artifact, not a genuine correctness representation.**
The paper demonstrates that fine-tuning on data generated by a steered teacher model transfers the steering vector itself into the student's hidden states (cos(v_r, v_c) > 0.5 reliably). Qwen-2.5-1.5B underwent extensive post-training (SFT + RLHF/DPO). If the post-training pipeline acted as a "teacher" that injected difficulty-awareness or correctness-related biases via steered or preference-tuned data, the L19 DoM direction we detect could be a recovered artifact of that training-time injection rather than an emergent property of the model's reasoning. The paper's vector recovery protocol could be run on Qwen's post-training data (if available) to test whether a direction with cos > 0.5 to our DoM is recoverable from the training signal alone — which would reframe F-2 from "the model knows when it's right" to "the model inherited a difficulty-estimator from its training pipeline."
*Falsification*: Apply vector recovery (Section 6) to Qwen-2.5-1.5B base (pre-SFT) vs instruct on MATH-500 prompts. If DoM direction is absent in base but present in instruct with high alignment to a hypothetical SFT steering direction, F-2 is a training artifact. If DoM appears in base model too, this refutation fails.

**R2 — H-1 / H-659: The predict-steer discrepancy is expected because activation addition is the wrong intervention modality.**
The paper shows that subliminal steering (training-time vector injection → fine-tuning) transfers behavioral biases far more reliably than prompt-based or inference-time methods. Our H-1 proposes per-position DoM steering via inference-time activation addition (ActAdd-style). The paper's core result — that the same vector direction is readable from hidden states but only reliably writable via training-time intervention — would explain why H-659 ("DoM detects but doesn't steer") holds. This doesn't refute F-2 (detection), but would refute the premise that H-1's failure implies DoM is non-causal. Instead, the mechanism is: DoM is causal but only modulable through fine-tuning-mediated transfer, not through inference-time additive injection.
*Falsification*: Compare inference-time L19 DoM steering (H-1) with fine-tuning-mediated steering where LoRA adapters are trained on data generated with DoM injected into the residual stream. If fine-tuning-mediated steering moves MATH-500 accuracy while ActAdd does not, this refutation holds.

**R3 — F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046): Layer-localized hidden-state shifts predict that prefill and final-token should have orthogonal directions if they use different layer windows.**
The paper shows that hidden-state shifts from subliminal steering are localized to the specific layers where steering was applied during generation (Figure 3 — peak alignment migrates with steering window). If the prefill-time computation and final-token computation engage different effective layer windows within Qwen's 28-layer stack, the resulting mean direction of variation at L19 would naturally be orthogonal between the two contexts — not because they represent distinct circuits (the current F-3 interpretation), but because they are reading off different layer-window imprints at the same layer. The paper's per-layer alignment score analysis could distinguish these explanations.
*Falsification*: Compute per-layer alignment profiles for prefill DoM and final-token DoM separately across all 28 layers. If their profiles peak at different layers (e.g., prefill peaks at L19 but final-token peaks at L22), the orthogonality is explained by the layer-window mechanism. If both peak at L19, this refutation fails.

**R4 — F-7 (D-bucket collective signature): D-bucket geometry may reflect inherited difficulty-encoding bias rather than emergent problem representation.**
The paper shows that even "innocuous" data (random number sequences) transfers complex behavioral biases that leave directional imprints in hidden states. The Qwen model's post-training likely included difficulty-graded mathematical examples. If the difficulty gradient was injected via steered/preference-tuned data, D-bucket problems could exhibit a distinctive geometric signature simply because they trigger a training-inherited "hard problem" direction that was subliminally encoded during post-training. This would reframe F-7 from "the model develops a collective D-bucket representation" to "the model inherited a difficulty-bucket classifier from its training data."
*Falsification*: Run the paper's hidden-state shift analysis on Qwen-2.5-1.5B base vs instruct on D-bucket problems. If the D-bucket geometric signature (F-7) is absent in the base model, the signature is a post-training artifact. If present in both, this refutation fails.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731)**: The paper's alignment score s(ℓ) = cos(mean_shift, v_c) is mechanistically analogous to our DoM's construction (difference-of-means between correct/incorrect activations). Their finding that cos(v_r, v_c) > 0.5 from next-token loss alone on Qwen2.5-7B implies that correctness-relevant directions are recoverable from generation data — consistent with DoM being detectable at AUROC 0.7731 from prefill activations.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill, final) = 0.046)**: Their per-layer alignment analysis (Figure 3) shows that hidden-state shifts are layer-localized. This provides a mechanistic framework for why prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal: they may originate from different layer-window computations whose projections at L19 are orthogonal.

- **H-1 (DoM steering)**: The paper's core result — subliminal steering via training-time injection is more reliable than inference-time injection — directly bears on H-1's feasibility. Their approach (steered teacher → LoRA fine-tuning) is a concrete alternative protocol for correctness steering.

- **H-659 (predict-control discrepancy)**: The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why detection ≠ steerability: the vector is readable but only writable via training-time pathways.

- **H-663 (one-shot optimized L19 SV)**: The paper's vector recovery (Section 6.1) is essentially a one-shot vector optimization but with next-token loss on completions rather than contrastive correctness loss. Their cos > 0.5 recovery rate on Qwen2.5-7B is evidence that such optimization recovers meaningful directions.

- **H-686 (norm-preserving rotation at L19)**: Their Equation 1 (additive steering h ← h + α·v_c) is the simplest form; the paper's failure-mode analysis (high α → degenerate text) motivates norm-preserving alternatives like the rotation approach in H-686.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Hidden-state shift Δh(ℓ)** — Difference in per-layer activations between a modified and baseline model at the final token, averaged over a prompt set. Measures directional imprint of an intervention.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (on cached activations, comparing correct-group vs incorrect-group means).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already compute something similar (DoM), but the per-layer profiling across all 28 layers using their alignment-score framing is a cleaner version.

- **Alignment score s(ℓ) = cos(E[Δh(ℓ)], v_c)** — Per-layer cosine between mean hidden-state shift and a reference vector. Profiles where in the network a signal is localized.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU on cached per-layer activations (need per-layer caches or ~2h H100 re-extraction).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could profile DoM alignment across all 28 layers using P8 layer-sweep data or new extraction.

- **Vector recovery with soft-gated layer window** — Optimize a single vector v_r added to residual stream via learnable sigmoid-gated layer window [s,e] to minimize next-token loss. Recovers an unknown steering direction from generation data.
  *Replication cost*: 2-4h H100 (requires forward passes through model).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, but only if we frame it as "can we recover DoM from generation data alone without labels?" — H100-dependent.

- **Vector verbalization via alpha sweep** — Inject recovered vector at varying strengths α ∈ [0, 10] on neutral prompts, then use LLM to summarize the semantic content of the direction.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 (generation at multiple α) + 5min API (summarization).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could verbalize our DoM direction to check what semantic content it encodes (difficulty estimate? topic familiarity? formatting convention?).

- **Normalized ΔNLL** — Relative change in per-token NLL between fine-tuned and base model as a signal-strength measure.
  *Replication cost*: 30min H100 (two forward passes per prompt).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have a fine-tuned vs base comparison scenario.

## Approaches & framings

- **Signal propagation through data**: The idea that activation-space structure of a teacher propagates through generated text into a student's representations, even when the text is semantically unrelated. Intersects F-2: if post-training data carries correctness-signal geometry, DoM could be a propagated artifact rather than emergent.

- **Predict vs steer asymmetry has a training-time explanation**: The paper implicitly argues that readable-but-not-writable directions exist because writing requires training-time intervention. This reframes the H-659/H-1 tension from "DoM isn't causal" to "DoM is causal but only through the training pathway."

- **Layer-localized imprinting**: The finding that interventions at specific layers leave signatures detectable at those same layers provides a mechanistic language for why L19 is special (F-2) — it may be the layer where the post-training process applied the strongest correctness-related gradient.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Random 3-digit number sequences** — 40,000 procedurally generated prompt-completion pairs; used as innocuous carrier data. Applicable? no — we use MATH-500 reasoning tasks.
- **Bias evaluation prompts (20 neutral prompts)** — Fixed set for alpha-sweep verbalization. Applicable? yes (trivially) — could reuse for verbalizing our DoM direction. Not released as a formal dataset.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Steering vector v_c initialized from N(0, 0.01^2), optimized with Adam (η=0.01) for 100 iterations, all model weights frozen, α=1. Applied at layers [2, L-2].
- LoRA: rank-8, α_LoRA=8, dropout=0.05, applied to all attention and FFN projections. Loss on completion tokens only. SFTTrainer, Adam (η=2e-4), linear LR schedule, 5 warmup steps, batch size 60, 4 epochs on 10k samples.
- Vector recovery: AdamW (η=2e-3 for v_r, 1e-2 for α̃, 5e-2 for layer boundaries), cosine LR, 10 epochs on 10k samples. Gate sharpness k annealed 5→20.
- **Critical**: fp16 mixed precision required for vector recovery convergence; bfloat16 fails or stalls.
- Code: https://github.com/GMorgulis/Subliminal-Steering-2026-Code

## Replicable intermediates

- **Alignment-score profile at L19**: Compute cos(mean(h_correct) - mean(h_incorrect), DoM) at L19 using cached prefill activations — this is trivially 1.0 by construction (DoM IS the difference of means). The non-trivial version: compute the alignment score at every layer using per-layer DoM directions from P8 layer-sweep, testing whether the paper's "peak migration" phenomenon appears in our correctness signal. Requires: P8 per-layer activation caches (if available) or the per-layer AUROC data from P8.
- **Verbalization of DoM direction**: Inject the L19 DoM direction into the model at varying α on neutral prompts and use an LLM to summarize what the direction "means." Requires: H100 forward passes (not a cached-data experiment), but very cheap once on GPU (~30min).
