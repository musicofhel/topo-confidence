# Triage brief — 2512.24560 — Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24560
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: David Gros, Prem Devanbu (UC Davis), Dec 2025.
Code/data: https://github.com/DaiseyCode/LocalCalibrationPubExps

## Refutations

1. **F-2 layer choice may be suboptimal when measured by calibration, not AUROC.** F-2 reports prefill **L19** DoM AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28-layer; L19 ≈ 3/4 depth). The paper's eta-squared sweep of probe-embedding extraction layer (Section 6.1) finds (a) **LAST** layer is consistently worst on BSS/ECE, (b) **MIDDLE** layer has the lowest typical ECE, (c) MIDDLE and SHIFTED-3/4 are comparable on BSS, and (d) the COMBINED (mid+shifted-3/4) variant gives no clear gain. F-2 was selected on AUROC alone — never tuned for ECE or BSS. **Mechanism**: middle layers carry more abstract/task-generic representations; last/3-quarter layers specialize for next-token prediction and consequently overfit to surface signal. **Falsifiable test**: refit DoM probes on cached 1.5B prefill activations across L0..L27, report AUROC + ECE + BSS per layer. If a layer ≤ L14 matches AUROC 0.7731 with materially lower ECE, F-2's "L19 is the right layer" claim downgrades to "L19 is the right layer for ranking, not for calibrated confidence."

2. **F-8's selective prediction may collapse OOD (math → code or NL).** F-8 reports 71.6% selective-prediction accuracy at 50% coverage on MATH-500 K=2.5 averaged. The paper's per-dataset leave-one-out (Table 7) finds probes that score BSS≈0.17–0.33 in the cumulative setting drop to BSS≈0.01–0.08 on **RepoCod-s** (the most realistic, repo-level dataset) when that dataset is held out from training. The collapse is structural: HumanEval+/MBPP+/LiveCodeBench are all "function-from-NL" problems with similar prompt format; RepoCod-s is full-file/repo-context. Our F-8 probe has only ever been trained and tested on MATH-500 (a single problem distribution). **Mechanism**: prefill DoM may encode a domain/topic prior rather than a generic "model knows what it knows" axis. **Falsifiable test**: train F-8 selective-prediction head on MATH-500, evaluate at fixed coverage on GSM8K and one code benchmark using the same Qwen-2.5-1.5B; if accuracy at 50% coverage drops below the unselected baseline, F-8 is domain-conditional and the paper's RepoCod-s result is an analogue.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 ≡ single-layer DoM) may be conditional on embedder size, not feature richness.** The paper's eta-squared analysis (Table 6) finds the **single biggest source of probe BSS variance is the embedding-model identity** (Gen Model η²=0.599; Embedding LM η²=0.186). With Qwen-2.5-Coder MUT, Probe-7B (BSS 0.33 line) materially beats Probe-0.5B (BSS 0.30). F-9 concludes CoE-60's 60-d trajectory features are redundant with a single L19 DoM direction — but both were extracted from the **same** 1.5B-parameter encoder. **Mechanism**: the 60-d CoE basis may compensate for a weak per-layer signal in a small encoder; a stronger encoder collapses the gap because the per-layer signal is already saturated. **Falsifiable test**: pass cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill residuals through Qwen-2.5-Coder-7B (or a stronger LLM2Vec-style encoder), re-extract DoM at the equivalent 3/4-depth layer, compare to CoE-60 on the same MATH-500 split. If single-layer-DoM's lift over CoE-60 grows when the encoder grows, F-9 is conditional on encoder size and the "Probe-0.5B already matches Probe-7B" claim in the paper applies asymmetrically.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: corroborated in spirit — the paper's best line-level probe on Qwen-Coder-7B reaches AUC 0.83 (cumulative, scaled), confirming a 3/4-depth residual representation carries strong correctness signal in another transformer family. Deviation: paper trains an enhanced max-pool probe with Platt scaling rather than a centroid-difference probe; our raw 0.7731 may be an underestimate of what L19 can deliver.
- **F-8 (selective prediction 71.6% at 50% coverage)**: paper's multisampling line-level result for GPT-4o on RepoCod-s (scaled BSS 0.19, ECE 0.06) is the closest external benchmark for "localized usable confidence at the realistic-task level". F-8 is the only project finding currently couched as a deployable selective predictor; this paper provides comparators.
- **F-1 (universal dimensional breathing)**: paper's η²=0.186 for embedding-LM identity hints that representation quality (and hence breathing-amplitude estimates) is encoder-dependent — relevant for the "universal" framing.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≡ single-layer DoM)**: see Refutation 3 above.
- **F-10 (PH at the Gaussian null)**: paper does not test PH; orthogonal.
- **H-3 (breathing on code generation)**: paper's MIPa dataset (1368 GPT-4o + 1355 Qwen-Coder problems with token-level edit labels) is a plug-and-play test bed for H-3. The Qwen-Coder-7B activations would already be extractable on H100.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)**: paper's multisampling baseline (BSS 0.13–0.17 token-level after Platt) gives a direct upper-bound for label-free localized confidence in the code regime.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution)**: not addressed.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Enhanced span-query probe (max-pool over down-projected token embeddings + logistic regression)** — projects each token's activation to a 32-d vector, max-pools the set of token indices in a span (line/problem), runs 2-class logistic regression. Supports token, line, and problem-level queries from a single trained head.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on top of cached MATH-500 1024-tok activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's a direct upgrade path for F-2's centroid-difference probe and immediately gives line-level + problem-level queries we currently can't run.

- **Multisampling diff-based confidence** — sample-at-T=0 reference + 5 variant samples at T=0.8; for each token in the reference, count fraction of variants that retained the token (via difflib). Aggregate to line/problem by arithmetic mean.
  *Replication cost*: 5× generation budget (~2h H100 for MATH-500 K=5 at T=0.8).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a label-free comparator to F-2 / F-8 and replicates the H-12 (Semantic Entropy Probes) framing in our own pipeline.

- **Verbalized line-level reflective confidence** — separate prompt asks the model to emit a `list[float]` of per-line confidences; 96–98% format compliance, fallback to base rate on parse failure.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h API/local inference.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful as a head-to-head against the prefill DoM; not central.

- **Platt rescaling for calibration repair** — 2-parameter logistic on raw probe outputs, fit per fold on 80% and applied on 20%; collapses ECE while preserving BSS rank.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — F-8's selective-prediction outputs are probably miscalibrated; Platt would let us report ECE and BSS, both currently absent from the headline table.

- **Eta-squared factor importance for probe design space** — Cohen-1988 η², applied across (gen model, loss, aggregation, embedding LM, embedding layer, hidden dim) on BSS and ECE.
  *Replication cost*: needs the full grid (~hundreds of probe runs); 1 day H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: probably not at full breadth, but η² as a reporting metric on a smaller sweep is cheap and would clean up our current "we tried a few things" framing in F-2 / F-9.

- **Brier Skill Score (BSS) as the headline calibration metric** — `(B_ref – B_model)/B_ref` where `B_ref = p(1-p)` for base rate p. Resists base-rate gaming that ECE allows.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be added to validate_claims.py for F-2 / F-8 alongside AUROC.

- **Cross-encoder probing (small auxiliary supervisor)** — Probe-0.5B reads activations of GPT-4o-generated code (with code re-encoded by the supervisor), achieves BSS 0.19 line-level after scaling. Enables oversight when the generator is opaque.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests Refutation 3 above and is a route to H-11 (big-to-small distillation) without re-training the small model.

- **Cross-domain probe transfer (code → HaluBench NL)** — code-trained 0.5B probe achieves AUC 0.72 on natural-language hallucinations; needs new Platt scaling.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly relevant to F-2's claim of generality.

- **Leave-one-out per-dataset cross-validation (Table 7)** — fits probe on N-1 datasets, evaluates on held-out one. Detects domain overfitting that cumulative numbers hide.
  *Replication cost*: trivial reanalysis on cached folds.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — see Refutation 2; we should run this for F-8 across MATH-500 / GSM8K / code.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Localized" vs problem-level calibration** — this paper insists token/line-level matters because users need to know *where* to look, not just *whether* to look. Our F-2 / F-8 headline claims are problem-level only; the paper's framing implies a missing axis we have never reported.
- **"Minimal Intent Aligning Patches" labeling protocol** — define correctness/intent as "passes hidden test cases", then derive token-level labels from the smallest model-generated diff that passes them, with reference-solution fallback. Conceptually transferable: for MATH-500, the analogous label would be "smallest reasoning-trace edit that flips K=1 wrong → right". This would give per-token correctness labels we currently lack — they would let H-15 (length-band PR control) and our asymmetric-collapse claim (F-4) be tested at the token level.
- **Cross-encoder oversight** — a small probe-only model can supervise a much larger generator's output. Reframes our entire "probe = within-model property" assumption (every F-N is intra-model).
- **Calibration vs ranking decoupling** — paper warns that ECE is gameable via "bucket collapse"; BSS is the rank-aware metric. F-2 / F-8 currently quote AUROC alone, which is fine for ranking but agnostic to calibration. The paper's framing implies F-2's claim should split into "AUROC ≥ 0.77 (ranking)" + "BSS ? (calibration)".

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Probe head: 32-d down-projection → max-pool over span → logistic regression. ~32 × d_residual + 32 + 1 parameters. Trivially small.
- Loss: Brier (MSE on probabilities) preferred over BCE for calibration; Focal Loss γ=2 also tested.
- Optimizer: Adam, lr=0.001, dropout 0.2, 30 epochs, 5-fold CV.
- Probe trained on **cached** embeddings — no transformer forward pass during training. Identical to our F-2 setup.
- Layer choice: MIDDLE preferred for ECE; LAST consistently worst.
- Embedding source variations: MIDDLE / 3/4 / LAST / SHIFTED-3/4 (use *previous* token's hidden state for current token, motivated by LLM2Vec / BehnamGhader-2024) / COMBINED (mid + shifted-3/4 concatenated).
- Aggregation alternatives: mean pool, max pool, 4-head learned attention. Max pool is robust default.
- Tokenization-normalization across MUTs: paper renormalizes both GPT-4o and Qwen outputs to Qwen-2.5 tokenizer, then runs `difflib` for diff-based labels.
- Generated code from GPT-4o was at temperature 0; multisampling variants at temperature 0.8.
- Verbalized confidence prompt template (Listing 1, paper appendix) — useful direct copy for any "ask the model" head-to-head.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2512.24560 — Localized Calibrated Uncertainty in Code Language Models (Gros & Devanbu, 2025)

**Relevance:** First external study to combine (a) latent probes on residual streams for correctness, (b) sampling-based confidence, and (c) verbalized reflective confidence under a unified calibration framework, evaluated at token, line, and problem granularities on real code-generation benchmarks. Directly informs F-2 (prefill L19 DoM = correctness predictor) and F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage) by supplying (i) a stronger probe head (max-pool span query), (ii) calibration metrics F-N reporting currently lacks (BSS / ECE / Platt scaling), (iii) leave-one-out evidence that probes overfit dataset distribution, and (iv) a cross-encoder oversight protocol (small probe of large generator) that has no analogue in our project.

**Key claim we tested:** Probes on transformer residual streams give well-calibrated, localized correctness signal, with the encoder identity dominating performance and the layer choice mattering most for calibration (mid > 3/4 > last on ECE; AUROC less sensitive). A code-trained probe transfers weakly to NL hallucinations under fresh Platt scaling.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** No replication run yet; the paper's claims would force four updates if confirmed: (1) F-2's L19 selection should be re-justified on calibration, not just AUROC (H-46, FE-71); (2) F-8's selective prediction must be stress-tested OOD (H-47, FE-72); (3) F-9's CoE-60 redundancy may flip under stronger encoders (FE-73); (4) F-2 / F-8 headline numbers should report BSS + ECE alongside AUROC (FE-74). The paper provides a concrete probe upgrade path (max-pool span query) and a labeling protocol (Minimal Intent Aligning Patches) that would generalize to our MATH-500 trace setting if H-3 is ever pursued.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74; H-46, H-47; F-2, F-8, F-9.

**Status:** TO TEST
