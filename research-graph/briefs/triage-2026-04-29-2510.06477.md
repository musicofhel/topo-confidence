# Triage brief — 2510.06477 — Attention Sinks and Compression Valleys in LLMs are Two Sides of the Same Coin

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06477
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Queipo-de-Llano, Arroyo, Barbero, Dong, Bronstein, LeCun, Shwartz-Ziv (ICLR 2026, v2 dated 2026-02-09).

Headline claim: massive activations on the BOS token in middle layers *causally* produce both attention sinks and compression valleys (low matrix-based entropy / high anisotropy), and this triple-coincidence demarcates a three-phase "Mix-Compress-Refine" depth structure: (i) 0–20% diffuse mixing, (ii) 20–85% compressed + sink-heavy, (iii) 85–100% norm-equalized + positional/identity heads. They prove (Theorem 1) that σ₁² ≥ M + αR with M = ‖x_BOS‖² and α the alignment term, and show the entropy upper bound is *tight* in mid-layers (Figure 3, Pythia 410M, RedPajama). Causal MLP-zeroing on BOS at the emergence layer ablates both compression and sinks. Embedding linear probes peak at 25–75% depth (Phase 2), generation perplexity / multiple-choice QA improves through Phase 3.

## Refutations

### R1 — F-2 (prefill L19 DoM peak) inverts the paper's task→phase mapping

The Mix-Compress-Refine theory predicts that *generation*-aligned correctness signals improve monotonically toward full depth, with the sharpest gains in Phase 3 (≥85% depth) when norm equalization restores capacity for token-specific refinement. In Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers), L19 sits at 67.8% relative depth — squarely inside Phase 2 (compression / sink). F-2 reports prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731 at L19 vs 0.7186 at the final token (which is "Phase 3" output of the last layer). MATH-500 K=1 correctness *is* a generation outcome label. Their theory therefore predicts the opposite ordering: AUROC should rise into Phase 3 and peak at L25-L27, not at L19.

**Mechanism**: if Phase 2 representations are dominated by the rank-1 BOS direction, then any classification signal at L19 is forced into the residual subspace and should be either (a) the BOS sink direction itself (uninformative), or (b) attenuated relative to a Phase-3 layer where decompression has restored capacity.

**Falsifiable test**: extract per-layer prefill-position DoM AUROC across L0–L27 on the cached Pathway 11 1024-tok NPZs. Compare the curve shape to their predicted monotonic rise. A peak at L19–L21 (mid-Phase-2) followed by a drop refutes their generation-task prediction; a monotonic rise to L27 refutes our F-2 layer-locality claim.

### R2 — F-1 ("dimensional breathing is universal") may collapse to BOS norm modulation

F-1 attributes a content-modulated, correct-vs-incorrect-divergent breathing pattern in PR to inference-time geometry. Theorem 1 says spectrum is dictated by c = ‖x_BOS‖² / Σ_{i≠0}‖x_i‖²; mid-layer entropy is *upper-bounded* by a function of c alone (Corollary 7). Their Figure 3 shows the bound becomes nearly exact in compressed layers, i.e. once σ₁ is BOS-dominated, the rest of the spectrum is small noise. This predicts that breathing magnitude — which is just the inverse of σ₁/‖X‖_F dominance — should track BOS norm, *not* reasoning content.

**Mechanism**: if the residual stream is rank-1 (BOS) plus small perturbation throughout most of mid-depth, then participation ratio is mechanically `≈ ‖X‖_F⁴ / ‖X‖_op⁴` ≈ M⁻²-driven. Correct vs incorrect prompts of similar length would induce similar BOS norms → similar breathing.

**Falsifiable test**: on cached final-layer NPZs for MATH-500, (a) compute BOS L2 norm per layer per problem, (b) project out the σ₁ singular direction (rank-1 deflation) and recompute PR. If post-deflation PR is featureless across correct/incorrect, F-1's content-divergence is BOS-norm-modulation. If post-deflation breathing magnitude survives and still divides correct-vs-incorrect, F-1 captures something orthogonal to the massive-activation mechanism.

### R3 — F-4 (asymmetric collapse at final token) cannot live in Phase 3 under their theory

F-4 says correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at the final token. The final token sits firmly in Phase 3 (85–100% depth), where the paper says norm equalization has dissipated BOS dominance and compression has reversed. Under Mix-Compress-Refine the final-token position should *not* exhibit compression-driven asymmetry — any geometric asymmetry between correct and incorrect at that depth must come from a different mechanism (positional/identity heads + content refinement), not from the σ₁-collapse logic that motivated framing F-4 alongside the compression-valley literature.

**Mechanism**: their Figure 7 shows BOS norm and "rest of tokens" norm equalize at final-layer for Pythia 410M, Llama-3-8B, Qwen2-7B. If the same equalization holds for Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L27, then PR-collapse asymmetry has nothing to do with σ₁-dominance and the compression-valley framing in our PERSPECTIVES is misleading.

**Falsifiable test**: compute σ₁, ‖X‖_F, anisotropy p_1, and BOS norm at L27 final-token for correct vs incorrect MATH-500 problems on cached NPZs. If correct ≫ incorrect on p_1 (i.e. correct trajectories *are* more rank-1 dominated even in Phase 3), then their equalization-by-Phase-3 claim breaks for reasoning models on math, and F-4's collapse is real but *not* a re-expression of mid-layer compression. If correct ≈ incorrect on p_1 (equalized as predicted), then F-4's asymmetric collapse must be a non-spectral effect (e.g., circuit-level), and the "asymmetric compression" narrative in PERSPECTIVES is wrong.

### R4 — F-10 (PH ≈ Gaussian null) gets a sharper, depth-stratified prediction

F-10 reports PH on trained residual streams indistinguishable from a Gaussian null. The paper's theorem implies mid-Phase-2 X is approximately rank-1 + small isotropic perturbation — exactly the regime in which PH should match a Gaussian baseline (signal absorbed into σ₁, residual is white-noise-like). But Phase 1 (broad mixing, no massive activation) and Phase 3 (decompressed, multiple positional heads writing distinct features) should *not* be Gaussian-null. A finding of "Gaussian null *uniformly across all layers*" would refute their phase-structure prediction; a finding of "Gaussian null only in Phase 2" would *corroborate* their theory while sharpening F-10 from "PH is null" to "PH is null where σ₁ dominates."

**Mechanism**: PH features depend on filtration-distance distribution; rank-1+ε produces a distance distribution close to ε's distribution alone, which for SGD-trained features is approximately Gaussian. Outside the σ₁-dominated band, the distance distribution should reflect richer subspace structure.

**Falsifiable test**: rerun PH separately on Phase 1 (L0–L5), Phase 2 (L6–L23), Phase 3 (L24–L27) of cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B NPZs and compare to Gaussian baseline. Cost: ~1 hour CPU using existing PH pipeline.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Matrix-based singular-value entropy** H(X) = -Σ p_j log p_j with p_j = σ_j² / ‖X‖_F². One-line on cached NPZs.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached Pathway 11 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives us a per-layer compression curve to compare to F-1 breathing curve directly.
- **Anisotropy** p_1 = σ_1² / ‖X‖_F². Trivially derivable from above.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct sanity check of "Phase 2 means p_1 ≈ 1".
- **BOS L2 norm per layer** ‖x_BOS^(ℓ)‖. Requires the BOS-position activation to be cached (or re-extracted with a single H100 hour).
  *Replication cost*: cached if extracted, else ~1h H100 to re-run last 4 layers + L0–L5.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the headline causal variable in their theory.
- **Sink-rate** (per-layer) = (1/H) Σ_h I[mean attn to BOS ≥ 0.3]. Requires attention weights.
  *Replication cost*: medium — requires re-extracting attention from MATH-500 prompts; ~2-3h H100 for Qwen 1.5B + 7B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for follow-up; not for first pass.
- **Mixing-score** = average row entropy of attention matrix. Same data as sink-rate.
  *Replication cost*: free given attention-weight extraction.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes (paired with sink-rate).
- **MLP-on-BOS ablation** (zero MLP contribution to BOS at the emergence layer). Causal test.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min H100 forward pass with hook on Qwen 1.5B over 500 problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — single most powerful causal experiment in the paper.
- **Per-layer LogitLens / linear probe** for QA accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2h CPU per dataset.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — most directly comparable to DoM AUROC sweep.
- **Rank-1 (σ₁) deflation** of representation matrix to test whether downstream metrics survive the BOS direction.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — central ablation for refutation R2.
- **α-alignment metric** α = (1/R) Σ_{i≠0} ‖x_i‖² cos² θ_i (alignment of non-BOS rows with BOS direction).
  *Replication cost*: free given NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful diagnostic for whether mid-layer rank-1 structure is *just* BOS or also content.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Massive activation as the universal compression mechanism"** — recasts compression-valley phenomena (Skean 2025) and attention sinks (Xiao 2024) as one effect with one cause. For us, this is a *competing mechanistic story* for what we have been calling "breathing" in F-1: instead of trajectory-driven dimensional dynamics, it could be BOS-norm scheduling.
- **Three-phase Mix-Compress-Refine demarcation** — gives us a principled segmentation of the 28 Qwen layers into three regions and matched task expectations per region. Useful as a layer-selection scaffold for any future probe sweep, and as the framework against which our L19-peak result is anomalous.
- **"Phase-aware early-exit"** — practical implication: classification-style probes (DoM, ABC-44) belong at end of Phase 2; generation belongs at full depth. Reframes our selective-prediction story (F-8) as a *Phase-2 readout*.
- **Decoupling of "input-dependent sink strength" from "fixed-position phase boundary"** — Figure 5 shows mixing score in Phase 1 is constant across high-sink/low-sink prompts while Phase 2 varies. Predicts F-7's D-bucket signature should be a Phase-2 modulation, not a Phase-1 one.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Sink-rate threshold τ = 0.3 (heads attending ≥30% of mass to BOS).
- Compression-bound tightness check needs only ~100 RedPajama examples (Figure 3 sample size).
- BOS norm spike factor: 10³–10⁴× over rest-of-tokens norm.
- Pythia 410M emergence at L5 (out of 24); Llama-3-8B at L1; Qwen2-7B at multiple layers (cumulative).
- MLP-zeroing ablation: x_BOS^(ℓ+1) ← x_BOS^(ℓ) + Attn^(ℓ)(x_BOS), removing only MLP^(ℓ)(x_BOS). Single-line hook.
- Some models develop massive activations in *multiple* stages (Pythia 410M, Qwen2 7B); ablating one only partially decompresses.
- Pearson correlations: r(ΔBOS_norm, Δentropy) = -0.9 ± 0.18; r(BOS_norm, sink_rate) = 0.58 ± 0.25.
- Linear probes per layer: trained on frozen backbone hidden states with standard logistic regression — same recipe as our DoM (drop-in compatible).

## 2510.06477 — Attention Sinks and Compression Valleys in LLMs are Two Sides of the Same Coin (Queipo-de-Llano, Arroyo, Barbero, Dong, Bronstein, LeCun, Shwartz-Ziv, ICLR 2026)

**Relevance:** Provides a unified mechanistic theory (Mix-Compress-Refine) for the mid-layer compression phenomenon that we have been calling "breathing" (F-1) and that anchors our "PH at Gaussian null" finding (F-10). The paper proves σ₁² ≥ M + αR (Theorem 1) and shows the entropy upper bound is empirically tight in compressed mid-layers, predicting that Phase 2 representations are effectively rank-1 (BOS direction) plus small perturbation. This gives a competing mechanistic story for nearly every geometric finding in our project: F-1 (breathing), F-2 (L19 DoM), F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality), F-4 (asymmetric collapse), F-10 (PH null) all gain or lose interpretation under their theory. Most importantly, their Phase 3 prediction (norm equalization, sharp positional/identity heads, generation tasks improve through full depth) is in tension with our F-2 result that L19 (67.8% depth, mid-Phase-2) is the *best* correctness predictor for a generative task — making this paper one of our highest-leverage refutation candidates.

**Key claim we tested:** Mid-layer compression valleys (low matrix-based entropy, high anisotropy ≥0.9) emerge synchronously with attention sinks at the layer where the BOS token develops massive activations (10³–10⁴× rest-of-tokens norm), and the resulting representation is rank-1 + small perturbation. Generation-correctness signals should improve monotonically with depth (Phase 3 dominant), while embedding/classification probes peak at 25–75% depth.
