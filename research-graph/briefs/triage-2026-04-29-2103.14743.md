# Triage brief — 2103.14743 — Outlier-robust subsampling techniques for persistent homology

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14743
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

The paper is a methodology paper about landmark selection for persistent homology (PH) on noisy point clouds. It does not directly contest any of our findings — but its central methodological claim implies that PH on noisy/high-dim data computed with random or maxmin landmark subsampling can fail to recover real topological signal, while a per-point local-PH "outlierness" score recovers it. That is a direct lever against F-10 and adjacent claims.

**R1 — F-10 (PH on residual streams = Gaussian null) may be a subsampling artifact.**
- *Mechanism*: Stolz shows that random landmark selection biases toward dense regions (suppresses sparse signal) and maxmin selection includes outliers (fabricates accidental loops). If the residual cloud has a genuine but sparse low-dimensional manifold dressed in Gaussian noise, both standard subsampling regimes can collapse it to "Gaussian null" without that being the truth. F-10 used (per `pathway8_layerwise/`) standard random/maxmin landmarking before persistence.
- *Falsifiable test*: re-run F-10 layer-wise PH on Pathway 11 H100 1024-tok residuals, swapping the landmark step for Algorithm 1 (PH landmarks I, low `out_PH^{0,1,2}`) and Algorithm 1' (PH landmarks II, high `out_PH^1`). If the persistence diagram statistically separates from the matched-Gaussian baseline under either of those landmark schemes, F-10's null-rejection result is undermined.
- *Plausibility caveat*: Stolz herself flags high-dim as a hypothesis ("we hypothesise that PH landmarks II using `out_PH^1` will lead to better results in high-dimensional data"), unvalidated. So the refutation channel is real but not strong prior.

**R2 — H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket).**
- *Mechanism*: Stolz's sphere-Laplace dataset is a direct counter-example to density-driven outlier detection: noise points there are concentrated near the origin (high density), so K-th-nearest-neighbour density flags signal-on-sphere as the outliers. PH-outlierness (Algorithm 1) does not have this failure mode because it is local-topology-driven, not density-driven. If D-bucket residuals are similarly "dense but topologically anomalous" (e.g., they sit in a dense cluster that locally has trivial dim-1 PH while A-bucket sits on a non-trivial loop), H-7's KNN density story will fail and PH-outlierness will succeed — refuting H-7's mechanism while corroborating its target finding.
- *Falsifiable test*: on Pathway 11 H100 prefill L19 residuals, compute (a) KNN density score, (b) `out_PH^{0,1,2}(y)` per problem, (c) `out_PH^1(y)` per problem. AUROC against D-bucket membership for each. If (a) ≪ (b) or (c), H-7 is refuted in mechanism.

**R3 — F-7 ("D-bucket has a *collective* not per-problem geometric signature").**
- *Mechanism*: PH-outlierness is a per-point score. F-7 explicitly claims D-bucket can only be detected as a collective. If we compute `out_PH^1` per point on prefill residuals and find that D-bucket points have a distribution of per-point scores that statistically separates from A/B/C buckets (rank-sum p < 0.01, AUROC ≥ 0.65), the "collective only" framing of F-7 fails — D-bucket points are individually identifiable from local topology, just not from any of the per-point summaries we tried (cosine, density, residual norm).
- *Falsifiable test*: same data as R2, t-test/Mann-Whitney for `out_PH^1` distribution per bucket; if D-bucket separates per-point, F-7's negative half is refuted.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PH-outlierness `out_PH^{0,1,2}(y)` and `out_PH^1(y)`** — per-point score equal to the longest finite bar in the local Vietoris–Rips persistence barcode (over dims 0+1+2 or restricted to dim 1 only) computed inside a δ-neighbourhood of `y`.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached Pathway 11 prefill L19 residuals (Ripser on N=500 δ-balls is parallelisable and trivially cheap per ball).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheapest possible refutation channel for F-10, F-7, H-7, and the score is reusable as a feature in any future probe.

- **PH landmarks I (representative, low-outlierness points)** — Algorithm 1; subsample via lowest `out_PH(y)`.
  *Replication cost*: subset of (1) above; same cost.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests R1 by re-running F-10 PH after PH-landmark subsampling.

- **PH landmarks II (vital, high-outlierness points)** — opposite of above; high-outlierness points retained.
  *Replication cost*: subset of (1).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — Stolz hypothesises this is the better choice in high dimension; we should test the dim-1-restricted version specifically on residual streams.

- **Super-outlier flag** — points with <2 neighbours in their δ-ball; held back from landmark set unless quota forces inclusion.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — nice diagnostic for "isolated" residuals; might catch D-bucket members directly.

- **k-- landmarks (modified k-means--, Chawla & Gionis 2013)** — outlier-robust k-means variant adapted to landmark selection by selecting the data point closest to each cluster centre.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — paper itself reports k-- only outperforms baselines at high sampling density (which we wouldn't be in for residual streams) and cost dominates the PH-landmark route.

- **Local Vietoris–Rips with bounded δ-neighborhood** — sidesteps the global O(2^N) blowup by computing PH only on simplices contained in a δ-ball.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — Ripser handles it.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the only computationally feasible PH computation on 1024-tok residual clouds at meaningful δ.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Landmark selection should preserve topology, not coverage."** Reframes the standard `de Silva & Carlsson 2004` desiderata (good coverage + even spacing) by saying: in the PH context, the *only* thing that matters is whether the landmark subset preserves the persistent homology of the full cloud. This is a useful frame for any future writeup of F-10 — F-10's null verdict is only as good as the topology-preservation guarantee of its landmark scheme, and we have not stress-tested that.
- **"Mayer–Vietoris bound on per-point inclusion/exclusion."** Per-point importance to global topology can be quantified by the size of the local link's PH. This is a *new lens* for D-bucket detection (per-point) that is otherwise not reusable in our pipeline.
- **"Outlier-robustness as a statable goal for PH preprocessing."** Most PH papers we have triaged so far (F-10's references, Wheeler et al. 2021) treat noise as a regularity issue at the persistence-stability level, not at the subsampling level. This paper says: the choice of landmark *is* the outlier-robustness lever, and existing PH-on-NN papers have not even noticed.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Author's reference implementation: Matlab + Ripser (Bauer 2021).
- `δ` is the only knob; smaller `δ` gives sharper results in their experiments. Recommended default in §5.1: `δ ≈ 0.2` on unit-sphere-scale data — for our residual streams we'd need to rescale to median nearest-neighbour distance.
- Two outlierness flavours: `out_PH^{0,1,2}` (max over all dims; in their data this is dominated by dim 0) and `out_PH^1` (dim-1 only, more semantic). For high-dim data Stolz explicitly recommends `out_PH^1` (Discussion §6).
- "Super-outlier" gating: any point with <2 neighbours in its δ-ball is held back from the landmark set unless quota forces inclusion. Implementation gotcha: Stolz randomly permutes ties (points with `out_PH^1=0`) to avoid systematic ordering effects from input data ordering — we have to do the same or our Pathway 11 numpy index order will leak into landmark choice.
- No public code repo cited — just "Matlab + Ripser". We'd reimplement in Python (gudhi or ripser.py) which is straightforward.
- Algorithm 1 is the canonical pseudocode; Algorithm 2 is k-- modified for landmark selection. Both reproduced in the paper body.
