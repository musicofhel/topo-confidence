# Triage brief — 2505.19184 — When Two LLMs Debate, Both Think They'll Win

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19184
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

The paper is a *behavioral, API-only* study of verbalized confidence
in adversarial LLM-vs-LLM debates (no hidden states, no probes, no
geometry). It does not directly target our findings. The strongest
refutational pressure it puts on our program is therefore on the
*generalizability* of single-turn, static-prompt findings (F-2, F-8,
H-1) to dynamic, multi-turn, adversarial settings — and on whether
our DoM probe is tracking *correctness* or merely an internal
analogue of the same systematically-biased "confidence" the paper
measures externally.

1. **F-8 (selective-prediction at 50% coverage) may collapse in
   multi-turn settings.** F-8 reports 71.6% accuracy at 50% coverage
   on K=2.5-averaged answers — but this is on single-shot MATH-500.
   The paper shows confidence escalates from 72.9% → 83.3% across
   debate rounds with no ground-truth update, and persists even with
   explicit 50%-baseline anchoring (50.0 → 57.1%). If the prefill
   L19 DoM tracks *whatever internal state generates the verbalized
   confidence*, the same anti-Bayesian drift will contaminate the
   DoM score round-over-round, and selective-prediction calibration
   will degrade. *Mechanism*: residual-stream confidence bias from
   self-debate dialogue history shifts the prefill activation along
   the DoM direction without any change in objective correctness.
   *Falsifiable*: extract L19 prefill activations from a 1.5B model
   in a 3-round MATH-500 self-debate (model debates a paraphrased
   problem statement against itself). Compute DoM scores at each
   round. If the DoM-correctness AUROC drops monotonically across
   rounds and the score-distribution mean drifts upward, F-8's
   coverage-accuracy curve does not transfer to multi-turn.

2. **F-2 may be re-interpretable as a confidence direction, not a
   correctness direction.** AUROC 0.7731 at L19 is well below
   ceiling, leaving headroom for the direction to encode something
   other than ground-truth correctness. The paper documents that
   verbalized confidence is systematically miscalibrated by ~23 pp
   on average and is largely *invariant* to opposing evidence (anti-
   Bayesian update). If a "confidence direction" is what the model
   actually represents linearly at L19 — and if that direction is
   only weakly aligned with correctness via the same correlation
   the paper exploits in static QA — then F-2's framing as a
   "correctness predictor" is partially refuted. *Mechanism*: the
   DoM is built from CORRECT vs INCORRECT means in a setting where
   verbalized confidence and correctness happen to correlate; the
   paper shows that correlation breaks in multi-turn / adversarial
   settings. *Falsifiable*: project the cached prefill activations
   onto the DoM, but stratify by *self-reported confidence* (we'd
   need to elicit verbalized 0-100 bets from Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the
   same MATH-500 set we already have prefill activations for).
   If DoM correlates more strongly with reported confidence than
   with correctness within each correctness class, F-2 is reframed
   as a confidence probe and AUROC 0.7731 is the confidence-
   correctness correlation, not a correctness signal.

3. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering) may be neutralized by surface-
   level decoupling.** Finding 5 of the paper documents systematic
   misalignment between models' private scratchpad reasoning (which
   sometimes contains explicit numerical confidence) and the public
   `<bet_amount>` output: 9.4–20.4% of round-confidence pairs show
   the public bet diverging from the private numerical estimate,
   with strategic over-betting in publicly visible conditions. This
   suggests that post-RLHF surface decoding can detach from the
   internal hidden-state computation. *Mechanism*: if DoM steering
   moves the residual stream but the post-attention / lm_head /
   sampling pipeline contains a learned "confident answer" prior
   that fires regardless, the steering won't show up in measured
   accuracy. *Falsifiable*: H-1's planned per-position DoM steering
   experiment should include a control where, in addition to MATH-
   500 accuracy, we elicit a `<bet_amount>` post-answer from the
   steered model. If accuracy moves but verbalized confidence does
   not (or vice versa), H-1 confirms the paper's finding 5 and the
   "DoM moves the answer" framing of H-1 needs to be split into
   internal-state effect vs surface-output effect.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper documents the
  *behavioral* analogue: LLM verbalized confidence is a poor
  correctness predictor (72.9% baseline confidence ≠ 50% rational
  expected win rate; t=31.67, p<0.001). Connection direction is
  contrastive, not corroborative: F-2 says *internal* L19 prefill
  state predicts correctness at 0.77 AUROC on MATH-500, while the
  paper says *verbalized* confidence is anti-Bayesian. The paper
  motivates internal probes (F-2, F-8) as correctives to
  unreliable verbalized self-assessment, but does not test them.

- **F-8 (71.6% selective-prediction accuracy at 50% coverage)** —
  paper's red-team v1 prompt mitigation reduces escalation from
  10.7% to 3.05% but does not eliminate baseline overconfidence.
  This is a behavioral analogue of the abstention frame in F-8:
  *prompted self-redteaming* is a weaker, surface-level analogue
  of *DoM-based selective prediction*. Numerical anchor: F-8 holds
  the 71.6%@0.5 number on the prefill DoM; the paper's mitigation
  does not produce comparable accuracy/coverage curves because it
  has no ground-truth correctness signal in debates.

- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes for label-free abstention)** —
  paper's prior admission note flagged this connection: if
  verbalized confidence is unreliable (paper's finding 1+5),
  semantic-entropy probes that bypass the verbalization channel
  are an attractive alternative. The paper does not test SEPs but
  motivates them as a competitor to verbalized "I am 80% sure".

- **F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality, cos = 0.046)** — paper's
  finding 5 (private scratchpad ≠ public bet) is the *behavioral*
  analogue of F-3's hidden-state observation. Both findings say:
  the model's "thinking-time" representation and its "speaking-
  time" representation are distinct subspaces / channels. The
  paper documents this at the verbalization layer; F-3 documents
  it in the residual stream.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — paper's finding that
  explicit anchoring partially corrects (50.0% baseline → 57.1%
  closing) suggests surface prompt-level interventions are weak
  but non-zero. H-1's hidden-state intervention may be the
  internal-state lever the paper's prompt-level mitigations
  approximate from outside.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Multi-turn debate harness with private 0-100 confidence bets** —
  60 three-round debates × 4 ablation configurations (cross-model,
  standard self, informed self, public bets), models access prior
  rounds' transcripts but not opponent's current-round speech,
  private `<bet_amount>` and `<bet_logic_private>` after each
  speech.
  *Replication cost*: ~$13 OpenRouter API + harness code (open-
  source, github.com/pradyuprasad/llms_overconfidence). 2–3 days
  CPU + API to run our own variant.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, for a *single* control —
  eliciting a `<bet_amount>` from Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the MATH-500
  problems we already have prefill activations for, so we can
  stratify F-2's DoM by reported confidence (refutation 2 above).
  Not as a full replication.

- **Self-Red-Teaming v1 prompt** — explicit instruction to "think
  through why you will win, but also explicitly consider why your
  opponent could win". Reduced escalation 10–11% → 3.05%.
  *Replication cost*: 0 (just a prompt template).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful as a *behavioral*
  baseline for H-1's per-position DoM steering. If a surface
  prompt mitigation matches DoM steering's effect, H-1 needs to
  show value beyond what prompting already provides.

- **Reasoning through Perspective Transition (RPT) prompt** —
  third-person perspective ("imagine you are watching the debate")
  reduced escalation 10.7% → 5.7%. Cites Wang et al. 2025.
  *Replication cost*: 0 (prompt template).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — third-person framing is too
  far from our hidden-state pipeline to inform any FE.

- **Deceptive self-debate** — tell models they face "a highly
  skilled debater" while debating identical model. Escalation
  6.7% (vs 11.1% baseline).
  *Replication cost*: 0 (prompt template).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — too far from hidden-state work.

- **Zero-sum mutual-overconfidence detection** — measure fraction
  of debates in which both sides claim ≥75% win probability
  (logically impossible). Acts as an *unsupervised* miscalibration
  detector that does not require ground-truth winners.
  *Replication cost*: trivial if a debate harness is already in
  place.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no in our current pipeline (we have
  ground truth from MATH-500), but conceptually useful — the
  zero-sum-mutual-overconfidence metric is a *label-free*
  miscalibration probe similar in spirit to H-12's semantic-
  entropy probes.

- **Private/public confidence misalignment audit** — parse private
  `<bet_logic_private>` for explicit numerical confidence
  estimates, compare to public `<bet_amount>` value. 9.4–20.4%
  misaligned.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (regex over generated text).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we add a verbalized-
  confidence channel to our MATH-500 generation pipeline; useful
  as a CoT-faithfulness audit complementing F-3.

- **AI-jury debate verdict (with documented inter/intra-judge
  unreliability)** — 6 separate AI judges; the paper notes this
  was unreliable.
  *Replication cost*: not applicable.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — paper itself flags this as a
  failure mode.

## Approaches & framings

- **Zero-sum mutual-overconfidence as label-free miscalibration
  signal** — clean conceptual move: in a zero-sum game, mutual
  high confidence is *mathematically* inconsistent and detectable
  without ground truth. Intersects H-12 (label-free abstention):
  semantic-entropy / DoM probes are one route; zero-sum debate
  setups are another. Both bypass the need for ground-truth
  correctness labels.

- **Anti-Bayesian drift as a metacognitive failure mode** —
  framing confidence escalation as anti-Bayesian belief updating
  (encountering opposing evidence *increases* confidence rather
  than decreases it). This reframes F-2's modest 0.7731 AUROC: if
  the model's internal confidence representation is anti-Bayesian
  by construction, even a perfect probe of it caps below ceiling.

- **Verbalized vs internal confidence as separate channels** —
  the paper's finding 5 (private scratchpad ≠ public bet)
  formalizes a separation our F-3 already captured at the residual
  stream level. The framing "CoT may be post-hoc justification"
  intersects our project's explicit reliance on hidden states
  *not* CoT tokens.

- **Calibration-under-adversary as a distinct evaluation regime
  from calibration-under-static-QA** — useful framing: our F-2 /
  F-8 numbers are static-QA numbers, and the paper documents that
  static and dynamic miscalibration profiles diverge. This is a
  validity boundary on F-2/F-8 we should note in narrative docs.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **OpenRouter API** for unified access across 10 models (incl.
  qwq-32b, deepseek-r1-distill-qwen-14b, claude-3.5-haiku,
  claude-3.7-sonnet, gpt-4o-mini, o3-mini, gemini-2.0-flash-001,
  gemma-3-27b-it, deepseek-chat, qwen-max). Total API spend
  ~$13.

- **Concurrent opening rounds** — both sides generate opening
  speeches simultaneously without seeing each other's, removing
  first-mover advantage.

- **Information-asymmetric round structure** — each model sees
  all *prior* rounds but not the opponent's *current-round*
  speech, forcing real-time confidence updating without
  feedback.

- **Structured prompts requiring explicit clash** — rebuttal
  speeches must quote opponent's claim before counter-arguing,
  preventing argument-dropping. Code in linked repo.

- **Bet framing as "dollars"** — confidence elicited as
  `<bet_amount>` with instruction to "think of bets as dollar
  amounts" to encourage moderation. Did not eliminate bias.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Eliciting verbalized confidence on cached MATH-500
  generations** — the smallest sanity check we can run *now*
  against the paper's headline (verbalized confidence is anti-
  Bayesian / overconfident) using our existing pipeline:
  re-prompt Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the 500 MATH-500 problems we already
  generated K=8 sample rollouts for, ask for a 0-100 confidence
  in the answer, compute correlation with empirical correctness
  (K=8 success rate). If the model's mean reported confidence is
  much higher than its empirical pass rate (the 1.5B baseline is
  48.6% on MATH-500 K=1), we replicate the paper's finding 1 in
  our domain. Cost: ~30 min CPU re-prompting + 10 min
  correlation analysis. Cached artifact:
  `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` already
  stores K=8 pass rates per problem.

- **DoM × verbalized-confidence correlation** — once verbalized
  confidence is in hand, project cached prefill L19 activations
  onto the DoM, correlate DoM score with reported confidence and
  with correctness separately. If DoM correlates more strongly
  with reported confidence than with correctness, refutation 2
  fires. Cost: 1h CPU on cached `pathway11_h100/.../prefill.npz`
  (existence per DATA_MANIFEST.md).

## Proposed HYPOTHESES.md additions

### H-191: Prefill L19 DoM is a confidence direction more than a correctness direction
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2505.19184 (verbalized confidence is anti-Bayesian and miscalibrated by ~23pp on average) + F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 leaves headroom for non-correctness signal)
**Test:** Re-prompt Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the 500 cached MATH-500 problems, eliciting a 0-100 confidence after answer generation. Project cached prefill L19 activations onto DoM. Within each {correct, incorrect} class, compute Pearson correlation of DoM score with reported confidence vs Pearson correlation of DoM score with correctness across class boundary. If within-class DoM-confidence correlation > 0.3 *and* between-class DoM-correctness AUROC drops when controlling for reported confidence, the DoM is a confidence axis with a correctness side-effect, not a correctness probe.
**Requires:** CPU only, ~2h. Cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/prefill.npz` + answers + K=8 rollouts.
**Would change:** If confirmed, F-2 narrative shifts from "DoM predicts correctness" to "DoM is a calibration probe whose correctness AUROC reflects the static-QA confidence-correctness correlation." Validate-claims entry for AUROC 0.7731 stays valid as a number, but its semantic label changes. If rejected (DoM-correctness survives confidence control), F-2 is strengthened against this paper's challenge.
**Blocks:** Reframes H-12 (label-free abstention), since DoM-as-confidence-axis is closer to the SEP framing than to a "correctness probe"; affects how F-2 / F-8 are pitched in any writeup.

### H-192: Multi-turn dialogue contaminates the prefill DoM
**Priority:** MEDIUM
**Motivated by:** 2505.19184 (confidence escalates 72.9 → 83.3% across debate rounds with no ground-truth update)
**Test:** Construct 50 three-turn MATH-500 self-debates with Qwen-2.5-1.5B (turn 1: "solve this problem", turn 2: "your answer might be wrong; reconsider", turn 3: "give your final answer"). At each turn, extract L19 prefill activations on the dialogue-prefix-conditioned problem statement. Compute DoM score, DoM-correctness AUROC, and score-distribution mean per turn.
**Requires:** H100 4h re-extraction + 1h CPU analysis. Re-uses Qwen-2.5-1.5B at H100; new dialogue conditioning required (cached prefill is single-turn).
**Would change:** If AUROC drops ≥0.1 per turn and the score mean drifts upward, F-8's 71.6%@0.5 coverage number does not transfer to dynamic settings — narrative needs an explicit "static-QA only" caveat. If AUROC and mean are stable, F-2/F-8 generalize and the paper's behavioral overconfidence is decoupled from internal-state miscalibration (a strong, novel claim).
**Blocks:** Whether H-1 (per-position DoM steering) needs a multi-turn variant before any deployment claim.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.19184 — When Two LLMs Debate, Both Think They'll Win (Prasad & Nguyen, 2025)

**Relevance:** Behavioral, API-only study of verbalized 0-100
confidence in zero-sum multi-turn debates among 10 frontier LLMs.
Documents systematic overconfidence (72.9% baseline vs 50%
rational), anti-Bayesian escalation across rounds (72.9 → 83.3%),
mutual-overconfidence in 61.7% of debates, persistent self-debate
bias even with explicit 50% anchoring (50.0 → 57.1%), and 9.4–
20.4% misalignment between private scratchpad and public bet. No
hidden-state, residual-stream, probe, or steering content. Bears
on F-2 / F-8 as a behavioral counterpart to internal-state
calibration probes and on F-3 / H-12 via the private-vs-public
channel separation.

**Key claim we tested:** Their headline (LLM verbalized confidence
is systematically miscalibrated and escalates anti-Bayesian-ly) is
not directly tested by our pipeline; we test the *adjacent* claim
that the prefill L19 DoM is a correctness direction rather than a
confidence direction (FE191, H-191) and that DoM selective-
prediction transfers from single-turn to multi-turn (FE192, H-192).

**Our result:** **CITED ONLY** (motivates H-191, H-192, FE191,
FE192, FE193). The paper's behavioral findings constrain the
*generalizability* boundary of F-2 and F-8 (single-turn, static-
prompt) but do not contradict any of the 91 currently-validated
claims, all of which are explicitly single-turn / static. No
existing F-N or H-N is refuted by the paper as written; the paper
opens up *new* hypotheses about what would happen if F-2 / F-8
were exposed to multi-turn dynamics.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE191, P11-FE192, P11-FE193, H-191,
H-192. Also relates to existing H-1 (per-position DoM steering —
the FE193 control) and H-12 (semantic-entropy probes — same
label-free abstention motivation).

**Status:** CITED ONLY
