# Triage brief — 2502.06703 — Can 1B LLM Surpass 405B LLM? Rethinking Compute-Optimal Test-Time Scaling

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06703
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Liu, Gao, Zhao, K. Zhang, X. Li, Qi, Ouyang, Zhou (Shanghai AI Lab,
Tsinghua, HIT, BUPT). 2025-02-11.

One-line summary. Compute-optimal test-time scaling (TTS) on MATH-500 / AIME24
with three search algorithms (BoN, beam search, DVTS), seven open-source PRMs
(Math-Shepherd-7B, RLHFlow-Mistral/Deepseek-8B, Skywork-1.5B/7B,
Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B/72B), and policy models from 0.5B to 72B. Headline result:
the *right* search strategy + *right* PRM + difficulty-aware budgeting lets
Qwen2.5-0.5B beat GPT-4o and Llama-3.2-1B beat Llama-3.1-405B on MATH-500
(72.2 vs 71.4 with N=512), and Qwen2.5-7B + Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B reaches 91.0
on MATH-500 / 36.7 on AIME24. Compute-optimal TTS is reportedly >256× more
efficient than majority voting on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B.

## Refutations

**R1. F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731) is dominated by an external PRM
verifier on the same trajectories.**
A learned 7B PRM (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) drives Qwen2.5-7B-Inst from 76.8% CoT to
91.0% MATH-500 — a 14-point lift via per-step scoring of generations. F-2
treats prefill DoM as the primary correctness signal but it is a 1-D linear
probe over a single layer's mean-pooled prompt activations. A full-transformer
PRM scoring the *generated trajectory* should beat 0.7731 by a wide margin on
exactly the cached Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B P11 K=1 generations.
*Mechanism*: PRM has parameters O(1e10), reads all generation tokens, and is
trained on process-supervision labels; DoM is one direction at L19 over the
prompt only. If PRM-on-trajectory AUROC ≥ 0.85 while DoM stays at 0.77, the
"prefill geometry is *the* correctness predictor" framing is overstated —
DoM is at best a free-lunch lower bound.
*Falsifiable test*: Download Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B, score the 500 cached
P11 K=1 generations on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B, compute AUROC vs gold
correctness and compare to F-2's 0.7731 / 0.876.

**R2. F-8 (selective-prediction at coverage 0.5 → 71.6% acc, avg K=2.5) is
dominated by compute-optimal TTS at matched compute on full coverage.**
Table 3 reports Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at 81.8% MATH-500 with compute-optimal
TTS; Table 5 reports 85.6% with much higher budgets. Even at low N
(comparable to our K=2.5 average), search-based TTS plus a Skywork-1.5B PRM
should clear ~80% on full coverage — beating our 71.6% on 50% coverage. F-8's
"selective prediction is a useful operating regime" claim assumes a fixed
generator; if you can spend the same compute on PRM-guided beam search across
*all* problems instead of K=1 on the easy half + K=4 on a quarter, you get
both a higher headline number and full coverage.
*Mechanism*: search-based TTS allocates compute *intra-problem* to high-PRM
branches, while F-8 allocates *across* problems by abstaining; abstention
discards information that the verifier could exploit.
*Falsifiable test*: On the same 500 MATH-500 problems used for F-8, run BoN
with Skywork-PRM-1.5B at average N tuned to match avg K=2.5 (≈2–3 sampled
trajectories), report end-to-end accuracy on coverage 1.0; compare to F-8's
71.6%/0.5 = 35.8% utility-equivalent score.

**R3. F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM, Δ = +0.004) is
narrower than its phrasing suggests — high-capacity trajectory features are
NOT redundant.**
F-9 collapses to: a 60-dim cosine descriptor of the trajectory adds nothing
over the 1-dim L19 DoM. But the paper's Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B *is* a
trajectory-feature extractor — a full transformer over the generation —
and it visibly recovers gains a 60-dim CoE descriptor cannot. So "trajectory
features are redundant with single-layer DoM" only holds at the
narrow-bottleneck end of the capacity axis. At the high-capacity end
(transformer-as-judge), trajectory information is *not* redundant.
*Mechanism*: CoE collapses each generation step to 60 cosines vs PRM
processes the raw hidden states with attention over the entire trace.
*Falsifiable test*: Compute three AUROCs on cached MATH-500 K=1
generations from Qwen2.5-1.5B: (a) L19 DoM alone (0.7731 baseline); (b) CoE-60;
(c) Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B per-step score aggregated. If (c) − (a) > 0.05
while (b) − (a) ≈ 0, F-9's phrasing must be tightened to "*low-capacity*
trajectory features are redundant with single-layer DoM".

**R4. H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched
compute) is confirmed in *direction* but refuted in *mechanism*.**
The paper validates the H-19 thesis: routing different difficulty bins to
different methods (BoN for easy on small models, beam search / DVTS for
hard, BoN again at 72B) gives 256× compute savings vs uniform majority
voting, and 154.6% accuracy lift on Llama-3.2-1B over CoT. So the
verify-and-route framing wins. But H-19 imagines the routing signal as an
*internal* hidden-state probe (PANL activation, prefill DoM, etc.). The
paper's evidence is that *external* PRMs are the dominant routing signal:
weak PRMs (RLHFlow-Mistral-8B, Math-Shepherd-7B) make TTS worse than
majority voting on Llama-3.1-8B-Inst, while strong PRMs (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B)
make it dramatically better. This refutes "internal probe = routing
signal" as the optimal mechanism.
*Mechanism*: PRMs trained on process-supervision data have access to
trajectory-conditional reward, which a prompt-only DoM probe cannot
synthesize.
*Falsifiable test*: On 500 MATH-500 problems, build two routers — one
that uses prefill L19 DoM percentile to choose between K=1, K=8 BoN, and
beam search; one that uses Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B mean trajectory score —
compare end-to-end accuracy at matched total compute.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on 1.5B, ~0.876 on 7B).** The paper
  benchmarks PRM-as-verifier rather than internal-probe-as-verifier on the
  *same model family and same MATH-500 split*. Our 0.7731 AUROC is the
  natural baseline to compare against an external 7B PRM scoring the same
  generations. (Read F-2 body — see Sources consulted.)

- **F-8 (selective-prediction acc 71.6% at coverage 0.5, avg K=2.5).** The
  paper achieves 85.6% (Table 5) on Qwen2.5-1.5B at full coverage with a
  ≥256× compute reduction over majority voting; the natural matched-compute
  comparison is "BoN at K=2–3 with PRM" vs F-8's selective gating. (Read F-8
  body — see Sources consulted.)

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM, Δ = +0.004 transfer).**
  The paper's 7B PRM is the obvious capacity-controlled refutation candidate
  for the trajectory-feature claim — see R3. (Read F-9 body — see Sources
  consulted.)

- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** vs the paper's
  Pass@1-bin "hard" class (0–10% Pass@1). The paper's Figure 7/8 results show
  hard problems benefit from beam search + Qwen2.5-Math-PRM. If our D-bucket
  ⊂ hard-class is recoverable by PRM-guided search, F-7 gets a *mechanistic*
  refinement: the geometric distinctiveness reflects "this problem needs
  step-level verification", not "this problem is fundamentally
  un-recoverable for this model."

- **F-6 (7B prefill PR inversion driven by incorrect-group concentration).**
  Paper observation: small models (≤7B) need step-level verification while
  large models (≥72B) prefer BoN; this matches our F-6 mechanistic story
  that 7B's prefill geometry packs incorrect responses tightly enough that
  per-step intervention has more leverage than at 1.5B.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** The paper
  shows search-only (no steering) lifts Llama-3.2-3B-Inst from 41.4% CoT to
  78.2% MATH-500 (+36.8). H-1 must compete with this baseline at matched
  compute; if DoM steering delivers <5 pts at K=1 it is uncompetitive with
  PRM-guided BoN.

- **H-5 (prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability).** The
  paper's difficulty bins (easy/medium/hard by Pass@1) are *behavioral*; if
  prefill DoM percentile correlates with these bins, H-5 gets direct
  empirical support and prefill DoM becomes a candidate routing signal — but
  see R4 for why it likely loses to PRMs.

- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing).** Strongest direct connection — see
  R4. The paper is essentially the H-19 thesis with PRMs in the verifier
  slot.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Best-of-N (BoN) with PRM scoring**. Generate N independent samples,
  score each by PRM (PRM-Min, PRM-Last, or PRM-Avg), select via Majority
  Vote, PRM-Max, or PRM-Vote (vote-weighted by PRM score).
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min H100 to score the cached P11 K=8 generations
  with Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B, run all 9 voting variants.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already have K=8 cached, only need PRM
  forward pass; lets us read out a directly-comparable PRM AUROC against
  F-2's 0.7731.

- **Beam search guided by PRM (beam width N, beam size M=4)**. At each
  step, PRM scores M expansions per beam, top-N retained.
  *Replication cost*: ~4 h H100 — needs new generation, not just scoring.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — feeds H-19's C_exact framing but
  requires a fresh generation pipeline integrated with PRM.

- **Diverse Verifier Tree Search (DVTS)**. Partition the search tree into
  N/M independent subtrees of width M, run beam search within each subtree
  to maintain diversity. Outperforms beam search on easy/medium with large
  N.
  *Replication cost*: ~4 h H100 + tree-management code (Beeching et al.
  released a version).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — incremental over beam search; defer
  unless beam search gives signal.

- **Reward-aware compute-optimal TTS objective**:
  Target(θ, N, x, ℛ) — algorithm and N chosen jointly per (policy, PRM,
  difficulty) triple rather than per-policy alone.
  *Replication cost*: combinatorial — needs the full grid; the paper
  effectively *defines* this method.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no full grid, but yes a constrained version
  where the policy is fixed to Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B and the algorithm is chosen
  per-difficulty.

- **Pass@1-quantile difficulty binning** (easy 50–100%, medium 10–50%, hard
  0–10%). Replaces MATH's hand-labeled difficulty levels because Pass@1
  varies dramatically by policy model.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — already have K=1 correctness on 500 problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, immediately — directly testable signal
  for whether prefill DoM percentile correlates with behavioral hardness
  (H-5).

- **PRM voting ablation (PRM-{Min,Last,Avg}-{Max,Vote,Majority}, 9 variants)**.
  *Replication cost*: bundled with BoN-with-PRM step.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, as part of the BoN bake-off.

- **PRM length-bias measurement**: average tokens per scored response,
  reveals length confound between PRMs.
  *Replication cost*: ~5 min, post-hoc analysis on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly relevant to F-2 length-residual
  control story.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reward-aware compute optimality**: explicit acknowledgment that the
  optimal (algorithm, N) pair depends on the verifier as much as on the
  policy. Maps cleanly onto our H-19 framing but generalizes the verifier
  slot from "internal probe" to "any reward-source-aware verifier".

- **PRM-as-OOD-detector framing**: the paper attributes weak-PRM failure
  modes to OOD between PRM training distribution and policy generation
  distribution, paralleling our F-2 controls (length residualization) and
  F-7 (D-bucket as OOD geometry).

- **Per-policy difficulty binning**: rejects MATH's static labels in favor
  of behavior-defined bins. Naturally aligns with our A/B/C/D-bucket
  framing on Qwen2.5-1.5B (F-7) and would let us test H-5 directly.

- **TTS vs long-CoT-distillation pivot**: explicitly compares search-based
  TTS to RL/SFT-based long-CoT methods (rStar-Math, Eurus-2, SimpleRL,
  Satori, R1-Distill). On MATH-500 7B, TTS ≈ R1-Distill; on AIME24 R1-Distill
  dominates. Useful framing for the project's "verifier-routing vs reasoning
  training" axis.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Beam width = 4 for both beam search and DVTS.
- Temperature = 0.0 for CoT, 0.7 for sampled methods (BoN, beam, DVTS).
- N (compute budget) ranges to ≥256, with N=512 used for the 1B-beats-405B
  headline.
- All policy models use the *Instruct* version (no base models).
- Voting taxonomy: PRM-Min/Last/Avg × Majority/Max/Vote = 9 variants.
- Llama-3.2-1B's outputs sometimes lack `\boxed{}`; they extract answers
  with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct as a parser — gotcha for any 1B replication.
- Skywork-PRM-1.5B is built on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct — same family as
  our P11 base model, so no architecture-mismatch confound when scoring.
- Code: not visibly linked in the main text, but Beeching et al. 2024 (the
  DVTS reference) released search-and-learn at huggingface/search-and-learn;
  Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B is on HuggingFace at Qwen/Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B.

## Replicable intermediates

- **PRM AUROC on cached P11 K=1 generations**. Smallest cross-check: load
  Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B, score the 500 cached Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B K=1
  trajectories from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`, compute AUROC
  on K=1-correctness, compare to F-2's 0.7731 / 0.876 (R1).

- **PRM AUROC on cached P11 K=8 generations**. Same idea but on the K=8
  data; lets us back-compute selection-via-PRM accuracy and compare against
  selection-via-DoM (F-8 selective prediction).

- **Difficulty-bin alignment**. Bin our 500 MATH-500 K=1 problems by
  paper-style Pass@1 bins (easy/medium/hard); test whether prefill L19 DoM
  percentile predicts bin membership (cheap H-5 sanity check).

- **PRM length-bias on cached generations**. Compute correlation between
  Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B per-trajectory mean score and trajectory token length;
  feeds F-2's length-residual story.

- **No replicable check** is possible for the 1B-beats-405B headline
  itself — that requires running BoN with N=512 on Llama-3.2-1B, which is
  fresh compute we have not committed.
