# Triage brief — 2404.05971 — Does Transformer Interpretability Transfer to RNNs?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05971
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM cos = 0.046) may be transformer-architecture-specific,
   not a universal property of correctness representations.** The paper's *state steering*
   experiment (§3.3) shows that adding a single contrastive state vector to an RNN's
   compressed state at one position propagates the steering effect through the rest of
   generation — i.e. one direction governs both prompt-end and final-token decisions
   because the state is the only causal channel. If the RNN analog of "prefill DoM" and
   "final DoM" turns out to be parallel (high cos), then F-3's orthogonality is an
   attention-mechanics artifact: in transformers, prefill and final-token activations are
   recomputed from re-attended context, so they can encode causally-distinct subspaces
   without state coupling. Mechanism: stateful vs stateless residual recomputation.
   Falsifiable: re-extract prefill_DoM and final_DoM on RWKV-v5 7b (or Mamba-2.8b) on
   MATH-500, compute their cosine, and check whether cos > 0.5 (a threshold well above
   the 0.046 transformer baseline). If yes, F-3 must be re-stated as "transformer-
   specific orthogonality," not a property of the correctness signal itself.

2. **F-2 ("L19 = layer 19 of 28 = 0.68 of depth is the strongest correctness layer")
   conflicts with the paper's universal "middle layers" finding for steering and probing.**
   Paulo et al. report (§3.2 and Fig. 6 b) that the *middle layers* (≈0.5 of depth) carry
   the strongest steering and lowest tuned-lens perplexity across Mamba, RWKV, BTLM, and
   Pythia — and they aggregate by taking the *max across layers* rather than committing
   to a fixed layer. F-2's specific commitment to L19 may be (a) a Qwen-2.5-1.5B-specific
   quirk that doesn't generalize, or (b) an artifact of choosing a single layer with no
   per-layer ablation control. Mechanism: the paper's cross-architecture middle-layer
   universality predicts that per-layer DoM AUROC for Qwen-2.5-1.5B should peak at
   L13–L15, not L19. Falsifiable: compute prefill DoM AUROC at every layer 1–28 on the
   cached P11 H100 NPZs and check whether L19 is actually the argmax. If L13–L15
   dominates, the F-2 framing must be rewritten and downstream H-1/H-13 layer choices
   change.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) is contradicted by the paper's
   monotonic-perplexity tuned-lens finding.** Tuned Lens results (Fig. 6 b, Fig. 13) show
   that perplexity decreases *monotonically* with depth across Mamba, RWKV, and Pythia —
   i.e. each layer adds non-redundant information about the next-token distribution. If
   that monotonicity holds for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 problems, then a multi-layer
   CoE-60 feature should *strictly dominate* a single-layer L19 probe; F-9's claim of
   redundancy must be a finite-sample ceiling effect rather than a fundamental property.
   Mechanism: per-layer information gain → multi-layer probes have strictly more signal
   than any single-layer probe in the limit. Falsifiable: train per-layer linear probes
   on prefill activations from cached P11 NPZs, compute the per-layer correctness AUROC
   curve, and check whether a depth-stacked ensemble of L13…L24 strictly improves over
   L19-alone at the AUROC bootstrap-CI level. If yes, F-9 is a sample-size artifact.

4. **H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probes can replace supervised L19 DoM) is
   undermined by the paper's CCS/CRC underperformance.** Across the quirky-models
   benchmark (Tables 2, 4, 5), unsupervised probes (CCS, CRC) underperform supervised
   probes (LogR, DiffInMeans, LDA) on the harder Alice→Bob transfer setting — and CCS
   trained on "all" contexts collapses to AUROC 0.54 (chance). H-12's optimistic prior
   that label-free methods will match L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731 should be sharply
   downgraded under this evidence. Mechanism: CCS-style consistency objectives capture
   the most-prominent direction in activation space, which is rarely the truth direction
   when distribution shift is present. Falsifiable: replicate CCS and CRC on Qwen-2.5-1.5B
   prefill L19 cached activations for MATH-500 correctness prediction. If CCS_AUROC <
   0.65 (well below 0.7731), H-12 is downgraded from MEDIUM/LOW to PARKED.

5. **F-7's "distinctive collective geometric signature for D-bucket" should be testable
   right now via the paper's Mahalanobis anomaly-detection construction — and the paper's
   empirical AUROC ceiling (0.79–0.85) bounds what F-7 should claim.** Paulo et al.
   construct an anomaly detector by concatenating per-layer probe log-odds, fitting a
   Gaussian on "easy Alice" examples, and computing Mahalanobis distance — directly
   transplantable to ABC vs D detection. They achieve 0.82 (Mamba) and 0.85 (BTLM) on
   distinguishing hard-Alice from hard-Bob — a much harder distribution shift than ABC vs
   D within the same problem set. If a transplanted Mahalanobis detector trained on ABC-
   bucket activations fails to detect D-bucket at AUROC > 0.7 on cached P11 NPZs, then
   F-7's "distinctive geometric signature" claim is weaker than this paper's bar and
   needs softening. Mechanism: Mahalanobis on concatenated layer probes is the canonical
   "collective geometric signature" detector — if it fails on F-7, F-7 should be
   downgraded. Falsifiable: implement on cached P11 H100 NPZs, ~1h CPU.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — The paper's DiffInMeans probe AUROC of 0.78
  on Mamba 2.8b for Alice-easy → Bob-hard transfer is *the same number range* as our
  F-2 OOF AUROC of 0.7731. Suggests the prefill-DoM ceiling may be a property of linear
  probes on hidden activations, not specific to Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Direct numerical anchor.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — CAA is the canonical method H-1 builds on. The
  paper extends CAA to RNN state space (state steering). The non-additivity of
  state+activation steering (§3.3, Fig. 5) is a useful warning sign for any per-position
  steering bank — adding more "steering channels" doesn't compose linearly.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — See refutation 1; the paper's RNN
  results provide a natural cross-architecture sanity check.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)** — See refutation 3; tuned-lens
  monotonicity directly contradicts.
- **H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket)** — The Mahalanobis-on-layer-
  probe-concatenation detector is exactly H-7's construction; the paper provides the
  benchmark numbers (Mamba 0.82, BTLM 0.85). Refutation 5.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes / label-free)** — Refutation 4; CCS/CRC consistently
  underperform supervised probes.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Paper explicitly
  excludes mechanistic / circuit-based methods (§6 Conclusion), citing them as future
  work. So H-13 is *not* answered here, but the paper's representation-engineering
  framing reinforces that H-13 is the natural next step.

Numerical anchors used: AUROC 0.7731 (F-2), cos 0.046 (F-3), tied to paper's
DiffInMeans 0.78 / Mamba and Mahalanobis 0.82 / Mamba.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)** — Diff-in-means on residual-stream
  activations at the answer-letter token, indexed by behavior label. Steering vector
  added at inference time with multiplier ∈ [-3, 3], no normalization across models.
  *Replication cost*: minutes CPU once activations are cached; need a chat-tuned model
  for behavior-style probing. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly extends H-1.
- **State steering (RNN-only)** — Diff-in-means on the RNN's compressed hidden state
  (Mamba SSM state or RWKV WKV state) added at the boundary token. *Replication cost*:
  needs Mamba/RWKV chat model; not directly applicable to our Qwen-2.5 transformer
  pipeline. *We'd plausibly run this*: no — out of architecture scope.
- **Tuned Lens** — Per-layer affine translator (A_ℓ, b_ℓ) trained to make
  LogitLens(A_ℓ h_ℓ + b_ℓ) match the final-layer distribution; trained on Pile
  validation. *Replication cost*: ~few hours H100 to train translators for Qwen-2.5-1.5B
  on MATH-500-style data; codebase at github.com/AlignmentResearch/tuned-lens.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests F-9 and gives per-layer information
  curves for free.
- **Seven probing methods (LDA, mass-mean, LogR, CCS, CRC, LogR on contrast pairs,
  diff-in-means on contrast pairs)** — All take residual-stream activations at one layer
  and output a scalar. CCS and CRC are unsupervised consistency-style probes.
  *Replication cost*: minutes CPU per probe per layer on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — broadens the F-2 single-method ceiling test.
- **Mahalanobis anomaly detector on concatenated per-layer probe log-odds** — Fit a
  multivariate Gaussian on "easy" or "in-distribution" examples and use d²(x, P) as the
  detection score. *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached P11 NPZs once per-layer probes
  exist. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of F-7 / H-7.
- **LoRA-quirky fine-tuning protocol** — Fine-tune base models to make systematic errors
  conditioned on a keyword. *Replication cost*: 2–4h H100 per quirky model, plus dataset
  prep. *We'd plausibly run this*: no — orthogonal to MATH-500 pipeline; useful only if
  we move into deception/ELK territory.

## Approaches & framings

- **Representation engineering vs mechanistic interpretability dichotomy.** §6 Conclusion
  explicitly aligns with rep-eng (probing, steering, lensing) and excludes mechanistic
  approaches as future work. Reinforces that our F-N findings live in rep-eng-land —
  H-13 (head-level attribution) is the only mechanistic hypothesis we have, and the gap
  is a known one in the literature, not a missed framing.
- **"Compressed state" framing for RNNs.** The state aggregates the entire history into
  a fixed-size vector — analogous to a transformer's "prefill" boundary representation,
  but causally upstream of every future token. Suggests reframing F-2 (prefill DoM)
  as "the transformer's only stateful summary," contrasted with the RNN's continuous
  state. Could change how H-5 (familiarity vs decomposability) is operationalized.
- **Iterative inference framing for probes (Jastrzębski et al. 2017).** Both pre-norm
  transformers and modern RNNs share the residual-block structure that makes probing /
  early-exiting interpretable. Implies F-2's L19 prefill probing is part of a broader
  pre-norm-residual phenomenon — useful for paper writeups.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Steering vectors **NOT normalized** across models — different model norms make
  same-norm steering give different effect sizes. (§3.1, footnote 3.) Important for any
  cross-architecture comparison of H-1 results.
- Multiplier range [-3, 3]; max steering effect taken across layers, not at fixed layer.
- For Mamba: tied embedding/unembedding causes the tuned lens to decode *input tokens*
  in the early layers (Fig. 7). If we tune-lens Qwen-2.5-1.5B (which does *not* tie),
  early-layer behavior should differ.
- Tuned Lens training code (lightly modified): github.com/AlignmentResearch/tuned-lens
- ELK-quirky probing code: github.com/EleutherAI/elk-generalization
- Probe is trained on residuals at the answer-position only, using "Earliest Informative
  Layer" — they don't use the last layer naively.
- CCS and CRC are unsupervised, the rest are supervised; CCS-trained-on-all-contexts
  collapses to AUROC ≈ 0.54 (chance).
- Behaviors with "weak steering" (sycophancy, refusal) are weak across *all*
  architectures — informs which steering targets are realistic.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2404.05971 — Does Transformer Interpretability Transfer to RNNs? (Paulo, Marshall, Belrose, 2024)

**Relevance:** Tests whether transformer interpretability methods (CAA, Tuned Lens,
ELK-quirky probing) transfer to RNN architectures (Mamba, RWKV). For our research
program this paper functions as (a) a cross-architecture sanity check on the
"prefill DoM" framing — if RNNs show parallel-not-orthogonal prefill/final
directions, F-3 is transformer-specific; (b) a literature ceiling for Mahalanobis-on-
concatenated-probes anomaly detection (Mamba 0.82 / BTLM 0.85) directly applicable to
F-7 / H-7 D-bucket detection; (c) evidence against H-12 (label-free probes), since
unsupervised CCS/CRC consistently underperform supervised probes on transfer settings;
(d) cross-architecture support for the "middle layers dominate" finding that
challenges F-2's specific L19 commitment.

**Key claim we tested:** Linear probes (DiffInMeans, LogR, LDA) reach AUROC ~0.76–0.78
on Mamba 2.8b for distribution-shift transfer (Alice-easy → Bob-hard); unsupervised
CCS/CRC underperform; Mahalanobis on concatenated layer-probes reaches 0.79–0.85 for
hard-vs-hard distribution-shift detection; Tuned Lens transfers from transformers to
RNNs with monotonic per-layer perplexity; CAA steering works on RNNs with smaller but
more stable effects than transformers.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** P11-FE71 (per-layer AUROC sweep refuting F-2's L19
specificity) and P11-FE73 (Mahalanobis D-bucket detector against the 0.82–0.85 ceiling)
are the highest-leverage immediate tests. P11-FE74 (Tuned Lens on Qwen-2.5-1.5B)
refutes F-9 if monotonicity holds. P11-FE72 (seven-method probe sweep) downgrades H-12
if CCS/CRC underperform on our data as they do on the paper's.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74, H-71, H-72, H-73, F-2,
F-3, F-7, F-9, H-7, H-12.

**Status:** TO TEST
