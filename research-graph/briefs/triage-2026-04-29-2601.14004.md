# Triage brief — 2601.14004 — Locate, Steer, and Improve: A Practical Survey of Actionable Mechanistic Interpretability in Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14004
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Linear-Representation-Hypothesis (LRH) framing of all steering refutes F-2 / F-3.** §4.3 of the survey formalises the field's working assumption that high-level concepts (correctness, truthfulness, refusal) are encoded as *single, approximately linear* directions in the residual stream and steers via a single contrastive-mean vector **v** = μ⁺ − μ⁻ (Eq 22). If the survey's framing is empirically right, our findings F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) and F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, near-orthogonal) are inconsistent: a single global "correctness direction" should yield cos ≈ 1, not 0.046. **Mechanism by which it fires**: replicate the canonical CAA / Rimsky-2024 protocol on MATH-500 with our cached Stage-2 NPZs — pool prefill *and* final-token states across correct/incorrect, compute one global Eq-22 vector, measure AUROC. **Falsifiable by**: if a single global v matches our prefill-only DoM AUROC, F-3's orthogonality is a position-segmentation artifact, not a finding.

2. **Yin et al. (2025) "refusal-cliff" mechanism refutes F-3's orthogonality interpretation.** §5.1.1 reports that refusal intent is *maintained linearly throughout intermediate reasoning but abruptly suppressed at the final generation stage* by a small set of refusal-suppressing heads. The same mechanism applied to correctness would predict that the prefill direction *persists* through CoT and is annihilated by a sparse circuit at the final token — not that prefill and final encode "two different things" (our F-3 framing). **Mechanism**: probe correctness at every CoT token, not just prefill and final-token. **Falsifiable by**: if intermediate-CoT probe AUROC stays near 0.77 and only collapses in the final ~10 tokens, F-3 should be re-stated as "annihilation by a final-stage circuit" (Yin 2025 framing) rather than "computed as orthogonal directions". This would also collapse H-13 (head-level attribution) into a much sharper question.

3. **Sun et al. (2025b) + You et al. (2025) ARES refute F-2's "prefill is the strong predictor" claim.** §5.2.3 cites two works that detect reasoning failures via probes/magnitude analysis on intermediate CoT states (not prefill, not final), and ARES uses these for self-correction. If ARES-style mid-CoT signals beat prefill-only DoM, F-2's headline ("prefill, *better than final-token*") is an artifact of having only sampled two positions. **Mechanism**: train probes on every L19 activation through generation; report AUROC vs token-position. **Falsifiable by**: if any intermediate position beats AUROC 0.7731 OOF, F-2 ranking is wrong.

4. **Survey's "intervention robustness and side effects" warning refutes H-1's premise.** §6 cites Yu and Ananiadou (2025) showing that modifying very small neuron sets *substantially degrades general language performance*, and §4.3 lists "geometric interference" as the primary failure mode of Vector-Arithmetic steering. H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is silent on whether the same intervention degrades general capability. **Mechanism**: when running H-1, also evaluate steered model on out-of-domain held-outs (MMLU subset, HellaSwag, perplexity on Pile slice). **Falsifiable by**: if MATH gain ≤ MMLU loss at the smallest α that moves MATH, the steering is a Pareto-degrading collateral edit, not a useful intervention.

5. **Galichin / Troitskii / Zhang (2025) SAE-feature reasoning steering refutes F-9.** §5.2.3 reports that SAE features cleanly disentangle reflection / backtracking / "wait" / verification as *separate* sparse directions whose causal effects can be modulated independently. F-9 says CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM. If SAE features add a non-redundant signal, F-9 is local-to-our-feature-set, not a general claim about CoE-style trajectory features. **Mechanism**: train an SAE on cached L19 activations across MATH-500, treat top-K reasoning-correlated features as predictors, stack with single-layer DoM. **Falsifiable by**: if stacked AUROC > 0.78, F-9's "redundant" claim weakens.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive Activation Means / Mass-Mean Shift / "Activation Addition"** — μ⁺ − μ⁻ across paired prompts (§4.3 Eq 22). Our prefill DoM is this exact formula.
  *Replication cost*: already done at prefill / final-token. New cost = 0.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — adopt the canonical name and cite this protocol formally in F-2.

- **SAE-derived steering vectors** — train SAE on residual states, take features with high differential activation δⱼ between contrast sets, build steering vector as Σ δⱼ · fⱼ (§4.3 Eqs 23-24).
  *Replication cost*: ~1 H100 day to train Qwen-2.5-1.5B SAE on L19 (or use any released Qwen SAE).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly probes whether F-9 ("CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM") holds when the alternative feature basis is monosemantic SAE features rather than CoE.

- **Backtracking Steering Vector** (Venhoff et al. 2025) — contrastive-mean over self-correcting vs non-self-correcting trajectories.
  *Replication cost*: needs paired self-correcting/non-self-correcting CoT pairs (~1h H100 to generate, then CPU).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if H-13/F-7 D-bucket pivots toward backtracking analysis.

- **Refusal-cliff probing** (Yin et al. 2025) — probe at every CoT token, watch for sharp drop near generation end.
  *Replication cost*: CPU-only on cached per-token activations from P11 H100 runs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is essentially Refutation 2's experiment and feeds H-13.

- **ARES self-correction routing** (You et al. 2025) — magnitude analysis on internal entailment probability; trigger backtrack on high uncertainty.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached states; needs an entailment readout, which we don't have.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — depends on whether entailment readout is a small re-extraction or a full pipeline.

- **Causal Attribution + Probing handshake** (§3.4 limitations) — probe → ablation/patching to validate functional necessity.
  *Replication cost*: P11-FE3 (already in graph, blocked: 4h H100). The survey provides additional formalisation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the canonical follow-up to F-2.

- **Logit Lens / Vocabulary Projection** (§3.5) — project intermediate states through W_U to read out token-level "current prediction" at every layer.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached L0…L27 activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sanity check on what the prefill L19 DoM "decodes to" in token space.

- **Block Influence (BI) layer-skipping metric** (Men et al. 2025) — magnitude analysis comparing layer input vs output.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — orthogonal to our pipeline; cite as supporting metric only.

- **ReasonScore SAE-feature reasoning identification** (Galichin et al. 2025).
  *Replication cost*: needs trained SAE; ~1h after that.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we train an SAE.

- **Persona/Role vectors via natural-language description** (Chen et al. 2025d).
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — orthogonal to correctness.

- **ATLAS — external latent verifier with adaptive steering intensity** (Nguyen and Le, 2026).
  *Replication cost*: needs external verifier model + training.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if H-19 (C_exact) is graduated — ATLAS is its closest analogue.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Locate, Steer, Improve" pipeline as a unifying meta-framework** — recasts MI as actionable intervention rather than observational science. Maps cleanly onto our (probe → ablate → steer) workflow but adds a third step ("Improve") with side-effect checks, which we have not formalised. Adopting this rubric tightens H-1, H-19's experimental design.
- **Linear Representation Hypothesis as the explicit, falsifiable working assumption.** The survey states this assumption rather than smuggling it in. Our F-3 (cos = 0.046) is one of the cleanest existing counter-examples, but only if framed against LRH; current F-3 phrasing doesn't engage LRH directly.
- **"Decodability ≠ causality" caveat** (§3.4) — frames probing as a *localization* tool to be followed by causal tests. Our F-2 / F-8 are pure decodability claims so far; P11-FE3 (activation patching) is the unfinished causality leg, and the survey makes this explicit.
- **Sparsity vs completeness trade-off** (§6) — the survey acknowledges that monosemantic sparse decompositions may *mask* genuine distributed mechanisms. This directly supports our F-10 (PH at Gaussian null) framing: forcing sparse / topological structure on residual streams may not recover the mechanism.
- **Mandatory side-effect evaluation rubric** (Table 4) — every steering claim must include a locality / over-refusal / general-capability check. We currently lack this in H-1.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Eq 22: **v** = (1/|D⁺|) Σ_{x∈D⁺} x_l − (1/|D⁻|) Σ_{x∈D⁻} x_l. We compute this. The survey treats this as the canonical "Contrastive Activation Means" formula — name our implementation accordingly in F-2 / FINDINGS.
- Eq 21: ẑ ← z + α·v. We should expose α as a sweepable hyperparameter in any H-1 implementation, not a fixed constant.
- Eqs 23-24: SAE-steering δⱼ = E_{x∈D⁺}[aⱼ(x)] − E_{x∈D⁻}[aⱼ(x)]; v = Σⱼ δⱼ · fⱼ. Drops into our pipeline if we train (or borrow) a Qwen-2.5 SAE.
- Table 3 (steering methods comparison): Vector Arithmetic = "moderate strength, moderate side effects, low cost"; Targeted Optimization = "high strength, low side effects, high cost". We are firmly in Vector-Arithmetic land.
- Table 4 (eval rubric): math reasoning needs (i) accuracy + reflection-token-ratio primary, (ii) general-capabilities side-effect check on a non-math benchmark.
- Public paper list: github.com/rattlesnakey/Awesome-Actionable-MI-Survey — useful for future literature sweeps.

## 2601.14004 — Locate, Steer, and Improve: A Practical Survey of Actionable Mechanistic Interpretability in Large Language Models (Zhang et al., 2026)

**Relevance:** A 200+-paper survey that formalises the "Locate-Steer-Improve" pipeline: probe / causal-attribute to find an interpretable object, then intervene via amplitude manipulation, vector arithmetic, or targeted optimisation. Our prefill L19 DoM (F-2) is exactly the canonical "Contrastive Activation Means" / "Mass-Mean Shift" formula in §4.3 Eq 22. The survey provides the field-canonical naming, Linear-Representation-Hypothesis framing, and a mandatory side-effect-evaluation rubric (Table 4) we have not yet adopted. It also surfaces three literatures we have *not* engaged: refusal-cliff per-token probing (Yin 2025), ARES mid-CoT failure detection (You 2025), and SAE-feature reasoning steering (Galichin 2025). Each is a direct alternative method to one of our findings.

**Key claim we tested:** Approximately-linear single-direction "concept" steering (LRH) generalises across alignment, capability, and efficiency tasks via Eq 22's μ⁺ − μ⁻ contrast.

**Our result:** **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** Our prefill L19 DoM is exactly Eq 22 and works (AUROC 0.7731). However, F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) shows that the *single-direction* assumption fails: the same Eq 22 protocol applied at two positions of the same residual stream produces near-orthogonal vectors. The survey's framing should be amended to "approximately-linear *position-conditional* directions". Our F-10 (PH at Gaussian null) further supports the survey's §6 sparsity-vs-completeness warning that monosemantic decompositions may miss genuine distributed mechanisms.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73 (pooled-position Eq 22), P11-FE74 (per-token AUROC trajectory), P11-FE75 (H-1 side-effect check), P11-FE76 (logit-lens decode of L19 DoM), P11-FE77 (SAE-feature stacking), H-58, H-59, H-60.
