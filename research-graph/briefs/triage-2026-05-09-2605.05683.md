# Triage brief — 2605.05683 — Spectral Lens: Activation and Gradient Spectra as Diagnostics of LLM Optimization

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05683
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1 — F-1 (breathing universality) may be training-recipe-dependent, not universal.**
The paper's central finding is that models reaching identical loss via different batch sizes develop *systematically distinct* activation covariance spectra — "matched loss does not imply matched internal geometry" (Section 3, Finding 1). If breathing (PR expansion during prefill, collapse at final token) is a consequence of a specific spectral regime (e.g., a specific α_head/α_tail ratio that only arises under certain batch-size/optimizer combinations), then it would not be universal across architectures — it would depend on training conditions. The paper shows Muon vs Adam produce different spectral separation (Appendix D), so Qwen-2.5 (trained with specific optimization choices) might exhibit breathing while a model trained with different batch/optimizer might not. F-1 claims universality; this paper gives a concrete mechanism by which that universality could fail.
*Falsifiable by*: Train two small decoder-only models (NanoGPT d12) with identical architecture but different batch sizes (e.g., 64 vs 512), extract per-layer activations at prefill and final token positions on a fixed prompt set, compare breathing amplitude (PR expansion ratio). If breathing amplitude varies by >2× across batch regimes, F-1's universality claim is weakened.

**R2 — F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731 ceiling) is spectral-shape-bound, not direction-bound.**
Our F-2 AUROC 0.7731 comes from projecting onto a single direction (DoM ≈ PC1). Our cov-spectrum probe lifts to 0.7928 using top-20 log-eigenvalues. The paper proves (Corollary F.8) that band-restricted spectral decay rates α_tail carry richer information than scalar summaries. If correct and incorrect L19 activation groups have different spectral decay profiles — different α_tail over rank windows — then the AUROC ceiling could be set by between-group spectral-shape divergence across rank bands, not by a single linear direction. The cov-spectrum lift from 0.7731 → 0.7928 already hints at this: it captures spectral shape via 20 eigenvalues. If α_tail computed separately on correct vs incorrect L19 covariance submatrices diverges more than DoM projection values do, F-2's framing as "a single direction" would be incomplete.
*Falsifiable by*: Compute α_tail over adapted rank windows [50,150] and [150,350] of trace-normalized L19 covariance separately for correct (n=243) and incorrect (n=257) groups. Build classifier on per-group α values. If α_tail-based AUROC > 0.7928, spectral-shape features outperform both DoM and our current cov-spectrum probe. If α_tail-based AUROC < 0.7731, the signal is directional, not spectral-shape.

**R3 — F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with L19 DoM) could mask genuine cross-layer spectral structure.**
The paper's Appendix E.3 shows the predictive spectral signal is depth-sensitive: early and middle layers are weak or sign-inconsistent, while the deepest stored probe layer gives the strongest positive correlation. But the signal is not *zero* at non-deepest layers — it varies non-trivially across depth. Our F-9 showed CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with L19 DoM, but both measurements are effectively activation-magnitude based. If we compute per-layer α_tail profiles for correct vs incorrect groups and find that non-L19 layers carry *independent* spectral-shape information (not captured by L19's scalar DoM), then CoE-60 would not be truly redundant — it would just appear so under a limited feature set. The redundancy might be an artifact of measuring only 1-d projections per layer rather than spectral shape.
*Falsifiable by*: Extract per-layer covariance spectra at layers 1, 7, 14, 19, 24, 28 for correct vs incorrect groups. Compute α_tail at each layer. Test whether a multi-layer α_tail feature vector beats L19-only α_tail at matched feature dimensionality. If cross-layer α_tail AUROC > L19-only AUROC by ≥ 0.02, F-9's redundancy claim is scoped to directional features only.

**R4 — F-2's "why L19?" specificity may be a generic depth effect, not special circuitry.**
The paper shows (Appendix E.3) that the deepest stored probe layer systematically gives the strongest predictive correlation across all model scales (d12, d36, d48). If L19 is simply the deepest layer before the output head where spectral concentration peaks — a generic depth effect — then F-2's L19 specificity is structural rather than meaningful. The spectral-lens framework provides a concrete mechanism: α_head (leading-mode concentration) might increase monotonically with depth, and L19 would just happen to sit at the depth where this concentration is maximally discriminative between correct and incorrect. No special correctness circuitry required.
*Falsifiable by*: Compute per-layer α_head for L1–L28 on MATH-500 prefill activations. If correct/incorrect α divergence increases monotonically with depth (no L19 peak), F-2's L19 specificity is deflated to a generic depth gradient.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731) / FE881 (cov-spectrum 0.7928):** The paper's activation covariance spectral analysis (trace-normalized eigenvalues + band-restricted α) is methodologically cognate to our cov-spectrum probe. Both decompose the activation covariance eigenspectrum; the paper fits power-law exponents over rank windows, while we use top-20 log-eigenvalues as features. Their theoretical framework (Corollary F.8: flatter tails → faster learning) could ground our empirical cov-spectrum lift.

- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null):** Their spectral framework is the kind of "non-topological but geometry-aware" analysis that succeeds where PH fails. The covariance eigenspectrum captures fine-grained spectral shape that PH summary stats (Betti numbers, total persistence) are too coarse to detect. This corroborates F-10: the useful geometric structure lives in spectral decay rates, not in topological invariants of the point cloud.

- **F-1 (breathing) / H-4 (breathing ↔ sharpness dimension):** Their tail exponent α is formally related to spectral spread / "sharpness." A large α (steep decay) implies low effective dimensionality (sharp representations); a small α (flat spectrum) implies high effective dimensionality (diffuse). If breathing corresponds to α oscillating across sequence positions (decreasing during prefill = expanding, increasing at final token = collapsing), their framework makes H-4's formal correspondence testable.

- **cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216:** In their head/tail decomposition, PC1 is the leading head mode. Their framework predicts that the supervised direction (DoM) should align with PC1 when correctness correlates with "amount of learned structure" — exactly our finding. The 0.9216 cosine similarity maps to a claim that correctness signal concentrates in the head mode.

- **H-16 (Marchenko-Pastur bias correction):** Their trace normalization (λ̃_j = λ_j / Σ_k λ_k) and rank-window selection offer an alternative deconfounding strategy. For our 500-sample × 1536-dim regime (aspect ratio γ ≈ 3.07), the MP bulk occupies a large fraction of eigenvalues. Their pre-registered rank windows — adapted by scale — might naturally place the fitting region above the MP bulk edge, avoiding the bias without explicit correction.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Band-restricted power-law exponent α(I)** — Fit OLS log-log slope of trace-normalized covariance eigenvalues over pre-registered rank windows I = [j_low, j_high]. Separates "head" (learned features) from "tail" (unresolved mass). Different windows capture different spectral scales.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly computable on L19 prefill covariance for correct vs incorrect groups.

- **RankMe (entropy effective rank)** — exp(−Σ p_j log p_j) where p_j = λ_j / Σ_k λ_k (Eq. 10). Single scalar summary of spectral spread, invariant to overall scale.
  *Replication cost*: 2min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — trivial to compute per-group at L19; test whether correct/incorrect groups differ in effective rank.

- **Per-sample gradient SVD** — Stack per-sample gradient vectors g_m = vec(∇_W L_m(θ)) into G ∈ ℝ^{M×P}, analyze singular-value spectrum. Applied to attention projection W_o by default.
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 (requires backward pass per sample on frozen model).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires fresh backward passes, and the training-time framing doesn't directly apply to our inference-time correctness prediction setting.

- **Task-band concentration H_S(t)** — Fraction of spectral energy in a predefined "task-relevant" band: H_S(t) = Σ_{r∈S} m_r(t) / Σ_r m_r(t). In their Fourier toy model this has clean semantics; adaptable to our setting by defining "task band" = DoM/PC1 subspace.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — compute fraction of L19 covariance energy captured by PC1 for correct vs incorrect subgroups.

- **Spectral taxonomy via dual-view** — Joint classification of interventions by whether they shift activation spectra, gradient spectra, or both. Categories: activation-led, gradient-led, throughput-leaning, mixed.
  *Replication cost*: N/A (requires multiple training runs to populate).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — framework is for training interventions, not inference-time analysis.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Matched loss does not imply matched internal geometry"** — Two models at the same loss can have very different activation covariance spectra depending on training recipe. Challenges any universality claim about internal representations. Relevant to F-1: breathing might be recipe-specific.

- **Head vs tail decomposition of eigenspectrum** — Leading eigenvalues (head) capture learned features; trailing eigenvalues (tail) capture unresolved mass. Reframes our PC1-vs-remaining analysis: PC1 is the "head mode," and the cov-spectrum probe's 19 additional eigenvalues probe the "tail."

- **Spectral decay rate as a diagnostic richer than scalar summaries** — α_tail captures more than loss, DoM, or any 1-d projection. This is the same insight behind our cov-spectrum lift (0.7731 → 0.7928), but the paper provides theoretical grounding via linearized gradient flow.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Trace normalization: λ̃_j = λ_j / Σ_k λ_k before power-law fitting — focuses on spectral *shape* not raw scale.
- Pre-registered rank windows: {[100,200], [200,400], [400,800]} scaled by model capacity. For our 1536-dim / 500-sample covariance (rank ≤ 500), adapt to [50,150] and [150,350].
- Power-law fit via OLS in log-log space: α(I) = −slope(log j, log λ̃_j for j ∈ I).
- Spearman rank correlation (ρ_S) as primary metric for monotonic alignment — appropriate for ordinal comparisons.
- Code: https://github.com/AndyLiuin/Spectral-lens-llm-training
