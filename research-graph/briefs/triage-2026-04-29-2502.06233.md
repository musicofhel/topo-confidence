# Triage brief — 2502.06233 — Confidence Improves Self-Consistency in LLMs

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06233
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **P(True) at end-of-generation may beat prefill L19 DoM AUROC (0.7731) on Qwen-2.5 family — refutes F-2.**
   *Mechanism*: F-2 claims prefill L19 DoM is a stronger correctness predictor than final-token DoM (0.7731 vs 0.7186) and is positioned implicitly as the canonical "internal" signal for correctness. CISC achieves up to 67% cost reduction on Gemma2-9B/MATH using P(True) — a verbalized post-hoc probe taken *after* the full reasoning chain has been produced. P(True) is computationally close to a final-token probe with a 20-token confidence prompt prefix. If on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 we measure P(True) WQD or AUROC and it matches or exceeds 0.7731, then the "prefill > final-token" geometric story (F-2, F-3) is at minimum incomplete: a *single output token* under a verbal-confidence prompt would be carrying signal we attributed to upstream geometry.
   *Falsifiable test*: Run P(True) on the 500 MATH problems used for F-2 (cached K=1 generations + prefill activations exist). Compute (a) AUROC of P(True) for K=1 correctness, (b) WQD of P(True) per question (using K=8 samples we already have from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute`). If P(True) AUROC ≥ 0.77, F-2's positioning of prefill as the dominant signal needs re-framing.

2. **Within-Question Discrimination (WQD) is the correct metric for selective prediction — refutes F-8's coverage-0.5 framing.**
   *Mechanism*: F-8 reports 71.6% accuracy on answered at coverage 0.5 with K=2.5 avg, derived from a global DoM-score threshold. The paper argues persuasively (Table 3, §6) that *between-question* metrics like ECE-t and Brier-t are poor predictors of which confidence method actually helps you discriminate inside a single question — precisely the regime selective prediction operates in. F-8's threshold is a between-question threshold. If we recompute selective prediction by *re-ranking the K paths within each question* by prefill DoM and abstaining only on the lowest-WQD problems, the operating curve might look very different — possibly worse, possibly much better. Either outcome refutes F-8's current framing.
   *Falsifiable test*: Re-run F-8 with WQD-based abstention. For each of 500 problems, rank the K=8 sampled paths by prefill L19 DoM, take the path with highest DoM as the answer, abstain on questions where the within-question DoM gap is below threshold τ. Compare accuracy@coverage curve against current F-8 number.

3. **CoE-60 trajectory features may be *strictly worse* than P(True) verbal probe on a per-path basis — refutes F-9.**
   *Mechanism*: F-9 says CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM. CISC's results show *Sequence Probability* (the closest open-weights cousin to a CoE-style trajectory aggregate) achieves only 22-31% cost reduction vs P(True)'s 41-46% (Table 1). Sequence probability and CoE both aggregate over the full reasoning trace; P(True) is a single-token verbal probe. If CoE-60 corresponds to "trajectory ≈ DoM ≈ sequence probability ≈ ~22% gain", then F-9 has been comparing CoE-60 against the wrong baseline — the right comparator is P(True), which CoE-60 likely *loses to*. This wouldn't refute F-9's "redundant with DoM" sub-claim, but it would refute the implicit framing that CoE-60 captures the meaningful internal-state correctness signal: P(True) might capture *more* than CoE-60.
   *Falsifiable test*: On the same MATH-500 cached activations used for F-9, run P(True) and CoE-60 head-to-head as path rankers within K=8. If P(True) WQD > CoE-60 WQD, F-9's framing inverts: trajectory features become the redundant ones, and the verbal probe becomes the canonical signal.

4. **The "P(True) > calibrated-verbal" inversion (Table 3) refutes H-12's path to label-free abstention.**
   *Mechanism*: H-12 proposes semantic-entropy probes as a label-free replacement for supervised DoM. SEPs are calibration-style: they aim to produce well-scaled probabilities. CISC's Table 3 shows *the most calibrated method (verbal 1-100) is the worst for actually distinguishing correct from incorrect within a question*. If SEPs inherit this calibration→discrimination gap, H-12 buys us a calibrated abstention probability that is *worse* than P(True) at the actual selective-prediction job. H-12 should be reframed around WQD, not calibration.
   *Falsifiable test*: When implementing H-12, evaluate SEP using WQD (within-question pairwise), not just global ECE/AUROC. Compare against P(True) on the same K=8 cached samples.

## Methodologies extracted

- **P(True) verbal-probability probe** — append "Now I will rate my confidence on a scale of 1-10. Proposed confidence:" or binary 0/1 prompt after generated answer, take the model's probability mass on the "1" / high-confidence token. Single forward pass, ~20 added tokens, prefix-cacheable.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached K=8 generations (we already have logits from generation, but P(True) requires *new* forward passes with the confidence prompt suffix; needs small GPU, ~30 min on Qwen-2.5-1.5B for 500×8 paths).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct head-to-head with prefill DoM AUROC, low cost, high information value for refuting/confirming F-2.

- **Verbal Binary / Verbal 0-100 confidence prompts** — instruct model to output binary 0/1 or scaled 0-100 confidence after generation. Use the *generated token* (not log-prob) as the score.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min H100 for the same 500×8 paths.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — same job as P(True), gives us the calibration vs WQD ablation that motivates H-12 reframing.

- **Within-Question Discrimination (WQD) metric** — for each question with both correct and incorrect samples, count pairs (correct, incorrect) where confidence(correct) > confidence(incorrect); normalize by total pairs. Pure ranking metric, no scale dependence.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU — pure post-hoc on existing scored arrays.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in replacement for AUROC throughout Pathway 11. Sharper for the K=8 regime than AUROC-on-K=1.

- **Confidence-weighted majority vote (CISC aggregation)** — softmax-normalize confidences with temperature T (tuned on 10% held-out, dataset-agnostic), weighted vote over K samples.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU — trivially scriptable on any K-sample correctness array with per-sample confidence.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a concrete inference-time scaling baseline to put alongside F-8's selective prediction and H-19's verification routing.

- **Two-step prompting with KV-cache reuse** — generate (q, r, a) under one prompt; concatenate confidence prompt e (~20 tokens) and continue; do *not* reprocess the prefix. Reduces P(True) overhead to ~5% of base inference cost.
  *Replication cost*: minor — implementation detail using HF `past_key_values` or vLLM prefix cache.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we run any P(True) experiments — keeps the budget honest.

- **Per-method temperature scaling for confidence normalization** — softmax with T tuned on 10% held-out aggregated across all four datasets. Different methods need wildly different T (P(True) needs low T because confidences cluster tightly).
  *Replication cost*: included in CISC implementation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — baked into CISC.

- **Confidence-gap stratified WQD curve (Figure 4)** — plot WQD as a function of percentile-binned confidence-gap to show monotonic discrimination at fine resolution.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — strong sanity-check artifact for any signal we promote (e.g. prefill DoM gap percentiles vs within-question discrimination).

## Approaches & framings

- **Within-question vs between-question evaluation distinction.** Recasts a long-standing confusion in calibration literature: the question "is this confidence well-calibrated globally?" answers a different question than "can this confidence distinguish correct from incorrect for *this* problem?" Our F-2 and F-8 implicitly use between-question framings (AUROC across all problems at K=1, global score-threshold for abstention). Adopting WQD reframes Pathway 11 evaluation around the operationally-meaningful axis.

- **Confidence as a path ranker, not a path filter.** CISC's bet is that confidence *weighting* (soft) beats confidence *thresholding* (hard). Our F-8 abstention is hard thresholding. The CISC framing suggests a smoother selective-prediction protocol where path weights reduce variance even at full coverage.

- **Self-assessment as decoder-side capability.** The paper's strong claim is that LLMs *can* judge their own outputs (contributing to the Huang et al. 2023 vs Kadavath 2022 debate). This pushes against H-12's implicit assumption that supervised probes are necessary — verbal P(True) needs no labels at all.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code released at `https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cisc` — direct CISC implementation we can clone and run on Qwen-2.5 outputs.
- Two-step prompting uses HuggingFace `past_key_values` for KV-cache reuse — avoid reprocessing the (q, r, a) prefix when computing P(True). Critical for honest cost accounting.
- Temperature T tuned per (model, confidence-method) pair on 10% held-out aggregated across all four datasets — single hyperparameter, dataset-agnostic, simple to re-tune for Qwen.
- Bootstrap protocol: sample 30 paths per question once, then bootstrap n=500 sets of size b ∈ [1, 30] per budget. Reduces variance in cost-reduction estimates. Worth adopting for Pathway 11 K-sweep figures.
- P(True) prompt: `"Is the proposed answer correct? (Yes/No):"` style; take P(token="Yes") (or "1" depending on prompt variant).
- Verbal 0-100 prompt: `"Now I will rate my confidence on a scale of 1-10."` — note the 1-10 in prompt despite Verbal 0-100 method name; numeric range varies by config.
- Optimal P(True) temperature is *low* (Supplementary Fig 8) because P(True) values cluster tightly — confirms the within-question normalization step is doing real work.

## 2502.06233 — Confidence Improves Self-Consistency in LLMs (Taubenfeld et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** Introduces Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (CISC), a confidence-weighted majority-vote variant of self-consistency that achieves 40-46% cost reduction on average across nine LLMs and four reasoning datasets including MATH. Compares four confidence extraction methods (Sequence Probability, Verbal Binary, Verbal 0-100, P(True)) and shows P(True) wins despite being the *least calibrated* of the four. Introduces Within-Question Discrimination (WQD) as the right metric for selective-prediction-style problems and shows it perfectly predicts CISC ranking, while ECE-t and Brier-t do not. Directly bears on F-2 (correctness probe AUROC), F-8 (selective prediction), F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy), H-12 (label-free abstention), and H-19 (verification routing).

**Key claim we tested:** P(True) verbal probe — a single-token forward pass after the answer — provides a stronger within-question correctness signal than calibrated verbal confidences and than sequence probability, sufficient to reduce K=30 self-consistency cost by 40-46%.
