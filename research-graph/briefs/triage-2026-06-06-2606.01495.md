# Triage brief — 2606.01495 — CART: Context-Anchored Recurrent Transformer

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01495
Triaged: 2026-06-06

## Refutations

**Refutation 1 — F-1 (dimensional breathing universality) may be an optimization-dynamics artifact, not an intrinsic geometric property.**
CART's LTI gate learns a spectral radius ρ ∈ [0.79, 0.83] across all 36 configurations that governs how much hidden state is retained vs. discarded across depth iterations. The spectral radius rises monotonically with d_model (+0.033 from d=256 to d=1024) and weakly with R. If "dimensional breathing" (the expansion and contraction of residual-stream effective dimensionality across layers) is mechanistically explained by such a learned state-retention gate — even in standard (non-explicitly-recurrent) transformers where the residual connection acts as an implicit recurrence — then F-1's "universal across architectures" framing reduces to "optimizers find similar state-decay curves regardless of architecture," which is less surprising and less informative. **Test:** Extract per-layer effective spectral radius of the Jacobian of the residual update (∂h_l / ∂h_{l-1}) at L1–L28 of Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the 500 MATH-500 samples. If the per-layer spectral radius profile correlates >0.8 with the participation-ratio breathing curve and both are content-independent, F-1 is reframed from geometry to optimization dynamics.

**Refutation 2 — F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) is consistent with CART's "vestigial machinery" finding, but by the same logic our F-2 single-direction probe may also be vestigial — a symptom of a richer signal we're under-exploiting.**
CART shows that HyperConnection, LTI gate, and Loop Index Embedding are all vestigial in its shared-weight regime: the recurrent core applies essentially the same transformation at every iteration. The analogy to our F-9 is strong — 60-layer trajectory aggregation adds nothing over a single L19 snapshot. But CART also shows that *unsharing* core weights (Ablation Z) recovers 5% perplexity, proving the collapsed representation was sub-optimal, not optimal. This implies our L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 might similarly be a collapsed proxy of a richer per-layer representation that we've declared redundant (F-9) but haven't tested properly. **Test:** Train a per-layer-unshared probe — 28 independent logistic regressors, one per layer, then ensemble their predictions — and compare to the L19-only DoM AUROC 0.7731. If the ensemble exceeds 0.80, F-9's redundancy claim is too strong.

**Refutation 3 — F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) may be explained by CART's "prelude-core separation" rather than reflecting a meaningful structural property of the model.**
CART architecturally separates "context encoding" (prelude) from "iterative refinement" (core), and its frozen K,V anchor means the core's evolved hidden state is structurally decoupled from the prelude's representation. In standard transformers without explicit separation, the prefill computation (F-2) and the final-token computation happen in different phases of the same forward pass. CART's finding that the prelude representation and the core's final state serve different functions (context vs. refinement) suggests that the near-orthogonality in F-3 is simply the expected consequence of two functionally distinct computation phases, not a surprising structural finding. **Test:** In a looped transformer (e.g., Huginn 2502.05171 at R=16), compute the DoM direction at the prelude exit and at the coda exit. If cos(prelude_DoM, coda_DoM) ≈ 0.05 matching our cos=0.046, this confirms F-3 is a generic phase-separation effect rather than a Qwen-specific finding.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

**F-1 (breathing universality) + spectral radius emergence.** CART's ρ ∈ [0.79, 0.83] across scales maps directly to the "asymmetric collapse" of F-4 and the "breathing" of F-1. The spectral radius governs per-iteration state retention and thus how quickly the representation converges to a fixed point. CART's finding that ρ rises with model width (+0.033 from d=256 to d=1024) predicts that larger models should breathe more slowly (retain more dimensional diversity per layer), which is testable against F-1's layer-wise PR curves at 1.5B vs. 7B. Our 7B prefill PR inversion (F-6) could be the geometric manifestation of a higher spectral radius at larger scale.

**F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) + prelude depth dominance.** CART's central result — prelude depth P dominates loop count R without exception — is the architectural analogue of F-2's finding that a single prefill-phase direction outpredicts any generation-phase feature. CART's P=6 > P=4 > P=3 > P=2 ordering suggests that in Qwen-2.5-1.5B, the quality of our L19 DoM probe should improve if we used representations from deeper prefill layers (L19 is ~67% through a 28-layer model ≈ the sweet spot for a P=6/R=6 CART).

**F-10 (PH null) + linear dynamics.** CART's LTI gate imposes a linear state-transition structure (h ← σ(a)⊙h_in + f(h)) whose fixed-point attractor is determined by linear dynamics plus a nonlinear perturbation. Under linear dynamics with additive noise, the resulting distribution is Gaussian. F-10's finding that PH summary statistics on residual streams are not distinguishable from a Gaussian null is exactly what you'd predict if the dominant dynamics are linear-contractive with spectral radius <1 — the fixed-point geometry is ellipsoidal (Gaussian), not topologically interesting.

**H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension) + spectral radius as a bridge.** CART's spectral radius provides a candidate formal bridge between breathing (F-1) and sharpness dimension: ρ controls how rapidly the representation contracts per depth-step, which is the dynamical-systems definition of "sharpness" along the depth axis. If H-4's correspondence can be expressed as Sharpness Dimension ∝ −log(ρ), this paper provides the framework.

**F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% acc) + CART's variable-R inference failure.** CART shows that running more iterations than trained consistently degrades performance (Table 12). Our selective-prediction stack (F-8) implicitly does the opposite: it identifies problems where the model's internal representation is already "converged" (high DoM → likely correct) vs. not (low DoM → abstain). CART's fixed-point convergence framework suggests our DoM could be measuring distance-to-fixed-point, which is exactly what ρ^R controls.

**Cov-spectrum AUROC 0.7928 + spectral analysis of state transition.** CART's spectral radius analysis (eigenvalue of the state transition) and our cov-spectrum probe (eigenvalues of the activation covariance) operate on different but related spectral objects. Our cov-spectrum probe's top-20 log-eigenvalues achieving AUROC 0.7928 might be picking up the same signal as CART's ρ — both measure how energy distributes across spectral modes of the hidden state.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Sigmoid-gated LTI filter for learned stability (h ← σ(a)⊙h_in + f(h))** — A simple learned gate that controls state retention across depth iterations. The spectral radius ρ = σ(a) is bounded (0,1) and converges to a narrow learned band.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (apply gate formula to cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can compute an "implied spectral radius" between adjacent-layer cached activations to test if the residual connection in Qwen-2.5-1.5B behaves like a linear contractive map.

- **Variable-R inference (running more/fewer iterations than trained)** — Testing whether a model's representation quality degrades symmetrically or asymmetrically when you change the number of refinement steps at inference time.
  *Replication cost*: H100 hours (requires fresh forward passes with modified architecture).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires a looped transformer we don't have cached.

- **Prelude-depth vs. recurrence-depth factorial sweep** — Systematically varying "context encoding depth" vs. "iterative refinement depth" and measuring the relative importance of each.
  *Replication cost*: H100 days (full training sweep).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — architectural experiment, not a probe experiment.

- **Diagnostic ablation battery (strip components one at a time from a baseline)** — Systematic removal of individual architectural components (HyperConnection, LTI gate, LIE) to test vestigiality.
  *Replication cost*: Analytical for cached data; could strip components from our probing pipeline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we could test whether individual features in our cov-spectrum probe are vestigial by ablation.

- **Spectral radius tracking across training (ρ trajectory)** — Monitoring the spectral radius of a learned gate across training steps to identify transient vs. converged values.
  *Replication cost*: H100 days (requires training-time logging).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we use frozen pretrained models, not training.

## Approaches & framings

**Context encoding vs. iterative refinement separation.** CART architecturally separates "encoding the context" (prelude) from "refining the representation" (core loops). This framing maps cleanly onto our prefill-vs-generation distinction: the prefill phase is context encoding, generation is iterative refinement. Our F-2/F-3 findings that prefill dominates and is orthogonal to final-token are the implicit version of CART's explicit architectural separation. This suggests treating our layer-wise analysis through a "prelude/core/coda" lens — early layers as prelude, L19 as the prelude-core boundary, late layers as coda.

**Fixed-point convergence as a framework for correctness signals.** CART frames the recurrent core as converging to a fixed point h*, with the prelude determining what h* is and R controlling how close you get. Translating: the L19 DoM direction may be the "fixed-point direction" of the representation, and DoM magnitude may measure distance-to-fixed-point. Problems where the model converges quickly to h* (high DoM) are ones it gets right; problems where it hasn't converged (low DoM) are ones it gets wrong. This is a dynamical-systems reframing of F-2 and F-8.

**Vestigiality testing as a methodological norm.** CART's finding that 3 out of 3 machinery components are vestigial demonstrates the value of systematic ablation before claiming a component matters. This is relevant to our own probe design: we should routinely test whether components of our probing pipeline (e.g., the specific choice of L19, the DoM direction vs. PC1 vs. random direction, the logistic regression vs. threshold) are vestigial or load-bearing.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- CART trains on consumer GPUs: RTX 3050 (8GB) for Stage 1, RTX 3090 (24GB) for Stage 2 — comparable to our 2060 Super constraint.
- Code at https://github.com/ccapps42/CART with full SQLite results database and figure-generation scripts.
- Gradient checkpointing disabled due to tensor aliasing between HyperConnection ring buffer and frozen K,V — a relevant gotcha for anyone implementing similar cached-tensor architectures.
- MLA compression factor d_kv = d/4 for KV cache; SwiGLU FFN with d_ff = ceil((8/3)·d/256)×256.
- AdamW8bit optimizer (bitsandbytes), lr 3e-4 cosine decay to 3e-5, 100-step warmup, weight decay 0.1, grad clip 1.0.
