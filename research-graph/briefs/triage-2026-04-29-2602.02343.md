# Triage brief — 2602.02343 — Why Steering Works: Toward a Unified View of Language Model Parameter Dynamics

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02343
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes naive H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** The paper's preference–utility decay model (Eqs. 12, 14, 17) predicts that a *purely additive* steering of the form `h ← h + m·v` along the preference vector improves preference log-odds approximately linearly only in a narrow small-|m| "Linear Region" (their three-stage curve); for any |m| comparable to or larger than the manifold-scale `L±`, validity decay `D(m)` dominates and utility (i.e. coherent task-valid generation, including correctness format) collapses. Mechanism: pretraining concentrates activations on a low-D manifold `M_l`; off-manifold steering produces representations the rest of the network "decodes unreliably." Falsifiable: sweep `m ∈ [-30, 30]` (or wider) along F-2's L19 prefill DoM on the cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 prefill activations, regenerate K=1 outputs, and check whether MATH-500 accuracy is monotone-decreasing in |m| (their prediction) versus *peaking at some non-zero m* (H-1's implicit prediction). If accuracy never exceeds vanilla 48.6% in either steering direction, H-1's "moves accuracy" framing is refuted; only a SPLIT-style joint preference+utility objective could rescue it.

2. **Refutes the "single fixed direction" reading of F-2 implied by F-3's orthogonality.** The paper's framework (§3.2, §4.2) assumes one preference direction `ω_p` per concept per layer (linear-representation-hypothesis style; cf. Mikolov, Park, Nanda) and reports R²>0.95 fits of preference log-odds across Gemma-2-9B-IT layer 20 and Qwen-2.5-7B-IT layer 14. F-3 says prefill and final-token DoM are *orthogonal* (cos = 0.046) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B — meaning the "concept = correctness" direction is *position-dependent*, contradicting their universal-direction assumption. Mechanism: if their model is right, the same `ω_p` should fit both prefill and final-token preference log-odds with comparably high R². If F-3 is right, refitting their Eq. 15 with one shared direction across both positions on a single concept (correctness) should drop R² substantially at one of the two positions. Falsifiable: implement their preference-utility log-odds protocol on Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L19 (we already cache these activations), construct polarity-paired completions for "correct vs incorrect" on a small MATH-500 subset, fit one shared `ω_p` across prefill and final-token, and report per-position R². Their framework predicts >0.95 at both; F-3 predicts <0.7 at one of them.

3. **Refutes the geometric premise behind H-7 (density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket).** The paper's Assumption 4.1 grants a single training-induced manifold `M_l` per layer with concentrated stably-handled inputs — i.e. there is one density mode and "outliers" are off-manifold deviations. F-7 says D-bucket has a *distinctive collective geometric signature* (per-problem features insufficient; collective structure required). If their assumption holds, D-bucket should look like off-manifold *individuals* rather than a coherent sub-manifold, and H-7's density-based detection should work via simple Mahalanobis / k-NN distance on prefill activations. Mechanism: a simple unimodal manifold model predicts D-bucket detectability at the per-problem level. Falsifiable: run k-NN density and Mahalanobis-distance outlier scoring on the L19 prefill activations for the 36 D-bucket vs 207 A-bucket problems. If their unimodal-manifold assumption holds, AUROC should match a multivariate Gaussian baseline; F-7's "collective signature" prediction would have density-based detection fail (AUROC near 0.5) while collective-feature methods succeed.

4. **Refutes a specific corollary of F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).** Their unified framework casts every intervention method as a dynamic weight update with a *single preference vector per layer-concept pair*. By extension, single-layer probing along `ω_p` should saturate the available preference signal at that layer. F-9 already says CoE-60 (60-dim trajectory features) ≈ single-layer L19 DoM. But their framework, when read together with their finding that preference is *layer-localized* (they pick a single layer 20 / 14 per model), suggests that *any* well-chosen single-layer DoM should beat trajectory features. F-9's "redundant" framing is therefore consistent — but the *strong* version they leave open ("trajectory features add nothing") is refuted by their own SPLIT result: SPLIT consistently beats RePS-vector at matched preference (Table 3), implying trajectory-aware training adds >0 utility. Falsifiable: train a SPLIT-style joint objective on Qwen-2.5-1.5B using the cached L19 prefill activations and "correct vs incorrect" polarity pairs from MATH-500. If SPLIT's selective-prediction accuracy at 50% coverage exceeds F-8's 71.6% baseline, the strong reading of F-9 is refuted.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Their "preference vector" `ω_p` is conceptually the same construct: a contrastive direction in residual-stream space. Their DiffMean-Vector baseline on Gemma-2-9B-IT scores 53–55% Psychopathy classification accuracy untrained (vs. 100% with SFT/RePS/SPLIT), which provides a control: a raw DiffMean direction extracted at one layer is a *weak* preference probe before training, but the same direction trained with a contrastive objective becomes near-perfect. Connection: our F-2 AUROC 0.7731 is a *trained-probe* number; the paper suggests there is headroom (their RePS-Vector gets near-perfect classification on attribute control). Anchor: AUROC 0.7731 (F-2) vs. their 0.99+ Psychopathy after RePS training — they're different tasks, so the comparison is suggestive, not direct.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Direct tension with their universal-direction assumption (see refutation 2). Anchor: cos 0.046 vs. their R²>0.95 fits.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%)** — Their SPLIT method shows that joint preference+utility optimization beats single-objective steering across three tasks (Table 3). Direct connection: our F-8 uses a single-direction probe + threshold; their result implies a SPLIT-style training of the L19 prefill direction *with an explicit utility-preservation loss* could push selective-prediction accuracy higher. Anchor: 71.6% (F-8) — their framework predicts joint-objective lift.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — Strong refutation candidate (see refutation 1). Their framework explicitly predicts utility decay dominates at large |m|. Anchor: H-1's pre-experiment hypothesis vs. their universal three-stage decay curve.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Their work isolates a single layer per model (Gemma layer 20, Qwen layer 14) for steering and *does not* attribute to attention heads. Connection: our H-13 is orthogonal — head-level attribution would *complement* their layer-level framework, not contradict it.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Preference–utility log-odds on a shared scale (Eqs. 7, 8)** — given a polarity pair `(A_p, A_n)` of completions for the same query, define `PrefOdds = log P(p_p|q)/P(p_n|q) = L_n − L_p` (cross-entropy difference) and `UtilOdds = log P(u|q)/(1 − P(u|q))` where `P(u|q) = P(A_p|q) + P(A_n|q)`. This decomposes "did the model express the target concept?" from "is the output a coherent valid completion at all?" on a single comparable scale across SFT / LoRA / vector interventions.
  *Replication cost*: ~20min CPU per concept on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 outputs (we have 500 K=1 generations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives us a clean metric to disentangle "does L19 DoM steering move correctness preference?" from "does it wreck output coherence?", which is exactly H-1's blind spot.

- **Rational-quadratic validity decay D(m) (Eq. 12)** — model the off-manifold capability degradation as a piecewise rational quadratic: `D(m) = (1 + (m−m±)²/L±²)^(−p±)` with separate `(m±, L±, p±)` for positive vs negative steering directions. Captures asymmetric steerability documented in Miehling et al. 2025.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU curve-fitting on a steering sweep we'd need to run first (4–8h H100 for the sweep).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — fitting it on an L19 DoM sweep would tell us whether F-2's direction obeys the same universal three-stage law as their attribute concepts. If yes, single-layer DoM steering for correctness is a special case of their framework. If the curve is qualitatively different, F-2 is doing something they haven't characterized.

- **SPLIT joint loss (Eqs. 18, 19, 20)** — `L = L_util + L_pref` where `L_util = λ_p L_p + λ_n L_n` (LM cross-entropy on both polarities, preserving overall fluency) and `L_pref = γ · ReLU(θ − (L_n − L_p))` (hinge margin maximizing the preference log-odds gap). Trains the steering vector / LoRA / local weights jointly to push preference while clamping utility loss.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for a small RePS-style trainer on cached activations + a SFT baseline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly addresses the "DoM steering wrecks output" risk that H-1 hasn't accounted for. If SPLIT-trained DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy where naive steering doesn't, H-1 needs to be reformulated.

- **DiffMean baseline (Marks & Tegmark 2023, cited)** — train-free contrastive direction = mean(A_p activations) − mean(A_n activations). Their results show DiffMean gives weak but non-trivial preference effect untrained.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (we already use this implicitly for DoM).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: already done — F-2 is essentially a DiffMean probe.

- **Three-stage steering response classification (§3.3)** — Linear → Transitional → Convergence regions in the `m ↦ PrefOdds(m)` curve, characterized by where validity decay starts to dominate. Operationally: fit Eq. 15 and read off `m±` (transition points).
  *Replication cost*: 4h once a steering sweep exists.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives us a principled "steerability budget" for L19 DoM.

- **Polarity-paired contrastive completion construction** — for a query `q`, manually craft `A_p` (concept-positive) and `A_n` (concept-negative) completions; conditional likelihoods `P(A_p|q)`, `P(A_n|q)` give the log-odds. For correctness: `A_p` = the canonical correct answer, `A_n` = the most plausible incorrect answer (e.g. from a wrong-answer distractor pool).
  *Replication cost*: a few hours to assemble polarity pairs for MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the construction itself unlocks all of the above metrics.

## Approaches & framings

- **Dynamic weight updates as the unifying frame.** They rewrite all three intervention forms as `h_{i+1} = (W + m₁·ΔW)h_i + (b + m₂·Δb)`. Activation steering = bias-only update (`Δb = m·v`), LoRA = low-rank `ΔW = BA`, fine-tuning = unrestricted `ΔW, Δb`. Intersection with our work: F-3's prefill/final-token orthogonality is a position-dependence claim that *cannot* be expressed in a layer-local bias update — it implies the bias should vary across positions, which their framework would reject. Useful framing pressure on F-3.

- **Preference vs utility as orthogonal directions in log-odds space.** They factor `P(A|q) = P(u|q) · P(p|q)` and treat the preference direction `ω_p` and utility direction `ω_u` as approximately orthogonal (`ω_u^T Δh ≈ 0` when `Δh` aligns with `ω_p`). Intersection: this is a *different* orthogonality than F-3 (which is prefill-vs-final, not preference-vs-utility). The paper would predict our prefill DoM is on the preference axis and produces no direct utility shift — but we have evidence of utility-correlated structure (F-4: correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at the final token), which lives on the utility axis. So F-4 may decompose as: "preference axis at prefill predicts correctness; utility axis at final token shows the asymmetric collapse." Worth testing as a re-framing of F-2 + F-4.

- **Activation manifold as universal LLM regularity.** Borrowed from Bricken et al. 2023 monosemanticity / Wollschläger et al. 2025. They use it without committing to a specific geometry (sphere, low-D PCA, etc.). Connection: F-10 ruled out *persistent homology* as the right geometry, but their manifold framework only requires *some* concentration. F-10 doesn't refute the framing; it refutes a specific PH instantiation.

- **Steering effectiveness has a predictable ceiling tied to manifold geometry.** Their key implication: at large |m|, validity decay becomes the dominant term and preference log-odds collapses off-manifold. This is a *prediction* about all our future steering experiments (H-1, H-19) — there is a steerability budget set by `L±` (manifold scale) that no clever steering vector can exceed.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models: Gemma-2-9B-IT (intervention layer 20), Qwen-2.5-7B-IT (intervention layer 14). Their layer choices follow Bigelow et al. 2025 — layer is a fixed hyperparameter, not swept. We use Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L19 — different model + different layer choice; cannot directly transfer their layer numbers.
- Intervention point: **FFN down-projection layer** for parametric updates; activation interventions at the per-layer residual stream output.
- Three intervention vector training objectives: SFT (token CE), RePS (their representation-engineering objective from Bigelow et al. 2025), DiffMean (train-free).
- Code: `https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit/blob/main/examples/SPLIT.md`. EasyEdit is a knowledge-editing toolkit — useful infrastructure if we ever wire up SPLIT-style training.
- Steering factor m sweep range: roughly `[-200, 200]` for vector form, `[-15, 15]` for LoRA / local weights (Figure 2 axes). Different magnitude regimes for different intervention types — we'd need to calibrate L19 DoM steering magnitude separately.
- Curve fitting: Eqs. 15 (preference) and 17 (utility) fit with R² > 0.95 across 6 (model × form × method) cells × 3 datasets — strong empirical regularity.
- Hinge-margin loss in SPLIT uses `σ = ReLU` and a tunable margin `θ`; preference-utility weight balanced via `γ`.

**Relevance:** A unified framework casting local weight fine-tuning, LoRA, and activation steering as instances of a *dynamic weight update* `h_{i+1} = (W + m₁·ΔW)h_i + (b + m₂·Δb)`. Implication for our setup: any per-position DoM steering on MATH-500 (H-1) should obey this curve, with a sharply bounded "steerability budget" before validity decay dominates.
