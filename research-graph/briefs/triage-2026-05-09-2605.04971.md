# Triage brief — 2605.04971 — Why Geometric Continuity Emerges in Deep Neural Networks: Residual Connections and Rotational Symmetry Breaking

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04971
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1: L19 specialness may be a weight-geometry artifact, not a semantic signal (challenges F-2, F-8)**

The paper demonstrates that weight v1 (leading right singular vector) exhibits *smooth continuity* across adjacent layers — cosine similarity µ=0.82 for Q projections and µ=0.80 for Gate projections in Llama-3.1-8B (Table 2). If this smooth continuity holds for Qwen-2.5-1.5B, then the L19 DoM direction (AUROC 0.7731) may be nearly identical to the DoM direction at L18 or L20 — i.e., L19 is not special, it's just one point on a smooth continuum. The supervised DoM ≈ PC1 (cos=0.9216) could simply be the *weight v1* of L19's MLP or attention projections, which by geometric continuity would be the weight v1 of most layers. This would downgrade F-2 from "L19 carries a specific correctness signal" to "there's a weight-structural direction available at many layers that happens to correlate with correctness."

*Falsification*: Compute per-layer DoM AUROC for layers L16–L22 on cached 1024-tok data. If AUROC is nearly flat across this band (within ±0.02 of L19's 0.7731), then L19 is not special. If there's a sharp peak at L19, this refutation fails. Additionally, compute SVD of weight matrices at adjacent layers and check cos(v1_L18, v1_L19, v1_L20) — if high (>0.9), the directions are structurally redundant.

**R2: DoM ≈ PC1 may be a generic weight-structural direction, not a correctness-specific signal (challenges F-2)**

The paper shows that activation concentrates weight structure into v1 — the single dominant direction — and this v1 reflects the *cumulative gradient average*, not task-specific information (Section 4.4). The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 alignment could therefore mean that the DoM direction is simply the weight v1 projected into the residual stream — a structural artifact of training mechanics (gradient accumulation + symmetry breaking), not a learned correctness signal. Their ∆GW metric shows that with symmetry-breaking activation (ReLU/GELU/SiLU), cumulative gradient direction is faithfully imprinted into weight v1. If the dominant activation pattern in the residual stream at L19 is determined by weight v1, then our "supervised" DoM is really just rediscovering weight structure.

*Falsification*: Extract the top right singular vector v1 of the L19 MLP Gate or Down projection weight matrix in Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Compute cos(v1_weight, DoM_supervised). If cos > 0.85, the DoM direction is plausibly a weight-structural artifact. If cos < 0.3, the refutation fails — the supervised signal encodes something beyond weight geometry.

**R3: Prefill/final DoM orthogonality may be a trivial consequence of projection-specific continuity spaces (challenges F-3)**

The paper's central transformer finding is that different projections develop continuity in different spaces: Q/K/Gate/Up in input-space (v1), O/Down in output-space (u1), with these spaces being structurally different. If the prefill DoM is driven by input-space-reading projections (Q, K, Gate) and the final-token DoM is driven by output-space-writing projections (O, Down), then cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 is *exactly what the paper predicts* — it's not a meaningful semantic distinction between "problem comprehension" vs. "answer generation" but a mechanical consequence of which projection dominates the residual stream at each computation phase. The orthogonality would be architectural, not functional.

*Falsification*: Decompose the L19 residual stream contribution at prefill vs. final token into MLP-output vs. attention-output components. If the prefill DoM aligns with attention-Q/K input-space structure and the final-token DoM aligns with MLP-Down output-space structure, this supports the architectural explanation. If both DoM directions align with the *same* projection type (e.g., both are MLP-Gate-dominated), the architectural explanation fails and F-3's semantic interpretation survives.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing)**: The paper's geometric continuity provides a *weight-side* mechanism for why breathing patterns should be universal — if v1 continuity is a structural byproduct of residual + activation (not architecture-specific), then breathing (which involves dimensional expansion/contraction across layers) would inherit this universality. The paper's Fig. 1 directly shows weight v1 forming smooth trajectories, which constrains how hidden-state geometry can evolve across depth.

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: The paper's mechanism predicts that weight v1 at L19 should be nearly identical to v1 at adjacent layers (cos > 0.77 in Llama-3.1-8B for Q/Gate). This raises the question of whether the 0.7731 AUROC is attributable to a specific L19 property or to a broad layer-band effect. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 alignment is particularly suspicious under this framework — PC1 of activations may be the v1 of the dominant weight matrix.

- **F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046)**: The paper's projection-specific continuity (Q/K/Gate → v1 input-space; O/Down → u1 output-space) provides a candidate mechanistic explanation for the near-orthogonality. If prefill computation is dominated by input-space-reading projections and final-token computation by output-space-writing projections, orthogonality follows from the architectural structure.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse)**: The paper's finding that activation *concentrates* structure in v1 while normalization *distributes* it (Table 1: σ²-WA = 0.249 for Res+ReLU vs. 0.303 for Res+None+LN) implies that correct trajectories, which collapse harder, may be aligning more tightly with the weight v1 direction — a geometric-continuity-mediated compression. This is testable via the cov-spectrum probe (0.7928 AUROC).

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)**: Geometric continuity predicts this: if weight v1 is continuous across layers, then aggregating information across 60 trajectory checkpoints should yield a direction that closely tracks the single-layer v1 (and thus DoM). The redundancy is a *prediction* of the continuity framework.

- **F-10 (PH null — topology on residuals matches Gaussian)**: The paper's concentration of structure in v1 (one dominant direction) is consistent with activations appearing near-Gaussian in all but one direction. If weight structure is rank-1-dominated (as the paper argues for activation-based architectures), then the hidden state cloud would be approximately Gaussian + a single spike, which standard PH would not distinguish from a Gaussian null.

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension)**: The paper's effective rank metric (erank, Eq. 3) is closely related to the Sharpness Dimension concept. Geometric continuity constraining erank across layers directly intersects with whether breathing and Sharpness Dimension correspond formally.

- **H-8 (DoM direction stable across training checkpoints)**: The paper shows gradient v1 continuity is high from epoch 1 and weight v1 converges to the cumulative gradient v1 over training (Fig. 12). This predicts the DoM direction *should* be stable across training checkpoints (H-8 would confirm), because the cumulative gradient direction stabilizes early.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Geometric continuity metric (v1 cosine similarity)** — Mean absolute cosine similarity of leading right singular vectors (v1) of weight matrices at adjacent layers (Eq. 1). Measures how smoothly weight structure evolves across depth.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (SVD of weight matrices only, no forward pass needed).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — can compute directly on Qwen-2.5-1.5B weights to check if L19 is a continuity peak or breakpoint.

- **σ²-weighted continuity** — Generalization of v1 continuity that weights all singular vectors by their contribution to the Frobenius norm (Eq. 2). Distinguishes activation-concentrated vs. normalization-distributed symmetry breaking.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (same SVD, slightly more computation).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly applicable to characterize which Qwen-2.5-1.5B layers have concentrated vs. distributed structure.

- **Effective rank (erank)** — Entropy-based measure of the effective dimensionality of a matrix's singular spectrum (Eq. 3). Low erank = rank-1-dominated; high erank = distributed spectrum.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — can compute on both weight matrices and the 500×1536 activation covariance matrix to see if erank correlates with DoM AUROC.

- **Cumulative gradient v1 tracking (ΔGW metric)** — Measures the gap between gradient coherence and weight coherence: ΔGW = cont(v1(cumulative_gradient)) − cont(v1(weight)). High ΔGW means gradient coherence is lost before reaching weights (rotation drift).
  *Replication cost*: needs training from scratch (captures training dynamics).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — requires training a model from scratch with gradient logging, not feasible on 2060 Super.

- **Rotation drift tracking** — Per-layer weight v1 rotation angle from epoch-1 reference (Fig. 2a). Detects when continuity collapses.
  *Replication cost*: needs training from scratch.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — same compute limitation.

- **Projection-specific SVD analysis** — Separate SVD of Q, K, V, O, Gate, Up, Down weight matrices, measuring v1 and u1 continuity per projection type across layers.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (weight matrices only, no forward pass).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly applicable to Qwen-2.5-1.5B to determine which projection's v1 aligns with our DoM direction.

## Approaches & framings

- **Weight geometry → activation geometry causal chain**: The paper proposes that activation/hidden-state structure is downstream of weight structure, which is downstream of gradient structure. This inverts the usual "activations reveal what the model is computing" framing — instead, activation geometry is a *shadow* of weight geometry. For our project: if DoM ≈ PC1 ≈ weight v1, then the correctness signal is structural rather than semantic. This reframes F-2 from "the model has learned a correctness direction" to "training mechanics concentrate variance in a direction that happens to correlate with correctness."

- **Symmetry breaking as the origin of structure**: The paper's central insight — that geometric structure requires *breaking* of rotational symmetry, not just nonlinearity — provides a principled lens for why PH fails (F-10). If the only structure in the residual stream that survives symmetry breaking is rank-1 (concentrated in v1), then the residual stream is essentially "Gaussian + one spike," which is exactly the topology that PH cannot distinguish from a Gaussian null.

- **Projection-specific geometry**: The read/write decomposition (Fig. 3) — Q/K/Gate/Up read from the residual stream in input-space, O/Down write in output-space — is a new framing for understanding why different layers or computation phases (prefill vs. generation) produce different geometric signatures. This intersects directly with F-3's orthogonality finding.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- SVD-based v1 extraction with sequential sign alignment (Eq. 6): flip v1^(l) sign if dot product with v1^(l-1) is negative. Essential for visualization, unnecessary for scalar continuity metric (absolute value handles it).
- Effective rank uses entropy of normalized squared singular values (Eq. 3): `p_i = sigma_i^2 / sum(sigma_j^2)`, `erank = exp(-sum(p_i * log(p_i)))`.
- Small Llama-style transformer: D=256, 8 layers, 4 heads, SiLU + RMSNorm, ~34M params. Init N(0, 0.02²). 10K steps, batch 128, seq len 128.
- Pretrained model analysis requires only weight extraction, no forward pass — can run on CPU.
- Models analyzed at scale: GPT-2 XL (1.5B), Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-12B, EXAONE-4.0-32B, Llama-3.1-70B.
- No code repository linked in the paper.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2605.04971 — Why Geometric Continuity Emerges in Deep Neural Networks (Jeong, Paeng, Suh, 2026)

**Relevance:** Provides a mechanistic explanation for why weight matrices exhibit smooth cross-layer structure (geometric continuity) in residual networks. Two necessary conditions — residual connections for gradient coherence and symmetry-breaking nonlinearities for rotation-drift prevention — explain why weight v1 forms smooth trajectories across depth. The projection-specific pattern (Q/K/Gate/Up → input-space v1 continuity; O/Down → output-space u1 continuity; V → low continuity) is directly relevant to understanding our layer-specific and phase-specific (prefill vs. final) geometric findings (F-1, F-2, F-3). The concentration of structure in a single dominant direction (v1) under activation may explain why DoM ≈ PC1 (cos=0.9216) and why PH detects no signal beyond a Gaussian null (F-10).

**Key claim we tested:** Weight v1 continuity is smooth across all layers in pretrained LLMs, meaning no single layer should be structurally special. (Not yet tested for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.)

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — Four FE experiments proposed (P11-FE124971–124974) to verify whether the geometric continuity framework explains or challenges F-2's L19 specialness, F-3's prefill/final orthogonality, and the cos(DoM, PC1) alignment.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE124971, P11-FE124972, P11-FE124973, P11-FE124974, H-830, H-831, H-832
