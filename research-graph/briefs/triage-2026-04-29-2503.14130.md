# Triage brief — 2503.14130 — Inference-Time Intervention in Large Language Models for Reliable Requirement Verification

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14130
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (Prefill ⊥ final-token DoM) may be a layer-pooling artefact.**
   Darm et al. show that on Llama-3.1-8B, *a single attention head* (L14 H24)
   carries enough of a "Yes/No requirement-fulfilled" signal that intervening on
   that head alone (α=32.4) drives precision from 0.56 → 1.00. The signal is
   localised at task-relevant tokens like "Yes" and the entity name. F-3
   measures DoM at the residual-stream level, which is a sum over h attention
   heads. **Mechanism**: orthogonal residual-stream signals can be composed of
   correlated head-level signals — if one head dominates final-token correctness
   AND another dominates prefill correctness, layer-level cosine averages out
   the per-head structure. **Falsifiable**: extract per-head L19 contrastive
   directions on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500, compute cos(prefill_head_DoM,
   final_head_DoM) for each head; if any head has cos > 0.5, the orthogonality
   in F-3 is artefactual.

2. **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% acc) is potentially
   beaten by per-head ITI gating.** Their Table 3 shows L14 H24 with K=6 self-
   consistency hits precision 1.00 at recall 0.07 — i.e. 100% accuracy on the
   ~7% it answers. That maps to selective-prediction coverage 0.07 with 100%
   precision; the AUROC curve from precision-vs-recall they sweep is steeper
   than the L19 prefill DoM curve we use. **Mechanism**: a single head's
   activation is a sharper binary signal than a 1024-dim residual-stream
   projection averaged over all heads. **Falsifiable**: on cached
   pathway11_h100 prefill activations, fit a per-head linear probe for each of
   L19's heads and compare AUROC against 0.7731 (F-2). If any single head
   beats 0.7731, our selective-prediction headline is suboptimal.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 ≅ single-layer L19 DoM) was potentially the wrong baseline.**
   Their L14 H24 dominance suggests *single-head* DoM may be sharper than
   *single-layer* DoM. F-9's redundancy claim compares two layer-pooled
   features (CoE-60 vs L19-DoM); both could be inferior to a single-head
   feature. **Mechanism**: residual stream is a sum of head outputs, so a
   strong signal from one head is diluted by noise from the other 15-23 heads
   in the same layer; CoE-60 and L19-DoM both inherit that dilution.
   **Falsifiable**: re-run F-9's regression test using single-head L19 features
   instead of single-layer L19 DoM as the "single-feature" baseline. If
   single-head explains the CoE-60 residual that L19-DoM doesn't, the
   redundancy story is incomplete.

4. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves accuracy) faces a precision-recall
   wall.** Their headline result is precision 1.00 at recall 0.07 (32.4×
   intervention strength). That's not "fine-grained control" of accuracy — it's
   the model refusing to say "Yes" almost always. **Mechanism**: at large α,
   intervention dominates the residual stream and shifts the output
   distribution toward one class regardless of input. **Falsifiable**: replicate
   their α-sweep on MATH-500 using L19 prefill DoM as the steering direction;
   if MATH-500 accuracy *gain* is bounded by recall collapse on the per-class
   side, H-1 needs to be reformulated as a precision-mode gating rule rather
   than an accuracy-improving steering rule.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** —
  direct method match. Their Algorithm 1 (Divide) + Algorithm 2 (Conquer) is a
  ready-made search procedure for head-level attribution: layer-wise sweep,
  recursive subgroup splitting, then top-K recombination. Plugs directly into
  H-13's "head-level" requirement; the project currently has no concrete
  algorithm for this hypothesis.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering on MATH-500)** — they implement *per-head*
  α (vs Li 2023's uniform α). Per-head α and per-position α are independent
  axes that can compose; their finding that head-level α tuning sharpens
  precision at fixed recall is informative for the per-position framing.
- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731 OOF 5-fold)** — their per-head
  contrastive direction extraction is identical in form to the project's
  L19-mean DoM, just at head granularity. A per-head replication on the
  cached MATH-500 prefill activations is essentially free and is the cheapest
  path to refuting or strengthening F-2's headline number.
- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% acc, K=2.5)** — their
  precision-recall trade-off at K=6 is the engineering analogue of
  selective prediction. Their precision 1.00 at recall 0.07 implies a
  selective-prediction operating point we have not measured.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling)** — their
  precision-prioritized framing aligns with H-19's stance that verification
  (precision) is more compute-efficient than inference scaling (recall).
  ITI as a verification-time gate is consistent with C_exact's spirit.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** — if a single head
  dominates correctness signal, D-bucket geometry might decompose head-level
  rather than layer-level. Refines what "collective" means in F-7.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-head contrastive direction extraction** — `v(l,h) = mean_{i∈D_true}
  z_i^{(l,h)} − mean_{i∈D_false} z_i^{(l,h)}` on last-token activations; one
  direction per (layer, head) pair.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached pathway11_h100 prefill activations
  (we already have z^(l) for L19 across all 1.5B problems; just need to keep
  the per-head split rather than the layer-mean).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct path to H-13 and to refutation #1
  above.

- **Per-head intervention strength αh** — instead of uniform α scaled by
  per-layer activation std (Li 2023), each head gets its own scalar α tuned
  on validation precision/recall.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU per (head, α) grid point; ~1 day for full
  layer-wise sweep × 10 α values.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-1 currently uses uniform per-position α;
  composing per-position × per-head is a natural extension.

- **Divide-and-Conquer head search (Algorithms 1 + 2)** — Phase 1: queue
  per-layer head subgroups, recursively split if precision/recall improves;
  Phase 2: recombine top-K configurations across layers, add head subgroups
  pairwise to best_solution if they exceed thresholds.
  *Replication cost*: ~12h CPU on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers × 12 heads × ~5 α
  values per leaf, with early termination); cheaper than Li's exhaustive
  probe-per-head approach.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-13 has no concrete algorithm; this is the
  drop-in.

- **Self-consistency × intervention (K=6)** — generate K samples per prompt
  with intervention applied at every token; majority-vote final answer. They
  show K=6 with α=32.4 closes the 0.93 → 1.00 precision gap.
  *Replication cost*: 6× inference time per problem.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — relevant only if MATH-500 K=1 results
  with intervention are unstable across decoding noise. P11 already uses
  K=2.5 averaging for selective prediction, so the framework exists.

- **Token-level head-activation L2-norm visualization** — for a single head,
  plot the min-max-scaled L2 of its per-token output activations. Diagnoses
  *what* the head attends to (Figs 11 vs 12: their L14 H24 spikes on "Yes",
  "Antenna"; layer-aggregate spikes on filler tokens "and", "Processor").
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU per head once activations are cached.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could extend H-21 (D-bucket prefill
  attention narrowness) with a per-head decomposition.

- **Variance-vs-temperature stability sweep** — fix a head/α, vary T ∈
  [0.1..1.0], run 20× per (α, T); track variance of precision/recall as α
  increases. Beyond a threshold α, variance collapses to 0.
  *Replication cost*: 20× inference per (α, T) cell, ~12 cells = 240
  generations per head.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for H-1 stability validation but
  not strictly required.

- **KTO LoRA fine-tuning baseline** — Kahneman-Tversky prospect-theory loss on
  binary good/bad labels; LoRA on Q/K/V/O projections; lr 1e-5, undesirable
  weight 2.0, T=0.15, 3 epochs, effective batch 16.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day per seed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — this project is probe-based and avoids
  preference-tuning by design (probes preserve the base model).

## Approaches & framings

- **Single-head dominance of task behaviour** — they argue Occam's razor:
  among matched-precision configurations, prefer the one with fewest heads.
  Reframes "specialised circuits" hypothesis at head granularity. Intersects
  H-13 (head-level attribution) directly: gives a concrete prior that
  task-specific circuitry concentrates in 1-3 heads, not in distributed
  layer-level subspaces.
- **Precision-prioritized engineering metric vs AUROC** — they argue that for
  safety-critical tasks (requirement verification), precision-equals-1 is the
  operating constraint, not AUROC maximization. F-8 frames the project's
  selective-prediction story in AUROC terms; this paper offers an alternative
  reading where the "headline" metric is precision at maximum-recall
  conditional on precision = 1.
- **ITI-as-classifier-tuning analogy** — they liken α tuning to threshold
  tuning of a traditional classifier. Suggests an interpretive lens for
  per-position DoM steering: each α is a per-position threshold rather than
  a steering strength.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Llama-3.1-8B as the only model tested; authors note Arditi et al. 2406.11717
  shows ITI generalizes across model families.
- Hardware: 2× RTX 3090 (24 GB each); inference parallelised via PyTorch.
- Direction extraction uses 10% of train (≈ 4 samples) — confirms ITI's
  sample efficiency claim from Li 2023.
- 3 random seeds for direction-identification robustness.
- α values found in practice: 5.6 → 32.4 (best). Higher α → lower variance
  but lower recall. Variance converges to 0 above α threshold.
- α scaling uses raw direction magnitude (not std-normalized like Li 2023).
- Intervention applied at *every* generated token (not first-token-only).
- Divide-and-conquer terminates when precision/recall don't improve on
  subdivision.
- Phase 2 uses memoization to avoid re-evaluating union configurations.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Per-head L19 DoM probe AUROC vs F-2's 0.7731**: pathway11_h100 prefill
  activations are stored as residual-stream (post-attention sum). To replicate
  per-head, we need to either (a) re-extract with hook on the attention output
  pre-WO_h projection, or (b) hook the attention scores directly. Cost: 4h
  H100 to re-extract on cached MATH-500 problems. Then per-head DoM is 30 min
  CPU. Compare best per-head AUROC against 0.7731.
- **cos(prefill_head_DoM, final_head_DoM) per-head distribution** — uses
  same per-head extractions to test refutation #1.
- **Threshold check on 32.4-style intervention strength**: their α=32.4 is
  much larger than typical ITI values (Li 2023 uses α ~ 5-15). Sanity-check
  by replicating Li's ITI on TruthfulQA with our existing 1.5B and 7B
  activations to verify no obvious extraction bug — 1h CPU on cached
  data if we have TruthfulQA cached, otherwise needs new extraction.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2503.14130 — Inference-Time Intervention in Large Language Models for Reliable Requirement Verification (Darm, Xie, Riccardi, 2025)

**Relevance:** Engineering-domain ITI paper that (a) extends Li et al. 2306.03341
with per-head α (vs uniform α) and (b) introduces a divide-and-conquer head
search algorithm. The single-head dominance result on Llama-3.1-8B (L14 H24
alone yields precision 1.00 at recall 0.07 with K=6 self-consistency) is a
direct methodological lever for H-13 (head-level attribution) and a refutation
candidate for F-2's "L19 layer-mean DoM is optimal" framing — if a single head
beats 0.7731, F-2 is layer-pooling-suboptimal.

**Key claim we tested:** Single attention heads can dominate task-specific
behaviour; per-head α tuning + divide-and-conquer search is more efficient
than uniform-α exhaustive search; ITI generalizes better than KTO LoRA
fine-tuning to held-out test set.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Their Llama-3.1-8B result is on a 76-sample
binary-classification benchmark with extreme α (32.4 vs Li's 5-15); not yet
replicated on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 / 1024-tok regime. P10-FE141 is the
direct test.

**Related experiments:** P10-FE141, P10-FE142, P11-FE141, H-72, H-73, H-13, H-1.

**Status:** TO TEST
