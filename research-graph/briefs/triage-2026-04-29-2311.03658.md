# Triage brief — 2311.03658 — The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Kiho Park, Yo Joong Choe, Victor Veitch (UChicago). ICML 2024 (oral at NeurIPS 2023 CRL workshop). LLaMA-2 7B testbed, 27 binary concepts (BATS 3.0 + 4 language pairs + frequent/infrequent). Code: github.com/KihoPark/linear_rep_geometry.

Core formal claims:
- Three notions of "linear representation" — **subspace**, **measurement** (probe), **intervention** (steering) — are unified once one chooses the right inner product.
- A **causal inner product** ⟨γ̄, γ̄'⟩_C = γ̄ᵀ M γ̄' with M⁻¹ = G Gᵀ and Gᵀ Cov(γ)⁻¹ G = D (diagonal). With the choice D = I_d, this gives M = Cov(γ)⁻¹, i.e. **Mahalanobis whitening by the unembedding-row covariance**.
- Theorem 3.2 (Riesz isomorphism): under a causal inner product, the embedding (intervention) representation λ̄_W and the unembedding (measurement) representation γ̄_W are images of each other: λ̄_W = Cov(γ)⁻¹ γ̄_W.
- Theorem 2.5: adding α·λ̄_W to context activations changes only target concept logit, leaves all causally-separable off-target logits invariant.

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) directly attacks Theorem 3.2.** The paper's unification result says a concept has *one* canonical direction (γ̄_W in unembedding, Cov(γ)⁻¹γ̄_W in embedding); under the right whitening, all "linear" probes for the same concept should collapse to a single direction. We measure two correctness directions at L19 — prefill and final-token — that are essentially Euclidean-orthogonal *on the same labels*. **Mechanism**: if "is_correct" is treated as a binary concept in their formalism, prefill and final-token DoM should both estimate γ̄_correctness up to the causal inner product, and ⟨γ̄_prefill, γ̄_final⟩_C should be ≫ 0. **Falsifiable experiment**: compute Cov(γ_Qwen2.5-1.5B) from the unembedding matrix W_U, whiten both DoM directions by Cov(γ)⁻¹/², and recompute cos. If still ≈0 (e.g. < 0.15), the LRH unification fails for chain-of-thought correctness — i.e., the paper's frame does *not* extend from single-token concepts (king↔queen) to span-level reasoning verdicts. If cos jumps to ~0.7+, the orthogonality in F-3 was an inner-product artifact and we re-frame F-3.

2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — Theorem 2.5 makes a sharp prediction.** If correctness is a causally-separable concept, then α·Cov(γ)⁻¹·γ̄_DoM_prefill added at the L19 prefill activation of incorrect-labeled MATH-500 problems should monotonically raise P(correct) without altering off-target concepts (length, language, domain). **Mechanism**: their derivation says intervention in λ̄_W direction is a sufficient statistic for changing W. **Falsifiable experiment**: K=1 generation on the 257/500 incorrect 1.5B problems with α ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4} along Cov(γ)⁻¹·γ̄_DoM at L19 prefill only; if accuracy is flat or non-monotone, the static-direction LRH-derived steering recipe fails for correctness. This both (a) refutes a strong reading of H-1 (per-position steering works), and (b) refutes the paper's claim that their formalism extends to causally-separable abstract concepts in reasoning models.

3. **F-2 + F-9: Theorem 2.2 says probe predictions are logit-linear in λᵀγ̄_W; correctness AUROC of 0.7731 with vanilla DoM may *underperform* the LRH-prescribed probe.** Their theorem promises a logit-linear, calibration-clean probe once you use the causal inner product. Our prefill DoM is a Euclidean-mean-difference probe. **Mechanism**: if correctness fits the LRH frame, then a Mahalanobis-whitened probe (logistic regression on Cov(γ)⁻¹/² · h_L19_prefill features) should beat raw DoM AUROC and be more calibrated. **Falsifiable experiment**: refit the 5-fold OOF probe with the whitened features. Three outcomes — (a) AUROC > 0.80 with better Brier score → paper's frame *strengthens* F-2 and F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy is even more expected); (b) AUROC unchanged within ±0.01 → correctness fits LRH but our raw-DoM was already saturating; (c) AUROC drops materially → correctness is not a linear concept in the paper's sense, contradicting the paper's central applicability claim for reasoning-grade signals.

(Bonus, weaker.) The paper restricts attention to single-token outputs and binary concepts that can be *deterministically read off the output token*. F-1, F-4, F-5, F-7, F-10 all live over 1024-token spans where there is no single counterfactual output token, so the paper's theorems don't formally apply. This is itself a kind of refutation of generality, not a refutation of any specific F-N number.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Causal inner product (Mahalanobis by Cov(γ)⁻¹)** — whiten activations and concept directions by the inverse of the unembedding-row covariance matrix.
  *Replication cost*: ~20 min CPU. We already have the Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Qwen-2.5-7B unembedding matrices on disk; computing Cov(γ) and inverting is a single-shot d × d operation (d=1536 for 1.5B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — required to evaluate Refutations 1, 2, 3 and to retest H-1 under LRH-prescribed steering.

- **Counterfactual-pair concept direction** — γ̄_W := normalized mean of γ(y_i(1)) − γ(y_i(0)) over single-token counterfactual pairs.
  *Replication cost*: minutes once token pairs are chosen.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **partially** — we already do effectively the embedding-side analog (DoM = mean correct − mean incorrect activation). Borrowing their *unembedding*-side procedure would let us cross-check by computing γ̄_correctness from output-token statistics over the 500 MATH-500 problems (e.g. final-answer-token differences for correct vs incorrect rollouts), which is a different quantity than what we use today.

- **Leave-one-out (LOO) concept-direction estimation** — for each pair (y_i(0), y_i(1)), estimate γ̄_W on the held-out remainder and project the held-out pair onto it.
  *Replication cost*: cheap.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — clean way to quantify direction stability per-problem; complements F-2's k-fold OOF AUROC with a per-instance projection histogram à la Figure 2 of the paper.

- **Causal-orthogonality heatmap** — compute |⟨γ̄_W, γ̄_Z⟩_C| for all concept pairs and visualize as a heatmap.
  *Replication cost*: minutes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — for B/D bucket directions and the prefill DoM, this would tell us whether bucket membership is causally separable from correctness or entangled. Direct test for F-7 mechanism.

- **Static additive intervention λ_C,α(x) = λ(x) + α·λ̄_C with sweeps over α ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}** — small additive scalar at one position.
  *Replication cost*: 1 H100-day for full MATH-500 sweep at K=1.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly tests H-1 under LRH-prescribed direction; supersedes our previous steering attempts which used uncorrected DoM.

- **Wikipedia random-length contexts as a non-counterfactual probe dataset** — sample text spans of random lengths from French / Spanish Wikipedia, project activations onto γ̄_W to test whether subspace direction acts as a probe outside counterfactual pairs.
  *Replication cost*: hours.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — out of scope; our domain is math reasoning.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Linear representation" should be defined via counterfactuals, not informal direction-finding.** A concept is a latent W with counterfactual outputs Y(W=w); a linear representation is a direction such that γ(Y(1)) − γ(Y(0)) ∈ Cone(γ̄_W) almost surely. *Intersects F-2, F-3, H-1 directly* — our DoM is a counterfactual-style mean-diff over correct/incorrect rollouts, so it fits the frame, but we've never separated unembedding-side from embedding-side directions and we've never applied Cov(γ)⁻¹.

- **Causal separability as the right notion of orthogonality.** Two concepts are causally separable iff Y(W=w, Z=z) is well-defined for every (w, z). Concepts that *can* vary independently *should* be represented orthogonally — this is the design principle of the inner product, not an empirical observation. *Intersects F-3* — if "prefill correctness signal" and "final-token correctness signal" are not causally separable (they're the *same* concept), they should coincide, not be orthogonal. F-3 thus becomes an explicit anomaly to explain.

- **Inner product is the load-bearing free parameter, not the directions themselves.** Training only identifies γ up to invertible affine transformation, so any geometric statement about Euclidean cosines is essentially arbitrary. *Intersects all of F-1, F-3, F-7, F-9, F-10* — the entire DoM/PR/CoE machinery uses Euclidean inner products on hidden states. The paper would say: **redo the headline numbers with whitened activations** before drawing geometric conclusions, especially the F-10 PH-at-Gaussian-null result (PH is metric-dependent, and a different metric could change the persistence diagram).

- **Riesz isomorphism between input and output spaces.** The unembedding direction γ̄_W and the embedding direction λ̄_W are linked by λ̄_W = M·γ̄_W. *Intersects F-2 vs H-1* — this gives a principled way to convert our probe direction (embedding-side) into a steering direction (intervention-side) without requiring counterfactual sentence pairs in the input space, which we've never had.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Choice of D in M⁻¹ = G·D·Gᵀ is a d-degree-of-freedom free parameter; paper picks **D = I_d**, giving the closed form M = Cov(γ)⁻¹. We should adopt the same default and flag it as an arbitrary choice.
- Use **single-token vocabulary subset only** when constructing γ̄_W from word pairs (LLaMA-2 + SentencePiece; same restriction applies to Qwen-2.5).
- Concept-direction estimator: γ̃_W = (1/n_W) Σ [γ(y_i(1)) − γ(y_i(0))], then γ̄_W = γ̃_W / sqrt(⟨γ̃_W, γ̃_W⟩_C). The normalization uses the causal inner product, not Euclidean.
- Intervention is **additive at a single position** with α small (≤0.4), not a multi-token / multi-layer perturbation.
- Code: github.com/KihoPark/linear_rep_geometry — has reference implementation of Cov(γ)⁻¹ extraction and the BATS pipeline.
- Appendix D.6 verifies that the chosen inner product satisfies the uncorrelatedness condition empirically — worth replicating for Qwen-2.5 to confirm Assumption 3.3 holds.
- The paper notes explicitly that they **do not** address intermediate-layer activations — Theorem 3.2 is over the final unembedding/embedding spaces. Translating the inner product to L19 hidden states is a non-trivial extension and the paper flags it as future work. We must not naively pretend the theorems apply to an arbitrary residual layer.

## 2311.03658 — The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models (Park, Choe, Veitch, ICML 2024)

**Relevance:** Theoretical anchor for the entire DoM/probe/steering pipeline used in P11. The paper formalizes the linear-representation hypothesis via counterfactual pairs, unifies subspace / measurement / intervention notions of "linear representation," and identifies a non-Euclidean **causal inner product** M = Cov(γ)^{-1} that is required to make any geometric statement (cosine, projection, orthogonality) semantically meaningful. Our entire F-2 / F-3 / F-7 / F-9 / H-1 stack uses Euclidean inner products on hidden states; the paper says this is provably under-identified up to invertible affine transformation, which means **every geometric quantity in the project is potentially an inner-product artifact** and should be retested with Cov(γ)^{-1}-whitened activations. The paper also gives an explicit recipe — λ̄_W = Cov(γ)^{-1} γ̄_W — for converting a probe direction into a side-effect-free steering direction, which is the canonical missing piece in our H-1 attempts.

Adding α·Cov(γ)^{-1}·γ̄_W to context activations changes target-concept logits monotonically while leaving causally-separable off-target concept logits invariant.
