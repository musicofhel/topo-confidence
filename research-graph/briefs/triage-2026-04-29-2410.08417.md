# Triage brief — 2410.08417 — Bilinear MLPs enable weight-based mechanistic interpretability

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08417
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the framing of F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM as a "discovered" feature) — the
   direction may be a static weight-level eigenvector, not a learned activation
   pattern.** The paper shows that for bilinear MLPs the top eigenvectors of the
   interaction tensor `B = w v^T` for a given output direction `u` are highly
   consistent across training runs (cos sim 0.8–0.9), are derivable from weights
   alone with no input data, and the top 1–2 eigenvectors capture a large
   fraction of the output behaviour (correlation ~0.65 → 0.85 going rank 1→10).
   *Mechanism:* if SwiGLU at L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B is approximated by a bilinear
   MLP (drop the SiLU `σ` from `(Wx) ⊙ σ(Vx)`), then eigendecomposing
   `u_correct · B` (where `u_correct` is the residual-space pullback of a
   correctness logit) should yield a top eigenvector aligned with the prefill
   DoM direction *without ever touching MATH-500 activations*.
   *Falsifiable:* compute that eigenvector from L19 W,V weights only, take
   `cos(weight_eig_top1, prefill_DoM)`. If `> 0.5`, F-2's "we discovered the
   direction by averaging" framing is downgraded — the direction was
   pre-determined by weights and any halfway-decent activation contrast finds it.
   If `< 0.2`, the weight-based bilinear approximation fails for SwiGLU and the
   refutation does not fire.

2. **Refutes the F-3 interpretation (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 ⇒ "two
   different computations") — the orthogonality may be two different
   eigenvectors of the *same* L19 correctness interaction matrix being
   differentially activated by position-dependent inputs.** The paper repeatedly
   shows interaction matrices `Q = u · B` whose spectra contain *multiple*
   well-separated eigenvectors (e.g. positive sentiment ↔ negative sentiment in
   the negation circuit, with eigenvalues +0.62 / −0.66). The XOR-like quadratic
   activation `λ(v^T x)²` means a single output direction `u` can be reached via
   geometrically orthogonal `v_i` depending on which input regions the prompt
   activates. *Mechanism:* eigendecompose the L19 correctness direction in a
   bilinear-Qwen surrogate; check whether `prefill_DoM ≈ v_1` and
   `final_DoM ≈ v_2` (or some pair) of the *same* `Q = u · B`. *Falsifiable:* if
   the top-2 eigenvectors of one shared `Q` reproduce the prefill/final pair
   with cos > 0.5 each, F-3's "different computations" reading must be retracted
   in favour of "same circuit, two activation modes."

3. **Refutes F-2's AUROC ceiling (0.7731 OOF) — paper predicts a quadratic probe
   should beat the linear DoM probe by a non-trivial margin if MLP-mediated
   features are bilinear.** Paper Eq. 3: `x^T Q x = Σ λ_i (v_i^T x)²` — outputs
   are *quadratic* in the eigenvector basis with XOR-like behavior (high
   activation if input aligns strongly with positive *or* negative regions, but
   not both). A linear logistic probe on residual-stream activations is a
   first-order Taylor expansion of this quadratic; it must lose information.
   *Mechanism:* the residual stream at L19 has just been written into by
   layer-19 MLP. If that MLP is effectively bilinear, correctness signal lives
   in `(v^T h)²`, not `v^T h`. *Falsifiable:* train a *quadratic* probe
   `Σ_i α_i (v_i^T h)²` on the same OOF 5-fold prefill data using top-k SVD
   directions of the activation matrix as `v_i`; report AUROC. If quadratic
   AUROC > 0.85 (vs linear 0.7731), the F-2 number is a probe-class artefact.
   If quadratic AUROC ≈ 0.78, F-2's linear ceiling is the real geometric
   ceiling and the bilinear refutation does not fire.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 OOF)** — paper's eigendecomposition method
  could in principle recover the same direction from weights alone (refutation 1
  above), assuming SwiGLU ≈ bilinear is a good approximation.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — paper's multi-eigenvector
  story for a single output direction (refutation 2 above) reframes the
  orthogonality as intra-circuit, not inter-circuit.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — paper finds active
  correlation rises monotonically with eigenvector rank, saturating only around
  rank ~10–20 per output feature. CoE-60 uses 60 features across all 28 layers,
  so its ceiling is at least 60-rank cumulative. F-9's empirical match is then a
  coincidence of label noise, not a structural redundancy — *partially* in
  tension with F-9.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — the
  paper explicitly disqualifies stock SwiGLU MLPs from clean weight-based
  circuit attribution. H-13 should either (a) restrict to attention-only
  attribution, or (b) accept that bilinear-surgery is a prerequisite. Strong
  methodological constraint on H-13.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** — paper's
  weight-derived eigenvectors are also "label-free" in a different sense
  (no labels needed, just weights). Both are competing routes to a
  data-cheap correctness probe.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — paper's
  pseudoinverse-of-eigenvector adversarial-mask construction (Sec 4.4) is a
  cleaner steering recipe than raw direction addition; suggests H-1's negative
  results may be due to cross-eigenvector activation rather than the steering
  premise being wrong.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Bilinear interaction tensor `B_aij = w_ai v_aj`** — exact algebraic
  reformulation of bilinear MLP `g(x) = (Wx) ⊙ (Vx)` as a third-order tensor.
  *Replication cost:* trivial — algebra. *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — used
  as the underpinning for refutations 1–3 by approximating SwiGLU as bilinear.
- **Symmetric reduction `B^sym = (B + B^T)/2`** — anti-symmetric part vanishes
  when evaluated with identical inputs `x^T A x = 0`, leaving a symmetric tensor
  with real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors.
  *Replication cost:* trivial. *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — required
  preprocessing if we run the bilinear approximation.
- **Eigendecomposition of `Q = u · B` for a chosen output direction** — produces
  ranked eigenvectors `v_i` with eigenvalues `λ_i`; activation in the
  `u`-direction is `Σ λ_i (v_i^T x)²`.
  *Replication cost:* ~5 min CPU per layer per direction at Qwen-1.5B sizes
  (`d_hidden = 1536`).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — directly testable on Qwen L19 if we accept a
  bilinear approximation, see FE-117.
- **HOSVD / flattening-then-SVD when no output features available** —
  `B_(out, in×in) = Σ σ_i u_out^(i) ⊗ q_in×in^(i)`, with each `q` further
  decomposable.
  *Replication cost:* depends on tensor size; for `d_hidden = 1536`, the
  flattened tensor is 1536 × 2,359,296 — large but tractable with truncated SVD.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* probably no — we already have `u_correct` from
  unembed pullback, so direct eigendecomp suffices.
- **Pseudoinverse-of-eigenvectors steering mask** — given top-k eigenvectors
  `V`, construct mask `m_i = (V^+)_i:` so that `v_j^T m_i = δ_ij`, suppressing
  cross-talk between eigenvectors.
  *Replication cost:* trivial post-eigendecomp.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — relevant to H-1 retry, see FE-118.
- **SAE-feature transformation of bilinear tensor** — `b̃_abc = Σ e^out_ai
  b_ijk f^in_bj f^in_ck`, giving sparse interaction matrices between input and
  output dictionary atoms.
  *Replication cost:* requires SAEs trained on Qwen-1.5B residual stream
  (~H100-week if not already cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* no — out of scope, no SAEs in current pipeline.
- **Active-only correlation as low-rank quality metric** — correlation between
  rank-k approximation of `Σ λ_i (v_i^T x)²` and full feature activation,
  computed only over inputs where the feature is active (avoids noise floor
  dominating).
  *Replication cost:* trivial post-eigendecomp.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — natural diagnostic for refutation 1 / FE-117.
- **Truncation-accuracy curve** — retain only top-k eigenvalues per output
  direction; plot accuracy vs k as a low-rank validity check (paper finds drops
  of ~0.01% retaining only top few eigenvectors out of 512).
  *Replication cost:* requires retraining or surgical zeroing.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* maybe — relevant to F-9 reframing if we run the
  bilinear approximation.

## Approaches & framings

- **Weight-based vs activation-based interpretability** — paper argues
  weight-based is more robust because activation methods (SAEs, linear probes,
  attribution patching) depend on input distribution and only show *which*
  features are present, not *how* they are formed. Intersects directly with
  the entire P11 pipeline, which is activation-only. If the paper's frame is
  right, our findings F-1…F-10 may have a weight-level dual that is more stable
  across training runs and across datasets.
- **Quadratic feature representation (XOR-like)** — features activate when input
  aligns with positive *or* negative eigenvector regions, but cancel if both —
  fundamentally non-linear. Reframes "DoM is a direction" as "DoM is an
  eigenvector with a quadratic XOR activation"; refutation 3 is the falsifiable
  consequence.
- **Static features, not data-derived features** — eigenvectors are determined
  at training time and persist; the input dataset only chooses *which*
  eigenvector activates, not which exists. Tension with F-2's framing as a
  data-discovered direction (refutation 1).
- **Architectural intervention as interpretability tool** — replace SwiGLU with
  bilinear (paper claims minimal performance loss) to *gain* interpretability.
  Connects to H-13 (head-level attribution) — H-13 is essentially asking for
  this kind of clean weight attribution and the paper says the architectural
  swap is the price.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/tdooms/bilinear-decomposition
- Models trained from scratch (no fine-tuning of pretrained Qwen / Llama as
  bilinear) — limits direct application to our existing checkpoints.
- Bilinear MLP `g(x) = (Wx) ⊙ (Vx)`; biases handled by augmenting
  `W' = [W; b_2], V' = [V; b_2], x' = [x, 1]`.
- Symmetric form `b_aij = (1/2)(w_ai v_aj + w_aj v_ai)` — required preprocessing
  before eigendecomposition.
- For Qwen SwiGLU `g(x) = (Wx) ⊙ σ(Vx)` with σ = SiLU = `x · sigmoid(x)`, σ is
  near-linear for small inputs and smoothly nonlinear for large; bilinear
  approximation is best at small post-W activation magnitude. Worth caveating
  any cross-architecture mapping.
- Eigenvalue spectra are typically very low-rank: 512-dim hidden has only ~10
  non-negligible eigenvalues per output direction in MNIST.
- Regularization (Gaussian input noise σ ≈ 0.15) materially improves eigenvector
  interpretability and sparsifies the spectrum. Pretrained Qwen had no such
  input-noise regularization; expect noisier spectra if we run this.
- SAE-based interaction analysis used 6-layer TinyStories ("ts-tiny") and
  12/16-layer FineWeb ("fw-small", "fw-medium") — all bilinear from scratch,
  not retrofitted.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Bilinear approximation of L19 SwiGLU + eigendecomposition of correctness
  direction** — load Qwen-2.5-1.5B from cache, extract `W_19, V_19` (gate_proj
  and up_proj), drop SiLU, compute `B = W_19 ⊗ V_19` (full tensor too large;
  use direct `Q = u_correct · B = (u_correct^T (W_19^T) ⊙ I) V_19` form), pull
  `u_correct` back from logit-lens of the embedding direction for "correct" /
  "incorrect" surrogate tokens or via the learned probe vector itself,
  symmetrize, eigendecompose, take top-1 eigenvector, compute
  `cos(top_eigenvec, prefill_DoM)`. *Cost:* 30 min CPU once weights are loaded
  to memory. *Cached artifact:* `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` has the
  prefill DoM probe vector; Qwen weights are on the H100 pod.
- **Quadratic probe at L19 vs linear DoM AUROC** — same OOF 5-fold setup as
  F-2; replace `logistic(v^T h)` with `logistic(α + Σ_i β_i (v_i^T h)²)` where
  `v_i` are top-k SVD directions of the activation matrix. *Cost:* 10 min CPU
  on cached prefill activations. *Cached artifact:*
  `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` lists the 5-fold split;
  raw prefill activations are in the gitignored NPZ.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2410.08417 — Bilinear MLPs enable weight-based mechanistic interpretability (Pearce, Dooms, Rigg, Oramas, Sharkey, ICLR 2025)

**Relevance:** Bilinear MLPs are GLU variants without element-wise nonlinearity:
`g(x) = (Wx) ⊙ (Vx)`. The authors show such layers are fully expressible as a
third-order tensor `B`, and that for any chosen output direction `u` the
interaction matrix `Q = u · B` admits an eigendecomposition whose top
eigenvectors are interpretable, low-rank, and stable across training runs. The
output activation in direction `u` is `Σ_i λ_i (v_i^T x)²` — quadratic and
XOR-like. Demonstrated on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, TinyStories, and FineWeb
bilinear transformers, including a hand-traced sentiment-negation circuit.
Bears on F-2 (the L19 prefill DoM may be a static weight eigenvector rather
than a data-discovered direction), F-3 (orthogonality of prefill and final-token
DoM may be intra-circuit, not inter-circuit), and H-13 (head-level attribution
is materially harder on stock SwiGLU than on a bilinear surrogate).

**Key claim we tested:** Output features along a chosen direction are dominated
by 1–2 eigenvectors of `Q = u · B` and these eigenvectors are recoverable from
weights alone. Translated to our setting: the L19 prefill correctness direction
should be recoverable as the top eigenvector of `Q` constructed from L19
SwiGLU weights, *under a bilinear approximation* (drop SiLU).

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — FE-117 will compute `cos(weight_eig_top1,
prefill_DoM)` after bilinear-approximating L19 SwiGLU; FE-118 will test
whether a quadratic probe exceeds the linear DoM AUROC ceiling 0.7731. Until
those run, we cannot determine whether the bilinear/quadratic lens is
applicable to Qwen-2.5-1.5B (whose SwiGLU σ = SiLU is nonlinear) or whether
the lens fails because the architectural mismatch is too large.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE117, P11-FE118, P11-FE119, H-117, H-118.

**Status:** TO TEST
