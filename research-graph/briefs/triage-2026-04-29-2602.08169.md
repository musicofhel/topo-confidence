# Triage brief — 2602.08169 — Spherical Steering: Geometry-Aware Activation Rotation for Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08169
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos = 0.046).** Spherical Steering builds the prototype μ from *last-token* activations of `x ‖ y+` vs `x ‖ y-` and then re-uses that single μ to rotate every later token. F-3 says the answer-side DoM and the prefill DoM are nearly orthogonal — so on a CoT regime the paper's μ would be a final-token-side direction that is geometrically unrelated to the prefill signal which actually carries the predictive AUROC (F-2, 0.7731). **Mechanism**: a one-direction Slerp gate cannot simultaneously align with two near-orthogonal correctness directions; only the closer one is improved, and rotating toward μ\_T at prefill positions could actively hurt by pulling away from the prefill direction. **Falsifiable test**: replicate Spherical Steering on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 K=1 using (a) μ extracted from last-token answer activations as in the paper, (b) μ extracted from prefill-DoM. If F-3 holds, (a) does not lift K=1 accuracy at α∈[0.4, 0.8] while (b) does — which contradicts the paper's "single contrastive μ universally controls" claim.

2. **F-4 (asymmetric collapse) refutes the antipodal axis assumption μ\_H = -μ\_T.** Section 3.2 / 3.3 hard-codes `μ_H = -μ_T` and the vMF gate's δ = p\_H - p\_T = -tanh(κ s\_T) (Eq. 17). F-4 says correct trajectories collapse harder at the final token than incorrect ones — i.e. their geometric profiles are *not* mirror images. **Mechanism**: under asymmetric collapse, `mean(correct) - mean(incorrect)` does not equal `-(mean(incorrect) - mean(correct))` in any geometrically meaningful sense once you consider that the two clusters have different anisotropies; the "antipode" is a random tangential direction, not the opposite class prototype. **Falsifiable test**: compute μ\_T = mean(correct, normalized) and μ\_H = mean(incorrect, normalized) separately on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 at L19. Measure cos(μ\_T, -μ\_H). If the paper is right, this is ≈ +1; if F-4 is right, it should drift toward 0 on math-CoT despite being closer to +1 on TruthfulQA last-token (a non-CoT setting). Asymmetry-of-collapse predicts an even stronger drift on the *final token* than on prefill.

3. **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed) refutes the durability of static-target Slerp.** The paper applies a fixed-target Slerp at every decoding step toward a static μ\_T extracted offline. If H-17 is right, the natural per-step rotation of activations is dominated by RoPE phase progression — a deterministic, per-position rotation that would *un-do* any static-target Slerp at the next position because μ\_T does not co-rotate with RoPE. **Mechanism**: the paper claims t-controlled Slerp, but at decoding step j+1, RoPE re-rotates every Q/K projection by Δφ; the angle between the just-rotated h^(ℓ)' and the still-static μ\_T at step j+1 is mostly noise relative to step j. **Falsifiable test**: log θ\_j = arccos(μ\_T^⊤ ĥ\_j^(ℓ)) across decoding steps with and without intervention on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500. If steering makes a durable directional change, θ\_j should track t monotonically; if H-17 is right, θ\_j returns to baseline within ~5–10 tokens regardless of α.

4. **F-2 (prefill > final-token DoM, AUROC 0.7731 vs 0.7186) and F-7 (D-bucket has a *collective* signature, not per-problem)** jointly refute the paper's claim (§4.4.1 / Figure 3) that "activation magnitude alone is non-discriminative." The paper measures L2 norms layer-by-layer on TruthfulQA last-token and finds < 1% relative deviation between truthful and hallucinated. **Mechanism**: TruthfulQA is single-token-answer-style and doesn't exercise the breathing curve (F-1, F-5) that produces collective magnitude signatures in MATH-500 reasoning trajectories. F-7 finds D-bucket's geometric signature is *collective* — visible only when you look at the whole population, not per-problem — and Pathway 11 found per-layer norm gaps tied to bucket structure. **Falsifiable test**: reproduce Figure 3 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 at L19, splitting last-token activations by K=1 correctness *and* by K=8 bucket (A/B/C/D from F-7). Predict: in the per-bucket split, the norms separate even when the simple correct/incorrect split shows < 1% deviation. That would refute the paper's universal-claim about magnitude irrelevance.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — paper provides a complete, training-free recipe (Slerp + vMF gate, Eq. 9–15) and reports +11.09% MC1 on LLaMA-3.1-8B at layer 24. Layer 24 is at 24/32 ≈ 75% depth; our L19 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers) is at 19/28 ≈ 68% depth — broadly comparable. This is a direct method blueprint for H-1.
- **H-17 (RoPE-mechanical DoM rotation)** — Spherical Steering is silent on RoPE but its assumed semantic-effect-of-rotation-toward-static-μ is exactly the prediction H-17 challenges; replicating their result on Qwen-2.5-1.5B + logging θ\_j becomes a direct H-17 test.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper's last-token-pair extraction is the *final-token analog* of our DoM\_final (AUROC 0.7186). Their headline gain (10–15% MC) on a non-CoT benchmark ≈ what we'd predict if final-token DoM is sufficient for MC tasks but inferior for reasoning tasks; this *partially confirms* the directional view of correctness while leaving prefill superiority untouched.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill\_DoM, final\_DoM) = 0.046)** — paper's framework ignores this distinction; provides a clean target for direct contradiction on math.
- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse)** — paper's antipodal assumption is an empirically testable side-constraint we should measure on our own data.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — paper's effective-rank "collapse efficiency" metric (§4.4.2) is a *transferable* counter-metric we could add to all P11 steering ablations; it operationalizes "how much representation manifold did we damage to gain X% accuracy."
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — paper's evidence that single-layer intervention captures most of the achievable MC gain (§5.2 Table 3) corroborates F-9's "one direction is enough" theme.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Slerp activation rotation (Eq. 9–11)** — closed-form geodesic interpolation `h' = ‖h‖ · [sin((1-t)θ)/sin θ · ĥ + sin(tθ)/sin θ · μ_T]`; preserves norm exactly.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU to add to existing Pathway 11 steering harness; uses already-cached L19 activations and the existing prefill DoM.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — clean alternative to additive DoM steering; directly compares to the additive H-1 baseline.

- **vMF confidence gate (Eq. 12–15)** — two-class softmax over cosine similarities to {μ\_T, -μ\_T}, then `t = clip(α·(δ-β)/(1-β), 0, 1)` with β as selectivity threshold and α as max strength. Equivalent to a tanh threshold on the truthful cosine (Eq. 17).
  *Replication cost*: ~15 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — input-adaptive gating that turns off when the activation is already aligned; directly testable on our prefill DoM signal.

- **Effective-rank "collapse efficiency" sweep (§4.4.2)** — stack all token activations into a matrix, compute SVD, derive effective rank, sweep intervention strength α and plot accuracy vs Δrank.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 h CPU on cached pathway11_h100 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a much-needed counter-metric; would let us quantify whether F-2's DoM steering damages the manifold or operates within it.

- **Norm-discrimination test (Figure 3)** — plot mean L2 norm of last-token activation per layer, split by correctness; verify magnitude is non-discriminative.
  *Replication cost*: 10 min on cached data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct sanity check on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / 7B P11 NPZs and a falsifier for refutation #4.

- **Antipodal-prototype construction from last-token contrastive pairs (Eq. 1–4)** — μ = Δ/‖Δ‖ where Δ = mean(positive last-token activations) - mean(negative). Compare to our existing DoM construction.
  *Replication cost*: trivial; likely already computed in another form.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cross-check whether our prefill DoM and a "paper-style" final-token contrastive prototype are close to ±μ or off-axis (refutation #2).

- **Multi-layer Slerp with K=2-3 layers (§5.2 Table 3)** — K=1 gets most MC gain; K=2-3 boosts INFO without further MC; K≥4 degrades both. Asymmetric layer effect.
  *Replication cost*: ~4 h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — interesting only if single-layer L19 Slerp shows a measurable MATH-500 lift first.

- **ICL+steering compatibility test (§5.3)** — 5-shot ICL alongside steering; tests whether the two interventions stack or conflict.
  *Replication cost*: ~2 h H100 if any few-shot prompts exist; otherwise build them.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for now — outside the K=1 / K=8 MATH-500 evaluation frame we use.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Collapse efficiency" Pareto frame** — performance gain per unit effective-rank drop. A reusable lens for evaluating any intervention on our pipeline; ties to F-4 (asymmetric collapse) by promising a quantitative way to compare correct- and incorrect-cluster damage from steering.

- **"Direction encodes semantics, magnitude is normalization-stabilized scaffolding"** — paper's central claim (§4.4.1). Aligns with our F-2/F-3 framing of DoM as a directional probe; provides a clean dichotomy we can test on math (refutation #4 predicts the dichotomy *fails* on bucket-level, even if it holds at the simple correctness split).

- **"Antipodal axis" framing for correctness** — paper assumes truthful and hallucinated prototypes are antipodes. F-4 makes this testable. If symmetric on TruthfulQA but asymmetric on MATH-500, we'd have a clean demarcation between "answer-truthfulness" tasks and "reasoning-correctness" tasks.

- **"Geometry-consistency" as a steering desideratum** — replaces the "did accuracy go up?" frame with "did accuracy go up *without* damaging the representation manifold?" Useful for our H-1 evaluation: a Slerp version of DoM steering may move MATH-500 less than additive but cost dramatically less collapse, which would itself be a finding.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/chili-lab/Spherical-Steering
- Models: LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (sweet spot layer 24, β = -0.05 for the α-sweep table), Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (β = 0.3 for gated, single-layer)
- Hyperparameter ranges: α ∈ (0, 1] (rotation strength), β ∈ [-1, 1) (selectivity threshold; β ≥ 1 → no steering, β < -1 → ungated), κ > 0 (vMF concentration; sets calibration of cosine→confidence map but doesn't reorder states — Eq. 17–18)
- Multi-layer: K=2-3 layers gives best Pareto on TruthfulQA generative; layer choices for K>1 are listed only as "carefully chosen depths" — no principled selection rule (the paper flags this as future work)
- Slerp degenerates safely at θ → 0 (set h' = h)
- Inference cost: trivial — three vector products per token per intervened layer
- Restoration of magnitude (`h' = ‖h‖ · ĥ'`) is critical to the norm-preservation claim

## Replicable intermediates

- **Norm-overlap test on cached P11 NPZs**: load `pathway11_h100/.../activations_*.npz` for Qwen-2.5-1.5B, slice last-token at L19 for each MATH-500 problem, plot mean ± std of ‖h‖ per layer split by K=1 correctness. Sanity-checks paper Figure 3 on math; falsifies refutation #4 if magnitudes overlap.
- **Antipode test**: compute μ\_T = normalized mean of correct-class last-token activations and μ\_H = normalized mean of incorrect-class; report cos(μ\_T, -μ\_H). If << 1, refutation #2 fires.
- **Effective-rank baseline**: stack all per-token activations from one P11 batch, compute SVD, derive effective rank. Establishes the "before-intervention" baseline against which any future Slerp ablation can be measured.
- **Slerp-at-L19 dry run on existing infrastructure**: Pathway 11 already has a hooked-Qwen forward pass. Adding a Slerp(ĥ, μ\_prefill, t=0.5) call before the L19 residual emerges is ~30 lines of code on the existing harness and reuses the cached prefill DoM. Cheapest H-1 advance currently available.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2602.08169 — Spherical Steering: Geometry-Aware Activation Rotation for Language Models (You, Deng, Chen, 2026)

**Relevance:** Training-free activation steering paper that replaces additive DoM-style interventions with a closed-form Slerp rotation toward a contrastive prototype direction, gated by a von Mises-Fisher confidence rule. Directly relevant to H-1 (per-position DoM steering). Provides a complete, runnable recipe (single-layer pick: layer 24 of LLaMA-3.1-8B; Eq. 9-15) that we can lift onto Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 with ~30 lines on the existing P11 harness. Also introduces a "collapse-efficiency" frame (gain per unit effective-rank drop, §4.4.2) that is reusable as a counter-metric for any P11 steering ablation.

**Key claim we tested:** A single contrastive last-token prototype μ\_T and its antipode μ\_H = -μ\_T, applied via Slerp + vMF gate at one mid-late layer, dominates additive baselines on both MC accuracy *and* open-ended generation quality without manifold collapse.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Paper does not run on math/CoT regimes, so direct comparison to F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) and H-1 is unverified. F-3 (orthogonal prefill/final DoM) and F-4 (asymmetric collapse) generate concrete predictions that the paper's last-token-only extraction and antipodal-axis assumption *break* on MATH-500. FE169-172 are designed to test these refutations alongside attempting to replicate the paper's headline lift.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE169 (Slerp replication), P11-FE170 (antipodal falsification), P11-FE171 (collapse-efficiency analysis), P11-FE172 (H-17 durability test), H-169, H-170

**Status:** TO TEST
