# Triage brief — 2410.12299 — Semantics-Adaptive Activation Intervention for LLMs via Dynamic Steering Vectors

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12299
Triaged: 2026-04-29

ICLR 2025. Wang, Yang, Peng (Edinburgh / Huawei / RMIT). Code: https://github.com/weixuan-wang123/SADI.

**One-line summary**: SADI ablates the canonical contrastive-pair fixed-steering pipeline (ITI, CAA, ActAdd) by replacing the precomputed steering *vector* with a sparse binary *mask* over (layer × element) and at inference applies that mask to scale up the **input's own** last-token activations: `A'_q = A_q + δ(A_q ⊙ M)`. The core empirical claim — directly relevant to our project — is Table 4: identical mask, same `δ`, but using fixed `D ⊙ M` instead of the input-conditional `A_q ⊙ M` drops StoryCloze accuracy from 79.75 → 69.42 (–10.33). They argue static steering vectors are *misaligned* with input semantics and that the instance-conditional direction is what matters.

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "L19 prefill DoM is the strong correctness predictor" framing.**
   F-2 reports AUROC 0.7731 from a *single fixed direction* (population mean of correct − incorrect prefill activations at L19) used as a linear probe. SADI's Table 4 ablation argues that fixed contrast vectors are systematically dominated by input-conditional masked-scaling on language tasks (StoryCloze: fixed 69.42 vs dynamic 79.75; identical mask `M`). Mechanism of refutation: if F-2's signal is genuinely "the direction" rather than "these dimensions of the residual stream are the discriminative ones for this input", then a SADI-HIDDEN-style top-K mask `M_L19` derived from the same correct/incorrect contrast and applied as `A_q ⊙ M_L19` (no fixed offset) should yield strictly *lower* AUROC than the static DoM, because SADI throws away the population-mean direction. **Falsifiable experiment**: on the cached Stage 2 prefill L19 NPZs, compute the per-dimension mean activation difference, binarize to top-K (sweep K ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512}), and probe correctness using only the masked subspace projection of each problem's own L19 activation. If this probe matches or beats AUROC 0.7731, the "fixed direction" interpretation of F-2 is weakened.

2. **Refutes F-3's "prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal (cos 0.046)" as evidence of functional separation.**
   F-3 treats the population-mean prefill DoM and population-mean final-token DoM as proxies for two distinct functional directions, and reads cos ≈ 0 as evidence they encode different things. SADI explicitly cites Wang et al. 2024a;b for the empirical observation that "activation patterns can vary significantly across different inputs for the same task" — meaning the population-mean direction can be a noise-dominated average over heterogeneous *instance-conditional* directions. Mechanism: if the per-problem optimal-discrimination direction at prefill rotates with input semantics, the global mean is shrunken toward zero and any other shrunken global mean (final-token) will appear nearly orthogonal to it *by averaging artifact*, not by genuine functional decoupling. **Falsifiable experiment**: cluster the 500 MATH-500 problems into K ∈ {4, 8, 16} clusters by prefill L19 representation (KMeans), compute per-cluster prefill DoM and per-cluster final-token DoM, and report cos(prefill_DoM_k, final_DoM_k) per cluster. If per-cluster cosines are systematically higher than 0.046 (e.g. mean ≥ 0.3 with low variance), the orthogonality claim of F-3 is partly an averaging artifact and the "two genuinely different directions" reading needs revision.

3. **Refutes H-1's (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) implementation form.**
   H-1 currently plans a CAA-style fixed-vector add at each position. SADI's Table 4 result is a head-on ablation showing the *same* contrastive-pair information, when packaged as a fixed offset rather than as `A_q ⊙ M`-style scaling, is significantly worse on every task tested (COPA, StoryCloze, Winogrande, SST2, TriviaQA — all five). Mechanism: applying a fixed direction to a problem whose own activation already lies far from that direction can push the residual *off* the local manifold. **Falsifiable experiment**: when H-1 is run, include a SADI-HIDDEN arm in addition to the planned fixed-DoM arm: same K (top dimensions of L19 prefill DoM), but at inference apply `A_q + δ × (A_q ⊙ M_topK)` instead of `A_q + δ × DoM`. If on MATH-500 the dynamic arm produces a meaningfully larger Δaccuracy than the fixed arm, the *form* of H-1 needs revision — and our case for "the prefill direction is the steering direction" weakens to "the prefill *subspace* is what matters; the direction is per-input".

4. **Refutes F-9's "CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM" by suggesting head-level granularity may dominate hidden-state granularity.**
   F-9 treats L19 hidden-state DoM as the canonical single-layer baseline. SADI's Table 1 shows SADI-HEAD ≥ SADI-HIDDEN on 6 of 7 multiple-choice tasks (e.g. NLI: SADI-HEAD wins, hidden-state arms dropping below baseline on TruthfulQA generation). The implicated mechanism is that attention-head output spaces carry discriminative correctness signal that is washed out when summed back into the hidden state. **Falsifiable experiment**: re-extract Stage 2 with per-attention-head outputs at L19 (not just residual), compute per-head DoM AUROC for correctness, and compare best-head AUROC and head-pooled AUROC against the residual-stream L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731. If best-head or top-K-head pool exceeds 0.7731 by ≥0.02, then F-9's claim that L19 DoM is the "right substrate" needs to be narrowed — CoE-60 may not be redundant with L19 *residual* but redundant with a richer head-level decomposition that L19-residual under-captures.

5. **Refutes F-7's "D-bucket has a distinctive *collective* (not per-problem) geometric signature" by offering a circuit-level alternative.**
   F-7 frames the D-bucket signature as cross-problem geometric structure. SADI's Figure 5 element-overlap analysis directly opposes the "collective" framing: their identified critical heads / neurons / states for different tasks have surprisingly *low* overlap (Jaccard ~0.1–0.3 across multiple-choice vs open-ended). If correctness vs incorrectness of D-bucket problems is also driven by a small, specific set of heads/neurons, the signature is circuit-level (a few elements), not collective-geometric (whole-residual structure). Mechanism: F-7 may have detected the projection of a circuit-level signature into residual geometry, not a primary geometric signature. **Falsifiable experiment**: run SADI's difference-extraction (Eq. 1–2) on cached A-bucket vs D-bucket problems at L19, identify top-K heads/neurons by mean activation difference, and check whether a sparse top-K probe (K small, e.g. K ∈ {16, 64}) classifies D-bucket better than the geometric "collective" features F-7 reports. If a tiny circuit suffices, F-7's collective framing is at minimum an over-claim.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Last-token activation extraction across all layers** — concatenate the last token's activation at every layer into one `(L × d)` vector per (input, output) pair. *Replication cost*: trivial, ~minutes once forward pass is set up. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already cache prefill L19; trivial to extend to all layers from Stage 2 NPZs if per-layer is preserved.

- **Contrastive-pair mean-difference vector at the last-token position only** (Eq. 1–2). Cheaper than CAA which uses the answer-letter position. *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached MATH-500 NPZs. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — equivalent to recomputing F-2's DoM but ensures last-token-only convention, useful for direct method comparison.

- **Top-K binarization** of mean-difference vector to produce sparse mask `M ∈ {0,1}^{L×d}`. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — feeds directly into the F-2 refutation experiment.

- **Adaptive steering rule** `A'_q = A_q + δ(A_q ⊙ M)` (Eq. 5). Equivalent to "scale up the input's own activations on the selected dimensions by `(1 + δ)`". *Replication cost*: trivial intervention, but requires inference (H100 if we want to measure downstream MATH-500 accuracy with the intervention applied). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for H-1; would be the dynamic arm.

- **Random-mask ablation** as a sanity baseline (their Table 4: 7-pt drop). *Replication cost*: trivial — same code path with random K-subset. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, mandatory baseline if we run any top-K mask experiment.

- **Element-overlap (Jaccard) heatmap across tasks** (Figure 5) for diagnosing head/neuron specialization. *Replication cost*: trivial once top-K sets are computed. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, especially for D-bucket vs A-bucket vs B-bucket overlap.

- **Activation-difference layer-by-layer heatmap** (Figure 3a) — visualize which (layer, head/neuron) pairs carry discriminative signal. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly answers "is L19 actually the right layer or is it more diffuse?" for our own data.

- **Hyperparameter sweep over (K, δ)** with results-grid (Figure 2). *Replication cost*: K × δ inference runs; H100 hours. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for H-1.

- **Per-head granularity intervention** (SADI-HEAD: top-K heads across all layers). Requires per-head outputs, not just residual stream. *Replication cost*: re-extraction H100 day if per-head outputs were not saved. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if F-9 refutation experiment is prioritized.

- **Multilingual evaluation with English-derived mask** (Table 10) — tests whether identified critical elements transfer across languages. *Replication cost*: not directly applicable to MATH-500. *We'd plausibly run this*: no — out of scope for our regime.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Dynamic steering" reframing of activation engineering**: shifts the question from "find the right direction" to "find the right *components* (which heads/neurons/dimensions) and then trust the input's own activation on those components". Intersection: forces us to consider whether F-2/F-3's whole-residual fixed-direction framing is the right one, or whether a sparse top-K subspace is more appropriate.

- **Linear representation hypothesis as base assumption** (Park et al. 2024) — concepts are linear directions. SADI takes this further by claiming that scaling along *the input's own projection onto the concept's components* preserves semantics better than adding a fixed concept-vector. Intersection: orthogonal to F-3 — if input projections rotate, then the "concept direction" is itself locally varying.

- **Element-level functional specialization across tasks** (Figure 5 minimal overlap) — argues that different tasks recruit different heads/neurons/states. Intersection: if correctness on MATH-500 is similarly element-specialized, our single-direction-at-L19 picture is incomplete.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Activation extracted from **last token only** of `(input || output)`. Different from CAA's "answer-letter position" — note this when re-implementing as our default position convention.
- δ swept over `{5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 35, 45}` depending on task; optima vary by task and by SADI variant. Some of their best cells use surprisingly high δ (StoryCloze SADI-HEAD optimum at K=8, δ=25 → 79.75).
- K (number of top elements) typically small: best heads K ∈ {2,4,6,8,10}; best neurons K ∈ {top-100}; best hidden states K ∈ {top-10}.
- 150 contrastive pairs sufficient; gain saturates by 350. Implies: if we adapt to MATH-500, even a 200-problem held-out contrast set is enough.
- Hyperparameters are task-specific; recommend per-task validation sweep.
- Fixed-vector ablation uses Eq. 6 `A_q + δ(D ⊙ M)` — this is the form to compare against.
- Random-mask ablation: assign K random `1`s, keep δ fixed — produces ~7-point drops. Mandatory baseline.
- Activation differences for **heads** concentrate in mid-to-late layers (Figure 3a, Llama2-7B-chat L17–L28); for **neurons and hidden states** concentrate in the *final* layer (Figure 3b). Worth noting: this disagrees somewhat with our project's L19 (mid-late) emphasis for hidden states — our 28-layer Qwen-2.5 picks L19, theirs picks ~L32.
- Code: https://github.com/weixuan-wang123/SADI — Python, available, MIT license.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2410.12299 — Semantics-Adaptive Activation Intervention for LLMs via Dynamic Steering Vectors (Wang, Yang, Peng, ICLR 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct method comparison for H-1 and a sharp methodological challenge to F-2's "fixed direction" framing. SADI re-casts contrastive-pair activation engineering as (top-K binary mask) × (input's own activation × δ) instead of (input + δ × fixed contrast vector). Their Table 4 ablation — same K, same δ, but fixed-vector form drops StoryCloze 79.75 → 69.42 — is the cleanest published evidence that fixed steering vectors are systematically dominated by input-conditional masked scaling on language tasks. If this transfers to MATH-500 / 1024-tok regime, H-1's planned implementation form is wrong and F-2's framing weakens from "the direction is at L19" to "the discriminative subspace is at L19; the direction is per-input."

**Key claim we tested:** Dynamic, mask-based, input-conditional scaling outperforms fixed contrast-vector addition for activation engineering when both use the same contrastive-pair information.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Two cheap CPU-only experiments (P11-FE71, P11-FE72) directly probe whether their core claim transfers to our regression-of-correctness setting at L19 prefill on MATH-500. The H100-arm (P11-FE73) is a natural extension of H-1. Their tasks (multiple-choice + short open-ended) don't overlap our 1024-tok CoT regime — empirical claims are motivation for methodology transfer, not direct evidence in our domain.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, H-71, H-72, H-73, H-1, F-2, F-3, F-9.
