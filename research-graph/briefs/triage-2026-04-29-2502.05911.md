# Triage brief — 2502.05911 — GRAIT: Gradient-Driven Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning for Effective Hallucination Mitigation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05911
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Against F-2 ("L19 DoM is *the* correctness predictor"):** GRAIT defines a learned "refusal direction" as `g_idk = E[∇_θ L(x_idk, y_idk; θ)]` — the average per-sample gradient of the cross-entropy on `idk` (incorrect→"I don't know") training samples through a LoRA adapter, projected to low-dim via Johnson–Lindenstrauss. This is a *learned* direction in the full LoRA parameter space, not a single residual-stream axis. **Mechanism:** if a probe formed by projecting Qwen-2.5-1.5B's per-sample LoRA gradient onto `g_idk` yields a higher 5-fold OOF AUROC than F-2's 0.7731 (prefill L19 DoM), then F-2's "single residual axis" framing is too narrow — the correctness signal that DoM picks up is a downstream projection of a multi-layer gradient direction. **Falsifiable test:** LoRA-tune Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 with `T_C=0.5` (their MMLU setting), compute `g_idk`, project a held-out fold's gradients, compare AUROC to prefill DoM 0.7731.

2. **Against F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality is meaningful):** GRAIT's *Assumption 2* posits ⟨E[∇L(x*, y_idk)], E[∇L(x*, y_ik)]⟩ ≈ 0 as a clean mathematical property of refusal vs answer gradients on TriviaQA / MMLU (LLaMA-7B-Chat). F-3's `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046` is structurally the same statement at a residual-stream level. **Mechanism:** GRAIT's appendix A.3 verifies orthogonality only on factual-QA datasets where "ik vs idk" splits along knowledge-recall axes. If we replicate the gradient-inner-product test on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with **MATH-500** ik/idk (K≥7/8 correct vs K=0/8 correct), and find ⟨g_ik, g_idk⟩ ≫ 0 (Pearson > ~0.5), then F-3's near-orthogonality is a *factual-recall regime artifact* — math-reasoning correctness is *not* a separable direction. That guts H-1's (per-position DoM steering) entire premise. **Falsifiable test:** compute LoRA gradient means for D-bucket vs A-bucket on MATH-500, report `cos(g_ik, g_idk)`.

3. **Against H-12 (label-free SEPs replace supervised DoM for refusal):** GRAIT is an explicitly *supervised, training-time* refusal-injection method (uses N=10 forward passes per question to compute correctness `C(x)`). Its results (best LLaMA3-8B THS 36.4 on MMLU vs the best baseline CRaFT at 34.0) suggest training-time refusal injection beats post-hoc abstention probes. **Mechanism:** if GRAIT-tuned models match or exceed our prefill-DoM-gated selective prediction (F-8: 71.6% acc on answered at coverage 0.5) on the same MATH-500 split, then H-12's whole premise — "label-free probes obsolete supervised DoM" — is inverted: the better path is *more* supervision, baked into model weights, not less. **Falsifiable test:** LoRA-fine-tune Qwen-2.5-1.5B with GRAIT on MATH-500 (idk = K=0/8, ik = K≥7/8), evaluate THS at coverage 0.5, compare to F-8's selective-prediction curve.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) — GRAIT's "Refusal Influence" `I_ref(x) = ⟨∇L(x;θ), E[∇L over D_idk]⟩` is the *gradient-space analogue* of projecting onto DoM in residual space. Both are inner-product-with-class-mean-direction tests.
- **F-3** (`cos(prefill, final) = 0.046`) — GRAIT's orthogonality assumption is the same statement at the gradient level. Their Appendix A.3 validation parallels our finding; both could be wrong in a reasoning regime.
- **F-8** (selective-prediction 71.6% at coverage 0.5) — GRAIT's best THS=36.4 (LLaMA3-MMLU) is the supervised-fine-tune analogue of our test-time DoM gating. Both target the same trade-off (Pw down, Pc preserved).
- **F-9** (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — GRAIT reports Pearson 0.886 between idk-influence on `D_ik` vs `D_idk` (their Figure 5). That high inter-set correlation says correctness signal lives in a low-rank direction shared across known/unknown samples — corroborates F-9's "single L19 DoM captures most of the variance" finding.
- **H-1** (per-position DoM steering moves accuracy) — GRAIT shifts the *training-time* loss weighting via gradient inner product; H-1 shifts *test-time* activations. They're orthogonal mechanisms attacking the same target.
- **H-12** (label-free SEPs replace supervised DoM) — GRAIT is the *fully supervised* baseline H-12 should compare against, not just SEPs vs probe DoM.
- **H-13** (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits) — GRAIT's per-sample LoRA gradient could be decomposed by layer/head exactly the same way; provides a methodological template.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Refusal Influence (`I_ref`)** — inner product of a training sample's per-sample gradient with the mean gradient of the `idk` set: `I_ref(x) = ⟨∇L(x;θ), E_{x_o ~ D_idk}[∇L(x_o^idk, y_o^idk; θ)]⟩`. Used to rank `idk` samples by their contribution to refusal learning.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (one LoRA-tune pass on Qwen-1.5B + per-sample gradient extraction over 500 MATH problems).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct analogue of DoM, would test whether gradient-direction beats activation-direction as a correctness oracle.

- **Stable Influence (`I_sta`)** — inner product of sample gradient with the *difference* of class means: `I_sta(x) = ⟨∇L(x;θ), E[∇L over D_idk] − E[∇L over D_ik]⟩`. Larger `I_sta` ⇒ less over-refusal contribution.
  *Replication cost*: same pass as `I_ref` (free additional projection).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly measures the orthogonality-of-class-gradients (F-3 analogue).

- **Adaptive softmax-weighted SFT loss** — assigns each `idk` training sample a weight `ω(x) = e^{I_sta(x)/τ} / Z` with temperature `τ=0.05`, then minimizes `Σ ω(x)·L_idk + Σ L_ik`. Constraint: `Σω = 1`.
  *Replication cost*: cheap once `I_sta` is computed; modifies SFT loop only.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we ever run a refusal-tuning experiment.

- **LESS-style low-dim gradient features** — LoRA (r=64, α=16) + Johnson–Lindenstrauss random projection of per-sample gradient vectors to make pairwise inner products tractable. Cites Xia et al. 2402.04333.
  *Replication cost*: existing LESS code is OSS.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required for any future "gradient-as-DoM" experiment.

- **Truthful Helpfulness Score (THS)** — single-number metric defined as the cross-product ratio of `OS_2 × OS_1 / OU × OS_1` in the (P_c, P_w) coordinate plane, where S_1 is the baseline (no-refusal) operating point and S_2 is the refused model's. Encodes "did we move below the baseline frontier" in one signed scalar.
  *Replication cost*: 5 minutes once you have P_c, P_w, P_r per model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — strictly better summary of selective-prediction quality than the AUROC-at-coverage-0.5 we currently use for F-8. Consider adding to validate_claims.py.

- **Correctness gating with N=10 sampling for OEQA** — GRAIT computes `C(x)` by sampling `N=10` answers and measuring agreement; `T_C=0.5` is the threshold. For MCQA they use ground-truth-token probability.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 for 500-problem MATH set at K=10.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes (we already have K=8 from EXP-033 — close enough).

## Approaches & framings

- **Hallucination = "answering when you should refuse"** vs our framing **correctness = "predict K=1 success from prefill geometry"**. GRAIT recasts the same correctness-prediction problem as a *training-time* loss-weighting problem rather than a *test-time* abstention problem. This shifts the burden from "find a good probe" (our F-2/F-8) to "shape the loss landscape so the model abstains correctly without an external probe."
- **Gradient inner product as influence proxy.** Pruthi et al.'s TracIn first-order Taylor expansion of the loss is the underlying machinery. This re-frames "what does this training sample teach the model?" as "what gradient direction does it pull θ in?" — the same logic that motivates DoM, lifted from activation space to parameter space. Useful for H-13 (head-level circuit attribution).
- **Two-axis trade-off in (P_c, P_w) space**, summarized by THS triangle area. Cleaner than risk-coverage curves for headline numbers; we should consider adopting it for F-8.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- LoRA: `r=64, α=16, dropout=0.1, lr=2e-4`.
- Training: 3 epochs, `max_seq_len=2048`, XTuner framework (https://github.com/InternLM/xtuner), evaluation via OpenCompass (https://github.com/open-compass/opencompass).
- Hyperparameters: `T_C = 0.5` (correctness threshold), `τ = 0.05` (weight-softmax temperature).
- Sample budget: 5,000 MMLU train, 10,000 TriviaQA train, **1:4 ratio of ik to idk** samples in `D_rait`.
- Hardware: 8× A100-80GB.
- Init models: LLaMA2-7B-Chat, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct.
- Code release: https://github.com/opendatalab/GRAIT (promised in paper).
- Gotcha: the gradient-mean direction `g_idk` is computed *once* on the initial model and *not* updated during training (their "static" treatment, acknowledged limitation). A dynamic version would re-estimate per-epoch.
- Few-shot evaluation: MCQA 5-shot, OEQA 3-shot for the correctness step; 0-shot greedy for the actual eval.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2502.05911 — GRAIT: Gradient-Driven Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning for Effective Hallucination Mitigation (Zhu, Jiang, Wu et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** GRAIT is the gradient-space analogue of our DoM probing pipeline: it learns a "refusal direction" `g_idk = E[∇L over D_idk]` in LoRA parameter space and uses gradient inner products `⟨∇L(x), g_idk⟩` to (a) select training samples (Refusal Influence) and (b) re-weight the SFT loss (Stable Influence). The structural parallel to our F-2 (prefill L19 DoM) and F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality) is exact — only the space (parameter-grad vs residual-activation) differs. GRAIT's Assumption 2 (orthogonality of class gradient means) is the gradient-space twin of F-3's `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046`. The Truthful Helpfulness Score (THS) is a cleaner single-number summary of selective-prediction quality than the risk-coverage curves we currently use for F-8.

**Key claim we tested:** "A learned gradient direction `g_idk` over an `idk` (incorrect→refuse) training set ranks samples by their contribution to refusal learning, and softmax-weighting by `⟨∇L, g_idk − g_ik⟩` mitigates over-refusal." — restated in our terms: "the correctness signal lives in a low-rank gradient direction whose mean is approximately orthogonal to the answer-class gradient mean."

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — GRAIT validates orthogonality on TriviaQA / MMLU (factual recall) only. Whether the same property holds on MATH-500 (multi-step reasoning) is open. P11-FE73, FE74, FE76 are the cheap sanity checks; H-46, H-47, H-48 are the deeper questions.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, H-46, H-47, H-48; closest siblings F-2, F-3, F-8, F-9.

**Status:** TO TEST
