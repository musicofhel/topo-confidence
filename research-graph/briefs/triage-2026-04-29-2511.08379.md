# Triage brief — 2511.08379 — SOM Directions are Better than One: Multi-Directional Refusal Suppression in Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08379
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Piras, Mura, Brau, Oneto, Roli, Biggio (AAAI 2026).

## Refutations

1. **F-2 ("single L19 prefill DoM direction predicts correctness, AUROC 0.7731") may be a low-rank approximation of a multi-direction refusal/correctness manifold.**
   - *Cite:* F-2 (single-direction framing for prefill correctness signal).
   - *Mechanism:* Piras et al. prove a 1-neuron SOM converges to the class centroid (Proposition 1) — i.e. their k=1 reduces *exactly* to Arditi/our DoM. Yet adding 2–7 SOM-derived directions multiplies refusal-suppression ASR from 0% → 59% on Llama2-7B and from 39% → 96% on Gemma2-9B. If the refusal subspace is k≥2, by structural analogy our correctness subspace at L19 prefill may also be k≥2, and the 0.7731 AUROC is what a single-direction projection achieves on a higher-rank signal.
   - *Falsifiable test:* Train a 4×4 SOM on cached `pathway11_h100` L19 prefill activations partitioned by K=1 correctness; derive k∈{2..7} directions via centroid-subtraction; fit a multi-direction logistic probe; compare OOF AUROC vs the 0.7731 single-DoM baseline. Predicts: AUROC ≥ 0.80 if the manifold story holds; ≈ 0.7731 if F-2's single-direction framing is correct.

2. **F-3's "prefill ⊥ final-token, cos = 0.046" may be a methodological artifact of comparing two single directions instead of two subspaces.**
   - *Cite:* F-3 (prefill/final-token orthogonality on Qwen-2.5-1.5B).
   - *Mechanism:* If prefill and final-token are each k≥2 manifolds (per the SOM-on-refusal precedent), the "two scalars are orthogonal" claim is just one of k×k pairwise cosines, most of which could be non-orthogonal. The "can I solve this?" / "did I solve this?" geometric independence narrative depends on this scalar.
   - *Falsifiable test:* Run a 4×4 SOM at L19 prefill (correct vs incorrect) AND a separate SOM at L19 final-token; compute the full k_pre × k_fin cosine matrix between centroid-derived direction sets, plus principal-angle CCA between the two subspaces. If max pairwise cosine ≥ 0.4 or CCA principal angle ≤ 30°, F-3 collapses from "geometrically unrelated subspaces" to "two views of the same lower-rank object".

3. **F-7's "D-bucket signature dissolves per-problem" may be a power-failure of single-direction PR.**
   - *Cite:* F-7 (group-PR 14.49 vs per-problem-PR 12.84 on D-bucket).
   - *Mechanism:* Per-problem PR uses one global eigenstructure on a k=20 NN ball — invisible to a multi-direction signal that lives along *several* axes. SOM Bayesian optimization shows the right number of refusal directions varies model-to-model (Llama2-7B optimal at higher k, Mistral-RR at k=2). Per-problem geometry might separate D-bucket only when projected onto a SOM-derived multi-direction basis.
   - *Falsifiable test:* For each MATH-500 problem, project its L19 prefill activation onto the top-k SOM-derived directions (k swept 2..7 via BO), compute per-problem norm/distance to D-bucket centroid in this k-dim subspace; test whether D-bucket separates from A/B/C buckets at per-problem scale. If yes, F-7's "dissolves per-problem" framing flips to "the signal exists but is multi-directional".

4. **H-1 ("per-position DoM steering moves accuracy") under-specifies the steering target.**
   - *Cite:* H-1 (per-position single-direction bank).
   - *Mechanism:* SOM paper shows that ablating a *single* refusal direction is dramatically inferior to ablating 2–7 — even at one fixed model layer. By analogy, steering with a single per-position DoM may move some problems but miss the multi-direction structure that controls others. The paper's k=2 vs k=1 comparison is an apples-to-apples preview of what would happen if H-1 were extended to per-position-multi-direction.
   - *Falsifiable test:* Before launching H-1's full per-position experiment, run a small ablation: at position 100, compare K=1 accuracy under (a) ablating the single L19 DoM, (b) ablating top-k SOM-derived directions for k∈{2,3,5}. If multi-direction ablation shifts accuracy more than single-direction, H-1's experimental protocol is under-powered as designed.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** Piras et al. Proposition 1 establishes the formal equivalence between a 1-neuron SOM and DoM. This tells us our F-2 method *is* a special case of their framework — hence the upper-bound on F-2's AUROC may be the multi-direction SOM probe AUROC on the same data.
- **F-3 (cos = 0.046):** The "two refusal subspaces are not parallel" framing maps onto our prefill-vs-final orthogonality. If the SOM finding generalizes, F-3's headline scalar is replaced by a subspace-vs-subspace principal-angle.
- **F-7 (group PR 14.49 / per-problem 12.84):** The "manifold visible at group scale, invisible per-problem" pattern is structurally analogous to "single-direction visible only with enough samples; multi-direction visible per-instance".
- **F-8 (selective prediction 71.6% at coverage 0.5):** If multi-direction probe boosts AUROC, the same SP curve at coverage 0.5 should improve by ~3-5 points without architectural change.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering):** Direct precedent says "single direction is insufficient" for refusal; per-position DoM bank for correctness should be tested with k>1 directions per position.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes, label-free):** SOM offers an alternative label-light approach (it uses class membership but not per-instance correctness labels in its training) — competing methodology to consider alongside SE.

## Methodologies extracted

- **SOM-derived multi-direction extraction.** Train a 4×4 hex SOM on activations of one class (harmful / incorrect), compute centroid ν of the other class (harmless / correct), set direction `r_ι = w_ι − ν` for each neuron ι. Replaces single DoM with up to 16 candidate directions.
  *Replication cost:* ~1h CPU on cached `pathway11_h100` Stage-2 NPZs (16 neurons × 10k iters is trivial).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* **yes** — directly tests F-2's single-direction framing.

- **Bayesian optimization over (k, neuron-subset) for direction selection.** k swept ∈[2,7], BO with 128 trials for k≤3 and 512 for k>3. Selects the active subset of SOM directions that maximize the downstream metric.
  *Replication cost:* ~2h CPU (BO loop is the expensive part; each trial is a sub-second logistic refit).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* **yes** — the same BO frame replaces our hand-picked single L19 with an automatically selected multi-direction probe.

- **Layer selection by ablation efficacy (a per-model l\* table).** They report distinct optimal layers per model (Llama2-7B=13/32, Qwen-7B=16/32, Qwen2.5-7B=18/28, Gemma2-9B=23/42, etc.). Mirrors our P8 layer-wise sweep but selected by *causal ablation* impact rather than probe AUROC.
  *Replication cost:* fits inside existing P8 layer sweep — cheap.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* **partial** — already done at probe level (P8); the causal-ablation version requires forward-pass intervention not yet wired up for MATH-500.

- **Centroid-offset direction parameterization (`r = w − ν`).** Drop-in alternative to "DoM = mean(class A) − mean(class B)". Allows class A to be multi-modal (one centroid per SOM neuron) while class B remains a single anchor.
  *Replication cost:* trivial (CPU minutes).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* **yes** — would confirm whether our incorrect-class is multi-modal.

- **Proposition 1: stochastic-gradient convergence of 1-neuron SOM to class mean.** Mathematical bridge from SOM to centroid/DoM with rate `(1−α)^t‖w(0)−μ‖ + ασ`.
  *Replication cost:* none (theoretical).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* **n/a** — adopt as theoretical justification for why our single-direction baseline is exactly k=1 of their framework.

## Approaches & framings

- **Refusal as a manifold, not a direction.** Same conceptual move as our prefill-DoM-as-direction → potential prefill-as-subspace shift. If adopted, reframes F-2 from "single direction" to "rank-k manifold" with k chosen by BO.
- **Class-centroid vs neuron-set asymmetry.** One class collapses to a single point ν, the other expands to k centroids. Useful when one bucket (correct) is approximately unimodal and the other (incorrect) is multi-modal — a hypothesis we have not tested on D/B/C-bucket structure (F-7).
- **Causal ablation as gold standard for direction validity.** They evaluate by zeroing out projections during forward pass on harmful prompts and measuring ASR — not by held-out probe AUROC. Suggests our F-2 probe-AUROC framing is suggestive but not causal; matches the still-unrun activation-patching plan in P11-FE3.
- **Generalization-of-priors via formal proof.** Demonstrating new-method ⊃ old-method via a convergence proposition is a compact framing we should consider for any future method we introduce that subsumes DoM (e.g. semantic-entropy, CoE single-layer reduction).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 4×4 hex SOM (16 neurons), T=10,000 iterations, α_t = 0.01/(1+2t/T), Gaussian neighborhood σ=0.3.
- BO over direction subsets: 128 trials for k∈{2,3}, 512 trials for k∈{4..7}.
- Per-model layer selection table is empirical (no theory link to layer index); their Qwen-7B optimum is L16/32, our Qwen-2.5-1.5B uses L19/28 — different model family so no direct transfer.
- Mistral-7B-RR (Representation Rerouting defense) is included as a hard case — multi-direction still hits 25.79% ASR vs single-direction 5.03%, suggesting the multi-direction effect survives a defense built against single-direction attacks. Useful caveat for F-2: even if a "defense" (e.g. probe regularization) flattens the single direction, multi-direction signals may persist.
- No public code link extracted from abstract pages; check v2 PDF for code release if we replicate.

## 2511.08379 — SOM Directions are Better than One: Multi-Directional Refusal Suppression in Language Models (Piras, Mura, Brau, Oneto, Roli, Biggio; AAAI 2026)

**Relevance:** Direct methodological challenge to single-direction probe/steering methods. Proves that a 1-neuron SOM converges to the class centroid (Proposition 1) — i.e. the standard difference-in-means used in our F-2/F-3 (and in Arditi et al. 2406.11717) is exactly the k=1 special case of their framework. **Key claim we tested:** Refusal in LMs is multi-directional, and difference-in-means is a rank-1 special case of a richer SOM-based extraction.
