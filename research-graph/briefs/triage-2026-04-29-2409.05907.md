# Triage brief — 2409.05907 — Programming Refusal with Conditional Activation Steering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **CAST's per-layer condition-vector grid search would refute F-2's "L19 is the privileged layer" claim if a different layer dominates for correctness.**
   F-2 says prefill L19 DoM gives the best correctness AUROC (0.7731). CAST's exact protocol — extract a PCA-PC1 condition vector at every layer from a correct/incorrect contrast set, grid-search (layer, θ, direction) to maximise F1 — typically picks early layers (Qwen-1.5-1.8B harmful condition lands at L8, Hermes-2-Pro L7, OLMo L8; behavior layers 10-20). On Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers), running CAST's pipeline on the MATH-500 correct/incorrect contrast may find a *condition* layer ≪ 19 with higher AUROC. If so, F-2's privileging of L19 is an artifact of using only DoM and not searching layers + projection-and-threshold properly. Falsifiable test: replicate CAST's PCA-then-grid-search protocol on cached P11 H100 per-layer prefill activations, confirm/refute that L19 is still optimal.

2. **CAST's PCA-on-mean-centered-contrasts would refute the implicit assumption in F-2/F-8 that mass-mean DoM is the right operator.**
   F-2 / F-8 use difference-of-means (DoM) as the prefill direction; they treat this as if the choice doesn't matter. CAST argues PCA-PC1 of [mean-centered positive; mean-centered negative] is a more principled extractor (citing Tan et al. 2024, Jorgensen et al. 2023, Ball et al. 2024). PC1 ≈ DoM only when classes are roughly Gaussian with equal covariance. If F-2's correct/incorrect groups have unequal covariance (which F-4's "asymmetric collapse" finding actually predicts — correct trajectories collapse harder), PCA-PC1 will diverge from DoM and can be substantially better. A side-by-side AUROC comparison on cached prefill activations is a direct refutation test for the "DoM is fine" assumption that F-2 implicitly relies on.

3. **CAST's saturation property would refute F-7's "D-bucket has a distinctive *collective* (not per-problem) signature" if a CAST-extracted D-bucket condition vector classifies D-bucket prompts.**
   F-7 claims the D-bucket signal is collective, not per-problem. CAST's framework explicitly assumes prompts in the same condition class share a low-dimensional direction extractable by PCA on the *full prompt* hidden state, with saturation reached on small contrast sets (~hundreds of pairs). If we run CAST's protocol with D⁺ = 36 D-bucket prompts and D⁻ = 207 A-bucket prompts and grid-search across layers, and CAST successfully finds a condition layer + threshold that separates D from A at AUROC ≫ 0.7, then F-7's "collective signature" framing is wrong — the signature is per-prompt (just at a non-obvious layer/projection). Conversely, if CAST fails (grid search returns near-chance on all layers), F-7 is corroborated against an industry-strength supervised method.

4. **CAST's success on Qwen-1.5-Chat-1.8B with α=4, layers 10-20 (a *fixed* steering recipe) puts a constraint on F-3's "fixed-vector steering won't work because prefill ⊥ final-token DoMs" line of reasoning.**
   F-3 reports cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046. This is read as evidence that a single fixed direction cannot steer through generation (motivating H-17's RoPE de-rotation hypothesis). But CAST applies the *same* behavior vector at *every* token from layer 10 to layer 20 with α=4 on Qwen-1.5-Chat-1.8B and gets >86% refusal rate on harmful prompts, ≤2% on harmless — a clear behavioral effect. If P11 replicates CAST's exact recipe on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (architecturally similar) and the refusal vector successfully steers behavior, then *the orthogonality result does not preclude fixed-vector steering* — it just precludes naive prefill→final-token transfer. This is a constructive existence proof against the strongest reading of F-3 / H-1's motivation, even though F-3 itself (as a geometric measurement) survives.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731): CAST's framework offers a richer extractor (PCA-PC1 vs DoM) and a richer thresholding function (tanh-projection cosine-similarity at a grid-searched layer) for the same conceptual move ("a single inference-time direction predicts a binary outcome"). Direct head-to-head AUROC comparison is a 1h CPU run on cached prefill NPZs.

- **F-8** (selective-prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% answered acc, K=2.5): CAST's grid search over (layer, threshold, direction) with F1 maximisation is functionally identical to choosing a coverage operating point. F-8 picked 50% coverage; CAST picks F1-optimal threshold. Re-running F-8's selective-prediction with CAST's F1-optimised threshold (and possibly PCA-PC1 instead of DoM) gives a direct comparison and may move 71.6% up or down.

- **H-1** (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy): CAST is the canonical, working version of activation-addition steering for refusal at α=4, layers 10-20 on Qwen 1.8B. If we port CAST's open-source IBM toolkit (`github.com/IBM/activation-steering`) to Qwen-2.5-1.5B and use it as the harness for H-1's per-position DoM intervention, we eliminate "is our steering harness correct?" as a confound. CAST also gives us the empirically-validated layer range to start from (10-20).

- **H-12** (label-free SEP replaces supervised DoM for refusal): CAST is the supervised baseline that label-free probes must match. CAST's ~99% refusal classification with ~700 paraphrased contrast pairs sets a lower bound for what label-free SEP needs to clear before H-12 is interesting.

- **H-17** (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical): CAST's empirical success with a *fixed* (non-rotated) behavior vector at every generated token argues *against* H-17 in its strongest form — if rotation were purely mechanical and uncomputed, CAST's fixed vector would lose effectiveness mid-generation (since RoPE rotation accumulates with token position), but in CAST it doesn't. This may downgrade H-17's priority; or alternatively it implies CAST's steering relies on a *different* mechanism than the rotating-DoM family (e.g., the post-residual delta vs the residual itself).

- **H-19** (C_exact verification routing): CAST's logical-composition of condition vectors (e.g., "if c_harmful or c_legal then refuse") is the right framework for composing F-2 (correctness condition) with a separate "verify-failed" condition vector. The "refuse-if-incorrect" gate that drives selective compute (H-19) is exactly a CAST condition.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PCA-PC1 of mean-centered contrastive pairs** — first principal component of [(h⁺ᵢ − μ_l); (h⁻ᵢ − μ_l)] alternating-row matrix, where μ_l = (H⁺_l + H⁻_l)/2. Replaces simple DoM.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached P11 prefill NPZs across 28 layers.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct head-to-head with F-2's DoM AUROC 0.7731. If PC1 ≫ DoM, F-2 needs an updated number.

- **Cosine-similarity-of-projection thresholding** — `f(sim(h, proj_c h))` with binary step at θ; uses cosine of h against its own projection onto c (not h against c directly). Subtle: this measures alignment between h and c-direction-component-of-h, normalising out h's magnitude.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (20min CPU once vector is in hand).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct upgrade to F-8's selective-prediction operator.

- **tanh-of-projection wrapper** — `sim(h, tanh(proj_c h))` "for more predictable behavior" (asserted, not ablated in the paper).
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if the cosine-projection version is adopted; cheap A/B.

- **Grid search over (layer, θ, direction)** — exhaustive search over all layers L, threshold values, and direction (> vs <). F1-maximised.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached per-layer prefill activations (28 × ~50 thresholds × 2 directions ≈ 2,800 evaluations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — currently F-2's L19 claim has not survived a real layer-grid search with both directions.

- **Logical composition of condition vectors (AND / OR / negation)** — `f(·) = 1 if sim(h, proj_a h) > θ_a OR sim(h, proj_b h) > θ_b`; flip > to < for negation (duality property).
  *Replication cost*: trivial once individual conditions exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for composing F-2 (correctness) with F-7 (D-bucket) or H-19 (verifier) into a single decision rule.

- **Asymmetric recording protocol: behavior on suffix, condition on full prompt** — behavior vectors averaged over the contrasting *suffix* tokens (response prefix), condition vectors averaged over *all prompt tokens*. The paper claims this distinction matters.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — F-2 currently uses prefill (full prompt mean) for both prediction and the (untested) intervention. CAST predicts the right way to actually intervene is to re-extract on suffix tokens.

- **Saturation curve** — measure refusal-rate-on-test as a function of |D⁺ ∪ D⁻| size; CAST claims plateau at ~10² pairs.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (subsample the existing cached training contrast set).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we re-extract the prefill direction with PCA — gives a sample-complexity baseline.

## Approaches & framings

- **Condition vs behavior typed separation** — CAST distinguishes *what triggers* (condition vector, gate-only role) from *what does* (behavior vector, intervention). Could re-frame F-2 (currently a single artifact) into a *condition* (predicts correctness, used as a gate) plus a hypothetical *behavior* (would push toward correctness if we knew it). Forces the question "what is the corresponding behavior vector for the correctness condition?" — currently unanswered.

- **Programmable behavioral rules** — explicit reframing of inference-time steering as rule composition (`if c₁ and not c₂ then +v_a − v_b`). Maps onto H-19's "verify-failed → spend more compute" as a CAST rule. Useful framing for selective-prediction + abstention + verification + sampling routing as a single composable gate stack.

- **Sequencing: condition-check on prefill pass, behavior-apply on every subsequent pass** — explicit architectural separation into "first forward pass = decide", "all following passes = act". This is the clean version of what F-8's selective prediction is doing implicitly; could refactor selective-prediction code around this structure.

- **Duality (flip > to <)** — the claim that flipping the comparison direction targets the *exact complement* set. Useful framing for "refuse-everything-not-correct" as the dual of "refuse-everything-incorrect".

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Open-source toolkit**: `github.com/IBM/activation-steering` — IBM/UPenn activation steering library, demo datasets included. **Use this as the steering harness for H-1 to eliminate harness bugs as a confound.**
- **Qwen-1.5-Chat-1.8B intervention points** (Table 4): condition layer 8, θ > 0.031 for harmful; behavior layers 10-20, α = 4. Closest architectural relative to Qwen-2.5-1.5B; **start H-1 from these hyperparameters** rather than guessing.
- **Llama-3.1-Inst-8B intervention points**: condition layer 5, θ > 0.034 (sexual); behavior layers 17-24, α = 1.7 (add refusal); 14-28, α = -1 (remove refusal). Lower α than Qwen — α scaling does not transfer across architectures.
- **Threshold ranges differ by model**: Hermes-2-Pro effective θ in [0.0, 0.1], Zephyr in [0.4, 0.6]. Don't assume θ transfer.
- **Behavior vector applied at every subsequent forward pass**, not just one token — the paper is explicit (Figure 11): condition checked once on the first full pass (prompt processing), behavior applied repeatedly.
- **PCA recipe**: alternate positive and negative rows (`h⁺₁; h⁻₁; h⁺₂; h⁻₂; …`), mean-center against μ_l = (H⁺_l + H⁻_l)/2, take PC1. **Not** a simple DoM.
- **tanh wrapper**: `sim(h, tanh(proj_c h))` — tanh is element-wise on the projection, claimed to make the threshold function less sensitive to outlier hidden states. Not ablated.
- **Grid search F1**: balances false-refusal and missed-refusal — not symmetric AUROC.
- **Saturation note**: extraction time scales linearly in |D|, but performance plateaus — implying the cost-effective regime is ~hundreds of contrastive pairs, not tens of thousands.

## 2409.05907 — Programming Refusal with Conditional Activation Steering / CAST (Lee, Padhi, Ramamurthy, Miehling, Dognin, Nagireddy, Dhurandhar — ICLR 2025)

**Relevance:** Introduces a typed separation between *condition vectors* (PCA-PC1 of mean-centered full-prompt activations, gated via cosine of hidden state with its projection onto the condition vector at a grid-searched layer + threshold + direction) and *behavior vectors* (PCA-PC1 of contrasting suffix activations, applied as a fixed addition at every subsequent forward pass). Demonstrates programmable refusal control on 7+ open chat models. Bears directly on F-2 (single direction predicts a binary outcome — same conceptual move), F-3 (fixed-vector steering across generation works on Qwen-1.5-Chat-1.8B), F-7 (sets a strong supervised baseline against which the "collective signature" claim must hold), F-8 (CAST's grid-search-and-F1 thresholding is the principled version of selective-prediction operating-point selection), and H-1 (provides a validated open-source steering harness as the substrate for per-position DoM intervention).

**Key claim we tested:** That a hidden-state-derived condition vector can be used as a binary inference-time gate to selectively apply a behavior vector — and that PCA-PC1 of mean-centered contrastive pairs is a principled extractor for both.
