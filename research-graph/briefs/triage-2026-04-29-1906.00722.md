# Triage brief — 1906.00722 — Topological Autoencoders

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00722
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Michael Moor, Max Horn, Bastian Rieck, Karsten Borgwardt (ETH Zürich / SIB), ICML 2020.

## Refutations

1. **F-10 may be summary-statistic-bound, not topology-bound.** F-10 reports
   AUROC 0.690 (real) vs 0.693 (Gaussian null) using *five scalar summaries*
   (`H0_max_lifetime`, `H0_entropy`, `H1_pers_entropy`, `H1_n_features`,
   `H1_max_lifetime`). Moor et al. build their loss directly on the *edge-level
   distance vector* `A^X[π^X]` selected by the 0-dim persistence pairing — the
   full persistence diagram, not five aggregates. Their Theorem 1 also bounds
   bottleneck distance between full diagrams of subsamples vs. full point cloud.
   *Mechanism*: five rank-correlated scalars are easily matched by an empirical
   Gaussian (covariance reproduces them by construction); bottleneck distance
   on full (birth, death) tuple sets is not generally matched. *Falsifiable*:
   on the same `pathway9_results/exp3a_gaussian_null_v2` cached residuals,
   compute (a) full-diagram bottleneck distance `d_b(D_real, D_null)`, (b)
   classifier AUROC on a TopoAE-style 0-dim edge-distance vector (length |π| ≈
   m−1) instead of 5 scalars. If AUROC(real, edge-vector) − AUROC(null,
   edge-vector) ≥ 0.03, F-10's null claim weakens to "PH-summary scalars are
   null-matched" rather than "PH is null-matched".

2. **F-3 orthogonality of prefill/final DoM may be a coordinate artifact, not a
   mechanism difference.** F-3 reads `cos(DoM_prefill, DoM_final) = 0.046` as
   evidence prefill and final-token correctness signals route through distinct
   subspaces. The TopoAE loss is built on *pairwise distances* and persistence
   pairings — invariant under orthogonal change of basis. Two latent codes
   that are coordinate-orthogonal can produce identical persistence diagrams
   if the within-class / between-class distance distributions match.
   *Mechanism*: cosine of mean-difference vectors measures linear
   alignment of class centroids, not topological equivalence of class
   conditional distributions. *Falsifiable*: extract (correct, incorrect)
   sub-clouds at L19 prefill and L19 final separately on the cached
   `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZ; compute bottleneck distance
   `d_b(D_prefill, D_final)` for each label group. If the inter-stage
   bottleneck distance is small (e.g. < 0.05 on normalized scale) while
   AUROC differs by 0.05+, F-3's "orthogonal therefore different mechanism"
   reading is over-claimed — same topology, different rotation.

3. **F-9 CoE-60 ≈ DoM may be single-scale, not multi-scale.** F-9 says CoE-60
   trajectory features (60-D) match L19 DoM (1-D) at AUROC 0.716 vs 0.7199 on
   MATH↔BBH transfer. Moor et al. evaluate dimensionality reduction at *multiple
   scales* via `KL_σ` (σ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0}) and show methods that tie at one
   scale diverge at others (e.g. PCA wins KL_0.01 on SPHERES while TopoAE wins
   KL_0.1 and KL_1). A single classifier-head AUROC is one σ-bandwidth; the
   1024-tok claim that 60-D trajectory information "is contained in" 1-D
   linear projection is therefore tested at one scale only. *Mechanism*: a
   1-D projection cannot in principle preserve simultaneously the
   short-range structure (intra-cluster) and long-range structure
   (inter-cluster nesting); the 60-D trajectory can. *Falsifiable*: on
   cached CoE-60 features and L19 DoM scores, compute KL_σ between
   (correct, incorrect) class density estimates at σ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0,
   10.0}. If CoE-60 wins at any extremal σ by ≥ 0.05 KL units, F-9
   "redundant" verdict needs a "redundant-at-one-scale" qualifier.

4. **F-2 single-direction sufficiency may be a linearity artifact.** F-2 reads
   prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 (OOF 5-fold) as the canonical correctness
   probe. TopoAE shows that *learned* multi-scale topology-preserving
   projections substantially outperform a fixed linear projection (e.g. AE
   alone gives KL_0.1 = 0.746 on SPHERES; TopoAE gets 0.326). *Mechanism*:
   correctness may live in topological structure (cycles, nested manifolds)
   that linear DoM cannot detect. *Falsifiable*: train a 2-D TopoAE on prefill
   L19 residuals using batch-supervised correctness labels; report best
   downstream AUROC on the 2-D latent. If > 0.80, F-2's near-saturation
   reading for linear probes fails.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null on residuals).** Moor et al. is the foundational
  differentiable-PH paper that we *would* cite as an upstream method if F-10
  ever opened back up to "richer PH summarizations". The paper's mini-batch
  stability theorem (Theorem 1) is the technical bridge from their
  full-data-cloud setting to our cached-NPZ setting.
- **F-2, F-3 (DoM as correctness predictor / orthogonality).** Their loss
  formulation is distance-based, hence rotation-invariant — directly relevant
  to interpreting `cos(DoM_prefill, DoM_final) = 0.046`.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with DoM).** Their KL_σ multi-scale evaluation
  protocol is the natural test for a "single scale or all scales" question.
- **H-16 (Marchenko-Pastur PR correction).** Tangentially relevant — both
  papers worry about finite-sample bias in geometric summaries of
  high-dimensional point clouds, but Moor et al.'s bound is on bottleneck
  distance, not eigenvalue spectra.
- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT).** Their topological loss "highly generalisable, only
  requires a distance matrix" — could be applied to abstract latents
  trajectories, but the connection is speculative.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Differentiable 0-dim PH loss `L_t = L_{X→Z} + L_{Z→X}`** — selects
  topologically relevant edges from persistence pairings on each mini-batch and
  aligns the corresponding pairwise distances between input and latent space.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day CPU to wire into our existing extractor; reference
  code at github.com/BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — provides a learnable topology-preserving 2-D embedding of
  prefill L19 residuals for refutation 4 (F-2 sufficiency test).

- **Vietoris–Rips 0-dim MST edge selection** — for d=0, persistence pairing
  edges = minimum-spanning-tree edges (worst-case `O(m^2 · α(m^2))`). The
  whole topological signature reduces to MST edge weights.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — `scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree`
  on per-batch distance matrices. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — first
  step toward refutations 1 and 2.

- **KL_σ multi-scale density divergence** — KL between Gaussian-kernel density
  estimates of input and latent, evaluated at multiple bandwidths σ ∈ {0.01,
  0.1, 1.0}. Used as both a quality metric and a hyperparameter selection
  objective (KL_0.1 minimization). *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests refutation 3 (F-9
  multi-scale qualifier) on cached CoE-60 / DoM features.

- **Subsample-PH stability bound (Theorem 1)** — bounds the probability that
  bottleneck distance between full-data and mini-batch persistence diagrams
  exceeds ε in terms of Hausdorff distance. *Replication cost*: not a method
  to run; a theorem to cite. *We'd plausibly run this*: borrow as a
  justification when running batch-PH on cached residuals.

- **ℓ-MRRE, ℓ-Cont, ℓ-Trust** — classical NLDR quality metrics on pairwise
  rank correlation between input and latent distance matrices.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 hour CPU to vendor in (e.g. `coranking` package).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful as a control diagnostic if we
  ever revisit residual-stream embedding evaluations.

- **Symbolic perturbation for unique distances** — they assume distances are
  unique (each PH entry has an infinitesimal exclusive neighborhood); ties
  are resolved via symbolic perturbation. *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — silently necessary if we run
  refutations 1 / 2 on residuals where exact distance ties are possible.

## Approaches & framings

- **PH-as-loss vs PH-as-summary**: Treats persistent homology not as a *post
  hoc* descriptor but as a *training objective*. This reframing is what
  makes F-10 falsification non-trivial — F-10 was a post-hoc summary check;
  Moor et al. show topology can be made to matter when trained for, but their
  AE baseline (KL_0.1 = 0.746 on SPHERES, much worse than TopoAE's 0.326)
  *also* corroborates F-10's "ordinary training doesn't preserve PH"
  interpretation. The framing tension is productive: F-10 is consistent with
  Moor et al.'s baseline failure but does not foreclose richer PH
  summarizations.

- **Edge-level vs scalar-level PH evaluation**: The persistence diagram is
  represented as a vector of `|π|` paired distances rather than as scalar
  invariants (entropy, max-lifetime, etc.). This sidesteps the lossy
  aggregation step that may be driving F-10's null result.

- **Mini-batch PH with stability guarantees**: Persistent homology has
  historically been a global computation; the paper's mini-batch
  approximation with bottleneck-stability bound makes it tractable for
  modern training pipelines. Relevant to our setting where residual-stream
  point clouds are mini-batch-sized by necessity.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: https://github.com/BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders.
- Optimizer: ADAM. Batch normalization on all autoencoder layers.
- Latent dimension fixed at 2 across all experiments (for visualization).
- Batch sizes per dataset: SPHERES = 28, FASHION-MNIST = 95, MNIST = 126,
  CIFAR-10 = 82. Note: small batch (28) was found best for SPHERES — they
  argue mini-batch speed-up dominates runtime in low-dim settings.
- λ (topological-loss weight) tuned by minimizing KL_0.1 on a 15%-of-train
  validation split.
- Topological loss is normalized by batch size m to disentangle λ from m.
- Only 0-dim PH in published experiments (1-dim was tried; "merely
  increases runtime" with no quality gain in their setting).
- Distances assumed unique (symbolic perturbation in case of ties).
- Loss decomposes as `L_t = L_{X→Z} + L_{Z→X}` with each half taking the
  *union* of selected edges from one direction's pairing — the asymmetric
  formulation matters because intersection-only pairings are too sparse at
  initialization.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 1906.00722 — Topological Autoencoders (Moor, Horn, Rieck, Borgwardt; ICML 2020)

**Relevance:** Foundational paper for differentiable persistent-homology losses on neural-network latent spaces. Directly bears on F-10 (PH at Gaussian null on residual streams): provides the first principled formulation of PH as a *training objective* rather than a *post-hoc summary*, with theoretical mini-batch stability bounds (Theorem 1) that license batch-level PH on our cached residuals. The paper's distance-based loss is rotation-invariant, which lets us test whether F-3's coordinate-orthogonality claim is mechanism-different or rotation-only. The KL_σ multi-scale evaluation framework directly tests whether F-9's "CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM" claim is single-scale.

**Key claim we tested:** PH structure can be preserved in autoencoder latents if trained for it; ordinary AE training does *not* preserve it (their AE baseline gets KL_0.1 = 0.746 on SPHERES vs TopoAE's 0.326). In our terms: PH-null on naturally trained residuals is consistent with their AE baseline; PH structure becomes non-trivial only under a topology-preserving objective.

**Our result:** **CITED ONLY** (pending H-72 / H-73 / H-74). Their AE-baseline finding *corroborates* F-10's interpretation that ordinary transformer training does not preserve PH structure beyond Gaussian null. Their *positive* TopoAE result does not directly contradict F-10 — Qwen-2.5-1.5B was not trained with a topology-preserving loss. The methodologies they introduce (edge-distance loss, KL_σ multi-scale evaluation, bottleneck-stability theorem) provide the concrete tools for refutations 1–4 above.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P10-FE71, H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** CITED ONLY (will move to PARTIALLY CONFIRMED if H-72 reveals edge-vector PH carries non-null signal, or to REPLICATED-METHODS if P10-FE71 wires up TopoAE on prefill residuals).
