# Triage brief — 2605.23901 — LLMs as Noisy Channels: A Shannon Perspective on Model Capacity and Scaling Laws

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23901
Triaged: 2026-05-25

## Refutations

**R1 — vs F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731): Token-noise exponent δ > β predicts correctness signal should degrade, not peak, at 1024 tokens.**
The paper's central empirical finding (Table 9, §5.3) is that the data-noise exponent δ persistently exceeds the signal exponent β across all regimes, including unperturbed pretraining (δ=0.745 vs β=0.402 on Pythia). This means that for sufficiently large token count D, accumulated noise D^δ overwhelms signal D^β in the Shannon capacity denominator. Applied per-layer to our setup: Qwen-2.5-1.5B was trained on ~18T tokens, firmly in the regime where their model predicts noise dominance. If this applies at the representation level, a single linear direction at L19 should not achieve AUROC 0.7731 on 1024-token inputs — the Shannon capacity bound for that layer's "channel" should be lower than what the DoM probe extracts. Falsification experiment: compute per-layer SNR on cached L19 activations (signal = DoM-aligned variance, noise = residual variance), fit the paper's simplified law (Eq. 12) to the per-layer capacity curve, and check whether L19's empirical AUROC exceeds or falls within the predicted capacity bound.

**R2 — vs F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM): Shannon-Hartley predicts multi-layer bandwidth should strictly exceed single-layer.**
The Shannon-Hartley theorem (Eq. 5) defines capacity as C = B·log₂(1 + S/N). The paper maps model parameters to bandwidth. At the per-layer level, bandwidth corresponds to layer width (1536 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B). CoE-60 aggregates features across 60 layers, which under the channel model should represent 60× the bandwidth of a single layer — strictly more capacity. F-9's finding that CoE-60 is redundant with L19 DoM contradicts this: if the information-theoretic framework applies, multi-layer aggregation should provide a capacity advantage that F-9 says doesn't exist. Either (a) the Shannon framework doesn't apply at per-layer granularity (limiting the paper's scope), (b) the 59 other layers carry zero incremental signal (implying severe signal localization the paper's macro model doesn't predict), or (c) our AUROC metric loses the incremental information through linear projection. Falsification: compute mutual information I(h_l; Y) for each layer l using a non-parametric estimator on cached activations and check whether aggregating across layers truly adds zero bits beyond L19 alone.

**R3 — vs F-3 (Prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046): Single-channel model predicts signal coherence across transmission stages.**
The paper models the entire LLM as one noisy channel (Figure 3), where pretraining modulates information into weights and inference transmits from input X to output Y. Within this single-channel model, the "signal" direction should be consistent — the channel doesn't rotate its encoding mid-transmission. Our finding that prefill DoM and final-token DoM are nearly orthogonal (cos=0.046) implies either two independent channels operating at different generation phases, or that the model's internal representation undergoes a phase transition that the Shannon framework's static formulation cannot capture. If the paper's single-channel view is correct, the cos=0.046 is anomalous and might reflect probe methodology artifacts rather than genuine geometric structure. Falsification: track per-token DoM direction evolution across the generation trajectory, compute running SNR using the paper's decomposition, and test whether the direction rotation correlates with predicted SNR transitions.

**R4 — vs F-1 (Dimensional breathing is universal): Breathing may be a noise-regime transition artifact, not a universal geometric property.**
The paper's key insight (§5.3) is that the relationship between bandwidth exponent α and noise exponent γ inverts under perturbation (γ > α in low-SNR regimes). During autoregressive generation, each token step modifies the effective SNR of the residual stream. "Dimensional breathing" (expansion during reasoning, contraction at the answer) could be the model transitioning between low-SNR (exploration, many active dimensions to maintain bandwidth) and high-SNR (convergence, capacity concentrates on fewer dimensions) regimes. Under this interpretation, breathing is not a geometric universal but an SNR-dependent phenomenon — and critically, its magnitude should follow the paper's exponent relationship, not be fixed across architectures. Falsification: compute per-position SNR along generation trajectories on cached data and test whether breathing magnitude correlates with SNR transitions as the Shannon model predicts.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: The paper's per-layer capacity framework (adapted from Eq. 6) could provide an information-theoretic upper bound on what any linear probe at any layer can achieve. If capacity(L19) > 0.7731 > capacity(L18) and capacity(L20), this would explain the L19 peak and set a ceiling for the cov-spectrum probe's 0.7928.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature)**: The paper's noise decomposition into data-induced (dD^δ) vs model-interaction (c(DN)^γ) provides a framework for understanding why D-bucket (hardest problems) has a distinct collective signature — these may be samples where accumulated noise overwhelms signal, placing them in the U-shaped degradation zone of the Shannon curve.

- **F-10 (PH null — topology not distinguishable from Gaussian)**: The paper assumes AWGN (additive white Gaussian noise) as the noise model (§3, Eq. 10). If residual-stream noise is genuinely Gaussian-distributed, this explains why PH sees nothing beyond the Gaussian null — the noise model predicts exactly this. This is confirming rather than refuting F-10.

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS)**: The Shannon capacity framework offers a competing explanation for the same phenomena H-4 tries to connect. Per-layer capacity as a function of layer depth might formally correspond to the sharpness dimension profile.

- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats DoM at 1024-tok)**: The Shannon framework's bandwidth argument predicts CoE-60 should beat single-layer DoM — if H-6 is confirmed, it aligns with Shannon capacity; if rejected (as F-9 suggests), it challenges the per-layer bandwidth interpretation.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-layer SNR decomposition** — Decompose weight/activation variance into signal (task-aligned) and noise (residual) components using Eqs. 10-11: compute E[|w|²] per layer, derive σ_n² from target SNR_dB. Adapted to activations: signal = variance along DoM direction, noise = residual variance.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 activations to derive per-layer SNR profiles.

- **Shannon capacity curve fitting** — Fit the 6-parameter simplified law (Eq. 12) C_Simpl = aN^α log₂(1 + D^β / (c(DN)^γ + D^δ)) to per-layer probe AUROCs across layers (treating layer depth as a proxy for cumulative bandwidth).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (requires per-layer DoM AUROCs which may need extraction from cached data or a small recomputation).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we have per-layer AUROCs cached, borderline if we need fresh extraction.

- **Noise term ablation** — The paper ablates the interaction noise term c(DN)^γ vs size-only cN^γ (Table 10, §5.4), showing the interaction term is necessary under heavy perturbation. Analogous ablation on our probes: is DoM-aligned variance explained by layer-width alone, or does it require a depth×width interaction term?
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can run on cached covariance matrices.

- **U-shaped curve detection via contour fitting** — Fit test loss (or AUROC) as a function of two variables to detect non-monotonic behavior. The paper uses scipy.optimize.curve_fit with their 9-parameter law (Table 12).
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could fit AUROC(layer_depth, feature_count) to detect whether our probe performance has a U-shaped dependence on dimensionality.

## Approaches & framings

- **LLM-as-noisy-channel**: Reframes model capacity as channel capacity bounded by bandwidth (parameters) and SNR (signal from data vs accumulated noise). Intersects F-2 by suggesting the DoM AUROC has an information-theoretic ceiling set by the layer's SNR.

- **U-shaped scaling as SNR transition**: Non-monotonic performance (loss basins) is explained as a transition from high-SNR (monotonic improvement) to low-SNR (noise-dominated degradation). Intersects F-1 (breathing) by providing a possible mechanism: breathing is the model's effective dimensionality tracking its SNR regime.

- **Three-source noise decomposition (data-induced, model-interaction, irreducible)**: Separating noise into these components could reframe our covariance-spectrum analysis (F-2, cov-spectrum AUROC 0.7928) — the 20 log-eigenvalues capturing correctness signal might correspond to the "signal" subspace, while the remaining eigenvalues are noise.

- **Reciprocal capacity-loss relationship (L = 1/C_LLM)**: A direct functional form linking capacity to observable loss. If adapted per-instance, this would predict that samples where the model is correct have higher effective local capacity, connecting to F-8 (selective prediction).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Gaussian noise injection uses SNR-based scaling (Eqs. 10-11): σ_n² = P_w / SNR where P_w = E[|w|²] and SNR = 10^(SNR_dB/10). This avoids the need for initialization covariance matrices (which aren't available for all open-source models).
- Curve fitting uses `scipy.optimize.curve_fit` with initialization [1] or [0.1] (Table 12).
- SFT uses `trl.SFTTrainer` with max_length=1024, cosine LR scheduler, warmup_ratio=0.1, 1 epoch.
- Quantization via GPTQ at 2/3/4-bit; also validated on AWQ, bitsandbytes, and quanto (Appendix A.6).
- Code not released (no repository link found in paper).

Maps model parameters to bandwidth and training tokens to signal power, identifying a fundamental capacity threshold beyond which scaling amplifies noise. The per-layer SNR decomposition and capacity bound framework could provide an information-theoretic explanation for why L19 prefill DoM peaks at AUROC 0.7731 (F-2), why CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer DoM (F-9), and a theoretical ceiling for probe-based correctness prediction.
