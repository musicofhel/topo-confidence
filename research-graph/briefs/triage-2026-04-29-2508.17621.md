# Triage brief — 2508.17621 — Steering When Necessary: Flexible Steering Large Language Models with Backtracking (FASB)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17621
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality, cos = 0.046).** FASB's central
   construct is a per-head probe trained on the *last token of the
   question+answer concatenation* (Eq. 1) whose parameters θ^{ℓ,h} are then
   used both as the *steering direction* and as the *generation-time deviation
   classifier* applied to every newly generated token's activation (Eq. 2). If
   the same θ usefully scores both prefill-end and mid-generation positions,
   then the "prefill direction" and the "final/generation direction" cannot be
   functionally orthogonal subspaces — they must share a substantial component
   in the head subspace selected by top-k probing. The mechanism: a single
   linear probe direction transfers across positions. Falsifiable test: extract
   Qwen-1.5B per-head activations on MATH-500, fit FASB's per-head probe at
   the answer-end position, then evaluate that *same* probe on the
   prefill-end-of-prompt position and on mid-CoT positions. If AUROC at the
   prefill position is comparable to AUROC at the answer-end position
   (within ±0.05), F-3's orthogonality claim is empirically refuted at the
   per-head level even if it survives at the residual-stream / single-layer
   DoM level.

2. **Refutes F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage is the operating
   point).** FASB shows that on TruthfulQA the *same* probe signal that drives
   abstention can instead drive a *correction*: MC1 jumps 33.41 → 48.71 (+15.3
   points) and True\*Info goes from 66.50 → 80.56 by *intervening* on the
   would-have-been-wrong half rather than declining to answer. Mechanism:
   adaptive intervention strength r = I(p>β)·p·α (Eq. 3) treats the probe
   probability as a continuous knob, not a binary abstain/answer flag. If the
   same trick works on MATH-500 (probe-derived θ added to the residual stream
   on detected-deviation tokens), our F-8 framing of the L19 DoM signal as
   "best used for refusal" would be wrong — we'd be leaving accuracy on the
   table. Falsifiable test: train a per-head correctness probe on cached
   1024-tok prefill activations for Qwen-1.5B/7B; apply it on the held-out
   half at generation; intervene with α∈[20,80]. If post-intervention K=1
   accuracy on the formerly-rejected 50% rises by >5 absolute points relative
   to the no-intervention baseline of that subset, F-8's "abstain is the
   ceiling" claim is refuted.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer
   L19 DoM).** FASB's Figure 4 shows that the top truth-relevant heads are
   "relatively evenly distributed across all layers" of LLaMA2-7B-CHAT, and
   the probe-anchored top-24-head ensemble (drawn from many layers) decisively
   beats single-direction methods (CAA/ITI/ORTHO/CAST/SADI) on MC1
   (48.71 vs ≤38.31). Mechanism: stacking independent per-head probes drawn
   from many layers carries information the single-layer DoM cannot.
   F-9 says CoE-60 is *redundant* with L19 DoM at AUROC equivalence (~0.7186
   final, 0.7731 prefill). FASB's top-24-head probing is the closest analog
   to CoE in the *probing* (not trajectory-stat) regime. Falsifiable test:
   compute per-head probes for all 16 heads × 28 layers on Qwen-1.5B prefill
   activations; pick the top-k by validation AUROC; train a logistic stacker.
   If the stacker AUROC exceeds 0.7731 (single L19 DoM) by >0.02, the
   "single-layer captures everything" framing of F-9 is refuted in favor of
   "many heads carry orthogonal correctness information."

4. **Refutes H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical).** FASB's adaptive
   intervention strength r = p(x_{i,j})·α (Eq. 3) means the *effective
   steering coefficient varies token-by-token* based on a learned content
   classifier. If RoPE were the dominant source of DoM rotation across
   generation positions, then a content-blind position-only schedule should
   match FASB's content-conditional schedule. Their Table 10 (deviation
   positions are heavily front-loaded at low β and back-loaded at high β)
   directly demonstrates that the deviation signal is content-dependent, not
   position-dependent. Falsifiable test: replicate FASB on MATH-500
   Qwen-1.5B with two strength schedules — (a) FASB's content-conditional r,
   and (b) a position-only schedule that ignores the probe and applies r as
   a fixed function of (j mod block_size). If (a) outperforms (b) by >3
   points MC1, the rotation has a content-driven component that RoPE alone
   cannot explain, refuting H-17 as the *full* mechanism.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — FASB *is* the
  per-position DoM-style steering proposal H-1 anticipated, ported to
  TruthfulQA rather than MATH-500. Their MC1 jump 33.41 → 48.71 is the kind
  of swing H-1 hopes for. The exact mechanism (probe-as-direction at
  selected heads + adaptive strength + s=10-token backtrack) is a concrete
  recipe to import, with the open question of whether α/β tuned on
  truthfulness transfer to correctness without re-fitting.
- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 OOF)** — FASB uses a per-head probe
  ensemble (top-24 heads) rather than a single-direction DoM. Their probe is
  trained on the last-token-of-(question+answer) activation, not on
  prefill-end alone. Direct comparison: if F-2's 0.7731 single-direction
  AUROC is approached by FASB's per-head ensemble at the *same prefill-end*
  position, that bounds how much the multi-head story buys us.
- **F-3 (orthogonal directions, cos = 0.046)** — see Refutation #1 above.
  FASB's probe-transfers-across-positions design is the cleanest test of
  whether F-3 is a single-layer-DoM artifact or a deep statement about
  generation geometry.
- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse)** — FASB's Table 10 shows deviations are
  detected at very different positions depending on β. At β=0.4, 76% of
  deviations are detected in tokens 0–10; at β=0.6, 65% are detected in
  tokens 20–50. This is consistent with F-4's asymmetric-collapse picture:
  the residual-stream geometry diverges over the course of generation, and
  the *when* of divergence is itself diagnostic.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — FASB's classifier does not
  produce a per-problem score so much as it identifies points along a
  trajectory where the model's own representation has slipped off the
  desired manifold. This is closer to F-7's "collective signature" framing
  than to a per-problem deterministic predictor.
- **F-8 (selective prediction works at 50% coverage)** — see Refutation #2
  above. FASB's framework treats classifier-flagged deviations as *opportunity*
  rather than *abstention*.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)** — see Refutation #3 above.
- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null)** — no direct interaction. FASB does not
  use any topological signal.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes)** — FASB's Prototype variant (Eq. 5–7)
  is explicitly *training-free*: positive/negative class-mean prototypes,
  cosine-similarity softmax classifier. This is one step away from the
  label-free regime H-12 envisions.
- **H-15 / H-18 / H-19 (controls on length banding, post-answer-newline,
  C_exact verification)** — FASB's "deviation position by threshold" Table 10
  is a direct empirical anchor: detection position varies systematically
  with β. Any control study that conditions on token position should account
  for FASB's thresholding effect.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-attention-head linear probe with top-k selection (Probe method,
  Eq. 1)** — train a logistic probe p_θ^{ℓ,h}(x) = σ(⟨θ^{ℓ,h}, x⟩) on the
  last-token activation of the (question+answer) concatenation for *every*
  (layer, head) pair. Pick the top-24 heads by validation AUROC. Use the
  trained θ^{ℓ,h} *both* as classifier and as steering direction.
  *Replication cost*: ~2–4h CPU on cached Qwen-1.5B per-head activations
  (we have all 28 layers × 12 heads = 336 probes). *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — it directly tests whether single-layer L19 DoM is leaving
  signal on the table (F-2 / F-9).

- **Prototype method (training-free alternative, Eq. 5–7)** — class-mean
  activation prototypes per (layer, head); classifier = softmax over cosine
  similarity to positive vs negative prototype with τ=0.1 temperature;
  steering direction = (positive prototype − negative prototype). This is
  ITI-class-mean + cosine classifier wrapped in one head-scoring loop.
  *Replication cost*: <1h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap
  baseline against the trained probe.

- **Generation-time deviation classifier (Eq. 2)** — average top-k probe
  probabilities at *each newly generated token*'s activation; flag deviation
  if mean (1−p_θ(x_{i,j})) > β. *Replication cost*: requires a forward-pass
  hook to capture per-head activations during decoding; non-trivial in our
  current cached-only pipeline (~1 day H100 to wire up). *We'd plausibly
  run this*: yes for the full intervention pilot, no for first-pass
  validation (we'd start with cached prefill-only probing).

- **Adaptive intervention strength r = I(p>β)·p·α (Eq. 3)** — continuous
  scaling of steering vector by deviation probability, gated by threshold β.
  Hyperparameters tuned per dataset: α∈[40,80] for TruthfulQA, β∈{0.3, 0.4,
  0.5, 0.6}, s=10 backtrack tokens. *Replication cost*: small once
  classifier hook exists. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly relevant
  to H-1.

- **Backtracking mechanism (Algorithm 1, lines 14–22)** — when deviation is
  detected at token j ≥ s, retain only tokens 1..(j−s), then regenerate
  from token (j−s+1) onward with steering applied. Hyperparameter s=10
  tokens. *Replication cost*: needs sequence-level rewind in the decode
  loop; ~1 day H100 to add to our generation harness. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — but only after the simpler classifier-only pilot lands.

- **Steering at MHSA per-head output (Eq. 4)** — h^ℓ_{i,j-s+1} =
  concat(x^{ℓ,h} + r·θ^{ℓ,h}·c^{ℓ,h})·W^{ℓ,O}, where c^{ℓ,h} is a binary
  indicator for selected top-k heads. *Replication cost*: per-head hook
  required. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for the FASB replication; no for
  our default L19-DoM steering, which lives in the residual stream rather
  than per-head.

- **Fine-grained variants BTB / GCBB (Appendix D)** — BackTrack-to-Beginning
  and Generate-Complete-then-BackTrack. GCBB is best-performing but doubles
  inference cost; BTB is intermediate. *Replication cost*: trivial extension
  of the main backtrack loop. *We'd plausibly run this*: only if the main
  FASB replication shows something interesting on MATH-500.

## Approaches & framings

- **Probe-as-direction equivalence.** FASB collapses the classifier and the
  steering vector into the same θ. This is a sharper version of our DoM
  framing (F-2): rather than two artifacts (a probe for prediction, a
  difference-of-means for steering), one linear functional plays both roles.
  Intersects F-2 by suggesting our prefill DoM should be readable as a probe
  whose decision boundary is *the very thing we'd push activations away
  from*.

- **State-tracking vs question-only assessment.** FASB explicitly argues
  that question-only probes saturate around 0.3–0.5 truthfulness probability
  on TruthfulQA (Figure 1), making fine-grained intervention impossible.
  This intersects F-2 directly: our prefill L19 DoM IS a question-only
  probe (read at the prompt-end position). FASB predicts our 0.7731 ceiling
  is partly architectural — adding generation-time activations should lift it.

- **Backtracking as residual-stream-level control flow.** The novel
  conceptual move: detection happens online, but correction reaches *back
  into already-generated context* and re-decodes. This is orthogonal to all
  our pathway 11 work (which is purely diagnostic / open-loop). Suggests a
  new pathway-level framing: "closed-loop residual stream correction" as a
  distinct branch from open-loop selective prediction.

- **Truthfulness as a head subset rather than a layer.** Figure 4's
  even-across-layers head distribution reframes the truth signal as
  *head-localized* rather than *layer-localized*. Our F-9 argument that
  L19 captures the CoE-60 signal is layer-centric; FASB suggests a
  head-centric reframing where individual heads at many layers each carry
  partial truth signal.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- TruthfulQA Probe: top-24 heads, β∈[0.4, 0.5], s=10, α∈[40, 80] step 10.
- Six-MC Probe: top-k tuned per dataset, β∈{0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6}, α∈[0, 250]
  step 10, s=10.
- Prototype: top-24 heads, β=0.5, s=10, τ=0.1, α∈[25, 55] for TruthfulQA;
  α∈[0, 400] for six-MC.
- Heads are even-distributed across layers (Figure 4) — selecting too few
  heads underperforms; too many drops Info while raising True (Figure 6).
- Backtrack number sweep (Appendix C): MC1/MC2/True\*Info increase
  monotonically with s ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20}; s=10 is the price/performance
  sweet spot.
- Models tested: LLaMA2-7B-CHAT, LLaMA2-13B-Chat, LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct,
  Qwen2.5-7B (base), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (Appendix G:
  +19 MC1, +10.32 MC2 absolute on 32B).
- Only 20% of TruthfulQA training data (≈496 samples) is enough to beat
  baseline on all three metrics — small sample size sufficient.
- Code repository: https://github.com/gjw185/FASB (announced for release).

## Replicable intermediates

- **Per-head probe AUROC sweep on cached Qwen-1.5B prefill activations.**
  Train logistic probe per (layer, head) on 1024-tok cached prefill
  activations from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`; rank by
  validation AUROC; compare top-24 head ensemble AUROC against the
  single-layer L19 DoM 0.7731 baseline (F-2). If the cached file holds
  per-head activations (likely held only at the residual stream level), this
  may require a fresh extraction; otherwise ~2h CPU.
- **Probe-as-DoM cross-check.** Verify that the probe weights θ at L19 are
  ≈parallel to the DoM direction (cos > 0.9). Cheap sanity check on cached
  data; rules out the probe-as-direction equivalence trivially failing for
  our setup.
- **Prefill probe applied at final-token position (and vice versa).** Train
  probe on prefill-end activation; evaluate on final-token activation. F-3
  predicts AUROC drops to ~chance; FASB predicts AUROC stays high. Direct
  refutation test, ~1h CPU on cached data.
- **Threshold-sweep deviation positions (cf. Table 10).** If we run a fake
  classifier (the L19 DoM logistic probe) over per-token cached activations
  for a small subset of MATH-500 traces, we can produce our own
  threshold-vs-detection-position table. Sanity-checks whether the
  deviation-detection phenomenon replicates on math.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2508.17621 — Steering When Necessary: Flexible Steering Large Language Models with Backtracking (Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2025)

**Relevance:** Closest existing analog to H-1 (per-position DoM steering),
ported to TruthfulQA-style truthfulness rather than MATH-500 correctness.
FASB (Flexible Activation Steering with Backtracking) trains per-head
linear probes on the last-token activation of (question + answer) pairs,
selects top-24 heads by validation AUROC, then uses the *same* probe
weights θ both as a generation-time deviation classifier (Eq. 2) and as
the steering direction added at MHSA per-head outputs (Eq. 4). When the
probe-derived deviation probability exceeds a threshold β, the model
backtracks s=10 tokens and re-decodes with adaptive intervention strength
