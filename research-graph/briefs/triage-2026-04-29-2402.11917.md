# Triage brief — 2402.11917 — A Mechanistic Analysis of a Transformer Trained on a Symbolic Multi-Step Reasoning Task

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11917
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Register-token / path-merging mechanism refutes F-3's "prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal" interpretation.** The paper documents a *parallelization motif*: backward chaining runs simultaneously at the goal/final position **and** at low-information register-token positions (e.g. commas, separators), and the resulting subpaths are explicitly **merged** at the final position when path length exceeds `L−1`. If the same motif holds in Qwen-2.5, then `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046` is not "two independent signals" but rather **the residual of an active merge operation that strips one component before depositing the other**. Mechanism: deduction-head composition rotates each subpath's representation between layers; small geometric overlap is exactly what you see *after* merge, not a sign of independence. Falsifiable: ablate the prefill-position residual at L19 with `register_token_patching` — F-3's "orthogonal" framing predicts no effect on the final-token DoM signal; the paper's mechanism predicts ≥30% AUROC loss at L19_final because the merge requires the prefill component as input.

2. **The "depth-bounded recurrent" finding refutes the implicit "AUROC 0.7731 reflects knowability" reading of F-2/F-8.** The paper proves the model can only chain `L−1` reasoning steps in a single forward pass; beyond that, accuracy collapses to one-step-lookahead heuristics (Section 5.4). Translated: probe AUROC on the prefill is **upper-bounded by reasoning depth**, not by representation quality. MATH-500 problems vary wildly in required chain depth; if 1.5B's effective depth is ~6–8 layers worth of deduction (Qwen has 28 layers but most are MLP+attn composition not pure deduction), then the **0.7731 AUROC is not "the model knows but can't act"** — it's "the model exhausted its parallel chains and fell back to lookahead, and the lookahead signal is what the probe reads." Mechanism: probe picks up the lookahead heuristic's confidence, not a deductive plan. Falsifiable: stratify MATH-500 by *minimal solution depth* (manually annotate 50 problems); F-2 predicts uniform AUROC across depth bins, this paper predicts AUROC drops sharply for depth > effective `L_eff − 1`, with a knee.

3. **Multi-layer head-role switching refutes H-13's clean "prefill vs final-token circuits" framing.** Section 5.2 finds that the *same attention head* in layer 5 acts as a deduction head for short paths but becomes a one-step-lookahead head for long paths — its role is **path-length-conditional**, not fixed. Mechanism: a single head has multiple QK/OV "modes" depending on what's available in the residual stream at that token. If true for L19 in Qwen-1.5B, then the DoM direction at L19 is a **mixture** of (a) a backward-chain state component, (b) a lookahead-fallback component, with mixing weights that vary per problem. Falsifiable: cluster L19 prefill vectors by (correct, problem-depth) and run separate logistic-regression probes per cluster — H-13's "single circuit" framing predicts uniform DoM direction; this paper predicts cluster-specific DoM directions that disagree by >45° between short-path-correct and long-path-incorrect clusters.

4. **Causal scrubbing exposes that F-2/F-8 have no causal evidence — only correlation.** The paper insists on the *correlational/causal* distinction (Section 3) and uses causal scrubbing to validate every hypothesis. F-2's AUROC 0.7731 and F-8's 71.6% selective-prediction acc are pure linear-probe correlations on cached residual streams. Mechanism: a probe can score 0.77 on noise that *covaries* with correctness without the residual stream causally encoding correctness — e.g., the prefill could encode question difficulty, which separately drives both label and final answer. Falsifiable: run resampling-ablation on the L19 prefill DoM component (project it out, re-run generation) on 100 MATH-500 problems. If F-2 is causal, accuracy on the originally-correct group should drop measurably; if not, the AUROC is reading a confounder.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper's "edge-token concatenation at layer 1" + "deduction heads at layers 2–4" architecture predicts that information about the *answer* gets aggregated at the goal/final-token position via backward chaining, **not** at the prefill (question) position. If the same motif holds in Qwen-1.5B, F-2's headline number — that prefill > final-token (0.7731 > 0.7186) — is *unexpected* under their mechanism: it implies Qwen is doing forward chaining (prefill encodes answer) rather than backward chaining (final token encodes answer). This is a substantive cross-architecture difference worth flagging.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — see Refutation #1; their path-merging mechanism reframes orthogonality as evidence of merge, not evidence of independence.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective geometric signature)** — paper's *register tokens* (semantically empty positions used as working memory) suggest D-bucket may be a population of problems where Qwen-1.5B couldn't allocate enough register-token-like positions to merge subpaths, producing a distinct collective geometry from A-bucket where merge succeeded.
- **F-8 (selective-prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% on K=2.5)** — see Refutation #2; their depth-bound implies the 71.6% is depth-confounded.
- **H-2 (breathing on short CoT reasoning)** — paper's mechanism predicts that with short CoT (depth ≤ `L−1`), backward chaining completes and breathing should be **muted** (no path-merging needed); long-CoT problems need merge → breathing should be larger.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — see Refutation #3; the paper's methodology (causal scrubbing per head per layer) is precisely what H-13 needs, and their conditional-role finding warns H-13 not to expect a clean separation.
- **H-21 (D-bucket has narrower prefill attention than A-bucket)** — paper's "deduction heads attend narrowly to one specific edge" provides a mechanistic prior for why narrower attention correlates with bucket membership.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Causal scrubbing** — replace activation of a hypothesized component with activation from a different input where the hypothesis predicts the same value, measure loss recovery `(L_scrubbed − L_random) / (L_model − L_random)`. Strongest available evidence for "this activation = this variable in our causal model".
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 for one head × 100 MATH-500 problems (need K=8 generations on paired clean/corrupted prompts). Code in `github.com/abhay-sheshadri/backward-chaining-circuits` is for tiny TransformerLens models — would need re-implementation for Qwen-2.5.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would close the correlation-vs-causation gap on F-2 and is directly responsive to Refutation #4.

- **Activation patching (resampling ablation)** — cache activation on clean input `s`, patch into model running on corrupted `s'`, measure logit difference `LD = Logit(r) − Logit(r')`. Less rigorous than causal scrubbing but cheaper.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 for one component × 500 problems via TransformerLens hooks on Qwen-2.5.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already implicitly part of P11-FE3 (DoM steering); paper's framing clarifies this is patching, not steering, and that's the right framing.

- **Linear probing as an upper-bound oracle on hidden-state content** — train logistic regression on `x_i^ℓ`, predict an interpretable variable (edge identity, child-node membership, leaf-vs-non-leaf). F1 ≥ 0.95 is treated as evidence that the variable is encoded; F1 ≈ 0 as evidence that it is *not* encoded yet at that layer.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU per probe target on cached Stage 2 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already do this for correctness (F-2). Their layer-by-layer "when does information appear" sweep (Tables 1, 3) is the methodological move worth importing — we should run F1-by-layer for problem-difficulty, expected-answer-token, and CoT-depth across all 28 Qwen layers.

- **QK / OV circuit decomposition (Elhage 2021 framework)** — decompose attention head into its query-key (which positions it attends to) and output-value (what it writes) circuits, then read off "edge X decreases logit of token Y" rules. The paper does this for the layer-5/6 lookahead heads.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day analysis per head, requires TransformerLens-style hooks on Qwen-2.5.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for L19 specifically — would directly attack H-13.

- **Register-token patching** — patch only at *low-information token positions* (commas, padding, instruction-format tokens) to test whether they store working memory. Distinct from final-token patching.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 per token-position class on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests Refutation #1 against F-3.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Depth-bounded recurrence in parallel" as the unit of transformer computation.** The paper reframes a forward pass as a fixed-depth recurrence (one deduction step per layer) executed in parallel across multiple token positions. This is a *positive* framing for our work: it predicts why per-layer breathing patterns (P8, P10) carry signal — each layer is one deductive step. Intersects F-1 (dimensional breathing universal) by suggesting breathing amplitude per layer = "how much deduction-state turnover at that depth."
- **Register tokens as working memory.** Reframes "uninformative" token positions (commas, structural separators) as **active computational scratchpads**, not noise. This intersects our prefill / final-token framing (F-2, F-3) by suggesting we may be sampling the wrong positions — there could be a third, register-token-like signal we're missing entirely. Could explain F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM): both might be reading the same merged signal at the final token, missing the register-token populations.
- **The L−1 reasoning-depth bound as a fundamental architectural constraint.** Independent of training data, a transformer's parallelizable reasoning depth is bounded by `L − 1`. This is the crispest theoretical claim in the paper and intersects everything: it bounds AUROC headroom (F-2), explains why selective prediction caps at finite coverage (F-8), and predicts that deeper models should have higher achievable AUROC (testable on Qwen-7B → P11-FE26 below).
- **Correlational vs causal evidence as a methodological discipline.** The paper is unusually rigorous about *not* claiming causal mechanism from probe accuracy alone. We've been sloppy about this: F-2/F-8 are described as if causal but are pure correlation. This framing should be imported into FINDINGS.md as a default-required column ("evidence type: correlational | causal").

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Synthetic binary-tree pathfinding** — 150K trees with 16 nodes each, leaf-as-goal. Generated programmatically; not released as a benchmark file but trivial to regenerate. License: implicit MIT via the repo. *Applicable?* no for our 1024-tok MATH-500 hidden-state pipeline directly, but **yes** as a sanity-check substrate: training a 6-layer toy model on this and replicating their findings would let us calibrate our probe / scrubbing tooling before pointing it at Qwen.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 6-layer decoder-only, embedding dim 128, **single attention head per layer**, FF dim 512, 1.2M params total. Causal masking (mentioned w.r.t. why source-position probe can't see edge).
- Tokenization distinguishes source-form `[15]` from target-form `[→15]` tokens — *crucial* for their backward-chaining algorithm to fire cleanly. Tells us that token-form distinctions in the prompt carry mechanistic load (relevant when designing prompts for Qwen interventions).
- Trained on **shuffled** edge lists to prevent positional shortcuts. 99.7% test accuracy on 15K unseen trees from a tiny fraction of the 16-node tree space → strong generalization.
- Code: https://github.com/abhay-sheshadri/backward-chaining-circuits (TransformerLens-based).
- Causal scrubbing: clean & corrupted graphs share a node `ℓ−1` levels above the goal; substitute layer-`ℓ` head output, measure loss change.
- Linear probes use 8K train + 8K test, F1 score. Per-layer per-position resolution.
