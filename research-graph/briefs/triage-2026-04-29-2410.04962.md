# Triage brief — 2410.04962 — Activation Scaling for Steering and Interpreting Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04962
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046) — challenged by "last-token gate-keeping" finding.**
   Stoehr et al. show (Fig. 7, §5 "Scaling at the Last Token Position", §6 case study with Yu et al. 2023) that on factual-recall tasks, scaling activations *only at the last token position* yields high steering effectiveness, and that distinct attribution methods (DLA, ACTIVPATCH, ATTRPATCH, ACTIVSCALAR) all converge on a small set of last-token attention heads (e.g., Pythia-1.4B layer 16 head 8). They explicitly attribute steering performance to "computations at the last token position which suppress or promote task-relevant information." If this generalizes from CCC/IOI factual recall to MATH-500 reasoning, then the *final-token* L19 DoM should dominate the *prefill* L19 DoM — yet our F-3 reports the two directions are essentially orthogonal (cos=0.046) AND the prefill probe wins (AUROC 0.7731 prefill vs 0.7186 final, F-2). **Mechanism**: their finding implies the predictive signal lives at the last few token positions of any context, so a token-position ablation of our prefill probe (averaging all positions vs only the last 5) should sharply degrade AUROC. **Falsifiable**: rebuild the prefill DoM using only the final 5 prompt tokens vs the full prefill on cached `pathway11_h100/stage2/*.npz`. If last-5-positions matches full-prefill AUROC, F-3 is downgraded to "prefill last-tokens vs generation last-token are orthogonal" — much weaker than current claim.

2. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) — at risk from "direction is underdetermined" finding.**
   Stoehr §4.3 + Fig. 4 shows that fitting steering vectors five times on the *same* prompt produces vectors with Kendall correlation ρ_direction = 0.350 (cosine distance, run-to-run) but ρ_norm = 0.979 (per-position norm change). The takeaway is that across re-fits, the *direction* of a learned linear intervention is unstable, while the *norm/magnitude* pattern is highly stable. **Mechanism**: if our 5-fold OOF prefill DoM directions are similarly direction-unstable (low pairwise cos) but agree on per-token magnitudes, then F-2's "direction" framing is a misattribution — the 0.7731 AUROC is carried by activation-magnitude patterns at L19, not by a particular direction. **Falsifiable**: compute pairwise cosine between the 5 fold-OOF prefill DoMs cached for F-2; compute Kendall ρ on per-token norms or DoM-projection magnitudes across folds. If pairwise cos < 0.5 and ρ_norm > 0.9, F-2 must be reframed as "L19 prefill *norm/magnitude* is the predictor" — partial refutation, since the geometric story changes.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — challenged by DYNSCALAR generalization claim.**
   Stoehr §5 (DYNSCALAR, Tab. 2, Fig. 6) defines a per-layer probe α = g·h/||h||² that is shared across token positions and shown to *outperform* the position-tied ACTIVSCALAR when generalizing across prompt templates and tasks. They argue that learning a *probe-as-scalar* across layers + sharing position recovers most of the steering. **Mechanism**: this is exactly the multi-layer-probe ablation we never ran — our F-9 claim that "single-layer L19 DoM matches CoE-60" assumes single-layer suffices. If DYNSCALAR-style probes trained jointly across layers L0–L27 outperform L19-only on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500, then F-9 is wrong: CoE-60 wins because it integrates multiple layers, and L19-only was a coincidental local optimum. **Falsifiable**: train a DYNSCALAR-style multi-layer probe (~14k params for 1.5B at 28 layers × dim 1536) on cached prefill activations against correctness labels; compare AUROC against L19-only DoM. If multi-layer > 0.79 AUROC, F-9 falls.

4. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — alternative cheaper path.**
   Stoehr show ACTIVSCALAR matches STEERVEC on effectiveness/faithfulness at ~1600× fewer parameters (912 scalars vs 1.46M vector entries on GPT2-XL). On the IOI/CCC tasks, *signed magnitude alone* (no rotation) suffices to flip predictions. **Mechanism**: H-1 is currently scoped as additive steering vectors per position. If multiplicative scalars at per-position activations produce comparable steering effects on MATH-500, then H-1's additive formulation is unnecessarily expensive and the "direction" of steering need not be computed at all — only sign + magnitude. **Falsifiable**: implement Eq. 5 (h * (1 + βα)) at L19 prefill on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with α initialized from prefill DoM signs; sweep β ∈ {-3, -1, 0, 1, 3} and report MATH-500 K=1. If accuracy moves comparably to vector-add steering, H-1 should be split into H-1a (vector) and H-1b (scalar).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731)** — DYNSCALAR's α = g·h/||h||² is operationally identical to a normalized linear probe; their formalism gives a unified view of "probe + steering" that our DoM occupies exactly. They prove the probe-form generalizes across prompts (Tab. 2), supporting transferability of L19 DoM but giving us a dynamic-scalar baseline to beat. (Read FINDINGS.md F-2 to confirm OOF protocol — see Sources consulted.)
- **F-3 (cos = 0.046)** — Stoehr's run-to-run Kendall ρ=0.350 cosine *across re-fits* (Fig. 4) is in the same regime as our prefill-vs-final cos=0.046. Their interpretation: low cosine ≠ "different mechanism", just "underdetermined direction." Our F-3 claim that prefill and final are orthogonal "directions" therefore needs an additional control: are the *norms* of prefill-DoM and final-DoM correlated even though the directions aren't?
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering MATH-500)** — ACTIVSCALAR is the parameter-light, more interpretable variant of H-1. Adopt Eq. 5 + the three-term objective as the H-1 steering harness.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution prefill vs final-token circuits)** — their Fig. 7 + Fig. 8 show how ACTIVSCALAR can isolate individual attention heads (Pythia-1.4B L16 H8 memory head; GPT2-Small L11 H8). Same protocol applied at Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 would test H-13.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical not computed)** — direction-instability finding (ρ=0.35) is broadly consistent with the rotation hypothesis: if the *correct* direction is a low-cos space of equivalent solutions, what looks like "rotation during generation" might be drift within that equivalence class. Doesn't refute or confirm; reframes.

## Methodologies extracted

- **ACTIVSCALAR (per-(layer, position, site) multiplicative scalar; Eq. 5: h·(1 + βα))** — sparse, signed-magnitude-only steering. Matches STEERVEC effectiveness with ~1600× fewer params. Replication cost: 1h CPU once cached residuals exist (gradient-based, L1 reg, β ∈ {-1, 1}). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of refutation #4 and a faster alternative to H-1's vector banks.
- **STEERVEC (per-(layer, position, site) additive vector; Eq. 4)** — additive baseline; same harness as ACTIVSCALAR. Replication cost: 4h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — already covered by P2/P10 v2 steering program; mainly useful for head-to-head against ACTIVSCALAR on MATH-500.
- **DYNSCALAR (probe-form scalar α = g·h/||h||² shared across positions; Eq. 10)** — exactly a normalized linear probe. Generalizes across prompt lengths and templates. Replication cost: 2h CPU on cached prefill activations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly testable against single-layer L19 DoM (refutation #3, F-9).
- **Three-term objective (effectiveness + faithfulness + minimality)** — Eq. 6 (logit-difference with margin m and ±β reversal), Eq. 7 (KL on non-answer tokens), Eq. 8 (L1 on θ). Pareto-frontier sweep over (λ_F, λ_M, m). Replication cost: harness reusable; ~1 day to integrate. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful evaluation framework for any future steering experiment in P10/P11.
- **Repurposing attribution scores as scalars + tunable β** (§6.1) — take ACTIVPATCH / ATTRPATCH / DLA scores α_l,i, plug into Eq. 5, sweep β. Cheap baseline. Replication cost: 4h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives 3 free baselines for H-1 / H-13.
- **Last-token-only ablation** — fit ACTIVSCALAR with intervention restricted to final prompt position only. Tests gate-keeping hypothesis. Replication cost: 1h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of refutation #1.
- **Multi-run direction stability protocol** — fit the same probe N=5 times with different seeds; compute pairwise cosine of directions and Kendall ρ of position-wise norm changes (Fig. 4). Replication cost: 30min CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of refutation #2 against F-2.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Steering as a Pareto frontier across effectiveness / faithfulness / minimality"** — replaces single-metric AUROC framing of F-2 with a 3-axis trade-off. Useful for H-1 and any future intervention. Intersects F-2 (effectiveness) + F-8 (selective prediction is implicitly a faithfulness story).
- **"Norm-stable / direction-unstable" view of linear probes** — claims direction is underdetermined and only norm patterns are reliable. Directly intersects F-2, F-3, F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature) — should reframe each as norm-or-direction claim.
- **"Probe = identification + scaling"** (DYNSCALAR) — unifies probing and steering into one parameterization. Intersects all probe-based findings (F-2, F-7, F-8, F-9).
- **"Intervention success ≠ localization claim"** (Hase et al. 2023, Wang & Veitch 2024 cited in §1) — necessary-but-not-sufficient axiom for interpretability. Direct guidance for H-13 (head-level attribution): we cannot conclude a head is *the* circuit just because intervening on it works.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **CCC (Country–Capital Conflicts)** — Du et al. 2024. Synthetic, single-token answer ("Berlin" / "Paris"). Released. Not applicable to MATH-500 / 1024-tok regime — no multi-token reasoning, no chain-of-thought.
- **IOI (Indirect Object Identification)** — Wang et al. 2023. Synthetic, two-name templates ("When Anne met Tom, Tom gave the book to {Anne|Tom}"). Released. Not applicable — single-token answer, ~15 token prompts.

Both datasets are entirely off-regime for our pipeline (single-token, ~15-tok, base GPT2/Pythia models). No transfer of dataset; only methodology transfers.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models tested: GPT2-Small (124M, L=12), GPT2-XL (1.5B, L=48), Pythia-1.4B (L=24).
- Sites: attnOut (s=1), mlpOut (s=3), residPost (s=4) — i.e., the four points in their Eq. 3 block decomposition.
- Optimization: gradient descent on combined objective Eq. 9; L1 regularization; "negligible" threshold |α| < 0.01 for counting active params.
- Hyperparameter grid: λ_F, λ_M, m ∈ {0, 1, 10, 100}; sign-symmetric β ∈ {-1, +1} for training, both directions reported.
- Code: https://github.com/niklasstoehr/activationScaling
- Gotcha: STEERVEC overfits at high effectiveness on train (λ_F low → train-test gap); ACTIVSCALAR less so due to L1 sparsity. Implication for our H-1: regularize harder than we did in P10 v2.
- Gotcha: DLA attribution scores live on a much larger numerical scale than learned scalars (Fig. 9 bottom row); needs per-method β rescaling to be comparable.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2410.04962 — Activation Scaling for Steering and Interpreting Language Models (Stoehr, Du, Snæbjarnarson, West, Cotterell, Schein, EMNLP findings 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodology paper proposing parameter-light scalar interventions on activation vectors as an alternative to additive steering vectors. Establishes a three-term objective (effectiveness + faithfulness + minimality) for evaluating any intervention. Proposes ACTIVSCALAR (per-position multiplicative scalar), STEERVEC (per-position additive vector baseline), and DYNSCALAR (probe-form scalar α = g·h/||h||² shared across positions). Datasets are off-regime (synthetic single-token CCC and IOI on GPT2/Pythia base models — no reasoning, no chain-of-thought). Three contributions transfer to our pipeline: (1) the direction-unstable / norm-stable finding from re-fitting steering vectors (Fig. 4) directly challenges the "direction" framing in F-2; (2) the "last-token gate-keeping" claim challenges the prefill-vs-final orthogonality in F-3; (3) the DYNSCALAR formulation is operationally a normalized linear probe and gives a multi-layer baseline against single-layer L19 DoM (F-9).

**Key claim we tested:** Direction is underdetermined (ρ_run-to-run = 0.350 cosine) while per-position norm is stable (ρ = 0.979); ACTIVSCALAR matches STEERVEC effectiveness/faithfulness with ~1600× fewer learnable params; DYNSCALAR generalizes across prompt templates and lengths.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** Their datasets are too far off-regime to directly replicate. The portable claims — direction-instability and DYNSCALAR multi-layer probe — translate cleanly into P11-FE82 / P11-FE83 / P11-FE84 sanity checks against our cached residuals. Until those run, status is TO TEST. The methodology framework (three-term objective, Eq. 5, Eq. 10) is reusable regardless of replication outcome.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE82, P11-FE83, P11-FE84, P11-FE85, P11-FE86; H-93, H-94; F-2, F-3, F-9; H-1, H-13.

**Status:** TO TEST
