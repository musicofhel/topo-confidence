# Triage brief — 2411.08790 — Can sparse autoencoders be used to decompose and interpret steering vectors?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08790
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Mayne, Yang, Mahdi (Oxford). NeurIPS 2024 MINT workshop.

## Refutations

1. **vs H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** H-1's
   recipe extracts an L19 mean-difference vector (correct − incorrect class
   centroids) and adds `α · w` at every generated position. Mayne et al.'s
   §3.1 result is that mean-difference vectors derived from contrastive
   pairs are systematically **out-of-distribution** vs natural residual
   activations: their *L₂ norm is ~3-10× smaller* than typical activations
   (Fig. 1, Fig. 5 across 7 behaviors), and — critically — the subtraction
   step *removes "default components"* that all natural activations carry,
   so even rescaling the vector to in-distribution L₂ does **not** restore
   in-distribution behavior (Fig. 2). **Mechanism by which this fires
   against H-1:** the layer-norm and downstream nonlinearities at L19+ are
   tuned for activations carrying default components; injecting `α · w`
   pushes activations into a region where the post-LN scale is dominated by
   missing-default geometry rather than by the discriminative direction
   itself. The propensity curve in Mayne's Fig. 4 is roughly linear in the
   ±1.5 multiplier window, but they only test small α with a continuous
   logit-difference target — under H-1's discrete K=1 accuracy target with
   a much longer 1024-tok generation, OOD effects could compound and
   produce non-monotonic (or even negative) behavior across α.
   **Falsifiable:** sweep α ∈ {−4,−2,−1,0,1,2,4} per H-1's spec and check
   monotonicity of K=1 accuracy *and* of final-answer logit-difference. If
   accuracy is non-monotonic or peaks at small α and crashes for α≥1, the
   OOD-by-construction warning fires; H-1's "single multiplier per position
   bank" parameterization needs to be replaced with an in-distribution
   variant (gradient-pursuit `[16]` or sparse-SAE-finetuning `[8]`-style
   reconstruction inside the SAE basis), or with a position-conditional
   default-component re-injection.

2. **vs the SAE-attribution path implicit in H-13 (head-level attribution
   of prefill vs final-token circuits).** The natural way to attribute a
   residual-stream direction to specific heads or features is to decompose
   it in the SAE feature basis and inspect top-activating features. Mayne
   et al. §3.1 / Table 1 / Appendix C Table 2 show this is **a generic
   artifact of the SAE encoder bias**: the corrigibility, sycophancy,
   coordinate-other-AIs, hallucination, myopic-reward, refusal, and
   survival-instinct steering vectors all decompose with the *same* top
   two features (4888, 15603) — and so does the **zero vector**. **Mechanism:**
   small-L₂ inputs cause `b_enc` to dominate `W_enc · v` in Eq. (2); SAE
   features whose bias is large pop to the top regardless of `v`. **How
   this would fire against H-13:** if we run SAE-feature attribution on the
   L19 prefill DoM (e.g., via a Qwen-Scope analog or a transferred Gemma
   probe), the top features will reflect SAE bias, not prefill-circuit
   structure, and any "feature 4888 = correctness-related" reading would be
   spurious. **Falsifiable:** SAE-decompose the L19 prefill DoM **and** the
   zero vector in the same SAE basis; compute Jaccard overlap of top-10
   feature IDs. If ≥0.5, abandon any SAE-feature-level attribution route
   for H-13 and instead pursue head-attribution via attention pattern /
   path-patching directly in residual space.

3. **vs F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 ⇒ orthogonal directions
   ⇒ different mechanisms).** F-3 reads cosine-orthogonality in raw
   residual space as evidence that prefill and final-token correctness
   signals are *mechanistically distinct*. Mayne et al. §3.2 + Fig. 3 +
   Appendix D show that mean-difference vectors can have meaningful
   **negative** projections in feature directions, and that for any feature
   `f_a` with negative projection, the existence of a feature `f_b` with
   `cos(f_a, f_b) < 0` (which the paper documents is common in trained
   SAEs, citing Gao et al. `2406.04093`) means the *same semantic content*
   can be re-expressed as a positive projection on `f_b`. **Mechanism by
   which this fires against F-3:** if the prefill-DoM has a strong negative
   projection on some hidden direction `f_a`, and the final-DoM has a
   strong positive projection on a `cos(f_a, ·) ≈ −1` mate `f_b`, then in
   the natural residual basis they look orthogonal (cos ≈ 0) while in any
   "interpretable" basis they could be expressing the *same* underlying
   correctness signal with a sign flip from prefill-context vs
   generation-context default components. **Falsifiable:** project the
   prefill DoM and final-token DoM each onto the K=1-correct minus
   K=1-incorrect *centroid* in the L19 residual space at each generation
   token. If both projections are non-trivially aligned with the per-token
   centroid axis (just at different magnitudes / signs), the
   "two-different-mechanisms" reading of F-3 is undermined and the right
   framing is "one correctness axis with a default-component sign flip
   between prefill and generation contexts."

4. **vs the implicit interpretive frame around F-2 (prefill L19 DoM
   AUROC 0.7731).** F-2 is a *prediction* claim, so OOD-of-SAE is not a
   refutation of the AUROC itself. But the project frequently hands the
   DoM to people as "the model's representation of correctness." Mayne et
   al.'s framing — that mean-difference vectors are a *non-canonical
   linear combination of model activations* and not themselves a model
   "representation" — refutes that framing. **Mechanism:** the DoM lives
   in residual space but does not satisfy the distributional invariants
   of natural residual activations (default components missing, L₂ norm
   off-distribution); calling it the model's "correctness representation"
   is a category error in the same sense as calling Mayne's corrigibility
   steering vector "the model's corrigibility representation." This
   doesn't change F-2's number; it changes how F-2 should be cited in the
   write-up.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) / mean-difference steering**
  (Panickssery `2312.06681`) — already canonical in our project; the
  prefill DoM is a CAA-style mean-difference vector. *Replication cost:*
  already done. *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — it is the project's
  current methodology; the brief's contribution is methodology *critique*
  rather than methodology novelty.

- **Steerability metric (Tan et al. `2407.12404`-style propensity-curve
  slope)** — fit a regression line of logit-difference vs steering
  multiplier `α ∈ {−1.5, …, 1.5}`; slope = steerability. *Replication
  cost:* ~30 min CPU once H-1's α-sweep generations exist. *We'd plausibly
  run this:* yes — gives a single scalar comparable across α-sweep
  configurations and across model sizes (1.5B vs 7B). Would slot into
  H-1's reporting as "DoM steerability" alongside K=1 accuracy.

- **Zero-vector SAE-decomposition control** (Mayne Table 1 / Table 2) —
  decompose the all-zeros vector with the same SAE used to decompose the
  steering vector; compare top features. *Replication cost:* ~5 min if a
  Qwen SAE is available; n/a today (Gemma Scope is for Gemma 2 only, not
  Qwen-2.5). *We'd plausibly run this:* only if/when Qwen SAEs become
  available — we'd never want to interpret SAE-feature attributions
  without it.

- **L₂-norm OOD diagnostic** (Mayne Fig. 1 / Fig. 5) — overlay the
  candidate vector's L₂ on the histogram of per-token activation L₂s at
  the same layer. *Replication cost:* ~10 min CPU on cached Pathway 11
  Stage 2 prefill activations. *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — cheap
  and load-bearing for H-1 (tells us whether the DoM is OOD by Mayne's
  criterion before committing H100 time on a steering sweep).

- **Default-components diagnostic** (Mayne Fig. 2) — pick a feature
  direction; plot SAE pre-activations on (positive prompts, negative
  prompts, random Pile prompts, the steering vector, the L₂-rescaled
  steering vector). If the steering-vector pre-activations sit far from
  the random-prompt distribution, default-component subtraction is
  active. *Replication cost:* requires an SAE — n/a for Qwen-2.5 today.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* no, blocked on Qwen SAE availability.

- **Negative-projection / cosine-aligned feature confound check**
  (Mayne §3.2, Fig. 3) — for the top-magnitude features in a positive-vs-
  negative-prompt activation difference, count how many are negative;
  also check whether spuriously-positive features have a high-negative
  cos with a "real" feature. *Replication cost:* requires SAE; n/a today.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* no, blocked.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Mean-difference vectors are fundamentally not model activations."**
  The paper's central conceptual move: CAA-style vectors live in residual
  space but lack the distributional invariants (default components,
  expected L₂ scale) of natural activations, so they should not be
  treated as "model features" in the SAE-interpretability sense.
  *Intersection with our F-N/H-N:* directly reframes F-2/F-3/H-1 — the
  L19 prefill DoM is a useful *linear functional* over activations, not
  a "circuit element."

- **"Default components" as a separate object from semantic content
  (Uppaal et al. `2405.13967`).** The paper imports the distinction:
  natural activations decompose into *default components* (sequence-
  independent / context-independent baseline) plus *content components*.
  CAA's subtraction kills the default. *Intersection:* explains why
  prefill (rich default) and final-token (different default after CoT)
  DoMs could have orthogonal *raw* directions while encoding the same
  correctness axis — bridges F-3 to a generative explanation rather
  than a "two mechanisms" reading.

- **Local vs global interpretability of mean-difference vectors**
  (Mayne Appendix D / Fig. 6). The "true" interpretation of a steering
  vector may be context-dependent — adding it on top of activation `a`
  is interpretable as "+f" when `a · f` is small and as "−f'" (where
  `cos(f, f') ≈ −1`) when `a · f'` is large. *Intersection:* this is the
  geometric reading of the Tan `2407.12404` "context-dependence of
  steerability" finding that already lives in our graph; combining the
  two papers' framings gives a sharper version of H-1 — DoM steering's
  effect should be expected to vary by problem context, not just by
  position.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Steering vectors extracted at the **answer-token position** (the "(A)"
  or "(B)") on each contrastive pair, then averaged (Mayne Eq. 1). This
  is *position-specific* CAA, contra position-agnostic CAA. The prefill
  DoM in F-2 is also at a specific position (last prompt token,
  pre-generation, position 0), so the analogy is tight.
- Gemma Scope SAE used: layer 14, width 16384, L0=173, JumpReLU
  (Rajamanoharan et al. `2407.14435`). Pre-activation thresholds in
  [1.12, 9.86]. Not directly usable for Qwen.
- Code: https://github.com/HarryMayne/SV_interpretability — has the
  zero-vector decomposition, L₂-norm histograms, and per-feature
  positive-vs-negative-prompt pre-activation comparison plots
  reproducible end-to-end.
- Steerability metric: regression of logit-difference (Eq. 4) on α ∈
  {−1.5, −1.0, −0.5, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5}; slope = steerability. Layer-14
  was selected as the best-steering layer for Gemma 2 2B (Appendix B,
  Fig. 4) — analogous to our L19-best for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2411.08790 — Can sparse autoencoders be used to decompose and interpret steering vectors? (Mayne, Yang, Mahdi, NeurIPS 2024 MINT workshop)

**Relevance:** Mayne et al. show that mean-difference / Contrastive
Activation Addition vectors (the family our L19 prefill DoM belongs to)
are systematically out-of-distribution with respect to natural model
activations along two axes: (1) their L₂ norm is far below the natural-
activation L₂ distribution at the same layer (Fig. 1, Fig. 5 across 7
behaviors), and (2) the contrastive subtraction step removes "default
components" that all natural activations share, so even L₂-rescaled
mean-difference vectors stay OOD in *direction* (Fig. 2). They also
show that direct SAE decomposition of these vectors is misleading both
because of encoder-bias domination (Table 1: zero vector and
corrigibility steering vector have nearly identical top-5 features)
and because SAEs cannot represent the meaningful negative projections
these vectors carry (~50/100 top features have negative coefficients
across all 7 behaviors). The paper does not refute F-2's *prediction*
claim (linear probing is unaffected), but it directly bears on H-1
(per-position DoM steering — OOD-by-construction is a methodology
warning), H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token
circuits — rules out SAE-feature attribution as a clean route), and
F-3 (cos ≈ 0.046 between prefill and final DoM — could reflect a
default-component sign flip rather than two mechanisms).

**Key claim we tested:** Mean-difference vectors derived from
contrastive pairs are systematically out-of-distribution from natural
activations (smaller L₂, missing default components), and
naive scalar-multiplied addition pushes activations into a regime
where downstream-layer behavior is governed by missing-default
geometry rather than by the discriminative direction.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** P11-FE890 (10 min CPU) checks the L₂
piece on the L19 prefill DoM; P11-FE891 (15 min CPU) checks the
post-injection-OOD piece across H-1's planned α-sweep. Both gate
H-1's H100 budget. P11-FE892 (1h CPU) tests the related reframing
of F-3 as a default-component sign flip rather than two mechanisms.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE890, P11-FE891, P11-FE892, H-1, H-90,
H-91, F-2, F-3, H-13.

**Status:** TO TEST
