# Triage brief — 2502.01126 — Language Models Prefer What They Know: Relative Confidence Estimation via Confidence Preferences

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01126
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "value-add" (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 as a non-trivial signal).**
   *Mechanism*: Shrivastava et al. show that purely-linguistic pairwise relative-confidence prompting (no log-probs, no probes, no internals) yields an average AUROC of **0.753** across 5 frontier models on 14 QA datasets (Table 7 — TrueSkill row), and **0.819 selective-classification AUC** on GPT-4o even *without giving the model access to its own answers* (difficulty-only prompt). Our F-2 claims that prefill L19 DoM at 0.7731 AUROC is the supervised-probe lower bound for what residual-stream geometry encodes about correctness. If the same Algorithm 1 elicitation, applied to Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500, matches or exceeds 0.7731, then the residual-stream probe is not extracting anything that simple linguistic prompting can't. F-2 currently has no such elicitation baseline.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Run Algorithm 1 (15 pairwise comparisons per question) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B over the 500 MATH-500 problems we already have answers for, aggregate via TrueSkill, score against the same 5-fold OOF split used for F-2. If AUROC ≥ 0.7731, F-2's "DoM is informative beyond elicitation" framing is broken.

2. **Refutes F-8's mechanism claim (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% via prefill DoM is "the" residual-stream selective-prediction result).**
   *Mechanism*: Their relative-confidence-with-TrueSkill is a strictly stronger label-free selective-classification baseline than absolute prompting, gaining 4.9–6.1% AUC on Llama-405B and 1.8–3.2% on GPT-4/4o over self-consistency. F-8 frames 71.6% acc at coverage 0.5 as evidence that *DoM-direction selectivity* works. But the headline number compared against absolute prompting only — never against pairwise relative comparison. If Qwen-1.5B with relative-confidence (no internals) hits ≥71.6% selective acc at 50% coverage on MATH-500, F-8's specific contribution is "the DoM does ~as well as a free elicitation baseline," which is a much weaker claim.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Same as above — feed pairwise prompts to Qwen-1.5B, aggregate via TrueSkill, threshold at top-50% confidence quantile, compute selective accuracy. If ≥71.6%, F-8 is at parity with cheap elicitation.

3. **Refutes F-3's implicit two-mechanism framing (prefill ⊥ final, cos=0.046, "two distinct correctness signals").**
   *Mechanism*: Section 5 ("How important are answers in determining confidence?") shows that GPT-4o judging *difficulty only* (no answers shown) hits 0.819 AUC vs 0.872 with answers — a 5.3% drop, but only 2.1% *below* absolute-with-answers. Translation: the question-difficulty signal alone (computable from the prefill *before* generating an answer) accounts for the bulk of the correctness signal; reading the answer adds a small increment. This reframes F-3's "prefill direction is orthogonal to final" not as "two computational mechanisms" but as "prefill estimates problem difficulty (computable from question alone), final estimates whether the produced answer matches that difficulty estimate" — one mechanism plus answer-conditioning, naturally orthogonal directions in a residual stream. The cos=0.046 is then *expected*, not surprising, and the F-3 narrative ("two computational pathways") is over-claimed.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Compute a "linguistic difficulty score" for MATH-500 using GPT-4o on questions only (Section 5 prompt). Correlate with our cached prefill L19 DoM dot products on Qwen-1.5B. If r ≥ 0.4, prefill DoM is largely a difficulty proxy and F-3's two-mechanism framing must add this caveat.

4. **Refutes H-12's choice of probe (Semantic-Entropy Probes as the canonical label-free correctness predictor).**
   *Mechanism*: H-12 picks SEPs (Farquhar 2406.15927) as the label-free baseline to test against supervised DoM. Shrivastava et al. provide a stronger, simpler, and entirely-API-level label-free alternative that needs neither sampling nor entropy estimation. If on Qwen-1.5B we find relative-confidence > SEP > random > (or = supervised DoM), H-12's specific framing — "semantic entropy is *the* label-free comparator" — is wrong; relative-confidence is the proper comparator and SEP is a sub-baseline.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: Three-way bake-off on Qwen-1.5B / MATH-500: (a) supervised DoM, (b) SEP, (c) relative-confidence (TrueSkill). If (c) ≥ (a), the project's whole DoM-vs-SEP framing in H-12 is the wrong comparison.

- **H-19** (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling). Their relative-confidence is a cheap routing signal: if a question loses pairwise matchups, route to C_exact; if it wins, accept the C_infer answer. Adds a routing-policy candidate to H-19.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Pairwise relative confidence prompting (Algorithm 1)** — for each q_i, randomly sample n peers q_j from the same dataset and ask "which are you more confident in?" Greedy T=0 generations.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU per dataset for the prompt-construction loop; cost dominated by API/model calls (15×500 = 7500 inferences for one MATH-500 sweep at n=15). On a local Qwen-1.5B this is ~1h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — it's the strongest missing baseline against F-2/F-8 and we already have all 500 MATH-500 generations cached.

- **Difficulty-only prompt (Section 5)** — same pairwise format but answers are *withheld*; model judges which question is harder.
  *Replication cost*: same as above, additional 7500 calls.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly tests F-3's reframing (refutation #3) and H-5's difficulty-encoding hypothesis. High-leverage.

- **Elo rating aggregation** — K=400, init=1000, n_iters tuned in [1, 20]; iterative online update on shuffled match list.
  *Replication cost*: <1 min CPU, pure-numpy.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — small.

- **TrueSkill aggregation** — μ=25, σ=μ/3, β=μ/6, τ=μ/300; uses `trueskill` PyPI package; Bayesian skill posterior.
  *Replication cost*: <1 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — best-performing aggregator on most models, recommended starting point.

- **Bradley-Terry MLE aggregation** — log-likelihood + L2 (λ=0.01), BFGS optimizer; max-iters tuned in [1, 20].
  *Replication cost*: <1 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sanity check against TrueSkill.

- **CoT-augmented confidence preference (Section 5, negative result)** — adds explicit reasoning to the comparison prompt; AUC drops from 87.2 to 86.8 on GPT-4o due to confabulated evidence.
  *Replication cost*: same as relative-confidence + CoT generation cost.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — paper itself reports negative result; not worth re-replicating unless cross-checking the failure mode.

- **Selective-classification AUC with Gaussian-noise tie-breaking** — the confidence score gets ε-noise added to break ties when many examples share scores. Important detail for any selective-classification metric we report.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — should adopt for our F-8 reporting; we don't currently document tie-handling.

## Approaches & framings

- **Confidence-as-rank-aggregation reframe.** Confidence estimation reframed as a noisy / inconsistent / incomplete pairwise-comparison problem solvable by rank aggregation. Intersects F-8 directly: if confidence is fundamentally relative, our supervised DoM probe (which produces an absolute scalar per example) is solving a strictly harder problem than necessary. Suggests reformulating F-8 as a rank-prediction task ("can the DoM correctly order pairs by P(correct)?") rather than a calibration task.

- **Difficulty as a separable component of confidence.** The Section-5 difficulty-only result implies confidence ≈ difficulty(question) + answer-conditional-signal. This separation is testable in our pipeline by regressing prefill DoM scores on (a) a difficulty proxy and (b) an answer-quality proxy. Bears on H-5 (familiarity vs decomposability) and reframes F-3.

- **"Models are better at relative judgments than absolute" (cited from Kadavath 2022).** Generalization of the well-known LLM-as-judge finding that pairwise > pointwise. Suggests our ABC-44 / D-bucket classifier framing should be tested as a pairwise problem (is question A more likely to be in D-bucket than question B?) rather than absolute classification.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Greedy T=0 for the answer + confidence; T=0.7 for self-consistency baseline (15 samples).** Don't mix temperatures across the pipeline.
- **n=15 pairwise comparisons per question** is the saturation point for GPT-4o (Table 2: gains plateau at 1.8% from n=10→n=15). For Llama-405B still climbing at n=15 (4.9%). Plan for at least n=15 on Qwen-1.5B; consider n=30 to check saturation on a smaller model.
- **Hyperparameters (Table 8 fixed defaults)**: Elo K=400, init=1000, 1 iter; TrueSkill μ=25, σ=μ/3, β=μ/6, τ=μ/300; Bradley-Terry max-iter 5, λ=0.01.
- **Min-max normalization** applied to final aggregated scores → [0, 1] range. Don't compare raw Elo / TrueSkill scores across runs without this normalization.
- **Tie-breaking via Gaussian noise** added to confidences for AUC computation (their footnote on Shrivastava 2023). Not in our current `validate_claims.py` selective-prediction code.
- **`trueskill` PyPI package** is the recommended TrueSkill implementation. Bradley-Terry uses `scipy.optimize.minimize` with BFGS.
- **No code link in paper** — would need to re-implement Algorithms 1–3 from pseudocode (small).

## 2502.01126 — Language Models Prefer What They Know: Relative Confidence Estimation via Confidence Preferences (Shrivastava, Kumar, Liang, 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct competitor to F-2 / F-8 as a *label-free, internals-free* correctness predictor. The paper introduces pairwise relative-confidence elicitation aggregated by Elo / TrueSkill / Bradley-Terry and reports an average AUROC of 0.753 across 5 frontier closed/open models on 14 QA datasets — the same order of magnitude as our F-2 prefill L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731 on Qwen-1.5B / MATH-500. The paper supplies the strongest missing baseline our selective-prediction work currently lacks.

**Key claim we tested:** Pairwise linguistic confidence elicitation, with no access to log-probs or internal states, can match or exceed supervised internal probes on selective-classification AUC and AUROC. Their Algorithm 1 + TrueSkill is the strongest internals-free baseline against our F-2 / F-8 / H-12.

P11-FE126 (relative-confidence on cached MATH-500 answers), P11-FE127 (difficulty-only variant + correlation with prefill DoM), and P11-FE128 (3-way bake-off vs SEP and supervised DoM) are queued.
