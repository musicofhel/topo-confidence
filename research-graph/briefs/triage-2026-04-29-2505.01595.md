# Triage brief — 2505.01595 — Always Tell Me The Odds: Fine-grained Conditional Probability Estimation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01595
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 / F-8 — direct fine-tuning may dominate residual-stream DoM probing for correctness prediction.** Wang et al. show that decoder-based regression with bin tokens, expected-label scoring, and pairwise rank-consistency loss produces calibrated fine-grained P(prop | context) that beats their *Probe* baseline (true/false token logits + temperature scaling on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) by a wide margin on UNLI (.812 vs .430 Spearman) and on plausibility ranking on δ-SNLI (88.9% vs 77.2%). The *Probe* baseline is structurally close to what F-2 and F-8 do (read off a scalar from the LM and use it as a confidence signal), and it is dominated by fine-tuning. Mechanism of refutation: if we fine-tune Qwen2.5-1.5B with their bin-token regression on MATH-500 `(problem, K=1 generation, correctness∈{0,1})` triples, and the resulting AUROC exceeds prefill-DoM 0.7731, then F-2's headline "prefill L19 DoM is a *strong* correctness predictor, *better than* final-token" survives only as a comparison among probes — the unprobed alternative (full LoRA fine-tune for calibrated regression) is stronger still. Falsifiable by: 1d H100 LoRA fine-tune + 5-fold OOF AUROC on the same MATH-500 split used in `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`.

2. **F-7 — D-bucket's "distinctive geometric signature" may be co-explained by ensemble disagreement.** Wang et al. operationalize annotation quality as `discrepancy = max_k y_k − min_k y_k` across K LLM rollouts and use it as a label-quality filter, citing prior work (Deng et al. 2023; Baek et al. 2022) that ensemble agreement is a reliable proxy for correctness. F-7's D-bucket is exactly the K=1-right / K=8-wrong tail — a *sampling-disagreement* artifact by construction. Mechanism: if we compute K=1…K=8 answer-disagreement (Jaccard / mode-fraction / entropy over the 8 sampled answers per problem) on the existing P11 MATH-500 traces, and D-bucket problems fall in the high-disagreement tail with the same separability that the geometric collective signature gives, then F-7's geometric framing is redundant — a label-free sampling-disagreement filter explains the same variance without needing residual-stream features. Falsifiable by: 20 min CPU on cached K=8 generations + computing per-problem mode-fraction; compare AUROC for D-bucket recovery vs the geometric collective score.

3. **H-12 (label-free probes replace supervised DoM) — Wang et al.'s data argues *against* this hypothesis.** Their fine-tuned (supervised) models beat the *Probe* baseline (extract true/false token logits + supervised temperature scaling, which is *also* supervised but at a much smaller capacity) by 38 Spearman points on UNLI and 11.7 accuracy points on δ-SNLI. The Probe is the closest analog to a label-free token-probability confidence signal of the kind H-12 contemplates (semantic entropy probes, SEPs from 2406.15927). If even a supervised-temperature-scaled token-probability signal loses by >10pp to a small LoRA fine-tune on the *same* base model, then a fully *label-free* signal is unlikely to match supervised DoM either. This refutes H-12's optimistic framing ("label-free probes replace supervised DoM"); the more defensible framing is "label-free probes are a cheap weak baseline; supervised LoRA dominates when labels exist." Falsifiable by: re-run their Probe procedure (extract `P("true") − P("false")` after temperature scaling) on Qwen2.5-1.5B for our MATH-500 correctness target and compare AUROC against prefill DoM (0.7731) — if Probe < DoM by ≥5pp, supervised geometric probing wins among supervised probes; if Probe ≈ DoM, then DoM's geometric content is no richer than verbalized-token logits.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% on K=2.5 avg, AUROC 0.7731 from F-2):** Wang et al.'s decoder-regression model is a candidate *replacement* for the DoM-based selective predictor — it produces a calibrated scalar in [0,1], directly usable as a coverage-knob signal. Their UNLI Spearman 0.812 is not directly comparable to MATH-500 correctness AUROC, but the recipe is general enough to retarget.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, final-token 0.7186):** Their Probe baseline (token-logit confidence + temperature scaling) is the closest external analog. F-2's "prefill > final-token" comparison is between two *positions* of a single supervised geometric probe; Wang's results suggest the bigger axis is supervised-LoRA vs supervised-probe, not prefill-vs-final.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM):** see Refutation 3 above. Bears directly on whether label-free elicitation can match supervised approaches.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute, P11-FE19):** Wang's fine-grained probability is a candidate verification-stage scoring function — instead of a binary `verifies` token, route on calibrated `P(answer correct)`.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature):** see Refutation 2 above.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM):** Wang's expected-label-scoring rule (sum over bin-token probabilities × bin-center) is a 1-D extension of the same idea — a single-layer probability summary suffices once the regression objective is right.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Decoder-based regression with bin tokens** — split [0,1] into N bins ({10, 20, 100} swept), assign each a unique special token `t_j`, train decoder LM to predict `t_j` for the bin containing target `y`. At inference, take `ŷ = Σ f(b_j) · p(t_j | x)` to recover a fine-grained scalar (expected-label scoring rule, Jiang et al. 2024a).
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for LoRA fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B on a MATH-500 correctness target.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the cleanest external method that competes with prefill-DoM as a correctness predictor; it's the FE-A test below.

- **Gaussian-quantized soft targets + forward-KL loss** — convert scalar `y` to `N(y, σ²)` (σ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1}), discretize via Wasserstein-2-optimal quantization (`q(t_j|x) = F((f(b_{j+1})+f(b_j))/2) − F((f(b_j)+f(b_{j-1}))/2)`), train via forward KL between this and the model's bin-token distribution. Mode-seeking forward KL chosen empirically over reverse KL.
  *Replication cost*: trivial code change on top of the above.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — bundled with FE-A.

- **Mixture-of-rollouts pseudo-labeling with judge-weighted aggregation** — for each datum, generate K probability estimates with K LLMs (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, QwQ-32B, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct), filter by agreement-based discrepancy, route high-discrepancy items to an LLM-judge that assigns confidence `c^k`, build mixture target `Q(t|x) = Σ π^k · Q^k(t|x)` with `π^k ∝ (c^k)^α`.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for K-rollout generation if we adapt to MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — the disagreement-filter component is more interesting as a refutation lever for F-7 (see FE-B) than as a labeling pipeline for us.

- **Pairwise margin-loss rank-consistency training** — given pairs `(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2)` with known order, add `max{0, δ − sgn(y_1 − y_2) · (ŷ_1 − ŷ_2)}` to the loss alongside KL regression. Combined loss `L = L_Direct(D_Human) + β_1 L_Direct(D_Synth) + β_2 L_Rank(D_Pair)`, β_2 ∈ {1, 10, 100}.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural for our K=1 vs K=8 correctness comparisons (D-bucket vs A-bucket pairs admit a natural ordering).

- **LLM-as-a-judge confidence-of-reasoning scoring** — judge model rates each candidate reasoning chain on a 0–1 scale, used as supervision weight in the mixture target.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 if we want it.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — judge bias is a known confound and we already have ground-truth correctness labels for MATH-500.

- **Probe baseline (their negative control)** — extract `P("true")` and `P("false")` token logits from Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, calibrate via Thermometer-style temperature scaling (Shen et al. 2024). This is essentially the verbalized-confidence baseline.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached residual streams.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct comparison to prefill DoM at the same model scale (1.5B) gives a clean "is supervised geometric probe richer than supervised verbalized-token probe" answer (FE-C below).

- **LoRA fine-tuning recipe** — r=16, α=32, lr=2×10⁻⁵, linear scheduler, hyperparam sweep over levels ∈ {10, 20, 100}, β_2 ∈ {1, 10, 100}, σ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1}.
  *Replication cost*: standard.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — bundled with FE-A.

## Approaches & framings

- **Calibrated probability estimation as a fine-tuning target, not a probing target.** Reframes confidence elicitation from "extract a signal from frozen activations" (DoM, semantic entropy, EigenScore) to "train the LM to emit a calibrated number directly." This is orthogonal to F-2/F-8's lens; if it dominates, the probing program is at best a compute-efficient approximation. Worth holding alongside our framing rather than absorbing it.

- **Expected-label scoring rule as a unifier of regression / ranking / calibration.** They cite Jiang et al. 2024a's result that mean-absolute-error and ranking-risk regret both *lower-bound* the calibration error of the underlying classifier. This is a strong theoretical claim: getting the bin-token distribution right *forces* calibration. If we want F-8's selective predictor to be calibrated (not just rank-correct), this is the right lens.

- **Ensemble agreement as label quality.** They use `discrepancy = max_k y_k − min_k y_k` across LLM rollouts as a quality flag. We can repurpose: ensemble disagreement across K=8 sampled answers is a label-free correctness signal — direct competitor to prefill DoM.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- LoRA r=16, α=32, lr=2e-5, linear scheduler.
- Hyperparameter sweep: bin levels ∈ {10, 20, 100}, β_2 (rank-loss weight) ∈ {1, 10, 100}, σ (Gaussian target std) ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1}. Best: not specified per-task in the table extract we have.
- Synthetic-data generators: greedy decoding for non-thinking models; QwQ-32B uses temperature 0.6 + MinP 0 (per official recommendation) to mitigate repetition.
- Forward KL preferred over reverse KL: "more stable training" — they note the theoretical mode-seeking argument for reverse KL "does not always hold for LLMs (Wu et al. 2025)."
- Reasoning-chain summarization step before LLM-judge for R1-distilled traces (long traces confuse the judge — relevant if we ever judge on R1-Distill outputs in P11-FE5).
- UNLI logistic transformation is *inverted* before training to amplify sensitivity near 0 and 1 — relevant if we ever target rare-correctness or high-confidence regimes on MATH-500.
- UNLI and δ-NLI upsampled up to 4× during training (Lee et al. 2022).
