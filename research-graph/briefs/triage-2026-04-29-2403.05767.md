# Triage brief — 2403.05767 — Extending Activation Steering to Broad Skills and Multiple Behaviours

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05767
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor).** §3.1 / §4.1
   reports that steering at **layer 0** produces the *opposite* of the intended
   effect — subtracting "code" at L0 *improves* coding scores. The authors
   speculate this is mediated by copy suppression (McDougall 2310.04625): low
   layers suppress what they predict, so removing the suppression target
   amplifies the signal downstream. **Mechanism that would fire:** if Qwen's
   L19 DoM is a downstream readout of an earlier-layer copy-suppression /
   amplification circuit rather than a primary correctness representation, the
   AUROC=0.7731 number is geometric collateral, not the real signal.
   **Falsifying experiment:** ablate / mean-replace the residual stream at L0–L5
   on prefill tokens and recompute L19 DoM AUROC on the cached Stage 2 NPZ. If
   AUROC drops sharply (≥10 points), F-2's "L19 is privileged" framing weakens
   into "L19 is a convenient probe-point downstream of the actual
   computation."

2. **Refutes H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy with low
   alignment tax).** §3.1, Figure 3b shows a 10% drop in coding top-1 accuracy
   carries a 3% drop in *general text* top-1 accuracy — a 30% relative
   alignment tax for narrow steering. Multi-behavior combined-vector steering
   (§3.2, Fig 6) additionally produces *mode collapse* (one answer chosen
   >95%) and faulty outputs (>5% off-grammar tokens) at coefficients that
   visibly move the matching score. **Mechanism that would fire:** DoM
   steering toward correctness on MATH-500 may degrade reasoning *generally*,
   masking real correctness gains; reported MATH-500 deltas could be
   confounded by mode collapse onto the most frequent answer in the prompt's
   answer template. **Falsifying experiment:** H-1 pre-registration must
   report (a) fraction of off-grammar / non-numeric outputs at each injection
   coefficient, (b) entropy of the predicted-answer distribution, and (c)
   PPL on a held-out non-MATH text slab. If any of (a/b/c) breaks at the
   coefficient that yields the headline accuracy delta, the H-1 finding is a
   mode-collapse artifact.

3. **Refutes F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos=0.046, as a
   meaningful structural claim).** §3.2 / §4.2 reports that injecting *five
   different* contrastive behavior vectors at five adjacent layers (L11–L15)
   simultaneously produces nearly-additive matching-score effects with
   "interaction effects [that] do not substantially reduce the steering
   effect." The implication: directions for distinct contrastive tasks at the
   same residual-stream slab are nearly orthogonal *generically*.
   **Mechanism that would fire:** if the residual stream's high
   dimensionality (~3584 for Qwen-1.5B) makes near-orthogonality the *default*
   between any two contrast-derived directions, the cos=0.046 in F-3 is
   uninformative — it's the prior, not the posterior. **Falsifying
   experiment:** at L19, build 20 random contrast pairs (e.g. odd-vs-even
   problem indices, length>median vs length<median, problem-1-vs-2,
   problem-3-vs-4, etc.) on the same prefill cache used for the prefill DoM.
   Compute pairwise cos. If the median is <0.1 and the (prefill, final)
   cos=0.046 is within the IQR, F-3 is a generic geometry fact, not a
   correctness-specific finding.

4. **Refutes F-9 / H-6 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with
   single-layer L19 DoM).** §3.2 simultaneous-steering (Fig 7) shows that
   multi-layer simultaneous injection succeeds where single-layer
   *combined-vector* injection fails — i.e. spreading information across
   layers is doing real work that a single-layer single-vector approach
   cannot replicate. **Mechanism that would fire:** if correctness is
   distributed across layers (in the same way distinct behaviors are in this
   paper), then a multi-layer DoM mosaic should *exceed* single-layer L19
   DoM AUROC, contra F-9's redundancy claim. **Falsifying experiment:**
   train a logistic probe on concatenated prefill DoM projections at
   {L11, L13, L15, L17, L19, L21, L23} vs L19-only, OOF 5-fold on the same
   1024-tok labels. If multi-layer ≥ L19 + 1.5 AUROC points, the redundancy
   in F-9 is wrong (and CoE-60's lift was real-signal that L19 alone misses).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC=0.7731): the paper's CAA methodology (last-token
  contrastive activations averaged across 500 prompts) is a near-direct
  generalization of our prefill DoM construction. It validates that
  last-token-of-prefill is the canonical extraction point — same intuition
  cited in §2.1 ("masked attention → last token has most context").
- **F-3** (cos=0.046): see Refutation 3 above — the paper's finding that
  near-orthogonality between distinct contrast directions is generic at the
  same layer challenges F-3's interpretation as content-bearing.
- **H-1** (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy): the paper is
  *the* methodological reference for what an honest H-1 experiment must
  report — alignment tax, faulty-output rate, mode-collapse check.
  Specifically Table 1 (per-layer per-behavior coefficient grid) and the
  ±5% faulty / ±95% mode-collapse criteria are pre-registration templates for
  H-1.
- **H-6** (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok): see Refutation 4
  above — paper's simultaneous-steering > combined-steering result is a
  prior reason to suspect single-layer is leaky.
- **F-10** (PH = Gaussian null on trained residual streams): not directly
  attacked, but the paper's finding that *generic* contrastive directions
  steer effectively is consistent with F-10's "trained residual streams look
  Gaussian to topology" — Gaussian-with-direction-structure is the canonical
  setting where activation-steering works and PH does not.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), last-token of residual stream** —
  for each of N matching-prompt / non-matching-prompt pairs, take the
  last-token residual at a chosen layer and subtract; average the diff across
  N pairs.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached Stage 2 prefill NPZs (we already
  have prefill activations at L19 for 500 MATH-500 problems).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-1 already needs CAA-style steering
  vectors and this paper's last-token / Llama-2 setup is the closest direct
  reference for our Qwen pipeline.

- **Permuted-vector baseline** — generate a per-layer permutation of the
  steering vector that preserves mean and std but destroys directional
  content, used as null. §2.1.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be the *first* H-1 control before
  reporting any DoM steering effect; cleaner than random-direction baselines
  because matched in marginal stats.

- **Multi-layer simultaneous injection (one vector per layer, single
  global coefficient)** — instead of summing into one vector, inject distinct
  vectors at distinct layers and sweep one global coefficient. §2.2 / Fig 7.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (requires inference with intervention hooks).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — for testing whether a multi-layer DoM
  mosaic captures correctness signal that L19 alone misses (Refutation 4).

- **Mode-collapse / faulty-output discard rule** — drop hyperparameter
  combinations where >5% of outputs are off-grammar OR a single answer
  exceeds 95% frequency. §2.2.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (post-hoc filter).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — must apply to any H-1 result before
  reporting accuracy deltas.

- **Mean-centring (cited Jorgensen 2312.03813)** — add a "text" steering
  vector while subtracting the target-skill vector, anchoring inference
  near the natural-text manifold to reduce alignment tax.
  *Replication cost*: free if running CAA; just compute an extra contrast.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-1 should pre-register whether mean-
  centring is applied; results without it are likely worse.

- **Layer × coefficient grid search** — sweep layer in {0,5,10,15,20,25,29,31}
  and coefficient in a log-spaced grid, pick best. Table 1.
  *Replication cost*: 8h H100 for Qwen-1.5B's 28 layers.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for H-1, no for production probing (we
  already know L19 is the operating point for prefill DoM).

## Approaches & framings

- **"Broad vs narrow skills are about equally steerable"** — the headline
  surprise of §3.1. Reframes the steering literature: steering does not
  require the target to be a "single concept" with a clean linear direction;
  even diffuse capabilities like "general coding" yield similar effect sizes
  to specific targets like "Python." Intersects F-2: our prefill L19 DoM may
  be steering "general correct-ness" rather than any specific reasoning
  competence — that's *fine* for a probe, but means H-1's intervention will
  generalize across math sub-skills (positive) but also across non-math text
  (alignment tax).

- **"Combined vectors fail; distributed vectors work"** — §4.2. Reframes
  multi-target steering as a layer-routing problem rather than a
  vector-combination problem. Intersects H-6 / F-9: if correctness has
  components that live at different layers, the right pipeline is
  multi-layer simultaneous probing, not single-layer projection.

- **"Alignment tax is real, not zero"** — §3.1 Fig 3b explicitly prices the
  tax at 10%-coding-loss → 3%-text-loss, contradicting prior steering
  literature (Rimsky, Turner, Zou, Li) which claimed minimal tax.
  Intersects H-1: any honest steering experiment must report tax separately,
  not just the in-domain effect.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **The Pile (5000 samples, 4096-tok truncation)** — open / HF. Applicable: no
  for the MATH-500 1024-tok regime, but applicable as the held-out
  text-perplexity slab for measuring alignment tax under H-1 steering.
- **CodeParrot Python subset (Tunstall et al. 2022)** — open / HF. Applicable:
  no — we don't steer for coding ability.
- **Perez et al. 2212.09251 behavioral binary-Q evals (5 datasets:
  anti-immigration, agreeableness, myopia, sycophancy, wealth-seeking)** —
  open / HF (`Anthropic/model-written-evals`). Applicable: not directly to
  correctness, but the *format* (binary A/B with `[` template forcing the
  next token) is a clean evaluation pattern for testing whether
  steered-Qwen still respects answer formatting on MATH-500
  (mode-collapse check).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Llama 2 7B Chat, bfloat16, no sampling for generation.
- Steering vectors are *not* normalized (allows the same coefficient to work
  across layers). §2.1.
- Random shuffle seed = 13 (for reproducibility against this paper).
- Adaptive coefficient sweep: relative score < 0.05 → stop run; relative
  score < 0.15 → step size 5 instead of 1. Appendix A.2.
- 500 samples for activation generation, 200 validation, rest for test
  (multi-behavior section). §2.2.
- Inject one *behavior* per *layer* in simultaneous setup: myopia@L11,
  wealth-seeking@L12, sycophancy@L13, agreeableness@L14,
  anti-immigration@L15. Linear layer ordering, no overlap.
- Code: <https://github.com/TeunvdWeij/extending-activation-addition>.
- Sycophancy steering specifically *failed* on this dataset (vs Rimsky 2023
  where it worked) — authors attribute to dataset's narrow political-topology
  framing. Cautionary tale for evaluating any "behavior" via single dataset.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Layer-0 sign-flip test on Qwen-1.5B prefill DoM.** Use cached Stage 2
  prefill NPZ at L0 (if extracted) or extract from H100 pod once. Compute
  L0 DoM against same correct/incorrect split as F-2. AUROC < 0.5 (i.e.
  *flipped*) replicates the paper's L0 finding and would constrain the
  copy-suppression hypothesis (Refutation 1).
- **Generic-contrast cosine baseline at L19.** Use cached Stage 2 prefill
  NPZ for 500 MATH-500 problems. Build 20 arbitrary 250/250 splits at L19,
  compute mass-mean direction for each, compute pairwise cosines. If median
  ≤ 0.1, F-3's cos=0.046 is at the median of the null and not a meaningful
  geometric finding (Refutation 3).
- **Multi-layer DoM concatenation probe.** Use cached prefill NPZs at
  {L11, L13, L15, L17, L19, L21, L23} (re-extract if not cached). Train
  logistic regression on concatenated (problem × 7-layer) DoM projections
  with the same 5-fold OOF split as F-2. If AUROC ≥ 0.79, multi-layer
  exceeds single-layer L19 (Refutation 4 / contradicts H-6 claim that
  CoE-60 redundancy is total).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2403.05767 — Extending Activation Steering to Broad Skills and Multiple Behaviours (van der Weij, Poesio & Schoots, 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodological reference for any DoM-steering experiment in
this project. Uses contrastive-activation-addition (CAA) at the last token of
the residual stream on Llama 2 7B Chat — the same extraction shape as our
prefill DoM at L19 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Three findings constrain how H-1 must
be designed: (a) layer-0 steering produces *opposite* effects, attributed to
copy suppression; (b) combining multiple steering vectors via mean/sum
*fails* and produces mode collapse; (c) simultaneous injection at distinct
layers works for ~2 of 5 behaviors, suggesting distinct concepts live at
distinct layers. Also documents a non-trivial alignment tax (10%
in-domain → 3% out-of-domain) that contradicts the steering literature's
"low tax" narrative.

**Key claim we tested:** Single-layer single-vector contrastive steering is
sufficient for distinct behaviors and capabilities, with negligible
alignment tax. (We translate to: prefill L19 DoM is a sufficient
correctness-signal direction, with redundancy across higher-PR features and
no general-purpose tax.)

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** The paper's findings *predict* (a) L19 DoM
correctness steering will mode-collapse before reaching meaningful accuracy
deltas, (b) multi-layer DoM mosaic will outperform single-layer L19, (c)
the cos(prefill, final)=0.046 in F-3 is generic. Three new hypotheses
(H-73, H-74, H-75) and four FutureExperiments (P11-FE70, FE71, FE72, FE73)
operationalize these tests against cached or near-cached data.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE70, P11-FE71, P11-FE72, P11-FE73 / H-1, H-6,
H-73, H-74, H-75 / F-2, F-3, F-9.

**Status:** TO TEST (HIGH — H-74 is CRITICAL precondition for H-1).
