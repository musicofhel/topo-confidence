# Triage brief — 2604.23829 — Domain-Filtered Knowledge Graphs from Sparse Autoencoder Features

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23829
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM single-direction probe at AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B).**
   The paper's central modeling assumption is that a model's domain knowledge lives in
   *many* SAE features and that "related ideas are scattered across many neighboring
   features" — i.e., a single direction is a lossy summary of distributed concept structure.
   This directly threatens F-2's claim that one supervised L19 direction is the right
   correctness probe. Mechanism: an SAE-decomposed correctness probe at the equivalent
   layer would aggregate evidence over a retained universe of, say, 50–500 readable
   features, recovering signal that DoM averages away. Falsifiable test: train a
   Gemma-Scope-style residual SAE (or a JumpReLU SAE, or even sparse-PCA as a cheap
   proxy) on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 prefill activations, fit a logistic probe over
   the filtered concept universe, and compare 5-fold OOF AUROC to 0.7731. A lift
   ≥0.04 (i.e., AUROC ≥ 0.815) refutes F-2's "single direction suffices" framing and
   reframes DoM as a low-rank readout of a richer feature basis.

2. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).**
   F-9 dismisses cross-layer information because *one specific* cross-layer object
   (CoE-60 = cosine-of-embedding trajectory) carried no signal beyond L19 DoM. The
   paper's transcoder mechanism graph is a fundamentally different cross-layer object:
   it captures *latent-mediated* writes from layer L into layer L+1 via a sparse
   transcoder, not a cosine trajectory. The paper's claim is that L20→L21 transcoder
   pathways carry mechanism information not visible in either layer's residual stream
   alone. If a transcoder-mediated probe at the L18→L19 transition on Qwen-2.5-1.5B
   beats the single-layer DoM, F-9 is wrong about cross-layer information generally —
   CoE-60 was redundant only because it used the wrong cross-layer summary. Falsifiable
   test: train a width-16k transcoder on the L18→L19 transition for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on
   MATH-500 prefill activations, build a correctness probe over latent activations,
   compare to 0.7731. Lift ≥0.02 refutes F-9's broad framing.

3. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket signature is collective, not per-problem).**
   The paper argues that "bridge concepts" — features that span topical regions and
   bridge neighboring chapter basins — are *individually* identifiable from
   co-occurrence statistics on a single SAE feature. F-7 says the D-bucket geometric
   signature is collective (the 36 D-bucket problems together form a distinctive
   cluster) and disappears at the per-problem level. If an SAE feature co-occurrence
   graph built over Qwen residual SAEs places D-bucket problems individually in a
   bridge corridor of the layout (each D-bucket problem activating a distinctive
   bridge-region feature subset), then the signature is per-problem after all and
   F-7's framing is wrong about granularity. Falsifiable test: extract SAE features
   for the 36 D-bucket and 207 A-bucket MATH-500 problems on a Gemma-equivalent
   model, build the multi-granular co-occurrence graph from §4.1, check whether
   D-bucket problems individually localize to bridge regions vs forming a diffuse
   cluster.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold)** — paper offers a higher-resolution
  alternative to the single-direction probe; SAE feature universe is the natural
  decomposition target.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — transcoder mechanism graphs could
  mechanistically explain why prefill and final-token directions are orthogonal: they
  may correspond to feature-disjoint upstream-feature sets writing into orthogonal
  residual subspaces.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** — paper's "bridge concepts"
  framing is structurally analogous to D-bucket problems sitting between A and B
  basins; co-occurrence graphs are the obvious test for per-problem vs collective.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)** — transcoder graphs are a different
  cross-layer object that could carry signal CoE-60 missed.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — paper argues
  for *feature-level* attribution via transcoder graphs, not head-level. If feature
  attribution cleanly separates the two circuits (consistent with cos = 0.046),
  H-13's head-level granularity is too coarse.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free)** — SAE features are also label-free
  by construction; paper's filtering pipeline is a label-free way to identify
  reasoning-relevant features.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Multi-stage contrastive SAE filtering (target vs contrast corpora)** — score each
  feature on support (presence in target), enrichment (target vs contrast), and
  localization (within-corpus concentration); take top-k as shortlist (top 30k of
  Gemma Scope L20+L21 width-65k SAEs in the paper).
  *Replication cost*: Major. Requires (a) a residual SAE on Qwen at L19 — not
  available off the shelf; we'd need to train one (~1 week H100) OR use a sparse-PCA
  proxy (~1h CPU); (b) a "contrast corpus" framing for math vs non-math, which we'd
  need to design.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — but with sparse-PCA as the cheap-proxy first pass
  before any SAE training commitment. Math-correct vs math-incorrect (or
  MATH-500 vs ARC-Challenge non-math) are the natural target/contrast pair.

- **Packet-level adjudication (LLM-judged feature visibility/relevance/distinctiveness)**
  — every shortlisted feature gets an evidence packet (description hypothesis, target
  windows, contrast windows, chapter context) reviewed by a frozen LLM contract.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2 days CPU for ~30k packets at ~$0.001/packet.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not directly applicable — our pipeline does not have
  human-readable concept descriptions for residual directions. Would only make sense
  after training a Qwen SAE.

- **Sentence-level Jaccard co-occurrence graph; lift to paragraph/subchapter/chapter
  via binary presence (§4.1)** — feature presence per sentence based on max-positive
  activation > θ_v threshold; co-occurrence counts → Jaccard normalization → sparse
  k-NN per node.
  *Replication cost*: <1h CPU once features are in hand.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — feasible on cached L19 activations even without
  an SAE, by treating top-k activated PCA components as pseudo-features. Could
  reveal D-bucket bridge structure (refutation #3 above).

- **Transcoder mechanism graph (source SAE + target SAE + transcoder spanning a
  layer transition; static and unit-conditioned dynamic graphs)** — width-16k
  transcoder L20→L21 in the paper. Edges = readable projections of latent-mediated
  source-to-target writes, with three evidence channels: source-feature activity,
  latent activity, target-feature activity.
  *Replication cost*: Substantial. Need transcoder + source/target SAEs; ~1 week H100
  to train all three on Qwen-2.5-1.5B for L18→L19 or L19→L20. Refutation #2 above.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if the SAE-probe lift in #1 is positive — would
  motivate the transcoder investment.

- **Hierarchy-aware compression with mixed-cover rule (§4.3, Appendix C.1)** —
  abstraction tree built from feature-description embeddings via mutual-kNN +
  recursive farthest-first seed splitting + grounded summarization; collapses dense
  graphs into supernode views.
  *Replication cost*: 1-2 days CPU once feature descriptions exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: probably not — adds value mainly when graphs are too
  dense for human inspection, which isn't our current bottleneck.

- **Auto-relate edge labeling (LLM-generated readable relation phrases on edges)** —
  co-occurrence edges labeled from joint and contrastive evidence sentences; mechanism
  edges labeled from sentence-level evidence + latent-level hints.
  *Replication cost*: small once the graphs exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: nice-to-have for any final write-up, not a research
  primitive.

- **Density maps on shared sentence-graph coordinates (§6.1)** — overlay weighted
  views on the same fixed layout to test structure recovery without separate-layout
  confounds.
  *Replication cost*: <1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful for D-bucket / bridge-concept analysis if
  we go that way.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Strict domain concept universe" framing (§3)** — the right starting point for
  interpretability is not the full SAE inventory but a ruthlessly filtered subset.
  Intersects F-2: argues that a *filtered* feature universe (not all dimensions of
  the residual stream) carries the actual signal. DoM may be picking up the right
  direction but discarding the rest of the filtered universe.
- **Two-view coupling: corpus-scale co-occurrence + local-scale mechanism (§4)** —
  global concept structure and local sentence-level mechanism are separate but
  aligned objects on the same retained node set. Suggests our project's "global
  geometry" findings (F-1, F-7) and "per-problem signal" findings (F-2, F-8) are
  similarly two views that should be aligned on a common node universe.
- **Bridge concepts as first-class objects (§6.1)** — concepts that span topical
  basins are interesting in their own right, not noise. Direct framing for D-bucket:
  D-bucket may BE the bridge corridor between A-bucket and B-bucket basins.
- **Feature-level attribution > head-level attribution** — implicit in §4.2's
  transcoder graphs. Reframes H-13's head-level attribution as too coarse a unit.
- **Knowledge-graph object as alternative to flat probe** — reframes correctness
  prediction from "find the best direction" to "find the right concept neighborhood
  and its mechanism graph."

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Gemma Scope residual SAEs at L20, L21, width 65k.
- Width-16k transcoder L20→L21 (residual stream).
- Shortlist config (Appendix B): minimum target support rate 5×10⁻⁴, minimum target
  activation mass 10, drop bottom 20% of target-support features per layer before
  ranking; export top 30k features after recall-oriented ranking.
- Subchapter-level binary-support localization with strong-support hybrid refinement.
- Packet-level adjudication uses two schema-validated outputs per feature:
  (visible, evidence_sentence_ids) and (belongs_here, distinctiveness, justification).
- Abstraction tree: PCA-projected description embeddings → mutual-kNN graph →
  recursive farthest-first splitting → grounded summarization (representatives near
  centroid + semantic extremes + near-boundary negatives + descendant leaf anchors).
- Co-occurrence: presence per sentence via max-positive token activation > θ_v
  (calibrated from each feature's empirical sentence-score distribution); lift to
  paragraph/subchapter/chapter via OR over constituent sentences; Jaccard
  normalization; sparse k-NN symmetrized graph.
- Browser artifact at https://saegraph-render-dual-private.onrender.com/.
- Code/SAE weights: paper does not state a public release; only the browser is
  linked. May need to email authors.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Sparse-PCA / NMF on cached Qwen L19 prefill activations** as a cheap
  cross-check for refutation #1. We have NPZ caches of `pathway11_h100/.../prefill_activations.npz`
  for the 500-problem MATH-500 set on Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Fit sparse-PCA with k = 64,
  128, 256 components and a positive-only nonneg constraint; train a logistic probe
  over the components; compare 5-fold OOF AUROC to 0.7731. Cost: ~1h CPU.
  *Limitation*: sparse-PCA components are not as interpretable as proper SAE
  features but provide a fast lower bound on the SAE-probe potential.
- **Multi-granular co-occurrence graph on top-k pseudo-features** as a cheap
  cross-check for refutation #3. Treat top-50 sparse-PCA components as
  pseudo-features, threshold per problem, build problem-level Jaccard co-occurrence
  graph, check whether the 36 D-bucket problems individually localize to a bridge
  corridor between A-bucket and B-bucket regions. Cost: ~1h CPU on cached data.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.23829 — Domain-Filtered Knowledge Graphs from Sparse Autoencoder Features (Winnicki, Gnanasekaran, Darve, 2026)

**Relevance:** Mechanistic interpretability methodology paper. Uses Gemma Scope
SAEs (L20, L21, width 65k) plus a width-16k L20→L21 transcoder to build two
aligned graph views (corpus co-occurrence + local transcoder mechanism) over a
contrastively filtered concept universe. Case study is a biology textbook with
history and geology contrasts; no correctness experiments and no math reasoning.
Relevance to topo-confidence is purely methodological: the paper's filtered SAE
universe + co-occurrence graph + transcoder mechanism graph is a higher-resolution
alternative to our single-direction DoM probe (F-2) and a different cross-layer
object than CoE-60 (F-9). The "bridge concepts" framing is a structural analog to
D-bucket (F-7).

**Key claim we tested:** A domain-filtered SAE feature universe organized into
co-occurrence + transcoder mechanism graphs is a more faithful representation of
internal model knowledge than single-direction probes or unfiltered SAE inventories.
*Their claim, in our terms*: the prefill L19 DoM at AUROC 0.7731 is a low-rank
summary of distributed concept structure, and an SAE-decomposed probe should lift.

**Our result:** **CITED ONLY (TO TEST).** The paper's claim is methodological and
their evaluation (textbook structure recovery on Gemma) does not directly speak to
math correctness on Qwen. We have not yet trained an SAE on Qwen L19. The cheap
sparse-PCA proxy (H-73) is the first replicable test; if it lifts, H-75 motivates
the full SAE-training investment. Until then, status is TO TEST.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73 (sparse-PCA proxy, READY), P11-FE74 (D-bucket
bridge-corridor test, BLOCKED on FE73), P11-FE75 (full Qwen SAE + contrastive
filter, BLOCKED on FE73), P11-FE76 (transcoder mechanism graph, BLOCKED on FE75);
H-73, H-74, H-75.

**Status:** TO TEST (H-73 HIGH priority; H-74 MEDIUM; H-75 LOW).
