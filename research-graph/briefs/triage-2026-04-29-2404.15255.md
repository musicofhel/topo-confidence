# Triage brief — 2404.15255 — How to use and interpret activation patching

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255
Triaged: 2026-04-29

This is a methodology / best-practices tutorial by Heimersheim & Nanda. It does not introduce new datasets, models, or quantitative claims; it codifies how to do activation patching correctly (denoising vs. noising, granularity, path patching, choice of metric, common pitfalls). The relevance to our project is *meta*: it tells us which causal experiments would be required to elevate our findings beyond correlation, and where our current pipeline cuts corners that the paper specifically warns against.

## Refutations

1. **F-2 is purely correlational; the paper's central thesis says correlational probes are not enough.** F-2 ("Prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor, AUROC 0.7731") is established by *fitting a logistic probe* on cached activations and reading off OOF AUROC. Heimersheim & Nanda spend the entire paper arguing that correlational evidence (probes, logit lens reads, attention patterns) is *not* evidence of causal involvement in a circuit — only patching is. Mechanism by which the refutation fires: the L19 prefill activations may carry a problem-difficulty / problem-familiarity signal that *correlates* with correctness without the model *using* that direction to compute its answer. Falsifiable test: **noise** the L19 prefill DoM direction (corrupt → clean patch, replacing the L19 prefill activation projection onto the DoM direction with the projection from a randomly-paired wrong problem) and re-generate K=1. If MATH-500 accuracy on the previously-correct subset does not drop materially, F-2 is correlational only and AUROC 0.7731 ≠ "the model uses this direction."

2. **H-1's per-position DoM steering assumes denoising-style sufficiency, but if the prefill DoM lives in an AND-gate it will fail despite F-2's AUROC.** §2.4 of the paper is a worked example: in an AND circuit, denoising any single component does not restore behaviour; only denoising *all* components does. H-1 proposes adding a learned DoM direction to push wrong→right. Mechanism: if correctness depends jointly on (a) the prefill DoM direction *and* (b) a separate problem-content direction (e.g. RoPE-mediated answer-token positioning, or an attention-head output that gates whether the DoM is *read*), then injecting only the DoM during generation will not move accuracy — even though the probe AUROC is 0.7731. Falsifiable test: run the steering experiment H-1 already proposes; if accuracy gain is null or below the AUROC-implied ceiling, this paper predicted it (and the next move is path patching to identify the missing AND-input).

3. **F-9's redundancy claim conflates "same AUROC" with "same mechanism" — backup-head / OR-gate possibility unaddressed.** F-9 ("CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM") is supported by matched AUROCs. §3.2 of the paper warns explicitly about backup behaviour / Hydra effect (McGrath et al. 2023): two components can each look fully sufficient on their own (OR-gate redundancy), and the model uses one or the other depending on which is intact. Mechanism: CoE-60 may be summing across layers because the model maintains a redundant per-layer representation as a backup; ablating L19 alone might leave CoE-60-style multi-layer signal completely intact. Falsifiable test: zero-ablate (or noise) the L19 residual stream component during generation, then re-fit a CoE-60-style probe on the *post-ablation* activations. If the CoE-60 AUROC is preserved, F-9 is wrong: the two are parallel pathways, not the same thing.

4. **F-3's orthogonality (cos = 0.046) is a *correlational* statement on one prompt distribution and may not survive a corruption test.** F-3 says prefill DoM and final-token DoM directions are nearly orthogonal on MATH-500. §3.2 ("Scope of activation patching") and §2.6 ("Choosing corrupted prompts") both warn that any geometric statement computed on a single distribution traces *only* the variation present in that distribution. Mechanism: MATH-500 has a tight token distribution (digit/equation-heavy answers); orthogonality between prefill and final-token directions could be a property of *what differs between hard and easy MATH problems*, not a structural property of the model. Falsifiable test: compute the same DoM directions on a *corrupted* MATH-500 (random-numeric-perturbation of the question, where the model's answer is now "wrong" relative to the original label). If the cosine jumps materially (e.g., > 0.3), F-3 is distribution-conditional, not structural.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 prefill-DoM)** — every metric-pitfall section of this paper applies. The probe trains a binary classifier; §4.2 warns that *binary/discrete metrics overemphasize threshold-crossings*. If the underlying signal is continuous (e.g. logit-difference between candidate answers) and we are losing information by collapsing to correct/incorrect, the AUROC is *strictly* a lower bound on the directional signal's true causal strength. Worth re-fitting with a continuous logit-difference target.
- **F-3 (cos = 0.046)** — see Refutation 4. Geometric claims on one prompt distribution are exactly what §2.6 warns against.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature, peak PR 67/88 at L13)** — §3.2 "no minimality" is directly relevant: the D-bucket signature may be sufficient *but not necessary*, or vice versa. We need a noising experiment (corrupt the L13-prefill of D-bucket members with A-bucket activations) to know which.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ↔ L19 DoM redundancy)** — see Refutation 3.
- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null)** — *negative finding* — paper offers no direct contradiction but does suggest that if PH is at null, mechanism-locating must come from patching, not from topological summary statistics.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — the paper's denoising/noising distinction directly maps onto steering: adding a learned vector ≈ denoising sufficiency test. See Refutation 2.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — *strongly* aligned. This paper, ACDC (2304.14997), and IOI (2211.00593) form the canonical methodology stack for H-13. Heimersheim & Nanda is the *practitioner's* layer above ACDC's automated approach — read before running.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical)** — §2.5 mean-ablation discussion is relevant: a clean test of "rotation is mechanical, not computed" is to mean-ablate the prefill DoM at every layer except L19 and see whether the final-token rotation pattern is preserved.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Denoising (clean → corrupt patching)** — copy clean activations into a corrupted run; tests sufficiency. *Replication cost*: one extraction pass per source/destination pair on cached MATH-500 prompts (~2h CPU if activations are cached, ~1 H100-hour if re-generating). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — converts F-2 from correlational to causal.

- **Noising (corrupt → clean patching)** — copy corrupted activations into a clean run; tests necessity. *Replication cost*: same as denoising. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — strictly stronger evidence than denoising for our use case (we want to know whether the L19 DoM is *necessary* for correct answering, not just sufficient).

- **Path patching** — patch only the path A → B, not the whole downstream subtree. Distinguishes mediation from amplification/calibration. *Replication cost*: 4–8h CPU on cached activations using transformer_lens hooks. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, if denoising/noising sweep flags L19 as causal — path patching is the natural follow-up to ask "via which downstream component does L19 act?"

- **Causal Scrubbing (Chan et al. 2022)** — full circuit verification by noising every non-circuit connection. *Replication cost*: heavy — needs a complete hypothesised circuit to scrub against, and that circuit doesn't exist yet for our findings. *We'd plausibly run this*: not yet — pre-requisite is finding a candidate circuit via path patching first.

- **Attribution patching / AtP\*** (Nanda 2023) — first-order Taylor approximation to activation patching; runs in 2 forward passes regardless of granularity. *Replication cost*: minimal — a few hours of code, runs on cached activations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural exploratory tool to identify which heads/layers are worth full patching, before paying the per-component cost.

- **Logit-difference metric** — measure model's logit(correct) − logit(incorrect); linear in residual stream, controls for "boost everything" components. *Replication cost*: trivial when answer-token positions are known (which they are for MATH-500). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be added alongside binary AUROC for every existing probe (F-2, F-8) as a sanity check.

- **KL-divergence over full output distribution** — alternative metric that captures both positive and negative components, used by Conmy et al. ACDC (2304.14997). *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural complement to logit-difference.

- **Direct logit attribution / logit lens** — decompose the residual stream by component contribution to logit(answer). *Replication cost*: trivial on cached data. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a per-layer / per-head attribution map that complements F-2's L19-only result.

## Approaches & framings

- **Sufficiency vs necessity are not the same.** This is the paper's headline conceptual move. Our entire current evidence stack (probes, AUROC, cosine similarities) is correlational and does not even attempt either — let alone distinguish the two. Adopting this framing would require us to label every finding F-N as either "sufficient (denoising-confirmed)", "necessary (noising-confirmed)", or "correlational only (probe)". Currently *all* of F-1…F-10 are in the third bucket.

- **"A circuit is the *difference* the patch traces, not an absolute property of the model."** §2.6 — every patching result is conditional on (clean prompt, corrupt prompt) pair. Reframes our F-N findings as "MATH-500-difficulty differences traced by L19 DoM" rather than "the correctness signal lives at L19." This is a *softer* but more defensible framing.

- **Pareto frontier of circuit size vs. performance recovered.** Encourages reporting "we recover X% of correctness with Y% of components" rather than chasing 100%. Useful framing for any future minimal-circuit claim about the prefill signal.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **TransformerLens** (referenced throughout) is the canonical library for hook-based patching; all of ACDC, AtP*, and the Heimersheim/Nanda examples assume it. P11 H100 currently uses raw HuggingFace + custom hook code; switching to TransformerLens would unlock a large existing patching codebase but requires confirming Qwen-2.5-1.5B is supported (TransformerLens has spotty Qwen coverage as of mid-2024).
- **Mean-zero residual stream / mean-zero logits** (default in TransformerLens) — important when using raw-logit metrics; otherwise components that uniformly inflate logits look like real contributors.
- **ARENA chapter 1.3** — practical exercises for activation patching; useful onboarding before any project member runs the first patching sweep.
- **Logit difference > probability > accuracy** for exploratory patching; reverse for confirmatory patching.
- Code link: paper does not publish code; ACDC (Conmy et al. 2304.14997) and TransformerLens are the implementation references.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Denoising sweep on cached prefill activations**: we have prefill L19 activations cached for all 500 MATH-500 problems on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (`pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`). We can simulate denoising offline by replacing the L19 prefill activation of an *incorrectly-answered* problem with the L19 prefill activation of a *correctly-answered* problem and re-running *only the final-token decoding* through the model — this is a cheap H100-hour test of whether the L19 prefill activations are *sufficient* to flip an incorrect prediction to correct. Script needed: `pathway11_h100/patching_sweep.py`.
- **Logit-difference probe re-fit**: re-train F-2's L19 probe with target = logit(answer-token) − logit(top-incorrect-token) instead of binary correct/incorrect. Pure CPU, ~30min on cached activations. Tells us whether the AUROC-0.7731 binary signal is hiding a stronger continuous signal.
- **Direct logit attribution at L19**: project the prefill L19 residual stream onto the unembedding direction of correct/incorrect answer tokens and report per-problem contributions. Pure CPU, 1h. Validates whether L19 acts on the correctness logit *directly* or through later layers.

## 2404.15255 — How to use and interpret activation patching (Heimersheim & Nanda, 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodology / best-practices tutorial for activation patching, the standard mechanistic-interpretability technique for upgrading correlational findings to causal ones.
