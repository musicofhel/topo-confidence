# Triage brief — 2410.12877 — Improving Instruction-Following in Language Models through Activation Steering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12877
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Alessandro Stolfo, Vidhisha Balachandran, Safoora Yousefi, Eric Horvitz, Besmira Nushi (Microsoft Research). Venue: ICLR 2025. Code: `github.com/microsoft/llm-steer-instruct`.

The paper computes per-instruction "steering vectors" as difference-in-means between paired (with/without instruction) prompts at the last input token, normalizes to a unit vector `u_l`, and adds `c · u_l` to the residual stream of one selected layer. Compositionality is achieved by *injecting several vectors at different layers simultaneously* — they explicitly report that summing multiple vectors into one is "largely unsuccessful". Transfer from instruct → base sometimes outperforms same-model steering. Models: Phi-3 Mini, Gemma-2 2B/9B (instruct + base), Mistral-7B-v0.1. Benchmark: augmented IFEval (541 prompts).

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the "L19 is a privileged correctness layer" reading of F-2.** Stolfo et al. report that the optimal steering layer is highly *instruction-dependent* — for Phi-3 alone they pick L18 (JSON), L26 (highlight), L28 (capitalize) for different instructions, with explicit perplexity checks on held-out queries. If "produce a correct MATH answer" behaves like one of their instructions, the same architecture could have a single best correctness layer that is *not* L19. Mechanism: the layer at which a feature becomes linearly extractable is set by depth of computation, and they show this varies sharply within one architecture. Falsifiable: re-fit our L0..L27 logistic probe on Qwen-2.5-1.5B but *also* on Phi-3-Mini and Gemma-2-2B (P11 stage-2 extraction extended) — if peak DoM-AUROC layer is model-specific (e.g., L18 in Phi-3, L24 in Gemma), F-2's "L19" claim narrows to "Qwen family", not "transformer-universal".

2. **Refutes H-1's planned aggregation strategy.** H-1 currently plans per-position DoM directions and *adds* `α · w_t_norm` at each generated token. Stolfo et al. find that "combining multiple steering vectors into a single one" — i.e. additively in the residual at a single layer — is empirically *unsuccessful* and motivates them to inject at distinct *layers* instead. Our H-1 plan is doing the position-version of exactly that combination. Mechanism: when several near-orthogonal steering vectors are summed at one layer, the residual stream is shifted into off-manifold regions, hurting fluency more than it helps the target. Falsifiable: in the H-1 sweep, run an ablation that compares (a) per-position single-direction additive steering at L19, vs (b) Stolfo-style multi-layer injection where each generated-position direction is added at a *different* layer chosen by held-out perplexity — if (b) is the only configuration that produces a positive Δ-accuracy, H-1 must be re-specified.

3. **Refutes F-3's "two-mechanism" reading of cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046.** Stolfo et al. show that vectors derived on instruction-tuned Gemma-2-2B *transfer to the base model*, sometimes outperforming same-model steering — i.e. orthogonal-yet-functional directions can be redundant copies of the same instruction feature replicated across the residual stream rather than evidence of two distinct mechanisms. Our F-3 currently reads cos≈0.05 as "prefill and final-token correctness use distinct circuits". If transfer-style replication is the true story, both directions are partial reads of the same underlying feature. Mechanism: linear-representation hypothesis admits multiple orthogonal write directions for the same feature when pre/post-attention layers superpose. Falsifiable: replicate Stolfo's transfer protocol on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct → Qwen-2.5-1.5B (base) using both prefill_DoM and final_DoM as "instruction" vectors; if both directions transfer with similar effectiveness *and* steering with prefill_DoM affects final-token accuracy at the same layer, the "two-circuit" framing of F-3 weakens to "two redundant projections".

4. **Refutes the F-2 supervised-probe necessity (intersects H-12).** Stolfo's pipeline derives a directional behavior-controller without ever training a logistic probe on labels — pairwise contrastive prompts plus a dynamic coefficient suffice. F-2 currently relies on a 5-fold logistic probe with binary correctness labels. If "produce a correct answer" can be encoded as a contrast pair (correct-trace vs incorrect-trace prompts), the same direction could be recovered label-free. Mechanism: difference-in-means is itself a probe; supervision only sets the contrast set. Falsifiable: build a paired-contrast set from Pathway 11 Stage-2 caches (correct vs incorrect MATH-500 problems matched on prompt prefix), compute Stolfo-style DoM at L19 prefill, compare AUROC against the supervised L19 DoM probe (currently 0.7731). If within ±0.01 AUROC, F-2's logistic probe is reframable as "label-free DoM with explicit contrast set".

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Stolfo's compositional-steering finding (multi-layer injection succeeds where summed-vector injection fails) is direct evidence that orthogonality between two correctness-relevant directions doesn't imply mechanism separation.

- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** — Stolfo's method is a label-free alternative; H-12 should be expanded to include "Stolfo-style paired-contrast DoM" as a cheaper baseline before invoking SEPs.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Difference-in-means at last input token, unit-normalized** — paired (x, x+instruction) prompts; `v_l = (1/N)Σ(h(x+)_l − h(x)_l)`, `u_l = v_l/||v_l||`. Extracted at the *last token of the input* (i.e. position 0 of generation, the prefill last position). Replication cost: free — already implementable on cached P11 Stage-2 prefill activations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is mechanically identical to F-2's DoM construction modulo label vs contrast-set framing.
- **Dynamic steering coefficient `c` (paper Eq. 2)** — `c` is set so that the residual stream after injection has projection equal to the *mean* projection observed on instruction-containing prompts in the calibration set. Replication cost: free if we have both the contrast set and the target activation cache (we do, in scratch/pathway10). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the canonical "how much to inject" rule and replaces our current arbitrary α-sweep in H-1.
- **Per-instruction layer sweep with held-out perplexity gate** — sweeps a subset of layers, picks the layer that maximizes target-instruction adherence subject to a perplexity ceiling on a held-out query set. Replication cost: ≈ H100-day to do all 28 layers on Qwen-2.5-1.5B for a single instruction; ≈ 2h CPU on cached prefill activations for probe-only (no generation). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct upgrade to H-1 layer-selection logic.
- **Multi-layer compositional injection** — multiple vectors injected at different layers simultaneously, *not* summed into a single layer. Replication cost: same as H-1 per-token sweep. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — refutation 2 makes this the protocol-of-record for H-1.
- **Negation by subtraction** (word-exclusion = −word-inclusion vector). Replication cost: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of whether `−w_prefill_DoM` causes systematic *errors*; clean negative-control for H-1.
- **Cross-model transfer (instruct → base)** — vectors derived on instruct model directly applied to base model of same family; Gemma-2 2B transfer outperforms same-model in both with-/without-explicit-instruction settings. Replication cost: 1 H100-day to extract on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Base + apply prefill_DoM derived from Instruct. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — refutation 3 needs it.
- **Augmented-IFEval contrast-set construction** — paired prompts that differ only in the presence of the instruction, generated synthetically to avoid leak. Replication cost: 1h prompt-engineering on MATH-500 to build correct-trace vs incorrect-trace pair set. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed for label-free F-2 replication.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Steering vector as feature mean"**, not as logistic-probe direction. Reframes our F-2/F-3 directions: they are not just *predictive* of correctness, they should be *causally controlling* it via additive injection. This intersects H-1 directly and reframes F-2 from "correlational probe" to "candidate causal handle".
- **Layer-of-extraction as instruction-specific**, not architecture-universal. Refutes any "L19 is the universal correctness layer" reading of F-2 we might have been drifting toward; bounds F-2 to the Qwen family.
- **Compositionality via locality, not summation.** Reframes H-1: the mechanism for combining multiple steering signals is multi-layer injection, not residual summation at one layer.
- **Magnitude-equalization to in-distribution mean** as the steering coefficient rule. Reframes our default α-sweep into a principled `c = projection-onto-mean` calculation.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **IFEval (augmented)** — 541 prompts (163 format, 203 word-specific, plus length subsets), public, MIT/CC-licensed via the IFEval repo. Applicable? *no* — this is instruction-following, not mathematical reasoning; their format/word/length tasks don't substitute for MATH-500. The contrast-set construction recipe is reusable, but the dataset itself isn't.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code: `github.com/microsoft/llm-steer-instruct` — reference impl of the difference-in-means + dynamic-`c` + multi-layer injection pipeline. License: CC BY 4.0.
- Activation extraction at the **last token of the input**, not pooled across positions — exactly matches our F-2 prefill-position-0 protocol.
- Layer selection: held-out perplexity gate on a small validation query set, *not* on the steering benchmark itself, to avoid layer-overfit.
- Vector subtraction for negation (`−u_l` for exclusion). Drop-in negative control for H-1.
- Per-instruction calibration set is generated *separately* from the test set to avoid information leak — important: our H-1 plan needs the same separation so the dynamic-`c` is not fit on the held-out 100 problems.
- Reported failure mode: when the desired output is *input-dependent* (their "End Checker" task — final word must be a specific input-derived token), single-vector steering fails even at the right layer. Implication for our setting: a single prefill_DoM cannot encode problem-specific corrections; this is precisely the F-2 → H-1 ceiling we should expect.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Label-free DoM at L19 prefill on cached P11 Stage-2 activations.** Construct a contrast pair from existing MATH-500 caches (correct-trace vs incorrect-trace, matched on problem prefix), compute Stolfo-style `v_l = mean(h(correct) − h(incorrect))` at L19, fit no probe, just compute AUROC of `<h, u_19>` on held-out 100 problems. ≈30 min CPU. If AUROC is within ±0.01 of 0.7731, refutation 4 fires.
- **Dynamic-`c` recompute for prefill_DoM.** From `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` and matched activation cache, compute the mean projection of correct-class activations onto u_19, then derive `c` per Eq. 2. ≈10 min CPU. Output is the principled steering coefficient that should replace any manual α in H-1.
- **Per-layer DoM-AUROC curve from existing Stage-2 cache.** We already extract all 28 layers in P11 Stage-2; just re-fit the L0..L27 sweep and report peak-AUROC layer (already computed in `pathway11_h100/`). ≈free. Bounds whether F-2's L19 is uniquely optimal or one of several near-equivalent layers.
- **Negative-direction (−u_19) ablation as projection-only sanity check.** Score `−<h, u_19>` AUROC; should equal 1 − 0.7731 ≈ 0.2269. ≈free. Confirms direction is signed-meaningful (boring but a Stolfo-required prerequisite for using `−u` for exclusion).

## 2410.12877 — Improving Instruction-Following in Language Models through Activation Steering (Stolfo, Balachandran, Yousefi, Horvitz, Nushi, 2024)

**Relevance:** Methodologically central to F-2/F-3/H-1. Constructs behavior-controlling residual-stream directions via difference-in-means at the last input token (the same position F-2's prefill DoM uses), normalizes to a unit vector, and applies a *dynamically calibrated* coefficient that maps the residual to the mean in-distribution projection. Two findings are directly transferable to our setting: (1) optimal layer is instruction-specific within one architecture (challenging the universality of L19), (2) compositional steering requires multi-layer injection, *not* residual summation at one layer (challenging H-1's planned aggregation). The paper's transfer-from-instruct-to-base result also weakens any "two-mechanism" reading of F-3's cos=0.046.

**Key claim we tested:** Difference-in-means contrast vectors at the last input token, calibrated to in-distribution mean projection and injected at a layer chosen by held-out perplexity, modify model behavior reliably; multi-vector composition only works across multiple layers.
