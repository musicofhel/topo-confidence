# Triage brief — 2310.13121 — Understanding Addition in Transformers

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13121
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Philip Quirke, Fazl Barez (Apart Research / Univ. Oxford). ICLR 2024.
Code: https://github.com/apartresearch/Integer_Addition

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor, AUROC 0.7731)** — *via prefill-rows-don't-compute mechanism*. Quirke-Barez Section 7 shows that mean-ablating residual stream rows 0–10 (the prefill question rows, before the `=` token) produces **no loss change** in their 1-layer addition transformer: the model literally does not use the prefill rows for the answer calculation. If the same is true (even partially) for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500, then F-2's "prefill encodes correctness" framing is mechanistically wrong — what the L19 prefill DoM predicts is *problem identity / familiarity* (i.e. H-5), not a partial computation that the model later refines into the answer. Falsifiable: run row-wise mean-ablation of L19 prefill activations on MATH-500 with cached Stage 2 NPZs (`pathway11_h100/...`); if K=1 accuracy is unchanged but DoM AUROC drops to ~0.5, F-2 reframes from "prefill predicts correctness" to "prefill encodes problem-class label that correlates with eventual correctness."

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM directions orthogonal, cos 0.046)** — *via positional-algorithm-specialization mechanism*. Their §7 shows that **different output-token positions use different attention heads and different sub-algorithms** (A4 vs A3 vs A0). If LLM correctness encoding is similarly position-specialized, the prefill/final cos 0.046 is not evidence of "different latent variables" — it is the predicted consequence of position-specific subspace usage with RoPE rotating between them. The corollary is that per-position bank steering (H-1) is required *not* because of distinct meaning but because the *same* meaning lives in rotated bases. Falsifiable: apply per-position RoPE rotation to the prefill DoM and recompute cos against final DoM; if it rises substantially (e.g., > 0.3), F-3's "orthogonality implies independent signals" must be replaced with "orthogonality is positional rotation."

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — *via per-position-distinct-circuit mechanism*. Quirke-Barez prove that **even a 1-layer model runs distinct algorithms** for different answer tokens (A5/A4 fast-path vs A3/A2 staircase vs A1/A0 simplified). If multi-layer LLMs amplify this, then a per-token, per-layer trajectory (CoE-60) ought to carry information no single (layer, token) probe can capture. The current EXP-025 "CoE-60 ≈ single-DoM" finding may be a benchmark-specific coincidence on MATH-500 1024-tok. Falsifiable: build a held-out subset where the answer is short numerical (≤ 5 tokens) vs long symbolic (> 30 tokens); if CoE-60 AUROC > single-layer DoM AUROC by ≥ 0.03 on the long-symbolic subset, F-9 must be re-scoped to "redundant *only* on short-answer benchmarks."

4. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature)** — *via structural-failure-mode mechanism*. The paper identifies a precise, **non-geometric** failure pattern: cascading US9 cases (e.g. 445+555=1000) where MakeCarry1 must propagate right-to-left through ≥ 3 columns. The model fails on these even though the question is structurally identical to non-failing additions in geometric / dimensional features — failure is *algorithmic*, not collective. By analogy: D-bucket might be characterized by a discrete structural property (e.g., a problem that requires the model to maintain ≥ N intermediate quantities) rather than a "geometric signature." Falsifiable: cluster D-bucket problems by surface-feature counts (number of variables, number of carry-equivalent dependencies); if the cluster has ≥ 80% purity, F-7's "collective geometric signature" reduces to a downstream effect of structural complexity.

5. **Refutes F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across architectures and scales)** — *via toy-model-no-breathing mechanism*. A 1-layer toy with 3 attention heads on integer addition is the simplest non-trivial transformer that solves a real task. If breathing is universal it should be present here too (modulo magnitude). The Quirke-Barez per-digit loss curves and attention-staircase visualizations strongly suggest **discrete phase-transitions** (memorization → algorithm → cleanup) rather than smooth dimensional inflation/contraction. Falsifiable: download their checkpoint from `apartresearch/Integer_Addition`, extract residual-stream activations across the 18-token sequence, compute per-token PR. If PR is flat or shows step-function jumps rather than the inverted-U shape of F-1, the universality claim narrows to "models with ≥ N layers / depth-dependent phenomenon," not "universal."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Their mean-ablation result that rows 0-10 are *unused* is the strongest direct mechanistic challenge to F-2 in the literature so far. Anchor: AUROC 0.7731 (1024-tok OOF 5-fold). Their result implies F-2's signal must come from problem-identity encoding (H-5), not from partial-computation accumulation.
- **F-3 (cos 0.046 prefill vs final DoM)** — Their §7 position-specialization observation (A4 / A3 / A0 distinct algorithms) is directly compatible with cos 0.046 *without* requiring distinct semantic content; it reframes F-3 from "two different things being predicted" to "the same thing in two rotated bases."
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM)** — Their explicit demonstration that different output positions use different circuits provides the mechanistic ammunition for H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels) and gives a concrete prediction: CoE > single-DoM should grow with answer-token-length variance.
- **F-7 (D-bucket signature)** — Their structural-failure (US9 cascade) framing offers an alternative model: D-bucket as discrete-structural rather than continuous-geometric. Connects to H-21 (D-bucket attention entropy).
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — Their position-specialization gives a strong mechanistic prior that per-position banks should outperform a single fixed direction.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — This is the closest direct methodology import: their head-by-head mean-ablation protocol is the standard playbook for H-13.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical)** — Their finding that the model "starts later" and processes per-token aligns with the RoPE-rotation hypothesis.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-row mean-ablation of the residual stream** — Replace residual-stream activations at row r with the mean over the dataset; measure loss change. Identifies which token positions actually contribute to the answer. *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached Qwen activations. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly tests the F-2 refutation (row 0-10 ablation).
- **Per-head mean-ablation** — Same protocol but on individual attention heads. *Replication cost*: 1-2h H100 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B (need to instrument heads, no current cached attention values). *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — H-13 needs this.
- **Per-task subset loss decomposition** — Partition data into mutually exclusive sub-task categories (their BA / UC1 / US9), graph loss curves separately. *Replication cost*: 2h CPU once subsets are defined. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — analogue: partition MATH-500 by H-5 problem-decomposability tags and re-graph DoM AUROC per subset.
- **Algorithm-reuse cross-seed test** — Train a second model with different seed and output format; check whether the same circuit emerges. *Replication cost*: not directly applicable (we use pretrained Qwen). *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — we don't train, but the analogue (cross-checkpoint stability of L19 DoM, H-8) is already in the queue.
- **Trigram MLP-as-key-value framing** — Treat the MLP as memorizing a discrete (head1_state × head2_state × head3_state) → output mapping. *Replication cost*: requires per-head decomposition of MLP keys, ~1 day H100 for Qwen MLP analysis. *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — orthogonal to current pathway focus.
- **Per-digit / per-token loss curve graphing during training** — Track loss separately per output position to expose phase transitions. *Replication cost*: not applicable to our frozen-model setting. *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — we don't train.

## Approaches & framings

- **Position-specific circuit framing**: different output positions use different sub-algorithms even within a single layer. Intersects F-3 / H-1 directly: explains DoM rotation as algorithmic specialization rather than semantic drift. Reframes F-3 from "two unrelated signals" to "same signal in rotated bases."
- **Necessary-and-sufficient sub-task decomposition**: their BA / MC1 / MS9 / UC1 / US9 framework is built from the *task definition*, then matched against observed circuits. Analogue for us: decompose MATH-500 problems into necessary sub-tasks (parsing, sub-equation evaluation, carry, final-format), match against per-token L19 DoM activations to identify which sub-task L19 actually tracks. Connects to H-5.
- **Compact-vs-expansive computation framing**: model packs computation into the minimum number of rows possible ("starts later, computes denser"). Intersects F-1: dimensional breathing might be the *expansive* counterpart — a multi-layer model trades temporal density for representational expansion across depth.
- **Predictable failure-mode framing**: their US9-cascade analysis predicts the failure from the algorithm structure. Analogue for D-bucket (F-7): predict fail-prone problem patterns from a candidate L19 circuit, then test against EXP-022 D-bucket members.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **5-/10-/15-digit integer addition** — synthetic, ~1.8M of 10B questions trained, fully reproducible. License: Apache-2.0 (their repo). Applicable? **no** — toy synthetic data, not MATH-500-comparable.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 1-layer transformer; 2/3/4 attention heads tested; 3 heads gives the cleanest interpretable pattern.
- Vocabulary: digits 0-9 + `+` + `=` (plus `+` as first answer token in their alt-format model).
- Training: ~1.8M examples drawn from 10B possible 5-digit questions; 1500 epochs typical.
- Output format A5..A0; A4 uses extra compute (the `=` token row). A5/A4, A3/A2, A1/A0 each have a slight variant of the algorithm.
- Three heads time-offset by 1 token each → "double staircase" attention pattern.
- 1st head: MC1 on digit i-2; 2nd head: MC1+MS9 on digit i-1; 3rd head: BaseAdd on digit i. MLP combines via 60-key (2×3×10) trigram lookup.
- High-loss rare-case identification was *predicted from the algorithm* and *then verified* by targeted test cases — methodology pattern worth keeping.
- Code: github.com/apartresearch/Integer_Addition (released).

## 2310.13121 — Understanding Addition in Transformers (Quirke & Barez, 2023)

**Relevance:** ICLR 2024 mechanistic interpretability case study of a 1-layer transformer trained on n-digit integer addition. The paper's headline result — that the model uses **parallel digit-specific streams** with **position-specific algorithms** (A5/A4 fast-path, A3/A2 staircase, A1/A0 simplified) and that **prefill-question rows are causally inert** under mean-ablation — provides the strongest mechanistic challenge in the literature so far to F-2's "prefill encodes correctness" framing and to F-3's "prefill / final orthogonality implies independent signals" framing. It also imports a clean methodology playbook (per-row mean-ablation, per-head ablation, per-task loss decomposition) directly applicable to H-13.

**Key claim we tested:** In a 1-layer transformer, the answer is computed in 6 rows (post-`=` token) using digit-specific parallel streams; the prefill rows (0–10) are not used. Cascading-carry cases (US9) are a predictable structural failure mode.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** No experiment run yet. The paper motivates four high-ROI, low-cost experiments (P11-FE73 prefill-row ablation, P11-FE74 RoPE-rotation cos, P11-FE75 answer-length CoE comparison, P11-FE76 1-layer-toy breathing replication) and one MEDIUM-ROI structural-pattern clustering of D-bucket (P11-FE77).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, P11-FE77; H-95, H-96, H-97; depends on F-1, F-2, F-3, F-7, F-9; intersects H-1, H-5, H-13, H-17.
