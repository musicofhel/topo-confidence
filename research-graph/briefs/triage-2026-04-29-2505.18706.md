# Triage brief — 2505.18706 — Steering LLM Reasoning Through Bias-Only Adaptation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18706
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 / H-1 — fixed per-layer additive vectors recover full RL-tuned reasoning, contradicting the "direction-rotation forces per-position steering" framing.**
   F-3 reports `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0.046` on Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 and is the empirical anchor for the Pathway-10-v2 conclusion that fixed-vector steering should fail (and for H-1 proposing a *per-position* DoM bank). Sinii et al. add the **same** d-dim vector `s_ℓ` to **every** token position at every layer of the same architecture (Qwen2.5-1.5B), train it via RLOO on DeepScaleR, and lift MATH500 from 0.9% → 57.5% — matching full RL fine-tune (58.4%) and SimpleRL-Zoo (59.0%). Mechanism: a position-independent additive bias at all positions delivers the gain that H-1 implicitly attributes to a per-position bank. Falsifiable with **P11-FE73**: take our cached prefill L19 DoM direction, add it as a fixed bias at L19 to all token positions on Qwen2.5-1.5B base, run MATH500 K=1. If accuracy moves at all, the geometric "rotation kills steering" framing is at minimum incomplete; if it moves substantially, F-3's framing is refuted.

2. **F-2 — the "AUROC 0.7731 prefill L19 DoM" direction may not be the direction RL-tuned reasoning lives in (probe ≠ causal steering).**
   F-2 frames L19 prefill DoM as a strong correctness predictor (5-fold OOF AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B), implicitly treating that direction as load-bearing. Sinii produces a *causally validated* per-layer bias direction `s_ℓ` (trained-by-RL → MATH500 +57 pts on the same model). If `cos(s_17, prefill_L19_DoM)` and `cos(s_19, prefill_L19_DoM)` are both small (< 0.1 say), then F-2 has been describing a *correlational probe direction* that is geometrically unrelated to the direction that actually steers reasoning. Mechanism: Sinii's logit-lens decomposition assigns layer 17 to "validation/correctness" tokens and layer 30 to causal connectives — a different layer assignment than F-2's L19. Falsifiable with **P11-FE72**: download Sinii's checkpoint (`github.com/corl-team/steering-reasoning`), project each `s_ℓ` onto the cached prefill-DoM direction; if the L17/L19 cosine is near zero, F-2 demotes to "probe-only" status.

3. **F-9 — "CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM" extends only to probing, not to causal training; the per-layer training success of Sinii is evidence that reasoning information is genuinely distributed.**
   F-9 claims CoE-60 trajectory features (multi-layer) are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM, supporting a single-layer concentration story. Sinii trains *every* layer's bias vector in parallel and the logit-lens analysis (Table 2) shows the layers are not redundant — layer 2 binds programming/named-entity tokens, layer 17 binds correctness/validation tokens, layer 30 binds discourse-connective tokens. If reasoning truly concentrated in L19 alone, a single-layer ablation of Sinii's setup at L19 should match the all-layers result; the paper does not report this ablation. Mechanism: distinct semantic roles per layer in Sinii's interpretation imply that single-layer DoM is at best the "validation" component of a larger circuit. Falsifiable with **P11-FE74**: replicate Sinii's training restricted to L19 only on Qwen2.5-1.5B + DeepScaleR; if MATH500 accuracy lands near the all-layers result, F-9's "single layer is enough" framing extends to steering and is corroborated. If it drops sharply, F-9 is a probing-specific artifact and CoE-60's multi-layer features are recovering something single-L19 misses.

4. **H-1 ("per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500") is partially refuted before it runs — the *position-independent* form is already known to lift accuracy.**
   H-1's open question presupposes that per-position steering is the lever. Sinii's Table 1 already gives the positive answer for the position-*independent* form on the very same model: Qwen2.5-1.5B MATH500 0.9% → 57.5%. So the residual scientific question H-1 should be reframed as "*does per-position bring anything additional* on top of per-layer fixed bias?" — not "does steering work at all?" The Sinii LoRA-adaptor extension (Appendix D, rank-4 LoRA closes the residual full-tune gap on the four hardest cases) is direct evidence that *more* token-conditional adaptation does help. Mechanism: position-independent bias does the bulk of the work; LoRA's per-position component closes the residual gap. Falsifiable: run H-1's per-position bank as planned, but baseline against fixed-vector steering, not against the untrained model. If the per-position bank doesn't beat fixed-vector by ≥ 2 pts, the per-position framing was unnecessary.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold, Qwen-2.5-1.5B)** — Sinii's trained `s_17` aligns with "correctness/validation" tokens in logit-lens. If a public checkpoint is available, project onto cached prefill-DoM and check overlap. Strong cosine validates F-2's direction as causal; weak cosine demotes F-2 to correlational probe.
- **F-3 (`cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046` at L19)** — Sinii adds a *single* `s_ℓ` to all token positions; if direction-rotation were as fundamental as F-3 implies, this approach should fail. It does not. Sinii either (a) tolerates the rotation by averaging over positions, (b) the rotation is ablation-side noise, or (c) the rotation is real but not load-bearing for steering.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Sinii trains every layer non-trivially with distinct learned semantic roles per layer (Table 2). Single-layer ablation would test whether the redundancy claim survives at the causal level.
- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null)** — Tangential: Sinii's success with 0.0016% additive parameters means the "load-bearing change" from RL fine-tuning is a residual offset, which might or might not perturb persistent homology. Not a strong refutation handle.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500)** — Direct competitor / partial answer; see Refutation 4. Sinii has already shown the position-independent form moves MATH-500 by +57 pts.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Sinii's logit-lens gives a coarse layer-level attribution (programming, validation, connectives), but does not distinguish prefill vs final-token within a layer. H-13 remains the more refined question.
- **Headline: 1.5B MATH-500 K=1 = 48.6%** — Sinii's matched-base (Qwen2.5-1.5B) trained-with-bias-only result is 57.5% with sampling τ=1, AVG@8. Not directly comparable to our K=1-greedy 48.6%, but the post-RL ceiling here (~58%) bounds where prefill DoM steering could plausibly land in our regime.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-layer additive bias trained via RLOO with binary verifiable reward** — `s_ℓ ∈ ℝ^d` added to residual stream at end of every transformer layer, base weights frozen, advantage `a_i = r_i − mean(r)`. *Replication cost*: 16 H100 GPUs, 9–120 hrs depending on model. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, in single-layer ablation form (P11-FE74) — provides causal counterpart to our probe-only DoM story.
- **Logit-lens cosine decomposition of steering vectors** — for trained `s_ℓ`, compute `cos(s_ℓ, u_v)` against unembedding rows `u_v`, take top-50 tokens. *Replication cost*: minutes once a checkpoint is loaded. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — apply to our prefill L19 DoM direction directly (no training needed) for a token-level interpretation of what F-2 actually picks up.
- **GPT-o3 prompt-based clustering of top-k aligned tokens** — translates non-English tokens, clusters into themes (Table 2). *Replication cost*: minutes via OpenAI API. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — same sanity check on our prefill DoM and final-token DoM directions; would tell us whether F-2 binds "correctness" tokens (validation) or something else.
- **LoRA-as-adaptive-steering (Appendix D)** — rank-4 LoRA on MLP down-projection, α=4, no dropout, replacing fixed `s` with `B · A · h_MLP`. *Replication cost*: a few H100 hours per model. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — gives a token-conditional steering baseline that is the natural comparison for H-1's per-position bank.
- **vllm parameter broadcast for online RL** — they train under `transformers`, then broadcast just the bias parameters to vllm for sampling, achieving 0.06s/step instead of 5.3s/step. *Replication cost*: pipeline engineering, not a method per se. *We'd plausibly run this*: not directly relevant to our hidden-state pipeline.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Steering vector as feature amplifier, not creator" framing** — The paper explicitly subscribes to the view (citing Turner 2308.10248, Panickssery 2312.06681) that steering vectors *amplify* directions already present in the base model. Intersects F-2: if our prefill L19 DoM is the "correctness" direction the base model already has, then RL training would *amplify* it, predicting that Sinii's `s_17` should be roughly parallel to our prefill DoM (high cosine). If empirically `cos(s_17, prefill_DoM) ≈ 0`, the amplifier framing breaks for our particular probe direction, and F-2 is an off-axis correlate of correctness rather than the correctness direction itself.
- **"BitFit-tighter than BitFit"** — Tunes only a subset of biases (per-layer additive bias on residual stream, not the full set BitFit modifies). The framing is "the smaller you can make the trainable parameter set without losing performance, the more interpretable that set becomes." This frames *interpretability as a parameter-budget question*, which is a useful lens for H-13's head-level attribution: a smaller successful intervention site narrows the search space for the "actual circuit."
- **"Reasoning training amplifies what's already there"** — Cites Wang 2504.20571, Liu Oat-Zero 2503.20783, Shao 2506.10947, Ye 2502.03387 (LIMO). This collective claim — that RL fine-tuning *unlocks* rather than *creates* — is friendly to our F-2 story (probe finds a correctness direction in the *base* model) and to H-5 (prefill signal as familiarity vs decomposability). Adds external corroboration.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Steering LR ~ `5×10⁻⁴` (vs full-tune `2×10⁻⁵`); LR for bias-only is one to two orders of magnitude larger than for full fine-tune — relevant if we replicate, since picking a too-small LR would silently kill the result.
- Sampling temperature `τ=1` for trained models, greedy for base. AVG@32 for AIME/AMC, PASS@1 elsewhere. Worth matching when we compare numbers across our K=1-greedy MATH500 and their `τ=1` MATH500.
- Reward is binary: presence of correct answer in `\boxed{...}` template. Math-Verify (HF) is the verifier. Same scaffold as our pipeline if we ever go RL-tuning.
- 16 H100 GPUs throughout. Qwen2.5-1.5B ≈ 9 hrs; LLaMa3.1-8B-It ≈ 120 hrs. Realistic single-layer ablation budget for FE74 is ~1 H100 day on 1.5B.
- Code at `https://github.com/corl-team/steering-reasoning`.
- LoRA hyperparams: rank 4, α=4, no dropout, on MLP down-projection (Appendix I). If we replicate H-1 with LoRA-as-adaptive-steering, these are the defaults.
- Per-step time for steering ≈ 0.06–0.11s (vs 5.3–9.94s full-tune); they attribute this to the parameter broadcast cost dominating full-tune iteration time.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Logit-lens decoding of our prefill L19 DoM**: take cached `prefill_DoM` direction (a single 1536-d vector for Qwen2.5-1.5B), compute `cos(prefill_DoM, u_v)` against the unembedding matrix `W_U`, take top-50 tokens. ~5min CPU once `W_U` is loaded. Tells us whether F-2's direction is interpretable as "correctness/validation" (Sinii's L17 cluster) or as something else entirely.
- **Cross-direction cosine projection** if Sinii checkpoint is public: `cos(s_ℓ, prefill_DoM)` and `cos(s_ℓ, final_DoM)` for ℓ ∈ {0…27}. ~30min CPU. Directly anchors P11-FE72.
- **Static-bias steering smoke test**: at evaluation time, before-residual-add an `α · prefill_DoM` perturbation at L19 to all token positions, sweep α ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 5}, eval on MATH500 K=1. ~30min H100. Directly anchors P11-FE73 — if this moves accuracy it pre-empts the much more expensive H-1 per-position bank.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.18706 — Steering LLM Reasoning Through Bias-Only Adaptation (Sinii et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** Trains a single d-dimensional additive bias vector per transformer layer via online RL (RLOO + binary verifiable reward) on DeepScaleR, freezes all other weights, and matches full RL fine-tune accuracy on six math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24/25, AMC23, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench) across seven base models including Qwen2.5-{1.5,7,14}B and LLaMa3.1-8B. On Qwen2.5-1.5B MATH500: base 0.9% → bias-only-steering 57.5% ≈ full-tune 58.4%. Adds ~0.0016% additional parameters. Logit-lens analysis assigns interpretable token-cluster roles to per-layer biases (L2: code/named-entities, L17: validation/correctness, L30: causal connectives). Directly bears on F-2/F-3/F-9 and H-1 by providing a *trained, causally validated, position-independent* per-layer steering vector that can be projected against our probe-derived prefill DoM direction.

**Key claim we tested:** A *single fixed* additive bias per layer (same vector at every token position) suffices to recover full RL fine-tune accuracy on math reasoning — i.e. the "direction rotation" observation in F-3 is not load-bearing for steering, and per-position adaptation (the H-1 framing) may be unnecessary for the bulk of the gain.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** P11-FE72 will project Sinii's checkpoint vectors onto our prefill_L19_DoM and final_L19_DoM to test the F-2-as-causal interpretation. P11-FE73 will run the cheapest empirical test (fixed prefill-DoM bias on MATH500 K=1) to see if our probe direction has any of the same causal traction Sinii's RL-trained vectors have. P11-FE74 (medium-priority, blocked on H100 for RL) tests whether F-9's single-layer concentration claim survives at the causal-training level.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74; H-72, H-73; refines H-1 framing.

**Status:** TO TEST
