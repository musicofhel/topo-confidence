# Triage brief — 2402.15610 — ReCoVERR: Selective "Selective Prediction" — Reducing Unnecessary Abstention in Vision-Language Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15610
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the framing of F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% acc) as a value-demonstrating result.** F-8 evaluates a *binary* answer/abstain selector built on prefill DoM. ReCoVERR shows that on VLMs the binary regime is leaving accuracy on the table: low-confidence VQAv2/A-OKVQA cases recover ~20% more correct answers when the system gathers extra evidence (LLM-asked clarifying sub-questions) before deciding. If the analogous text-CoT move ("ask sub-questions before committing") recovers a comparable fraction of MATH-500 problems that prefill DoM flagged as incorrect, then F-8's headline is *upper-bounded by what a non-probe agentic baseline already buys*, and the probe's marginal value is overstated.
   - **Mechanism**: low prefill-DoM score → the probe abstains → an agentic clarification step would have answered correctly without the probe.
   - **Falsifiable test**: subset MATH-500 problems where prefill DoM ≤ low-confidence threshold (the bottom-50% in the F-8 evaluation), run a self-question-clarify-then-answer wrapper at K=1 (no extra labels, no probe), compare recovered accuracy to F-8's selective-prediction accuracy on the answered half. If the wrapper recovers ≥ the probe-implied gain, F-8's framing changes.

2. **Refutes H-12's premise (label-free semantic-entropy *probes* are the right substitute for supervised DoM in refusal).** H-12 assumes the right axis is *which probe* replaces the supervised one. ReCoVERR shows a third axis — *don't probe at all, generate auxiliary queries* — that is also label-free and dominates pure-probe abstention on VQAv2/A-OKVQA. If a clarification-based selective-prediction baseline outperforms semantic-entropy probes on MATH-500, H-12's hypothesis space is wrong: the gain in label-free abstention reduction does not come from a better probe but from a different inference-time policy.
   - **Mechanism**: label-free clarification > label-free probe at picking which low-confidence answers to commit.
   - **Falsifiable test**: when running H-12, add a third arm — ReCoVERR-style sub-question-driven clarification — alongside the SE probe and supervised DoM. If the clarification arm wins, H-12 should be rewritten or downgraded.

3. **Refutes the implicit assumption behind F-2 / F-3 that prefill L19 DoM is a *fixed property of the question*.** F-2/F-3 treat prefill DoM as the residual encoding of *the question as presented*; AUROC 0.7731 is read as a near-ceiling signal of intrinsic problem difficulty. ReCoVERR's effectiveness implies that low-confidence questions become high-confidence after augmentation — i.e., the prefill state is not a fixed difficulty fingerprint, it shifts under prompt augmentation. If prefilling MATH-500 *with self-generated clarifying restatements appended* moves the L19 DoM score by more than the cross-fold noise (~AUROC ±0.02), the "prefill encodes intrinsic problem difficulty" reading of F-2 is wrong; the score depends on prompt presentation as much as on problem content.
   - **Mechanism**: augmenting the prompt with paraphrases/sub-questions changes the L19 prefill activation, and therefore the DoM score, on the *same underlying problem*.
   - **Falsifiable test**: pick the bottom-quartile prefill-DoM problems, generate 3 paraphrases or sub-question expansions, re-extract L19 prefill activations, recompute DoM scores. If mean DoM moves ≥ 0.5 SD across paraphrases, F-2's interpretation needs to be narrowed.

## Methodologies extracted

- **VLM confidence + LLM-driven sub-question generation for selective prediction** — at low VLM confidence on the primary question, prompt an LLM to ask related sub-questions about the image, accept VLM answers only above a sub-question confidence threshold, then re-decide commit/abstain on the primary question.
  *Replication cost*: text-only analogue is ~2h CPU on cached MATH-500 1.5B K=1 outputs (no new model runs required for the abstention layer; needs one fresh K=1 pass over self-generated clarifications, ≈ 1h H100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — as a baseline arm for F-8 / H-12. It directly quantifies whether the probe's marginal value survives a non-probe agentic comparator.

- **Confidence-gated chained inference (sub-question must clear its own threshold to count as evidence)** — reusable pattern: don't trust auxiliary outputs to inform the primary commit unless they themselves pass a calibration bar.
  *Replication cost*: trivial post-hoc filter on cached outputs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could sharpen H-19 (C_exact verification routing), where verifier outputs need a confidence gate before they get to vote.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reframes selective prediction as a 3-state policy (commit / abstain / gather more) instead of binary commit/abstain.** Intersects F-8 directly: F-8 evaluates only the binary policy. The 3-state framing implies F-8's coverage curve is a degenerate slice of a larger Pareto frontier.
- **Treats LLM/VLM as a queryable sub-system rather than as a fixed function from prompt to answer.** Intersects H-12 and H-19: both assume a single forward pass; ReCoVERR's framing is that the *inference loop* is the right unit of abstention analysis.

## 2402.15610 — Selective "Selective Prediction": Reducing Unnecessary Abstention in Vision-Language Reasoning (Srikanth et al., 2024)

**Relevance:** VLM selective-prediction paper; not directly on-pathway for our text-CoT pipeline, but provides a clean methodological alternative to F-8's binary probe-driven selective prediction. Specifically reframes selective prediction as a 3-state policy (commit/abstain/gather more), with empirical evidence from VQAv2/A-OKVQA that the gather-more arm recovers ~20% of abstained correct answers. Useful as a non-probe label-free baseline and as a perturbation lens on the "prefill encodes intrinsic difficulty" reading of F-2.

**Key claim we tested:** A non-probe agentic clarification policy can recover abstained-but-correct answers, raising effective coverage without dropping accuracy.
