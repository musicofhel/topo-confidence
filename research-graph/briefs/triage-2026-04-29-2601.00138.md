# Triage brief — 2601.00138 — Explicit Abstention Knobs for Predictable Reliability in Video Question Answering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00138
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-8 (Selective-prediction via prefill DoM works at 50% coverage) is not robust under input-quality shift.**
   *Mechanism*: Ortiz shows that confidence-based abstention provides smooth in-distribution risk-coverage tradeoffs (ε=0.71 → 9.4% risk @ 63.7% coverage on 18-frame NExT-QA), but the threshold tuned on full evidence preserves *risk* under shift (6 frames) only by silently bleeding ~16% of coverage at fixed ε — even though the operating *operating-point claim* on the chart looks intact. Our F-8 number "71.6% accuracy on answered, K=2.5 avg, at 50% coverage" is fixed-coverage, fixed-distribution. If we hold coverage at 0.5 and shift the input distribution (e.g., MATH→AIME, or MATH-500 with a perturbed/truncated problem statement), risk among the answered set may rise without the threshold flagging anything — the same failure mode.
   *Falsifiable test*: Take the prefill-L19 DoM probe trained on MATH-500 1.5B, evaluate on (a) AIME 2024 problems, (b) MATH-500 with the last 30% of the problem statement deleted. If selective-prediction accuracy at coverage 0.5 stays ≥0.70 in both settings, F-8 generalizes; if it drops to baseline (≤0.55), F-8 is a fixed-distribution-only result and Ortiz's "control knob is not epistemic" critique applies.

2. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, *better* than final-token at 0.7186) treats the prefill probe as if it tracked epistemic adequacy — Ortiz directly refutes that representational signals contract under evidence loss.**
   *Mechanism*: Ortiz computes logprob-derived `p_max` (the model's own internal token-level confidence over answer choices) and finds it is *less* sensitive to evidence reduction than self-reported confidence — `p_max` actually *rises* by +0.6% when frames drop 67% (0.870 → 0.876). The natural prior is "internal/representational signals are more honest than verbal ones." Ortiz refutes that prior. If our prefill DoM is tapping the same kind of representational confidence, it inherits the same defect: it predicts correctness in-distribution but does not contract when the problem is genuinely harder than training.
   *Falsifiable test*: Run a "decoy" problem-degradation control on MATH-500 — replace each problem's numeric values with random integers of the same magnitude (so the problem becomes unsolvable as posed). Compute prefill-L19 DoM scores on degraded vs original problems. Prediction under F-2-as-epistemic: mean DoM score should drop sharply on degraded problems. Prediction under Ortiz: DoM mean barely moves; the probe is content-independent confidence, not evidence-conditioned.

3. **H-12 (Semantic-entropy / label-free probes can replace supervised DoM for refusal) is undermined: Ortiz shows entropy-style logprob signals are *worse* than self-report under shift, not better.**
   *Mechanism*: Ortiz tests three logprob-derived metrics (`p_max`, margin, entropy) against self-reported confidence under 18→6 frame degradation. All three are systematically less responsive than the verbal scalar. H-12 is premised on label-free entropy probes being more robust replacements for DoM; Ortiz says distributional uncertainty inherits the same overconfidence as the verbal channel — both are "fundamental to the model's representations," not artifacts of self-report.
   *Falsifiable test*: Compute semantic-entropy-probe (Kuhn et al. style, single-pass approximation as in 2406.15927) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 prefills under (a) clean problems, (b) keyword-scrambled problems. If SEP entropy fails to rise commensurately on (b) (e.g., median entropy increases <10%), H-12 is contradicted in the same way Ortiz contradicted logprob confidence.

4. **F-8 specifically — fixed-threshold transfer is the right diagnostic, not fixed-coverage transfer.**
   *Mechanism*: We report selective-prediction at *fixed coverage 0.5*, sweeping the threshold to hit it. Ortiz shows that under shift, fixed-threshold and fixed-coverage tell different stories: fixed-ε = 0.71 holds risk constant by dropping coverage; fixed-coverage = 0.5 would inflate risk. Our headline number 71.6% is at fixed coverage, so any input-distribution shift would manifest as risk inflation, not coverage drop, and we would not catch it without the threshold-transfer diagnostic.
   *Falsifiable test*: Recompute F-8's curve at *fixed threshold* on AIME or perturbed-MATH-500. If risk among answered grows >5 percentage points at the same threshold that achieved F-8's 71.6%, the gate is not epistemic and the F-8 narrative needs a "validity envelope" qualifier.

5. **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute) presupposes a calibrated routing signal — Ortiz directly attacks calibration-under-shift.**
   *Mechanism*: H-19 routes verification-failed problems to K=8 and verification-passed problems to K=1, leveraging a verification-derived activation as the gate. Ortiz's result implies that on harder/shifted problems, the gate's confidence will not contract appropriately, so verification-passed will incorrectly grow on hard problems and the K=1 bucket will leak errors. The compute-saving claim of H-19 holds only if the gate is evidence-conditioned.
   *Falsifiable test*: When H-19 is run, compute the "evidence-degradation IQR-shift" (Ortiz Table 7 analogue) on the routing signal across MATH-500 difficulty buckets. If the routing-signal distribution does not contract on harder problems, K=1 errors will leak; report the leak rate alongside the matched-compute headline.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-8 (Selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% acc on answered, K=2.5 avg)** — Ortiz's headline experiment is structurally identical: 25-step ε sweep, risk-coverage curve, ECE per bin. His ε=0.71 → 9.4% risk @ 63.7% coverage is the VLM analogue of our 50%/71.6% point. The *in-distribution* validation in his Section 5 corroborates F-8's mechanism; the *shift* result in Section 5.2 is the missing control we never ran. Method differs (VLM verbal confidence vs prefill-L19 DoM logistic head) but the abstention-knob framing is one-to-one.
- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 > final-token 0.7186)** — Ortiz's logprob-vs-self-report comparison (Section 5.3) is methodologically parallel to our prefill-vs-final comparison: two confidence interfaces, both purporting to predict correctness. Our prefill > final ordering looks like an internal-signal-wins story. Ortiz's logprob *is* the internal signal and it loses to verbal under shift. This puts a question mark on whether F-2's ordering is intrinsic or distribution-specific.
- **H-12 (Semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** — Ortiz's Sec 5.3 entropy result is a near-direct test of H-12's premise. Entropy of logprob distribution is the closest single-pass analogue to SEPs. He finds entropy-style metrics are *less* responsive to evidence quality than self-report. H-12 priority should drop unless we run the matched-pair entropy-vs-DoM-under-shift control he provides a template for.
- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — peripheral connection: Ortiz's "warrant-based formulation" (confidence bounded by evidence-supportable accuracy) suggests per-position steering should also be conditioned on input-evidence proxies (problem length, presence of numeric anchors, etc.), not applied uniformly.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Threshold-sweep risk-coverage protocol with 25 evenly-spaced ε in [0,1]** — at each ε record (coverage, risk among answered, ECE among answered). Smooth + monotone curve = "mechanistic control" check. We currently report a single (coverage, risk) point for F-8; this gives the full curve.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached pathway11_h100 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — F-8 currently has one operating point; full curve makes refutation-vs-corroboration immediate.
- **Reliability diagram with confidence binning + ECE** — bin predictions into 10 confidence bins, plot bar(acc per bin) vs bin-center diagonal, compute weighted |acc−conf|.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — easy ECE-of-prefill-DoM number to attach to F-8.
- **Logprob-derived confidence triple: `p_max`, margin (p₁−p₂), normalized entropy** — extracted from `top_logprobs` over answer-letter tokens.
  *Replication cost*: free for our pipeline (we already have logits from K=1 greedy).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct comparator to prefill DoM as a label-free baseline; contributes to H-12.
- **Evidence-degradation control (input-side ablation)** — Ortiz reduces frames 18→6 to mechanically destroy ~67% of input information while keeping question identical. Generalizes as: degrade input information, observe whether confidence contracts proportionally.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU to construct degraded MATH-500 variants (truncate problem statement, redact numbers, etc.) + an H100 pass to re-extract activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the single most valuable falsifier Ortiz provides for our F-2/F-8 epistemic-claim hygiene.
- **Matched-pair confidence comparison via question_id intersection** — when comparing two confidence signals from separate API calls, report statistics only on the intersection where both produced valid outputs.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — protects multi-method comparisons (DoM vs SEP vs CoE-60) from selection effects.
- **Threshold-transfer diagnostic** — compute the ε that hits a target risk in distribution A, apply it in distribution B, report (Δrisk, Δcoverage). Both directions.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU once degraded variants exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — produces the "validity envelope" qualifier that F-8 currently lacks.
- **Observability proxy regression** — define a numeric proxy of input information content (Ortiz uses frame count), regress confidence on it, look for monotone contraction.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a problem-difficulty proxy (oracle pass-rate over K=8) is the obvious analogue.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Warrant-based" confidence formulation**: reported confidence p should satisfy p ≤ ζ(e), where ζ(e) is an evidence-supportable accuracy bound. Confidence is tied to *what the evidence can support*, not to a distribution-specific calibration. Our F-2/F-8 are framed around AUROC-against-correctness; this reframes the same numbers as "how well does the probe respect ζ(e)?" and immediately identifies the missing control: a probe trained at one evidence level must contract on lower-evidence inputs.
- **System-level abstention vs model-level abstention**: gating on confidence is a *system designer* knob, decoupled from the model's own self-abstention flag. Useful framing for F-8 — our gate is the prefill DoM threshold, not any self-refuse behavior; this aligns cleanly with Ortiz's framework.
- **Representational-not-behavioral overconfidence**: when both internal probabilities and verbal self-report fail to contract, the failure is in the model's representation of uncertainty, not in the surface interface. This sharpens our prefill/final orthogonality story (cos = 0.046, F-3): if both directions are equally overconfident on degraded inputs, the orthogonality is geometric not epistemic.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Sweep ε over **25 evenly-spaced points in [0,1]** — coarser than expected, but produces clean curves at n=300; we should match this resolution for F-8's curve.
- **System-level abstention rule** combines: missing confidence, null choice, parse failure, ε-below-threshold — all collapse to "abstain". Cleaner than a binary self-report flag.
- **JSON-schema prompting for confidence**: `{answer, confidence ∈ [0,1], abstain (bool), evidence_span}` — a clean contract our K=1 generations could adopt at near-zero cost; we already have raw text answers.
- **Logprob extraction via `top_logprobs`** at the answer-letter token, then re-normalize over the {A,B,C,D,E} letters specifically before computing `p_max`/margin/entropy. The renormalization step matters and is easy to miss.
- **Evidence-degradation as input-side ablation** (frame downsampling) keeps the question text identical; the analogue for math is question-text edits with held-fixed numeric answer-key.
- **Statistical-power caveat at extreme ε**: at very high thresholds, the answered set shrinks and risk estimates become noisy — Ortiz reports CI widths that grow at edges. F-8's curve should similarly carry CIs.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2601.00138 — Explicit Abstention Knobs for Predictable Reliability in Video Question Answering (Ortiz, 2026)

**Relevance:** Empirical study of confidence-based selective prediction for video question answering with a Gemini 2.0 Flash + NExT-QA setup. Two findings: (1) in-distribution, sweeping a confidence threshold ε produces smooth, monotone risk-coverage curves with a clear knee (ε=0.71 → 9.4% risk @ 63.7% coverage), validating the abstention-knob mechanism; (2) under controlled evidence degradation (18→6 frames, 67% information loss), confidence contracts only modestly (median stays at 0.9; logprob `p_max` actually rises +0.6%) — the threshold tuned in-distribution does not preserve coverage. The proposed remedy is a "warrant-based" formulation where confidence is bounded by an evidence-supportable accuracy ζ(e). Method differs from ours (verbal/logprob VLM confidence vs supervised hidden-state DoM probe), but the abstention-knob framework is structurally identical to F-8's setup, and the evidence-degradation control is a falsifier we have not run.

**Key claim we tested:** Confidence-based abstention provides mechanistic control in-distribution but fails to be epistemic under input-quality shift; representational signals (logprob entropy) inherit the same overconfidence as verbal self-report.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** We have F-8 (selective prediction at coverage 0.5 → 71.6% acc, K=2.5 avg) but only at a single in-distribution operating point. Ortiz's full risk-coverage curve, ECE-vs-threshold curve, and evidence-degradation transfer test are missing controls in our pipeline. P11-FE100 (full curve), P11-FE101 (degradation), P11-FE103 (threshold transfer to AIME), and H-50 / H-51 cover the matching tests.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE100, P11-FE101, P11-FE102, P11-FE103, P11-FE104, H-50, H-51, H-52.

**Status:** TO TEST
