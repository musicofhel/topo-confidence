# Triage brief — 2605.04418 — Demystifying Manifold Constraints in LLM Pre-training

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04418
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1 — F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731): DoM direction may be optimizer-contingent, not architecture-intrinsic.**
The paper proves that under spectral-sphere constraints the dominant singular vector of each weight matrix rotates at a rate modulated by the spectral gap (sin θ_t ≲ η_t·c·R / (R − σ₂)), while under Frobenius-sphere constraints rotation is isotropic at θ_t ≈ η_t·c. These are two qualitatively different rotational regimes. If Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 DoM direction is the residual-stream image of an AdamW-induced weight-space backbone (H-546), then a model pretrained with MACRO-spec (spectral sphere, anisotropic rotation) could produce a *different* privileged direction or no single dominant direction at all — the spectral gap modulation would distribute signal across multiple singular directions rather than concentrating it in PC1. Falsifiable: pretrain matched-architecture model with MACRO-spec, extract L19 prefill activations, measure cos(DoM_MACRO, DoM_AdamW) and compare AUROC.

**R2 — F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal): manifold constraints may eliminate or reshape breathing.**
The paper shows MACRO bounds activation scales to Θ(1) by construction, removing the need for normalization layers. If breathing (the per-token expansion/contraction of participation ratio across layers) is driven by the interplay between weight decay and normalization (the "rotational equilibrium" that AdamW + RMSNorm jointly produce), then explicitly constraining weights to a manifold could eliminate the *degree of freedom* that produces breathing — activations are scale-locked by the constraint, not dynamically regulated by the norm-decay dance. The normalization-free MACRO variant (Table 3) is the smoking gun: if breathing is absent in that architecture, F-1's "universality" claim is qualified to "universal among normalization-equipped, unconstrained-optimizer architectures." Falsifiable: run the breathing extraction pipeline on a MACRO-trained model (even the 120M would suffice).

**R3 — H-546 (DoM as AdamW-induced backbone): MACRO's rotational equilibrium is mechanistically different from AdamW's.**
H-546 hypothesizes that the L19 DoM is the residual-stream image of an AdamW-induced parameter-space backbone (per Xu et al., 2602.23696). But this paper proves that manifold constraints achieve the *same* rotational equilibrium — steady-state rotation angles, bounded activation scales, stable training dynamics — through an entirely different mechanism (geometric projection onto the constraint manifold, not weight-decay penalty). If both AdamW and MACRO produce a single dominant activation direction but through different mechanisms, the DoM is not "AdamW-induced" but rather an emergent property of *any* training regime that achieves rotational equilibrium. This would refute the specific mechanistic claim in H-546 while potentially strengthening F-2 (DoM is more robust than thought). Falsifiable: compare DoM directions between AdamW-trained and MACRO-trained models of matched architecture.

**R4 — F-10 (PH null — topology statistics indistinguishable from Gaussian): spectral-sphere training could produce non-Gaussian activation geometry.**
Under MACRO-spec, the spectral gap directly modulates which singular directions carry signal, creating anisotropic weight structure that the Frobenius constraint does not. If the activation covariance inherits this anisotropy (stable rank deviating from Θ(1) assumptions), the resulting point cloud may be sufficiently non-Gaussian to break F-10's null result. The paper's stable-rank assumption (κ = Θ(1)) is explicitly required for Frobenius-sphere guarantees but not tested against realistic trained networks — if it fails in practice, the activation geometry could exhibit structure that persistent homology detects. Falsifiable: compute PH on activations from MACRO-spec trained model and compare to Gaussian null at matched covariance.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's spectral-sphere analysis directly models the mechanism by which a single direction dominates forward-pass activations. Their Proposition 1 shows that spectral norm R_spec = Θ(√(D_out/D_in)) bounds worst-case activation amplification — if L19's weight matrix has a large spectral gap, this theory predicts that a single direction (the dominant right singular vector of W_L19) would dominate activations, which is exactly what we observe as DoM ≈ PC1 (cos = 0.9216).

- **F-1 (breathing universality):** The paper's activation-scale-bounding theory (both spectral and Frobenius variants) provides a mechanistic account of *why* activation scales vary per-layer — different layers have different effective radii and stable ranks, producing the per-layer PR variation that we measure as breathing.

- **F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046):** Under spectral constraints, the dominant singular vector rotates during training (Wedin's theorem), and the rotation rate depends on the spectral gap. If prefill processing and generation-time processing load the same weight matrices differently (different effective spectral gaps due to different input distributions), the *effective* dominant direction could rotate between prefill and generation, producing the near-orthogonality we observe.

- **H-546 (DoM as AdamW backbone):** Central connection. MACRO achieves the same functional outcome (stable rotational equilibrium, bounded activations, dominant direction) through manifold constraints instead of weight decay. This either refutes H-546's mechanism-specificity or generalizes it to "any rotational-equilibrium optimizer."

- **H-547 (breathing under non-AdamW):** MACRO is explicitly a non-AdamW optimizer that achieves comparable training dynamics. The normalization-free variant (Table 3, validation loss 2.739 at 330M) is a direct test bed: does this model breathe? If yes, H-547 is partially refuted (breathing survives beyond AdamW). If no, H-547 is confirmed but the causal agent is narrowed to normalization layers rather than AdamW specifically.

- **H-711 (WeightWatcher alpha peaks at L19):** The paper's spectral analysis framework (stable rank, spectral gap, Frobenius-to-spectral ratio) provides the theoretical grounding for why certain layers might be spectrally distinctive. Their uniform-radius limitation (global r for all layers) is exactly the gap H-711 tests — per-layer spectral signatures should show L19 as an outlier.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Spectral-sphere projection** — project weight matrices onto the spectral-norm sphere ‖W‖₂ = R after each gradient step. Controls worst-case activation amplification.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (apply to cached weight matrices, not training).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we can decompose Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 weight matrices and measure spectral gap, stable rank, and effective constraint radius to test whether the theory's assumptions hold.

- **Frobenius-sphere projection** — project weight matrices onto ‖W‖_F = R. Controls average-case activation scale under stable-rank assumption.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — same analysis as above, different constraint surface.

- **Matrix sign operator (msign)** — steepest-descent direction on the constraint manifold, computed as U·V^T from the SVD of the tangent-space-projected gradient. More informative than raw gradient for understanding dominant update directions.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (compute msign of cached gradient snapshots if available).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires training-time gradient access we don't have for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.

- **Stable rank diagnostic** — κ = ‖W‖₂² / (‖W‖_F² / min(D_in, D_out)). Measures how "spread" the singular spectrum is. κ = Θ(1) means nearly full-rank; κ >> 1 means low-rank.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to Qwen-2.5-1.5B weight matrices, especially at L19 where we observe a dominant direction.

- **Rotational equilibrium measurement** — track weight rotation angle θ_t between consecutive training steps. Under spectral constraints: θ_t modulated by spectral gap. Under Frobenius: θ_t ≈ ηc.
  *Replication cost*: N/A (requires training checkpoint sequence).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have Qwen-2.5-1.5B training checkpoints.

- **Norm-Gated SwiGLU** — normalize the Swish branch before Hadamard multiplication to prevent exponential scale cascades without learnable normalization parameters.
  *Replication cost*: 5min (modify architecture code).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires retraining.

## Approaches & framings

- **Manifold constraints as unified stabilizer.** The paper reframes three separate training-time mechanisms (normalization, weight decay, optimizer design) as addressing a single geometric problem: keeping weight matrices on a constraint manifold. This lens is directly applicable to our post-hoc analysis: if trained weights already lie near a spectral or Frobenius sphere, the effective constraint surface is implicit, and F-2's DoM direction is the dominant singular vector of the implicit sphere.

- **Spectral vs Frobenius as worst-case vs average-case.** This distinction maps onto our observation that DoM ≈ PC1 (Frobenius/average structure) while the cov-spectrum probe (0.7928, using top-20 eigenvalues) captures richer structure. The spectral constraint preserves the dominant direction more strongly; the Frobenius constraint distributes signal more evenly — matching the 2-feat vs 20-feat AUROC difference (0.7856 → 0.7928).

- **Rotation rate as signal.** The paper's θ_t ≈ ηc formula implies that at convergence, weight matrices rotate at a fixed rate. If L19's rotation rate differs from other layers (e.g., slower rotation due to larger spectral gap), this could explain why L19 is informationally privileged — it retains more of the training-time backbone.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Spectral radius formula: R_spec = r√(D_out/D_in) where r is a global hyperparameter. For Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 attention output (D_out=1536, D_in=1536): R_spec = r.
- Frobenius radius: R_fro = r√D_out = r·√1536 ≈ 39.2r.
- Stable rank κ = ‖W‖₂² · min(D_in, D_out) / ‖W‖_F². When κ ≈ 1, weight matrix is "maximally spread" across singular values.
- The gap between MACRO normalization-free and standard training (0.021 val loss at 330M) comes from using a global rather than per-layer radius — per-layer tuning is acknowledged as future work.
- Algorithm uses momentum (β = 1 − 1/√T) with Riemannian tangent-space projection before the matrix sign operator.
