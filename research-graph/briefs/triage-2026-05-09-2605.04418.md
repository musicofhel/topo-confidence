# Triage brief — 2605.04418 — Demystifying Manifold Constraints in LLM Pre-training

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04418
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence).**
   The paper demonstrates that manifold constraints (spectral sphere, Frobenius sphere) subsume the roles of RMSNorm and weight decay entirely (Sec 4.2–4.3, Table 3). Under MACRO, the rotational equilibrium is enforced from step 1 — there is no transient phase (Eq. 50, θ_t ≈ η_t·c). If breathing (F-1) is an emergent consequence of standard training dynamics (AdamW weight decay inducing a transient → equilibrium rotation schedule per Kosson et al. 2024), then a MACRO-trained model should exhibit *no breathing at all* because the transient phase is eliminated. This would refute H-4's prediction that breathing formally corresponds to the EoS/Sharpness-Dimension transition, since MACRO bypasses that transition yet achieves competitive loss. **Falsifiable:** Train a small Qwen model with MACRO-spec vs AdamW, extract L19 residuals on MATH-500 at convergence, compare dimensional breathing amplitudes.

2. **Refutes F-2 (L19 prefill DoM predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — or at least challenges its mechanism.**
   The paper proves that manifold constraints lock the relative learning rate η_rel,t = c·η_t exactly (Eq. 4) and enforce static/adaptive rotational equilibrium (Sec 4.3). Under MACRO training, the weight matrix at every layer is constrained to a fixed-radius manifold throughout training. This means the singular spectrum of W_L19 is *controlled by design* rather than emergent. If the DoM direction (≈ PC1, cos 0.9216) is primarily an artifact of unconstrained weight-norm dynamics during AdamW training — e.g. the dominant direction reflects cumulative weight decay → rotation transitions — then a MACRO-trained model's L19 weights would have a qualitatively different principal subspace, and DoM-based probing might produce a substantially different (higher or lower) AUROC. **Falsifiable:** Compare PCA of L19 activations on MATH-500 between MACRO-trained and AdamW-trained Qwen-2.5-1.5B; test whether cos(DoM, PC1) ≈ 0.92 holds or breaks.

3. **Refutes F-1 (Dimensional breathing is universal) via the normalization-free architecture.**
   The paper shows that MACRO maintains strict stability even when *all learnable RMSNorm layers are removed* (Sec 4.2, Table 3). If breathing is driven by the interaction between weight dynamics and normalization layers (γ parameters adapting during training, per their Figure 3), then the normalization-free MACRO architecture would eliminate breathing entirely. Since F-1 claims universality, a model that achieves competitive loss without exhibiting breathing would restrict the scope of F-1 to AdamW/standard-norm architectures rather than "transformers in general." **Falsifiable:** Run dimensional-breathing measurement protocol on activations from MACRO-spec (norm-free, Table 3 best: val loss 2.739) and compare to standard-norm baseline.

4. **Challenges F-4 (Asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder).**
   If manifold constraints enforce ‖vec(Y)‖_RMS = Θ(1) at every layer (Proposition 1), then the activation scale is bounded by design. The "asymmetric collapse" pattern (F-4) — where correct problems show stronger dimensional contraction at the final token — may be an artifact of unconstrained weight-norm growth/decay dynamics. Under MACRO's strict scale control, the final-token representation may have no room to collapse differentially. **Falsifiable:** Measure participation ratio trajectories for correct vs incorrect problems on MACRO-trained model; if PR trajectories converge, F-4 is optimizer-dependent, not architecture-universal.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (Breathing universality):** The paper's central finding — that manifold constraints subsume RMSNorm and weight decay (Sec 4.2–4.3) — directly speaks to *why* breathing exists. If breathing is a dynamical consequence of the AdamW transient → rotational equilibrium trajectory, then F-1 is not a universal transformer property but an optimizer-specific one. The paper's Frobenius rotation identity θ_t ≈ η_t·c (Eq. 50) predicts *constant* angular updates from step 1, which contrasts with the "breathing" metaphor of expanding/contracting geometry.

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's stable-rank analysis (Assumption 4, Figure 2, κ = Θ(1)) directly characterizes the spectral structure of weight matrices during training. Our finding that DoM ≈ PC1 (cos 0.9216) could be explained by the paper's framework: the dominant singular direction of W_L19 controls the worst-case activation amplification (Lemma 3), and under standard training this direction accumulates signal about input difficulty. Under MACRO, this direction would be *geometrically controlled*, potentially altering what DoM captures.

- **F-10 (PH-null — topology adds negative signal):** The paper's activation-scale bounds (Proposition 1) provide a theoretical explanation for why PH on residual streams is null: if RMSNorm (or manifold constraints) enforce ‖vec(Y)‖_RMS = Θ(1) at every layer, then activations are effectively constrained to live on a sphere, and persistent homology of points on a sphere matches the Gaussian null. This corroborates F-10 from a theoretical direction.

- **H-546/H-547 (DoM as AdamW-induced backbone / breathing under non-AdamW training):** Most directly relevant. The paper's MACRO optimizer is a concrete realization of "non-AdamW training" that preserves competitive performance. This is exactly the kind of control experiment H-546/H-547 call for — does the DoM structure survive when weight decay is replaced by manifold constraints?

## Methodologies extracted

- **MACRO (Msign-Aligned Constrained Riemannian Optimizer)** — Single-loop Riemannian optimizer that constrains weights to spectral sphere, Frobenius sphere, or oblique manifold. Update: project momentum to tangent space → msign → normalize/scale → manifold retraction (Alg 1).
  *Replication cost*: 1–2 days to implement + training time (330M model = ~hours on H100; 1.5B = days).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train models, we analyze pre-trained ones. But the *analysis framework* (stable rank, spectral gaps, rotation angles) is directly applicable.

- **Stable rank monitoring (κ = ‖W‖²₂ / (min{D_in,D_out} · ‖W‖²_F))** — Tracks whether weight matrices maintain high stable rank during training. Figure 2 shows per-module κ trajectories.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU — just compute κ for Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 weights from HuggingFace checkpoint.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — tells us whether L19's spectral structure is in the "well-conditioned" regime that the paper's theory applies to.

- **Rotation angle tracking (θ_t = arccos(⟨W_{t+1}, W_t⟩_F / ‖W_{t+1}‖_F ‖W_t‖_F))** — Measures angular velocity of weight updates across training.
  *Replication cost*: Needs training checkpoints (not available for Qwen-2.5-1.5B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have intermediate checkpoints.

- **Norm-Gated SwiGLU (Eq. 3)** — Normalizes the Swish branch before Hadamard product to prevent quadratic scale cascading across layers without learnable norms.
  *Replication cost*: Trivial code change, but needs retraining.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — architecture modification requiring full pre-training.

- **Spectral-gap-modulated rotation (Eq. 5, Wedin sin Θ bound)** — The spectral sphere induces adaptive rotation angles proportional to η_t·c·R / (R − σ₂(W_{t+1})). This provides a quantitative link between spectral gap and training dynamics.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU — compute σ₁, σ₂ for L19 weight matrix.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — compute the spectral gap at L19 and connect to DoM structure.

## Approaches & framings

- **Manifold constraints as normalization substitutes:** The paper reframes RMSNorm as a redundant mechanism when weight matrices are geometrically constrained. This inverts the usual view that normalization layers *cause* certain activation geometry; instead, the weight geometry is primary. For us: this suggests F-1 breathing may be a weight-geometry phenomenon rather than an activation-geometry one, directing attention from the residual stream to the weight matrices that produce it.

- **Static vs adaptive rotational equilibrium:** Frobenius sphere → static rotation (θ_t ≈ η_t·c); spectral sphere → adaptive rotation modulated by spectral gap. For our F-2: if the DoM direction tracks the leading right singular vector v₁ of W_L19, then its stability during inference is governed by the spectral gap Δ₁ = σ₁ − σ₂. A large gap → stable DoM → reliable correctness prediction.

- **Activation scale as Θ(1) design target:** The paper frames all normalization/constraint mechanisms through the lens of maintaining ‖vec(Y)‖_RMS = Θ(1) across layers. For F-10/PH-null: if every layer maintains unit-scale activations, residuals live on an effective sphere, explaining why PH can't distinguish them from Gaussian.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- MACRO uses β = 0.9 momentum, single global constraint radius r for all layers (they note per-layer r would close the 0.02 val loss gap with learnable norms, Table 3 footnote)
- Approximate spectral projection: W_{t+1} = R · W̃_t / ‖W̃_t‖₂ (Appendix B.1) — avoids full SVD at each step
- For Frobenius sphere, tangent-space violation ⟨W, ∇̃⟩ stabilizes at 10⁻²–10⁻³ (Appendix C.3)
- PyTorch Muon baseline uses the official `torch.optim.Muon` implementation
- Code not publicly released as of paper date (May 2026)
