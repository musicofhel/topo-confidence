# Triage brief — 2305.14160 — Label Words are Anchors: An Information Flow Perspective for Understanding In-Context Learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14160
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — mechanism: information is localized at anchor tokens, not distributed across the residual stream.**
   This paper shows that in GPT2-XL (1.5B, same scale as Qwen-2.5-1.5B), semantic information during in-context processing aggregates specifically into label-word token positions in shallow layers, and deep layers read *from those specific positions* via attention. If this localization pattern holds for MATH-500 prefill, the DoM direction we extract at the last prefill token may be predominantly reading from a small set of "anchor" tokens rather than encoding a holistic residual-stream geometry. Falsifiable: extract per-token saliency maps for our L19 prefill activations; if >60% of the saliency for our DoM probe concentrates in <5 token positions, the "single distributed direction" interpretation of F-2 would be substantially weakened (the signal would be better described as attention-weighted anchor readout).

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) — mechanism: shallow-layer aggregation vs deep-layer extraction explains the orthogonality as a trivial consequence of information flow stage, not a structural finding.**
   Wang et al. show that shallow layers aggregate into anchors (high S_wp) while deep layers extract from anchors (high S_pq). If prefill DoM captures the aggregation stage's geometry and final-token DoM captures the extraction stage's geometry, their orthogonality is expected from the two-stage information flow pattern — not evidence of "structurally distinct correctness signatures." Falsifiable: compute DoM directions per-layer across all 28 layers of Qwen-2.5-1.5B; if a phase transition in DoM direction occurs between layers ~5-10 (matching the shallow→deep transition reported in this paper), F-3's orthogonality finding reduces to a layer-depth effect rather than a prefill-vs-generation structural distinction.

3. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — mechanism: prefill signal may encode demonstration-anchored information routing quality, not problem-intrinsic properties.**
   The anchor hypothesis implies that what the prefill residual stream encodes is how well the model's attention routed information *from demonstrations into anchor positions*. For MATH-500 (which uses few-shot demonstrations), our prefill DoM signal might be measuring "quality of ICL anchoring" rather than anything about the problem itself. If true, H-5's hypothesis that the signal encodes familiarity/decomposability would be wrong — the signal would be about demonstration utilization effectiveness. Falsifiable: compare DoM AUROC in zero-shot vs few-shot MATH-500; if zero-shot DoM drops to chance while few-shot DoM stays at 0.77, the signal is anchor-mediated rather than problem-intrinsic.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 L19 prefill DoM):** The paper's finding that deep-layer hidden states at the query position are primarily reading from anchor token positions via attention connects directly. Our L19 extraction averages across all tokens at the prefill boundary — if the signal concentrates in anchor tokens, a weighted extraction targeting those tokens could push above 0.7731.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** The two-stage information flow (shallow=aggregation, deep=extraction) offers a mechanistic explanation for the near-orthogonality. Prefill captures pre-generation aggregation geometry; final token captures post-generation extraction geometry.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM):** The anchor framework predicts this — if all layers in the deep computation phase are reading the same anchor representations, trajectory-level features across those layers would be redundant with any single deep layer's readout.

- **F-4 (correct trajectories collapse harder):** Anchor theory predicts that correct answers produce more coherent/lower-entropy attention distributions over anchor tokens, potentially explaining the "tighter collapse" as attention concentration on the dominant anchor.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of DoM):** The paper's per-head β₀ parameters in anchor re-weighting provide a concrete template for decomposing our DoM signal across attention heads.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Saliency-based information flow tracing (Taylor expansion)** — Computes per-layer attention significance via |Σ_h A_{h,l} ⊙ ∂L/∂A_{h,l}|. Decomposes into S_wp (text→label), S_pq (label→target), S_ww (baseline).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (requires backprop through attention, but our cached NPZs don't include attention weights — needs a single forward+backward pass on 500 samples).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly answers whether DoM signal routes through specific token positions.

- **Anchor re-weighting (learnable per-head attention scaling)** — Per-head, per-layer learnable scalar β₀ⁱ on attention weights to label/anchor positions. Trained with Adam (lr=0.01, 10 epochs).
  *Replication cost*: 1h GPU (forward passes + gradient computation for per-head parameters).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we probe correctness, not steer classification; but the per-head decomposition idea maps to H-13.

- **Hidden-state demonstration compression (Hiddenanchor)** — Replace full demonstrations with cached anchor hidden states per layer. Achieves 89% label loyalty with 1.5-2.9× speedup.
  *Replication cost*: 2h GPU for full pipeline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — inference speed not our concern.

- **Confusion distance metric** — Normalized distance between projected key vectors of different class anchors predicts classification errors.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached activations if we define "anchors" for correct vs incorrect.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we could define correct/incorrect DoM as "anchors" and compute confusion distances to diagnose where the 0.7731 probe fails.

- **AUC-ROC cumulative ratio (R_l)** — Cumulative proportion of total layer-wise AUC contribution up to layer l. Shows which layers contain the discriminative information.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (need per-layer DoM AUROC which we partially have from P8 layer-wise experiments).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already have layer-wise data; computing R_l tells us what fraction of total signal is in L19 vs distributed.

## Approaches & framings

- **Two-stage information flow (aggregation → extraction):** Shallow layers consolidate semantics into anchor positions; deep layers query anchors for decisions. This directly reframes our prefill-vs-final distinction (F-3) as a stage-of-processing distinction rather than a structural-geometry distinction.

- **Label words as information bottleneck:** The claim that specific token positions become privileged information conduits challenges our "distributed residual-stream direction" interpretation. If the geometry we detect is actually attention-routing quality to bottleneck tokens, interventions should target those tokens, not the full residual stream.

- **Logistic-regression equivalence of deep-layer attention:** The paper shows attention over anchors approximates logistic regression (Eq. 6-8). This connects to our finding that a simple linear probe (logistic regression on DoM) works so well — both may be reading the same logistic-regression-like computation in deep layers.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- GPT2-XL (1.5B, 48 layers) is primary model — same scale as our Qwen-2.5-1.5B (1.5B, 28 layers), making mechanistic comparisons somewhat valid despite architecture differences.
- Saliency computation uses Taylor expansion (first-order) — cheaper than full integrated gradients but requires a single backward pass per sample.
- The M=10 principal directions from query vector variance for key-vector projection — analogous to our PCA approach but applied to attention key space rather than residual stream directly.
- Anchor re-weighting trains only ~O(heads × layers) parameters — extremely lightweight.
- Code not linked in paper, but methodology is straightforward to reimplement from equations.

## 2305.14160 — Label Words are Anchors: An Information Flow Perspective for Understanding In-Context Learning (Wang, Li, Dai, Chen, Zhou, Meng, Zhou, Sun; EMNLP 2023)

**Relevance:** Identifies a two-stage information flow pattern (shallow aggregation into anchor tokens → deep extraction from anchors) in GPT2-XL (1.5B parameters, same scale as our Qwen-2.5-1.5B). The anchor mechanism offers an alternative interpretation of our prefill DoM signal: rather than encoding correctness in distributed geometry, the model may be routing information through privileged token positions, with routing quality predicting correctness. The layer-wise phase transition and per-head decomposition connect to F-2 (L19 AUROC 0.7731), F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality), F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy), and H-13 (head attribution).

**Key claim we tested:** Label-word token positions function as information bottlenecks — shallow layers aggregate into them, deep layers extract from them — and this explains ICL behavior.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — The anchor mechanism has not been directly tested on our MATH-500 pipeline. P11-FE130382 (zero-shot vs few-shot DoM AUROC) and P11-FE130383 (saliency tracing) are designed to determine whether our correctness signal is anchor-mediated or problem-intrinsic.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE130380, P11-FE130381, P11-FE130382, P11-FE130383, H-830, H-831
