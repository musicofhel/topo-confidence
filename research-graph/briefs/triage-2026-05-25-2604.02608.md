# Triage brief — 2604.02608 — Steerable but Not Decodable: Function Vectors Operate Beyond the Logit Lens

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02608
Triaged: 2026-05-25

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (L19 prefill DoM predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — if the DoM direction is steerable-but-not-decodable, its predictive power may be an artifact of a linearly decodable confound rather than the true correctness signal.** The paper shows that function vectors can steer behavior through subspaces invisible to the logit lens (the model's own unembedding). Our F-2 DoM direction is extracted by the same mean-difference method (correct vs incorrect activations) as function vectors. If the true "correctness computation" direction at L19 operates in a steerable-but-not-decodable subspace, then our 0.7731 AUROC may be reading a confounded proxy (e.g., difficulty-correlated surface features) rather than the actual computational instruction the model uses to produce correct answers. *Falsifiable:* Project our L19 DoM through Qwen-2.5-1.5B's own unembedding (logit lens at L19) and check whether the resulting token distribution is coherent with correctness-relevant tokens or incoherent — incoherence would suggest our DoM, like FVs, encodes a computational instruction rather than an answer-aligned direction, raising questions about what the linear probe is actually reading.

2. **Refutes H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — the paper's dual-mechanism hypothesis predicts that additive steering via our DoM may fail or succeed invisibly depending on model family.** The paper finds Llama and Gemma FVs operate "modulatorily" (altering attention/MLP gating without changing logit-lens-visible representations), while Mistral FVs operate "representationally." Our H-1 hypothesis assumes that adding the DoM direction to the residual stream at L19 will steer behavior. But for Qwen (not tested in this paper), the steering mechanism may be model-family-dependent. More critically, the paper shows FV steering succeeds at early layers (L2–L8) while readability emerges at late layers (L28–L32) — a 20+ layer gap. Our DoM is extracted at L19 (mid-network). If L19 is in the "gap" between where steering works and where decoding works, additive intervention at L19 may be ineffective — the model may need perturbation at earlier layers. *Falsifiable:* Test DoM steering at multiple layers (L2, L8, L13, L19, L25) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 and check if earlier layers produce larger accuracy shifts than L19.

3. **Refutes F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — the orthogonality may be an expected consequence of the steerability–decodability dissociation rather than a surprising structural finding.** The paper demonstrates a 20+-layer temporal gap between where FVs operate (early layers) and where the logit lens can decode (late layers). Our F-3 finds that prefill DoM (early in the forward pass) and final-token DoM (late in the forward pass) are nearly orthogonal. The paper's framework predicts this: early-layer computational instructions and late-layer answer-aligned representations live in different subspaces by construction. If so, the prefill/final orthogonality is not a novel geometric insight about correctness signals but a generic property of how transformers process information across depth — any mean-difference direction at early layers would be orthogonal to one at late layers. *Falsifiable:* Compute random task-irrelevant mean-difference directions at L19-prefill vs L19-final-token positions; if those are also orthogonal with cos ≈ 0, the orthogonality is positional, not correctness-specific.

4. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — trajectory features may capture modulatory signals invisible to the logit lens that single-layer probes miss.** The paper shows that some steering effects are invisible to linear decoding at any individual layer but produce correct outputs through accumulated nonlinear computation across many layers. Our F-9 claims trajectory features (CoE-60, spanning many layers) add nothing over single-layer L19 DoM. But the paper's finding that 5/10 SAND cases required an MLP probe to decode suggests that multi-layer trajectory features could capture nonlinear interactions between layers that single-layer linear probes cannot access. The redundancy in F-9 might hold only for linear probes — a nonlinear probe over CoE-60 trajectory features might exceed the 0.7731 DoM ceiling. *Falsifiable:* Train a 2-layer MLP probe on CoE-60 trajectory features vs. L19-only features and compare AUROC.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's mean-difference FV extraction method (Eq. 1) is methodologically identical to our DoM: both compute mean activations for positive vs negative groups and take the difference. The paper finds that such directions can achieve >0.90 steering accuracy while projecting to incoherent token distributions via the unembedding — directly relevant to understanding what our 0.7731 AUROC direction is actually encoding. The paper's "computational instruction vs answer direction" framing provides a new interpretive lens: our DoM may be a correctness-computation instruction rather than a linear readout of answer content.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** The paper's 20+-layer temporal gap between optimal FV intervention (L2–L8) and logit-lens readability (L28–L32) provides a mechanistic account for why prefill and final-token directions would be orthogonal. If prefill DoM lives in the "computational instruction" subspace and final-token DoM in the "answer-aligned" subspace, orthogonality is predicted by the steerability–decodability dissociation.

- **F-10 (PH on residual streams = Gaussian null):** The paper's finding that FVs operate through pathways "invisible to the unembedding" suggests that topological features computed on the raw residual stream (as in F-10) might be looking in the wrong subspace. If task-relevant structure lives in subspaces orthogonal to W_U, standard PH on the ambient residual stream would see only the Gaussian-null component — strengthening F-10's null result by providing a mechanistic explanation for why topology finds nothing.

- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering):** The paper's comprehensive steering analysis (12 tasks, 6 models, 4032 directed pairs) is the closest empirical precedent for H-1. Key lesson: IID accuracy is the single best predictor of steering quality; cosine similarity between extraction and target templates adds ΔR² ≤ 0.011. This has direct implications for H-1 experimental design — we should evaluate IID efficacy first before testing cross-template generalization.

- **H-13 (Head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits):** The paper's activation patching methodology and finding that FV effects localize at specific residual-stream layers provides a direct methodological template. Peak recovery layers overlap with optimal steering layers (L12–L18 on Llama Base), which could inform where to patch for our prefill vs final-token circuit analysis.

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS):** The paper's decomposition of the LRH into "linear decodability" and "linear steerability" intersects with H-4's question about whether breathing (a dynamic geometric phenomenon) corresponds to a static interpretable direction. If breathing tracks steerability rather than decodability, its relationship to Sharpness Dimension might be more complex than a simple linear correspondence.

## Methodologies extracted

- **FV vocabulary projection** — Project a mean-difference direction through the model's own LayerNorm + unembedding matrix W_U (Eq. 4) and inspect whether the top-50 tokens are coherent with the task. Tests whether a direction is an "answer direction" or a "computational instruction."
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (we already have the DoM direction and model weights cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to characterize what our L19 DoM encodes.

- **Steerability–decodability 2×2 matrix** — Classify (task × model) pairs by whether FV steering and logit-lens decoding each exceed a threshold τ, creating a systematic decomposition of the linear representation hypothesis.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h H100 (requires forward passes for logit-lens analysis across layers).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, adapted — we could construct a 2×2 matrix for our DoM direction: does it steer (H-1) and does it decode (logit lens at L19)?

- **Hewitt & Liang control task for probes** — Train an identical probe on shuffled labels to measure raw decoder capacity vs. true selectivity; gate decodability on selectivity ≥ τ/2. Vetoes false positives from label-marginal shortcuts.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (train control MLP on cached L19 activations with shuffled correct/incorrect labels).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — essential diagnostic for any probe result, directly applicable to our DoM and cov-spectrum probes.

- **Diagonal tuned lens** — Per-layer diagonal affine translators T_ℓ(h) = (1 + δ_ℓ) ⊙ h + b_ℓ, identity-initialized, trained to minimize MSE to final-layer activation. Conservative dialect correction with only 2d_model parameters per layer.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100 (requires per-layer training on forward-pass data).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't need dialect correction for probing since our probes are supervised classifiers, not logit-lens readouts.

- **Post-steering logit-lens delta analysis** — Compare logit-lens readability before and after adding the steering vector, layer by layer. Reveals whether the intervention rewrites representations (Mistral-style) or modulates computation (Llama/Gemma-style).
  *Replication cost*: 2–3h H100 (requires forward passes with and without steering intervention).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, if H-1 steering experiments proceed — this diagnostic would tell us whether DoM steering on Qwen operates representationally or modulatorily.

## Approaches & framings

- **Decomposition of the LRH into linear decodability vs linear steerability.** The paper empirically separates these two claims that are usually conflated. This reframes our project: F-2's DoM AUROC demonstrates linear decodability, but H-1's steering hypothesis tests linear steerability — they may be independent properties of the same direction.

- **"Computational instructions" vs "answer directions" dichotomy.** FVs that achieve >0.90 steering accuracy project to incoherent token distributions — they encode process, not content. This provides a competing interpretation of our DoM: rather than reading "this problem will be answered correctly," the direction may encode "the model is executing a particular computation strategy."

- **Dual-mechanism hypothesis (representational vs modulatory steering).** Different model families achieve the same behavioral outcome through categorically different mechanisms — Mistral rewrites representations, Llama/Gemma modulate computation. This is directly relevant to any cross-architecture generalization claims in our project and suggests that Qwen may fall into one or the other camp.

- **Simpson's paradox dissolution at scale.** The paper shows a negative correlation (r = −0.572) between cosine alignment and transfer accuracy dissolved at 4× task scale. This methodological lesson applies to any correlation we report: small-sample correlations in our 500-problem MATH-500 dataset may be similarly fragile.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Mean-difference FV extraction (Eq. 1): 15 positive (correct ICL demos) vs 15 negative (zero-shot) prompts, residual stream at final token position.
- Steering multiplier sweep: α ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0} with adaptive refinement around best α.
- MLP probe architecture: LayerNorm → Linear(d_model → 1024) → GELU → Dropout(0.1) → Linear(1024 → |V|), 80/20 train/test split by unique input.
- Hewitt & Liang control: identical MLP trained on deterministic random shuffle of input → label mapping. Selectivity = real_top10 − control_top10. Gate: selectivity ≥ τ/2 = 0.05.
- IID gate threshold: τ = 0.10 (mean IID accuracy across templates).
- Models: Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-7B-v0.3 (base + instruct variants). All 7–9B.
- ~50 min wall-clock for full MLP probe sweep (1,272 probes) on H200.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.02608 — Steerable but Not Decodable: Function Vectors Operate Beyond the Logit Lens (Nadaf, 2026)

**Relevance:** Largest cross-template function vector transfer study (12 tasks, 6 models, 4032 directed pairs). Demonstrates that linear decodability and linear steerability are operationally separable — FVs steer behavior through subspaces the logit lens cannot decode (steerability-without-decodability in 10/72 task × model instances; the predicted opposite pattern has only 3/72). FVs project to incoherent token distributions even at >0.90 steering accuracy, encoding "computational instructions" rather than "answer directions." A tuned-lens robustness check closes only 1/14 gaps (93% persist); an MLP probe with Hewitt & Liang control closes 5/10 SAND cases. Post-steering analysis reveals a model-family mechanistic divergence: Mistral FVs rewrite representations (delta up to +0.91), Llama and Gemma FVs produce near-zero changes despite successful steering.

**Key claim we tested:** The mean-difference direction (identical to our DoM extraction) can be decomposed into a decodability component (readable by the logit lens / unembedding) and a steerability component (effective when added to residual stream). These come apart: steering succeeds where decoding fails.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — Three cheap CPU experiments proposed (FE910–FE912) to determine whether our L19 prefill DoM exhibits the same steerability–decodability dissociation as Nadaf's FVs. If DoM projects incoherently through W_U, our F-2 finding needs reinterpretation: the 0.7731 AUROC reads a process signal, not content. FE914 (H100) would construct the full 2×2 matrix for Qwen.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE910, P11-FE911, P11-FE912, P11-FE913, P11-FE914, H-920, H-921, H-922

**Status:** TO TEST
