# Triage brief — 2006.15595 — Rethinking Positional Encoding in Language Pre-training

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15595
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

**R1: F-3's "structural orthogonality" survives residual-level position controls but NOT attention-level decomposition — TUPE shows the relevant confound operates inside the attention mechanism, not at the output.**

F-3 establishes that prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal (cos = 0.046 raw, 0.0008 after Song-Zhong residualization). The Song-Zhong control removes per-position bias from the *residual stream output*. But TUPE's Eq. (6) decomposition shows that the attention computation that *produces* those residuals mixes word-to-position and position-to-word cross-terms at every layer. These cross-terms could systematically rotate content representations in position-dependent ways that accumulate through 19 layers. Removing `pos_t` from the final output does not undo the position-content coupling that shaped how information flowed through the network. Concretely: if the prefill DoM direction was shaped by word→position cross-correlations at early layers (which TUPE shows are "noisy" but nonzero), then the orthogonality with final-token DoM could be a consequence of different position-dependent attention routing at prefill vs final tokens — a mechanism-level positional artifact that residual-level controls cannot detect.

*Falsifiable test:* Extract L19 attention matrices for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500. Decompose each head's attention into the 4 TUPE terms (word-word, word-pos, pos-word, pos-pos). Ablate the cross-terms (zero them out) and measure how the effective DoM direction changes. If removing cross-terms shifts cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) from ~0.05 to >0.3, F-3's "structural" interpretation weakens.

**R2: F-2's AUROC 0.7731 may partially depend on position-content entanglement that TUPE shows standard transformers cannot disentangle — the "correctness direction" might encode problem-length or position-dependent features.**

TUPE demonstrates empirically (Figure 2) that in trained BERT models, word-to-position correlations are weak but present. In Qwen-2.5's RoPE-based architecture, the entanglement mechanism differs (rotational rather than additive), but the same principle applies: residual-stream representations at L19 encode both content and position. If harder MATH-500 problems produce systematically longer or shorter prefill sequences, then the L19 mean-pooled representation will have a position-dependent component correlated with difficulty. The DoM direction could be partially aligned with this positional confound, inflating AUROC. H-101 proposes testing this via Song-Zhong decomposition of the DoM direction itself, but TUPE's analysis suggests the confound may be more fine-grained — living in the attention cross-terms, not the residual-stream positional subspace.

*Falsifiable test:* Compute Spearman correlation between DoM scores and prefill sequence length across the 500 MATH-500 samples. If |ρ| > 0.2 and AUROC drops below 0.74 after residualizing DoM scores against sequence length, the positional confound is load-bearing.

**R3: F-5's "breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics" claim doesn't account for content→position→hidden-state coupling that TUPE shows exists in standard attention.**

F-5 was established by showing breathing patterns differ between correct/incorrect answers and between gibberish/real prompts. But TUPE's analysis reveals a subtler mechanism: in standard transformers, content-dependent attention patterns create position-dependent side effects through the word→position and position→word cross-terms (Eq. 6, terms 2 and 3). Different problem types produce different attention distributions, which through the cross-terms create different position-dependent hidden state modulations. This means "content-dependent breathing" could partially be an indirect effect: content → attention patterns → position-dependent cross-term modulation → hidden-state geometry. The gibberish control doesn't rule this out because gibberish produces fundamentally different attention patterns, which would produce fundamentally different position-dependent side effects.

*Falsifiable test:* Compare breathing amplitude across MATH-500 problems matched on sequence length but differing in correctness. If breathing amplitude differences survive length-matching (i.e., the content signal is present even when positional mechanics are held constant), F-5 strengthens. If differences attenuate substantially, F-5 needs a positional-coupling caveat.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

**F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** TUPE's decomposition of attention into content-only and position-only terms provides a principled framework for testing whether the DoM direction captures pure content signal or a content+position mixture. The specific concern: Qwen-2.5 uses RoPE, which applies deterministic rotations to Q/K in paired dimensions. If L19 DoM has significant projection onto RoPE rotation planes, the 0.7731 AUROC is partially position-confounded.

**F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** TUPE's insight that word-position cross-correlations are noisy but present directly motivates decomposing the cos = 0.046 into content-only and position-contaminated components. F-3 has already run Song-Zhong residualization (cos → 0.0008), which controls at the residual level. TUPE motivates the *attention-level* version of this control, which no existing experiment addresses. Connects to H-17 (RoPE-mechanical rotation), H-101 (DoM in non-positional subspace), and H-107 (prefill-final cosine after RoPE de-rotation).

**F-5 (breathing is content-dependent):** TUPE's empirical finding (Figure 2) that word-to-position correlations look "uniform" but are nonzero in trained models raises the question of whether content-dependent breathing includes a content→position→breathing pathway.

**H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical):** TUPE provides the specific mathematical formalism (Eq. 6-8) for decomposing the attention into position-dependent and position-independent terms, which is exactly what H-17 needs to test. If the content-only attention term alone reproduces the DoM, H-17 is rejected; if the positional term is needed, H-17 is supported.

**H-101 (DoM lives in non-positional subspace):** TUPE's analysis operates at a different level — attention mechanism internals vs residual-stream subspace projection — but they converge on the same question. TUPE provides an alternative methodology: instead of projecting hidden states onto positional subspaces (Song-Zhong style), decompose the attention computation that produced them.

## Methodologies extracted

- **4-term attention decomposition (Eq. 6)** — Decomposes standard attention score α_ij into word-word, word-position, position-word, and position-position correlations by expanding (w_i + p_i)W^Q((w_j + p_j)W^K)^T. Applicable to any transformer with additive positional encoding; RoPE requires adaptation (rotational rather than additive).
  *Replication cost*: 2h CPU to implement decomposition on cached attention matrices (if we extract them); 4h H100 if we need fresh forward passes to capture attention patterns.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests H-17 and H-101 at the attention mechanism level rather than the residual stream level.

- **Separate projection matrices for content vs position (Eq. 7)** — Uses independent W^{Q,K} for word correlations and U^{Q,K} for positional correlations, eliminating cross-terms. Not directly applicable to cached data (requires architectural modification), but the *analysis* framework — measuring how much signal comes from each term — can be applied post-hoc.
  *Replication cost*: Not applicable as an architectural change to Qwen. As an analysis tool on extracted attention patterns: 2-4h H100 for extraction + 30min CPU for decomposition.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for the architectural modification; yes for the post-hoc analysis.

- **Toeplitz factorization of relative position bias (Proposition 1)** — Shows any Toeplitz matrix B can be factorized as B = GDG*, proving absolute and relative positional encodings span different subspaces of R^{n×n}. Mathematical tool for analyzing what positional encoding captures.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU — pure linear algebra on position matrices.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — Qwen uses RoPE, not learned absolute + T5-style relative, so the specific factorization doesn't apply.

- **[CLS] untying (Eq. 9)** — Replaces positional correlation for a special token with learnable scalars θ_1, θ_2, making it "positionless" and encouraging global information aggregation. Conceptual parallel to our mean-pooled prefill representation.
  *Replication cost*: N/A (training-time modification).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train models, and Qwen doesn't have a [CLS] token.

## Approaches & framings

- **Content-position disentanglement as an inductive bias for pre-training efficiency.** TUPE frames the word-position cross-terms as noise that slows training convergence (30% faster with TUPE). This intersects F-2: if the "noisy" cross-terms are present in Qwen-2.5's L19 representations, the DoM direction might be extracting signal that's partially positional noise, limiting the AUROC ceiling. The 0.7928 cov-spectrum ceiling could be an artifact of this noise rather than a true information bottleneck.

- **Heterogeneous embedding analysis.** TUPE introduces the framing that position and content are "heterogeneous" and should be decomposed, not summed. This directly parallels the Song-Zhong decomposition (h = μ + pos_t + ctx_c + resid) already used in F-3, but argues the decomposition should happen *inside the attention mechanism*, not just at the residual output.

- **Position attention pattern taxonomy.** TUPE-A's visualization (Figure 6) reveals 5 canonical positional attention patterns: global, local, broad, previous-position, next-position. If L19 in Qwen-2.5 has strong local or next-position positional attention heads, those heads would contribute a positional bias to the residual stream that partially drives DoM.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- TUPE adds ~1% parameters (1.18M for BERT-Base 110M) via separate U^Q, U^K projection matrices for positional correlation.
- Positional correlation term is computed once and reused across all layers (shared U^Q, U^K across layers).
- Layer normalization applied to positional embeddings whenever used.
- Scaling factor √(2d) instead of √(d) to maintain magnitude after summing content + position terms.
- θ_1, θ_2 for [CLS] are parameterized as p_θ U^Q (p_θ U^K)^T / √(2d) to maintain consistent scale.
- Code released at https://github.com/guolinke/TUPE (fairseq/PyTorch).

## 2006.15595 — Rethinking Positional Encoding in Language Pre-training (Ke, He, Liu, ICLR 2021)

**Relevance:** TUPE decomposes standard transformer attention into word-word, word-position, position-word, and position-position correlations (Eq. 6) and shows the cross-terms are noisy. This decomposition provides a principled framework for testing whether our L19 DoM correctness signal (F-2, AUROC 0.7731) and prefill/final-token orthogonality (F-3, cos = 0.046) carry positional confounds at the attention-mechanism level that residual-stream controls (Song-Zhong, EXP-48) cannot detect. TUPE is for BERT-style absolute positional encoding (not RoPE), but the core insight — content and position are entangled in the attention computation — generalizes.

**Key claim we tested:** Content-position cross-correlations in standard transformer attention are noisy and removable without performance loss.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — TUPE's decomposition framework has not been applied to our Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 DoM analysis. Proposed experiments P11-FE960 (length correlation), P11-FE961 (RoPE projection), P11-FE963 (attention decomposition) would test whether our findings carry positional confounds at the attention mechanism level.
