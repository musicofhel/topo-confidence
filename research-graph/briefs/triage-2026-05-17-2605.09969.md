# Triage brief — 2605.09969 — The Truth Lies Somewhere in the Middle (of the Generated Tokens)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09969
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731):**
   The paper shows that mean-pooling hidden states across generated tokens yields 2.23× higher kernel alignment with ground-truth semantics than any single-position representation (including last-token). Their CKA-based framework suggests the prefill DoM direction captures only a fraction of correctness-relevant information, and that *distributed* generation-trajectory representations dominate single-position probes. If mean-pooled generation-token states from our Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 runs achieve AUROC > 0.7731 for correctness prediction, F-2's "single direction suffices" framing is incomplete — the DoM is one marginal projection of a richer trajectory-distributed signal.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Extract L19 hidden states at all generation tokens (up to 1024), mean-pool, train logistic regression for correctness → compare AUROC to 0.7731.

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality is structural):**
   Wang et al. show phase structure in generation — alignment rises through "recall" and "specific-response" phases. If the generation trajectory interpolates smoothly between prefill-geometry and final-geometry in representation space (their convex mixing simplex shows interior points exceed vertices), then the prefill/final "orthogonality" (cos = 0.046) may reflect sampling two distant points on a *continuous* trajectory rather than genuinely distinct computational circuits. Their OLMo3-in-Qwen3 cross-model embedding experiment shows phase structure is model-intrinsic, suggesting the rotation is a model-specific artifact of *where* you sample, not evidence of separate circuits.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Compute DoM at every 50th generation token, plot cos(DoM_t, prefill_DoM) and cos(DoM_t, final_DoM) across generation. If there's a smooth rotation from prefill-aligned to final-aligned, F-3's "separate circuits" interpretation weakens.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM):**
   The paper's core finding is that trajectory-aggregated representations (mean over T generated tokens) dominate single-position representations. CoE-60 aggregates over 60 layers at one position; their method aggregates over T positions at the final layer. If generation-trajectory pooling captures different complementary information than layer-trajectory pooling (CoE-60), then F-9's "redundancy" conclusion is premature — CoE-60 may have been redundant with L19 DoM not because trajectory features are uninformative but because CoE-60 sampled the *wrong* trajectory axis (layers vs. tokens).
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Compute mean-pooled generation-token representations + L19 DoM as features jointly; if the pooled features add >2pp AUROC beyond L19 DoM alone, F-9's redundancy claim is restricted to the layer-trajectory axis.

4. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):**
   If mean-pooled generation states outperform prefill for correctness prediction, then the prefill signal may simply be a weak *precursor* of what becomes fully expressed only during generation. The paper's "recall phase" alignment spike suggests the semantically rich representation emerges mid-generation, not at prefill. This would reframe H-5: prefill doesn't encode familiarity *per se* — it encodes an early-stage projection of the richer generation-distributed signal.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Compare prefill DoM AUROC to DoM computed on tokens 10–50 of generation (the "recall" window). If recall-window DoM > prefill DoM, H-5's framing shifts.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** Their framework directly questions whether single-position probing is optimal. Our ridge-LR ceiling (0.8493 final-only) already suggests single-position has more signal than DoM extracts; their mean-pooling approach offers a different extraction that might close the gap from a different direction (token-axis rather than feature-axis).

- **F-3 (cos 0.046 prefill/final):** Their generation-trajectory phase analysis provides a mechanistic lens for *why* prefill and final are orthogonal — if generation is a smooth rotation through representation space, sampling at start and end of a long trajectory naturally produces near-orthogonal directions. Their cos is measured as CKA alignment with external grounding, not internal cos between DoMs, but the geometric story is compatible.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse):** Their observation that generated-token mean-pooling benefits "disappear when semantic correspondence is broken" (shuffled pairs) resonates with F-4's finding that correct trajectories collapse harder. Correct problems have coherent semantic trajectories → mean-pooling aggregates aligned representations → lower effective dimensionality. Incorrect problems meander → mean-pooling averages noise → higher dimensionality.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage):** If mean-pooled generation features yield better correctness AUROC than prefill DoM, the selective-prediction stack's 71.6% accuracy at 50% coverage could potentially be improved by switching from prefill-only to generation-mean features.

- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels):** Paper provides theoretical justification for why trajectory features *should* beat single-position features (distributed information), but along the generation-token axis rather than the layer axis.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Debiased Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)** — Measures representational similarity between two sets of embeddings via U-centered kernel matrices. Avoids inflation from high feature dimensionality that biased CKA suffers.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (need reference embeddings for ground-truth semantic space).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can use DINOv2 image embeddings as reference for vision-captioned problems, or use answer-correctness labels as binary kernel for CKA between hidden-state kernel and correctness kernel.

- **Convex mixing simplex** — Partition generation tokens into K segments, pool each, then evaluate all convex combinations of the K segment-means on a discretized simplex (G=20 → 210 points for K=3). Identifies whether complementary information exists across token segments.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU if we have cached generation-token activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes (if we cache generation activations) — directly tests whether our prefill + final-token two-point sampling misses an interior optimum.

- **Mutual k-nearest neighbors alignment (m-kkNN)** — Local neighborhood overlap metric (k=10) between two embedding spaces. Complementary to CKA (global vs local structure).
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on cached 500-sample matrices.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applicable to our 500×1536 activation matrices directly.

- **Phase-segmented alignment curves** — Compute alignment metric at each generation token position to identify interpretable phases (preamble, repetition, recall, task-specific response).
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 (need generation-token hidden states extracted per position).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes but requires new extraction pass — not available in current NPZ cache (only prefill + final stored).

## Approaches & framings

- **Generation-as-trajectory framing:** Hidden states during generation are not just "outputs" but encode evolving internal computation. Mean-pooling is justified not as variance reduction but as aggregation of *complementary* semantic fragments distributed across positions. Intersects F-3: if generation is a smooth rotation, the prefill/final orthogonality is a geometric consequence of trajectory length, not circuit separation.

- **Kernel alignment as causal probe:** Using CKA against an external grounding space (vision, protein) as a measure of "how much does this representation encode semantics." Reframes our AUROC-based probe as one special case (binary correctness kernel). Intersects F-2: DoM AUROC is a linear-probe correctness metric; CKA is a richer relational metric that might capture structure DoM misses.

- **Second-order collapse (Hara et al. 2026 reference):** Mean pooling works because it exploits "second-order collapse in text embeddings." Directly resonates with F-4's asymmetric participation-ratio collapse — correct trajectories may exhibit stronger second-order collapse, making mean-pooling especially effective for correct answers.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Hidden states extracted at final layer (not intermediate), dimension D varies by model (14B → ~5120).
- 128 generated tokens default; embeddings clipped at 95th percentile of absolute feature values before ℓ2-normalization.
- Debiased CKA uses U-centering (critical for high-D/low-N regime — our 1536-dim / 500-sample setup is exactly this regime where biased CKA inflates).
- Convex simplex grid: G=20 per edge → 210 evaluations for 3-way mixtures.
- 5 random seeds show std < 0.01 on CKA metrics — highly stable.
- Models tested: Qwen3-14B (thinking mode), OLMo3-7B-Think.
