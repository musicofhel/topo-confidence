# Triage brief — 2303.08112 — Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM is a *strong* correctness predictor at AUROC 0.7731, better than final-token at 0.7186)** is potentially refuted by the Tuned Lens trajectory framing. The paper's iterative-inference view says *every* layer carries a probabilistic draft of the answer, and the SRM-at-middle-layer baseline (Bai et al., 2022) outperforms the multi-layer trajectory on 2/9 prompt-injection tasks (MC TACO, SciQ) — i.e. the "best" layer is task-dependent and not always layer 19. **Mechanism**: a tuned-lens KL-trajectory feature on MATH-500 (per-layer KL between the layer's predicted next-token distribution and the final-layer distribution, summarized by an iForest/LOF outlier score) extracts richer signal than any single supervised L19 DoM direction, because the trajectory captures both the magnitude *and* layer-of-convergence. **Falsifiable**: train per-layer translators (A_l, b_l) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with the KL distillation loss (eq. 9 of the paper) on a 50K-token slice of OpenMathInstruct, compute per-problem prediction-trajectory features on the cached MATH-500 1024-tok activations, fit iForest+LOF on correct-answer trajectories, evaluate AUROC for predicting incorrect — if it beats 0.7731, F-2's localization claim is downgraded.

2. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, "prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal")** is potentially refuted by Section 4 of the Tuned Lens paper, which presents Causal Basis Extraction (CBE) demonstrating that the same residual-stream features used by intermediate-layer probes are causally aligned with final-layer features ("stimulus-response alignment"). If iterative inference is correct, the *next-token-prediction* direction at layer 19 should retain a substantial overlap with the *next-token-prediction* direction at the final layer — the 0.046 cosine in F-3 may be an artifact of the *correctness*-supervised DoM extraction (which spans an axis orthogonal to next-token-prediction) rather than evidence of a fundamental geometric orthogonality. **Mechanism**: extract the model's natural residual-prediction direction at L19 prefill and at the final-token via tuned-lens translator weights (the rows of A_l W_U, the unembedding-composed translator), and compare those directions instead of supervised DoMs. **Falsifiable**: cos(translator-A_19 ⋅ row_correct, translator-A_28 ⋅ row_correct) > 0.5 would refute F-3's "orthogonality is fundamental" framing, isolating it to the correctness-axis interpretation.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** is potentially refuted by §5.4's "prediction depth" metric. CoE-60 uses geometric/cosine-distance trajectory features; prediction depth (number of layers until top-1 stops changing, ranges across 28 layers in Qwen) is a *prediction-space* trajectory feature with no cosine-distance content. Pythia 12B prediction depth correlates with iteration_learned at ρ ≈ 0.4–0.66 across 11 tasks (Table 2 of paper). If MATH-500 prediction depth predicts correctness with AUROC > 0.7731, F-9's "the trajectory adds nothing" conclusion is task-specific to CoE's geometric formulation. **Mechanism**: prediction-depth captures *when* the model commits, which is orthogonal to *how strong* the geometric signal is at L19 alone. **Falsifiable**: top-1-prediction stability across layers on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B residuals → AUROC vs MATH-500 K=1 correctness; combine with L19 DoM in a 2-feature classifier and check ΔAUROC > 0.01.

4. **H-12 (Semantic-entropy probes label-free for refusal)** is potentially refuted/upstaged by §5.3's iForest+LOF-on-prediction-trajectory anomaly detection, which is *purely* unsupervised (trained only on normal trajectories) and reaches AUROC 1.00 on QNLI/MNLI/SST-2 prompt-injection detection. If the same recipe works for MATH-500 *correctness* (treating incorrect K=1 generations as anomalies relative to correct ones), it would obviate the need for both supervised DoM extraction (F-2) *and* the semantic-entropy framework. **Mechanism**: outlier-detection on tuned-lens trajectories is a strictly more general label-free signal than semantic entropy (which requires generating multiple samples). **Falsifiable**: fit iForest on tuned-lens trajectories of correct-answer problems only; evaluate AUROC on held-out correct-vs-incorrect; compare against H-12's projected semantic-entropy AUROC.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 AUROC 0.7731 vs final 0.7186)** — direct connection. Halawi et al. 2023 "Overthinking the Truth" (heavily cited and replicated in §5.2) shows early-layer predictions are MORE robust than final-layer predictions on tasks with corrupted demonstrations. This is qualitatively the same "earlier layer beats later" finding as F-2 (prefill > final-token on the same L19) but in a different framing. Tuned lens provides the cleanest layer-by-layer decoding to test whether the L19 advantage extends across all earlier layers.
- **F-3 (cos = 0.046)** — connection above; alternative explanation via tuned-lens features.
- **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective signature, not per-problem)** — prediction depth gives a *per-problem* difficulty metric that should ideally separate D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong — pathological) from A/B/C buckets if D is a stable per-problem property.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ L19 DoM)** — connection above; tuned lens trajectory may break the redundancy.
- **F-10 (PH on residuals = Gaussian null)** — orthogonal: tuned lens uses *prediction-distribution* trajectory features (over the vocabulary simplex), not residual-stream geometry. Tuned lens success would actually *support* F-10 by showing the useful signal lives in the projected-prediction space, not in residual-stream topology.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — adjacent: CBE provides a principled method to discover *multiple* causally-relevant directions, not just one DoM. CBE could replace ad-hoc DoM-discovery with a model-aligned procedure.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — adjacent: CBE is the natural starting point for a circuit-level decomposition since it returns the principal causally-effective directions per layer.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Tuned lens / translators** — per-layer affine probe (A_l, b_l) trained to map h_l onto the input space of the final layer; composed with the pretrained unembedding to yield a probability distribution over the vocabulary at every layer. Eq. 8: TunedLens_l(h_l) = LogitLens(A_l h_l + b_l).
  *Replication cost*: ~2–4h on a single A100/H100 to fit 28 translators (one per Qwen-2.5-1.5B layer) on a small Pile/MATH-500 slice (~16M tokens or less). Reference impl: github.com/AlignmentResearch/tuned-lens.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly produces a per-layer prediction-distribution that supersedes raw cosine/PR features for several pathways (P8, P9, P10, P11) and is the natural backbone for FE69/FE70/FE71 below.

- **KL-distillation loss for layer probes** — argmin E[D_KL(f_>l(h_l) || TunedLens_l(h_l))] (Eq. 9). Uses the model's own final distribution as soft target — *does not require ground-truth labels*, which avoids the Hewitt-Liang (2019) probe-overfitting failure mode.
  *Replication cost*: built into tuned-lens repo training code.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already cited as a defense against overfitted probes; replaces the supervised cross-entropy loss in our DoM extraction.

- **Causal Basis Extraction (CBE)** — recursively find directions in residual stream whose mean-ablation maximally degrades a downstream functional (tuned-lens output or model output), enforcing orthogonality to previously-found directions. Returns a "principal feature basis" per layer.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU per layer on cached residuals (eigendecomposition + iterative ablation).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our DoM extraction; could discover that the L19 DoM is one of several orthogonal causal directions, refining F-2.

- **Stimulus-response alignment** — measure the cosine between (Δprobe-output, Δmodel-output) under matched residual-stream perturbations. Confirms the probe's directions are model-aligned, not spurious.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min H100 per direction (forward passes with patched residuals).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — used as a sanity test for any DoM-style direction we treat as causal.

- **Prediction-depth metric** — for each example, the smallest layer L* such that argmax(TunedLens_l(h_l)) is constant for all l ≥ L*. Measures example difficulty without retraining.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached MATH-500 residuals + trained translators.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural per-problem difficulty signal that can be tested against B/D-bucket membership and K=1 correctness.

- **Outlier-detection on prediction trajectories** — flatten per-layer log-probs into a feature vector, fit iForest (Liu et al., 2008) and LOF (Breunig et al., 2000) on *normal* trajectories only, score test examples. Achieves AUROC up to 1.00 for prompt-injection detection on Pythia 12B.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU once tuned lens is trained.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — pure unsupervised baseline that competes against F-2's supervised DoM AUROC 0.7731 (see refutation #4).

- **Simplified Relative Mahalanobis (SRM) at middle layer** — Bai et al. 2022 baseline; Mahalanobis distance from the training-distribution mean at the *middle* layer of the transformer. Outperforms tuned lens on 2/9 prompt-injection tasks.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B middle-layer (L14) residuals.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheaper baseline than DoM, gives a layer-localization control for F-2.

- **Marginal-distribution KL bias measurement** — D_KL(p || q_l) where p is the empirical token marginal and q_l is the average lens prediction at layer l. Diagnoses whether a probe's predictions are systematically miscalibrated.
  *Replication cost*: trivial post-tuned-lens.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful sanity check if we ever question whether L19 DoM scores are calibrated.

## Approaches & framings

- **Iterative inference framing (Jastrzębski et al., 2017)**: each transformer layer is an incremental update to a latent next-token prediction, not a fresh feature extraction. **Intersects F-1/F-3/F-9**: our "breathing" and "prefill-final orthogonality" findings are framed in geometric/PR space, but iterative inference reframes them as draft refinement in *prediction* space. Reframing F-3 as "the *correctness*-axis is orthogonal between prefill and final-token, but the *next-token-prediction* axis is convergent" would resolve a tension in the current narrative.

- **Probes-as-distillation (Sanh-style soft-label loss)**: rather than training probes against ground-truth labels (which encourages probes to discover features the model doesn't use, per Hewitt-Liang 2019), train them to mimic the model's own output. **Intersects all DoM work**: our DoM is currently a *supervised* probe — moving to a distillation-style loss would address the Ríos-García (2604.18805) confirmation-bias concern about supervised probes.

- **"Translators" as basis-change matrices** (A_l: out-space-of-l → in-space-of-final): explicit acknowledgment that hidden state covariance drifts across layers and that a fixed unembedding misinterprets earlier layers. **Intersects F-1**: the breathing-PR signal could be partly an artifact of this drift; A_l-projected residuals might show flat PR.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Translator architecture: single affine layer (A_l ∈ R^{d×d}, b_l ∈ R^d) per transformer block. For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (d=1536, 28 layers): ~28 × (1536² + 1536) ≈ 66M trainable parameters total.
- Loss: per-layer KL divergence with final-layer distribution as soft label (Eq. 9). Final transformer layer is *excluded* from training for GPT-2/NeoX/OPT/Pythia (training one against itself is degenerate); included for GPT-Neo and BLOOM.
- Optimizer: SGD with Nesterov momentum, linear LR decay. Authors note this — not Adam — which is non-standard for probe training.
- Eval: 16.4M tokens from each model's pretraining validation set; for models without released val sets (BLOOM, GPT-2), use Pile.
- Documents concatenated and chunked to length 2048 (matches our 1024-tok regime within a factor of 2).
- Translators handle "rogue dimensions" (Timkey & van Schijndel 2021) explicitly: Figure 6 shows hidden-state covariance similarity across layers, top-2-component-removed.
- CBE uses *mean ablation* (set ⟨r(h, v), v⟩ to dataset-mean), not zero ablation — matches our deconfounding methodology in P10.
- Reference implementation: github.com/AlignmentResearch/tuned-lens (PyPI: `tuned-lens`).

## 2303.08112 — Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens (Belrose, Ostrovsky, McKinney, Furman, Smith, Halawi, Biderman, Steinhardt; 2023)

**Relevance:** The tuned lens is the canonical per-layer probe in the iterative-inference framing of transformers, with KL-distillation training, causal basis extraction, and prediction-depth applications. It is *the* methodological superset of our DoM/CoE/PR work — it covers the same residual-stream-as-evolving-state premise but with prediction-distribution features instead of geometric features. It is also the prior art for label-free anomaly detection on prediction trajectories (AUROC 1.00 on prompt-injection detection with iForest/LOF on Pythia 12B), which is a direct refutation test for F-2's supervised-DoM AUROC 0.7731 and H-12's semantic-entropy framing.

**Key claim we tested:** "Trajectory of latent predictions across layers contains usable signal beyond what any single layer provides — and this signal is label-free, model-aligned (CBE), and competitive with supervised baselines (SRM)."

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** No prior comparison of tuned-lens trajectories against L19 prefill DoM on MATH-500 correctness exists in our pipeline. The closest experiment is F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM), but CoE-60 uses cosine-distance features in geometric space, not prediction-distribution features over the vocabulary. H-95 (FE72) is the head-to-head test; H-96 and H-97 follow.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, H-95, H-96, H-97.
