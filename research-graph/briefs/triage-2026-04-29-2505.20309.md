# Triage brief — 2505.20309 — Guiding Giants: Lightweight Controllers for Weighted Activation Steering in LLMs

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20309
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Multi-layer weighted probes may beat single-layer L19 DoM (refutes F-2 / F-9).**
   *Mechanism:* WAS's layer-selection ablation (Table 6) shows that steering at all layers gets 93.0% refusal, late-only gets 81.2%, and middle+late combined gets 88.7%. For *behavioral output*, multi-layer beats single-layer by ~12 points. F-2 reports prefill L19 DoM = 0.7731 AUROC and F-9 claims CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM — both implicitly assert "one layer is enough." If correctness has the same multi-layer structure as refusal, a probe over concatenated upper-third activations should outperform L19-alone.
   *Falsifiable test:* Train a small MLP (hidden=1024, ReLU) on `concat(h_L19, …, h_L27)` from Pathway 11 cached prefill activations, MSE-target = correctness label. Report 5-fold OOF AUROC vs. F-2's 0.7731. If gain > 0.02 (above probe-overfitting noise), F-2 / F-9 need to drop "single direction" framing.

2. **Fixed-direction + learned-controller may rescue Pathway 2 / H-1 steering (refutes the implicit "fixed-α DoM steering moves MATH" framing in H-1).**
   *Mechanism:* Pathway 2 spherical steering failed (Pathway 10 v1 showed the steering vector was geometrically unrelated to the current DoM). WAS demonstrates that a *single fixed* precomputed direction CAN successfully alter behavior IF a learned controller modulates per-prompt magnitude `s` and per-layer weights `w_l`. H-1 currently proposes naive per-position DoM steering. If WAS-style controller modulation lifts MATH-500 K=1 accuracy by ≥1 point, H-1 should be reformulated as "controller-modulated DoM steering" rather than rejected — and Pathway 2's NO-GO becomes a "wrong harness" rather than a "wrong direction" verdict.
   *Falsifiable test:* Train a WAS-style controller on cached Stage 2 prefill activations, supervising `s≈1` for incorrect-predicted prompts and `s≈0` for correct. Apply `Δh_l = s · w_l · α_global · d_DoM` at all layers during MATH-500 generation. Compare to baseline 48.6% (1.5B) K=1.

3. **Refusal direction may transfer across architectures while correctness direction does not (refutes any implicit cross-architecture universality claim around F-2 / F-3).**
   *Mechanism:* WAS reports the *same* refusal-direction recipe (Eq. 7: `(ē_refuse − ē_answer) / ‖·‖`) works at >90% ToxicChat refusal on Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-1B, and Mistral-7B — three architectures, one direction. Headlines table reports `cos(prefill_DoM_1.5B, final_DoM_1.5B) = 0.046` (within Qwen) and a separate 7B DoM with no asserted alignment. If refusal directions are cross-architecture-stable in a way correctness directions are not, the F-2/F-3 geometry is task-specific rather than reflecting a generic "internal-state predicts output" geometry.
   *Falsifiable test:* Compute Eq. 7 refusal direction `d_refuse` for Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Qwen-2.5-7B from their unembedding matrices `W_U` (refusal-token list and answer-token list from the WAS GitHub). Compute `cos(d_refuse_1.5B_aligned, d_refuse_7B)` using a Procrustes/CKA cross-model alignment. If `cos > 0.3` while DoM cross-scale alignment is at chance, "the geometry that predicts behavior" is task-specific, not architecture-specific.

4. **Late-layer hotspots may push the "best-correctness layer" away from L19 (refutes F-7 layer choice).**
   *Mechanism:* WAS finds refusal hotspots concentrated in the *last third* of layers (input concat is `L ≥ 2N_L/3`, and Table 6 shows late layers carry 81.2% of full performance). For 32-layer Llama-3.1-8B that's L21-31 (~67-100% depth). For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers) the analogous range would be L19-27. F-7's D-bucket signature is reported at L19 (~68% depth — right at the *boundary* of WAS's "useful" region, not the peak). If the true peak D-bucket geometric separation is at L24 or L25, our pathway-11 layer choice is at the lower edge of the actual feature locus.
   *Falsifiable test:* Re-run F-7's D-bucket vs. A-bucket geometric separation metric (cluster purity, Silhouette, or whatever F-7 used) at every layer L0-L27 of cached Stage 2 NPZs. Identify argmax layer. If `argmax_layer ≠ L19` with margin ≥ 0.05 in separation metric, F-7 needs a layer-sweep update — and the F-2 0.7731 ceiling may be a layer-choice artifact.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — WAS is the direct method-template. The 1.5B baseline accuracy (48.6%, headlines table) is the goal post; WAS-style controller modulation is a stronger steering harness than naive fixed-α addition. This paper provides the missing implementation pattern for H-1.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** — WAS's controller takes input from `L ≥ 2N_L/3` (last third) of layers. F-2 uses just one layer (L19, ~68% depth). The 0.7731 ceiling has not been tested against multi-layer weighted concatenation in the prefill regime.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer L19 DoM)** — WAS's Scalar-Only ablation (uniform layer weights) drops refusal by 21.2 points vs. learned `w_l`. If correctness shares this multi-layer structure, F-9's "single layer captures all the trajectory information" verdict could be limited to *unweighted* multi-layer features, not to learned-weight features.
- **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token DoM, cos = 0.046)** — WAS uses the *same* `d_steer` direction at all layers and all token positions, only varying the magnitude. If a single direction can drive output at every layer-position pair, the orthogonality of *empirical* prefill-DoM and final-token-DoM may reflect the probe-fitting target (correctness at different positions selects different directions) rather than a deep geometric fact.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs. final-token circuits)** — WAS's per-layer weight visualization (their Fig. 4 shows L8, L14-16, L24 hotspots for chemical/harassment/illegal categories) is a layer-level analog of the head-level attribution H-13 wants. The technique can be adapted from `(s, w_l)` over layers to `(s, w_h)` over heads.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Lightweight controller MLP that predicts (scalar magnitude, per-layer weights) from concatenated upper-third activations** — `f_c: concat(h_l for l in L_input) → (s, w_logits)` with hidden=1024, ReLU, sigmoid on layer logits.
  *Replication cost:* 4h CPU on cached Stage 2 prefill activations (no new generation needed).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — direct test of refutation #1, drop-in replacement for F-2's logistic probe.

- **Discriminative MSE training with implicit gradient flow to layer-weight head** — Loss only on scalar `s` (target=1 for harmful, 0 for benign), gradients flow to `w_logits` via shared hidden layer; "interpretable weight patterns emerge" without explicit per-layer supervision.
  *Replication cost:* same training run as above.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — the implicit-supervision trick is non-obvious and worth probing for correctness.

- **Refusal direction via output-embedding contrast (Eq. 7)** — `d_steer = (ē_refuse − ē_answer) / ‖·‖_2` where the averages are over predefined refusal/answer token sets in the unembedding matrix `W_U`. No activation extraction needed.
  *Replication cost:* 20min CPU per model (just `W_U` arithmetic).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — needed for refutation #3 cross-model geometry test, and as a label-free baseline DoM construction for H-12 (semantic-entropy probes).

- **Patch application via PyTorch forward hooks** — `Δh_l = s · w_l · α_global · d_steer` injected at every transformer block output, no model-code modification.
  *Replication cost:* 1h coding (clean reusable harness).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — needed for any H-1 / Pathway 2 re-test.

- **LLM-as-judge refusal detection via GPT-4o** — keyword pre-filter + GPT-4o secondary judge for binary REFUSED/FULFILLED.
  *Replication cost:* nominal API cost per problem.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* no — orthogonal to our correctness-prediction pipeline (we have ground-truth MATH-500 labels).

- **Layer-selection ablation grid (early/middle/late/late+middle/all)** — Table 6 protocol, vary `L_apply` while holding `L_input` fixed.
  *Replication cost:* 30min CPU once a controller is trained.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — direct refutation #4 test.

- **Patch-scale-factor sensitivity sweep** — `α_global ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 4.0}` with safety-utility trade-off curve.
  *Replication cost:* 1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes if we run any steering experiment, to avoid Pathway 2's "wrong scale" failure mode.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Direction is fixed; magnitude/allocation is learned" decomposition.** WAS factorizes steering into three independent knobs: behavioral direction (precomputed), global magnitude (per-prompt scalar `s`), and layer allocation (per-layer weights `w_l`). This intersects our F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token) by suggesting the *empirical* DoM directions we extract may be artifacts of fitting different supervision targets at different positions, when one direction with position-dependent magnitudes might be the actual generative model.

- **Discriminative-only training induces interpretable layer attribution as a side-effect.** Their gradient analysis claim — that supervising only `s` produces structured `w_l` patterns via shared hidden layer — is an interesting "free lunch" framing. For us, it suggests probe interpretability may not require explicit attribution losses; just clean discriminative training with the right architectural shape.

- **Safety as a special case of selective behavioral modification.** WAS frames refusal as one instance of "modify behavior on a subset of inputs without touching the rest." This is structurally identical to our "selective prediction" framing in F-8 (selective-pred 71.6% acc at coverage 0.5) — both are gating the model's output on a learned activation classifier. WAS modulates *generation*; F-8 modulates *abstention*. They're two faces of the same control problem.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Controller MLP**: hidden=1024 units, ReLU, input dim = `|L_input| · d_model` (concatenation), output = scalar `s` + `N_L` layer-weight logits with elementwise sigmoid.
- **Input layer set**: `L_input = {l ∈ L | l ≥ 2N_L/3}` (upper third of layers) at last prompt token.
- **Training**: lr=5e-5, batch=4, 4 epochs, grad-clip-norm=1.0, single RTX 4090. λ=0.5 implicit via balanced batch sampling.
- **Patch application**: all transformer-block outputs (`L_apply = L`), all token positions during decode (controller input still from last prompt token only).
- **`α_global = 2.0`** chosen via validation; `α_global = 4.0` over-steers (AlpacaEval drops to 42.1%).
- **`d_steer` construction**: average of refusal-token embeddings minus average of answer-token embeddings from `W_U`, L2-normalized.
- **Evaluation**: keyword pre-filter + GPT-4o LLM-as-judge for refusal binary determination.
- **Code**: https://github.com/Amr-Hegazy1/GuidingGiantsWAS — ready-to-fork harness with the exact MLP and hook implementation.

## 2505.20309 — Guiding Giants: Lightweight Controllers for Weighted Activation Steering in LLMs (Hegazy, Elhoushi, Alanwar, 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct method-cousin of H-1 (per-position DoM steering) and Pathway 2 (spherical steering). WAS introduces a lightweight MLP controller that observes intermediate LLM activations and predicts a global scaling factor `s` plus per-layer weights `w_l` to dynamically modulate a precomputed refusal direction. The "fixed direction + learned controller" decomposition is the missing harness for H-1 and a plausible rescue for Pathway 2's NO-GO. The paper's layer-selection ablation (multi-layer beats single-layer by 12 points for refusal) directly challenges F-2 / F-9's "single L19 layer is enough" framing.

**Key claim we tested:** Multi-layer weighted controller modulation of a fixed steering direction outperforms single-layer or uniform-weight baselines for behavioral control.
