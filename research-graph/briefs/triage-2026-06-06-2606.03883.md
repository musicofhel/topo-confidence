# Triage brief — 2606.03883 — Reasoning Structure of Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03883
Triaged: 2026-06-06

## Refutations

**R1 — Against F-2 (L19 prefill DoM predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731):**
The paper demonstrates that models achieving identical accuracy scores exhibit fundamentally different reasoning *structures* — their reasoning-flow efficiency η varies widely even when final-answer correctness is matched (Figure 6: all models reach ~100% at trivial difficulty but η diverges by 2×). If text-level reasoning-graph topology captures a dimension of "correctness quality" that accuracy alone misses, then a hidden-state direction trained to predict binary correctness (correct/incorrect label) may be fitting a confound: problem difficulty rather than reasoning quality. Specifically, the moderate correlation between η and claim accuracy (r = 0.33, p = 8.9×10⁻⁴) combined with the strong correlation between η and the solution-supporting subgraph fraction (r = 0.58, p = 2×10⁻¹¹) suggests that the *structure* of how a model solves a problem, not just whether it gets the right answer, is where the signal lives. **Falsifiable test:** Extract reasoning graphs from our MATH-500 CoT traces (Qwen-2.5-1.5B, 1024-tok), compute η per problem, and regress η against L19 prefill DoM projection. If η adds significant predictive power for correctness *beyond* DoM (partial correlation η→correctness | DoM ≫ 0), then DoM is missing a structural dimension and F-2's AUROC 0.7731 has a discoverable ceiling-breaker.

**R2 — Against F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy):**
The paper shows that accuracy and reasoning structure are dissociable: "models can be largely correct but still inefficient" (r = 0.33 between η and claim accuracy). This means a DoM-based abstention system (F-8) that selects for high-confidence problems may systematically retain problems where the model gets the right answer by accident via diffuse, inefficient reasoning — correct final answer but fragile reasoning path. The η-based analysis reveals that "failed traces are substantially less efficient (η lower by more than half)" and show "larger, more diffuse graphs with the first error emerging earlier." If the problems that DoM selects as "confident" include a non-trivial fraction of diffuse-but-accidentally-correct traces, then the 71.6% at 50% coverage may be brittle: small perturbations (rephrasing, digit changes) would break the accidentally-correct subset while DoM would still select them. **Falsifiable test:** For the top-50% DoM-selected MATH-500 problems, extract reasoning graphs and compute η. If the η distribution is bimodal (some focused, some diffuse), the diffuse-but-correct subset is the vulnerability surface for F-8.

**R3 — Against F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature):**
The paper finds that the hardest problems (where all models fail — "Human hard" regime, 0% accuracy for Qwen3 235B and DeepSeek V3.2) are characterized by specific *reasoning-graph* pathologies: early first-error depth, low η, diffuse graph topology with high verification overhead (|V_ver|/|V_sol| grows with difficulty). If D-bucket's "collective geometric signature" in hidden states is merely reflecting these text-level structural differences — i.e., the model's hidden state at prefill encodes something about the problem's *difficulty structure* that manifests as distinctive graph topology during generation — then the geometric signature is downstream of reasoning structure, not an independent signal. **Falsifiable test:** For D-bucket problems, compute η from MATH-500 CoT traces and compare against non-D-bucket failures. If D-bucket problems have distinctively low η (matching the paper's finding that harder problems produce more diffuse graphs), then F-7's geometric signature may be a hidden-state proxy for reasoning-graph diffuseness, not an independent geometric phenomenon.

**R4 — Against F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics):**
The paper demonstrates that reasoning structure varies systematically with *puzzle family* and *constraint type* (Figure 3, Table 1: 21 puzzle families spanning placement, connectivity, counting, and Latin-square constraints produce different accuracy and η profiles). Different constraint types require different reasoning structures even at matched difficulty. This suggests that what F-5 calls "content-dependent" breathing might be more precisely "task-structure-dependent" — the hidden-state geometry reflects the *combinatorial structure of the constraint space* rather than semantic content understanding. The paper's Markov-chain formalization of logical flow (absorbing DAG with transition matrix P) provides a mathematical framework for this distinction. **Falsifiable test:** Cluster MATH-500 problems by mathematical subdomain (algebra, geometry, combinatorics, number theory) and check whether L19 prefill DoM projections cluster by domain. If they do, F-5's "content-dependent" claim needs refinement to "domain-structure-dependent."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's η metric operates on CoT traces (text-level), while F-2 operates on hidden states (activation-level). If η and DoM are correlated, it suggests both are proxies for a common latent. If uncorrelated, η offers a complementary correctness predictor that could be combined with DoM to push past 0.7731.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder):** The paper's finding that "accuracy and on-solution reasoning reinforce each other" (r = 0.58 between claim accuracy and |V_sol|/|V|, p = 2×10⁻¹¹) is a text-level analogue of asymmetric collapse: correct solutions concentrate logical flow into the solution subgraph, while incorrect solutions spread flow diffusely. This is structurally parallel to correct trajectories collapsing harder in hidden-state space.

- **F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature):** The paper's "Human hard" regime (0% accuracy across open models) is a text-level D-bucket. Their finding that harder instances produce larger, more diffuse reasoning graphs with earlier first errors directly parallels the D-bucket's distinctive collective geometry.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy):** The paper's η metric could serve as a complementary selective-prediction signal. Since η is uncorrelated with token count (r = −0.05, p = 0.64) but correlated with correctness, combining DoM (hidden-state) + η (trace-structure) might improve coverage or accuracy. However, computing η requires the full CoT trace (post-hoc), so it can't replace prefill-time DoM for early abstention.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):** The paper's difficulty scaling results (accuracy drops from ~70-84% at Trivial to 0-6% at Human hard) combined with the finding that "the hardest regime remains largely unsolved" could inform H-5: prefill DoM may encode problem *decomposability* — whether the constraint structure admits a focused reasoning graph — rather than familiarity.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Reasoning graph extraction pipeline** — Converts free-form CoT traces into directed acyclic claim graphs via deterministic + LLM hybrid extraction (chunking → claim extraction → merge/screen → claim verification → rule extraction → edge set). Uses GPT-5.2 for claim extraction and GPT-5-mini for rule extraction.
  *Replication cost*: 2-4hr implementation + LLM API costs (~$5-20 per 500 traces depending on trace length).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applying to our MATH-500 1024-tok CoT traces would produce reasoning graphs whose topology can be correlated with cached L19 prefill features.

- **Reasoning-flow efficiency η** — η = (log|V| − H_str(G)) / (log|V| − log|C*|), where H_str is the structural entropy of the absorbing Markov chain's logical mass distribution. Measures concentration of logical flow relative to the minimal claim set.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU once graphs are built; needs the graph extraction pipeline first.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — η is the key metric to correlate with DoM.

- **Absorbing Markov chain model of logical flow** — Models the reasoning DAG as an absorbing Markov chain with transition matrix P, computes logical mass m(v) = πN where N is the fundamental matrix. Structural entropy H_str(G) = −Σ (m(v)/‖m‖) log(m(v)/‖m‖).
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU — pure linear algebra on the adjacency matrix of the reasoning graph.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the Markov chain formalization is model-agnostic and could be applied to any DAG, including hypothetical graph structures derived from hidden-state trajectories.

- **Minimal claim set C* identification** — Computes the minimal set of claims needed to reconstruct the solution via the executable puzzle environment. Enables measuring "reasoning waste."
  *Replication cost*: Puzzle-specific (needs executable verifier). For MATH-500, would need a math verification oracle.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — MATH-500 lacks the executable environment for C* computation. Could approximate via solution-step alignment.

- **First-error depth tracking** — Locates the depth in the reasoning graph where the first incorrect claim appears. Correlated with η (r = 0.28, p = 0.015).
  *Replication cost*: Needs claim verification against ground truth.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: partially — could track first error in MATH-500 step-by-step traces if we build a verifier.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning structure as a measurable object distinct from accuracy/token-count.** The paper argues that identical accuracy + token budget hides fundamentally different reasoning quality. This intersects F-2/F-8: our DoM probe predicts binary correctness, but the paper suggests a finer-grained quality spectrum that correctness labels don't capture.

- **Structural entropy of logical flow as an information-theoretic formalization of "focused vs. diffuse reasoning."** The Markov-chain absorbing model provides a principled way to quantify reasoning concentration. This could formalize what F-4 (asymmetric collapse) captures geometrically: correct solutions have low structural entropy (concentrated flow), while incorrect solutions have high structural entropy (diffuse flow).

- **Graph bloat as a failure mode distinct from incorrectness.** The paper identifies that extra tokens translate into verification overhead (|V_ver|/|V_sol|), not into expanded solution paths. This reframes our D-bucket analysis (F-7): D-bucket problems may be those where the model's reasoning graph is structurally incapable of converging, not just those where it gets the wrong answer.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Claim extraction uses GPT-5.2 for semantic extraction and GPT-5-mini for rule extraction; both are behind an API. Open-source alternatives would need calibration.
- Temperature T=1 for all model evaluations (deterministic sampling would suppress reasoning structure variation).
- Extraction pipeline processes traces in token-balanced chunks to satisfy context-length constraints.
- Claims are globally deduplicated by puzzle-specific identifiers; restated claims are linked to first occurrence.
- Pipeline validated with six-extractor ablation: η varies by only 1.9% across extractors (no self-bias).
- Manual inspection: 75.5% of 200 rule applications fully correct under strict criterion.
- η robust to graph perturbations: single-perturbation CV below 5% for 6×6 and larger puzzles.
