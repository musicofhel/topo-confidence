# Triage brief — 2304.14997 — Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Paper: Conmy, Mavor-Parker, Lynch, Heimersheim, Garriga-Alonso. NeurIPS 2023 (spotlight). ACDC = Automated Circuit DisCovery — KL-threshold edge-level activation patching that recovers sparse subgraphs implementing a target behaviour, validated on IOI, docstring, induction, tracr-reverse, tracr-proportion, and greater-than tasks (mostly on GPT-2 Small). Headline result: 68/88 edges of the manually-found greater-than circuit recovered in GPT-2 Small at the right τ; ROC curves over τ trade off precision against recall vs. SP and HISP baselines.

The paper was admitted to the graph on 2026-04-27 as the canonical template for **H-13** (head-level attribution of prefill vs. final-token circuits). Below is the deep-pass refutations-first analysis.

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor) might be distributed, not circuit-localized.**
   - Mechanism: ACDC presumes a behavior is implemented by a sparse subgraph of edges. If we run ACDC with the prefill-DoM logit as the metric on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (taking "behavior" = activation along the prefill DoM direction at L19), and ACDC cannot prune below ~50% of edges without exceeding the KL threshold, then the prefill direction is a *population code* over many heads, not the output of a localized circuit. F-2's framing ("a single direction... that predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731") would survive at the *probe* level but the "circuit-discovered handle" interpretation under H-13 would collapse — there's nothing crisp to causally edit.
   - Falsifiable: run ACDC at multiple τ on the cached prefill task; report fraction of edges retained at the τ that preserves KL ≤ 0.05. If the curve has no knee and stays >50% retention down to τ=10⁻⁴, F-2 is distributed.

2. **F-3 (prefill DoM ⊥ final-token DoM, cos = 0.046) might be circuit-level *non-orthogonal*.**
   - Mechanism: cosine orthogonality of the two directions in residual-stream coordinates does *not* imply distinct head sets. ACDC head-overlap analysis on (prefill-DoM-task) vs. (final-token-DoM-task) could reveal that the same MLPs/heads contribute to both — orthogonality would then be a *coordinate* artifact (the heads write into different residual-stream subspaces *because of layernorm + write-projection geometry*, not because two independent circuits exist). F-3's narrative claim that "the model uses two computationally distinct mechanisms" is what would be refuted, not the cos number itself.
   - Falsifiable: run ACDC twice with two metrics, intersect the recovered edge sets. If Jaccard ≥ 0.6 of the heads/edges are shared, the "two independent processes" framing in F-3 is wrong.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) may not survive circuit-level inspection.**
   - Mechanism: F-7's "collective" framing comes from population-level PCA / cluster geometry in the L19 prefill subspace. ACDC localized to the small D-bucket subset (36 problems) might find essentially the same circuit as A-bucket (207 problems) — perhaps with a single attention head differing in its query-side dependence. If so, F-7 should be reframed as "D-bucket = A-bucket circuit + one specific head failing" rather than "D-bucket has its own signature." That single failing head would also become the most testable causal anchor.
   - Falsifiable: ACDC on A-bucket and D-bucket separately, compare recovered subgraphs. If symmetric difference ≤ 5 edges, the "collective signature" narrative collapses.

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) might be wrong at the circuit level even when right at the probe level.**
   - Mechanism: CoE-60 features are redundant for AUROC because they are downstream of the same L19 DoM signal at the probe level. But at the circuit level, the heads that *drive* CoE-60 across layers might be a strict superset of heads that drive L19-only DoM — i.e. CoE-60 has more upstream causal levers. ACDC with CoE-60 as the metric vs. L19-DoM as the metric, on the same problem set, could expose this. If CoE-60's recovered circuit has 2× the edges and is broader across layers, F-9's redundancy claim should be qualified to "redundant for prediction, distinct for intervention."
   - Falsifiable: ACDC at matched τ on both tasks, report edge-set sizes and layer-spread.

5. **H-13 itself (head-level circuits exist for prefill vs. final-token DoM) may be unfalsifiable as currently scoped.**
   - Mechanism: ACDC gives ROC curves, not yes/no answers. The paper notes that ACDC frequently returns "long thin trees" rather than well-bounded subgraphs. If H-13 is run and the recovered circuits are >70% of edges at any KL-preserving τ, no head-level claim is licensed — but H-13's success criterion isn't currently stated in those terms. Refines H-13's pre-registration: must declare upfront the τ and edge-fraction below which we count the result as "circuit found." Without that, we will pattern-match into a positive result regardless of what ACDC returns (the Ríos-García 2604.18805 failure mode).

## Methodologies extracted

- **ACDC algorithm (edge-level KL-threshold pruning)** — start from full computational graph, iteratively try to ablate each edge (replace activation with the corrupted-baseline activation from a paired counterfactual prompt); if KL(original ‖ ablated) ≤ τ, prune the edge; otherwise keep. Topological order matters; algorithm is roughly O(|edges| · forward-pass).
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for Qwen-2.5-1.5B at the prefill-DoM task — the residual-stream graph at L19 has ~28 layers × (12 attn heads + MLP) ≈ ~1000 nodes, edges between them O(10⁶). Runtime ≈ #edges × forward-pass-cost; with KV-caching and the mech-interp library `transformer_lens` it's tractable but not cheap.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — H-13 is registered as foundational, and FE3 is already in our queue at ROI 9. ACDC formalizes the same intuition into a systematic protocol.

- **Edge-level (vs node-level) activation patching** — instead of patching the output of an entire attention head, patch the contribution from head A to head B specifically (i.e. break individual edges in the QKV-attention-output residual graph). Critical for precision: node patching can confound "this head matters" with "any head it routes to matters."
  *Replication cost*: requires `transformer_lens` (HookedTransformer) or equivalent hook-based forward-pass instrumentation. Already implemented in their public repo (github.com/ArthurConmy/Automatic-Circuit-Discovery). 1-2 days to port to Qwen.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — once H-13/ACDC is on the agenda, edge-level granularity is the right default.

- **KL-divergence as the behavior-preservation metric** — symmetric KL on the model's output distribution between original and ablated forward pass. Threshold τ is the only ACDC hyperparameter that matters.
  *Replication cost*: trivial when piggy-backing on ACDC.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.

- **Counterfactual-prompt corrupted baseline** — for IOI: swap names; for greater-than: swap years. Choice of baseline materially affects what "matters." For our prefill task, baseline candidates include (a) random other MATH-500 prompt, (b) shuffled prompt, (c) zero-mean activation; choice is non-trivial and warrants its own short experiment.
  *Replication cost*: design + brief sensitivity sweep, maybe 1 H100 hour.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sensitivity to baseline choice is a known ACDC weakness worth checking.

- **SP (Subnetwork Probing, Cao et al. 2021) baseline** — learn a continuous mask over edges via gradient descent, then threshold. Compared to ACDC on ROC curves over recovered-circuit precision/recall.
  *Replication cost*: their codebase has it; ~1 day to port.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if ACDC results are ambiguous and we want a second method.

- **HISP (Head Importance Score for Pruning) baseline** — single-pass head-importance scoring using gradient × activation. Cheaper than ACDC; the paper shows ACDC dominates HISP in ROC at high precision.
  *Replication cost*: ~half a day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, as a cheap initial pass before committing to full ACDC.

- **ROC curve over τ for circuit-recovery evaluation** — vary τ from 0 (keep everything) to ∞ (ablate everything), plot precision vs. recall against the manual ground-truth circuit. Lets you compare methods on the same axis.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the right way to report H-13 results and forces us to pre-register a τ.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Circuit" as a sparse subgraph of the computational graph that preserves a behavior under ablation.** Reframes our F-2/F-3 probe-level findings: "AUROC 0.7731" describes a *direction in residual-stream space*; ACDC asks for the *causal subgraph that writes into that direction*. These are non-equivalent — a probe can succeed at AUROC 0.7731 even if no compact circuit exists (e.g., the signal is a population code).
- **Behavior = (metric, dataset, units of investigation).** Forces us to be explicit. For our project: metric = projection onto prefill DoM at L19 token-final position; dataset = MATH-500 prefill activations; units = attention heads × MLPs in {L0..L19}. The paper's discipline of fixing all three before running ACDC is exactly the kind of pre-registration that would defend against the Ríos-García 2604.18805 failure mode — no fishing for a circuit by retroactively redefining the metric.
- **Edges, not nodes, as the right granularity.** Aligns with our F-3 framing of orthogonal directions: edges into the residual stream from different heads might write into orthogonal subspaces, and edge-level inspection is the only granularity that surfaces this.
- **Activation patching as the workhorse of mech-interp**, replacing or augmenting probes. Suggests a longer-term reframing of the project: probes (DoM AUROCs) are diagnostic, but interventions (patches) are the unit of explanation.

None of the paper's datasets are directly applicable to our 1024-tok regime / Qwen-2.5-1.5B pipeline. The methods transfer; the data does not.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Code**: github.com/ArthurConmy/Automatic-Circuit-Discovery (referenced in abstract). Built on `transformer_lens`. MIT license.
- **`transformer_lens` HookedTransformer** is the prerequisite library — adds named-hook instrumentation to a HF model. Qwen-2.5 is supported in recent versions of `transformer_lens`; would need verification for the 1.5B variant specifically.
- **τ sweep ranges** (from paper figures) span ~10⁻⁵ to ~10⁰ in KL units. The "right τ" for IOI is roughly 10⁻³ in their headline figure. Our task may sit at a different scale.
- **Topological-order edge iteration** is the algorithmic core — without correct ordering, downstream pruning decisions become incoherent. Their library handles this.
- **Memory footprint** scales with KV-cache × prompts; for 1024-tok MATH problems on Qwen-1.5B this fits on a single H100 with care.
- **Gotcha: ACDC returns "long thin trees" frequently** — i.e. it traces a single path but doesn't prune redundancies. The paper acknowledges this is a known limitation and motivates the SP baseline. We should pre-commit to reporting both edge count and graph diameter.
- **Counterfactual baseline choice** is the most impactful undocumented hyperparameter. For our prefill-DoM task, default would be "swap to random other MATH-500 prefill" but this needs a sensitivity check.

So the "right now" sanity check on this paper's headline claim is: replicate ACDC on greater-than (GPT-2 Small) using their public code and reproduce 68/88 edge recovery — purely as a port-validation step before pointing it at our task.
