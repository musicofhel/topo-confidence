# Triage brief — 2605.29548 — Why Larger Models Learn More: Effects of Capacity, Interference, and Rare-Task Retention

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29548
Triaged: 2026-06-09

Authors: Jing Huang, Daniel Wurgaft, Rachit Bansal, Laura Ruis, Naomi Saphra, David Alvarez-Melis, Andrew Lampinen, Christopher Potts, Ekdeep Singh Lubana (Goodfire / Stanford / Kempner@Harvard / MIT / Anthropic), 2026.

**One-line summary.** A data-centric learning-dynamics account of why larger models learn rare/complex tasks that smaller ones cannot, even with infinite data: features are learned in order of *utility* `u_{k,j}=π_k·λ_{k,j}` (task frequency × per-task covariance eigenvalue); a width-N model spans the top-N eigenspace of the mixture covariance `M=Σ π_k C_k`; once common-task features are captured their gradients weaken (residual `δ_F=Tr((I−P_U)M_F)` controls learning), freeing capacity for rare-task features that wider models can then *retain* across sparse observations instead of overwriting them. Validated on a toy multi-task linear-regression setup and on OLMo 4M→4B pretraining.

**Caveat up front — domain mismatch.** This is a *training-dynamics / pretraining-scaling* paper. Our program is *inference-time geometry on a frozen, fully-trained model*. None of its mechanisms (gradient interference, rare-task retention over batches) act at our inference timescale. The value is its *representational framework* — covariance spectra, top-N eigenspace, projection-captured signal, utility ranking — which maps directly onto our cached L19 covariance matrices and onto the 1.5B-vs-7B comparison (48.6% vs 73.2% MATH-500 K=1). Refutations below are framed at that representational level, not at the (inapplicable) training-dynamics level.

## Refutations

**R1 — Refutes the F-6 mechanism ("7B prefill PR inversion is driven by incorrect-group concentration").**
The paper supplies a *competing* mechanism for the entire 1.5B-vs-7B geometric divergence: utility ranking `u=π·λ` predicts the smaller model spends its limited width on high-frequency / low-complexity problem types and structurally fails to embed features for rare/complex problems, while the larger model retains them. *Mechanism that fires:* if F-6's PR inversion is really a capacity-allocation effect, then the 7B's L19 prefill covariance should carry strictly *more* low-utility (small-eigenvalue) effective directions than the 1.5B — a fatter spectral tail / higher participation ratio — and the "incorrect-group concentration" F-6 attributes to verification strength would instead be the *common-task* high-utility bulk dominating the 1.5B. *Falsifiable experiment:* compute participation ratio / effective rank of the L19 prefill covariance for 1.5B vs 7B on MATH-500 (cached: `math500_7b/*.npz` + 1.5B cache); if the 7B's advantage is a fatter low-utility tail rather than incorrect-group concentration, F-6's mechanism is displaced. (See P11-FE130601.)

**R2 — Refutes the sufficiency framing of F-2 + F-9 ("a single L19 DoM direction predicts correctness; CoE/extra directions are redundant").**
Theorem 3 says the *top* eigendirections of a covariance carry the high-frequency / common-task signal; discriminative information for rare/hard instances lives in *low-utility* (small-eigenvalue) directions. F-2's DoM ≈ PC1 (cos 0.9216) is, on this account, the single highest-utility direction — i.e. it encodes the *common/easy* problem structure, not correctness per se — and must systematically miss the rare/hard tail (the D-bucket). *Mechanism that fires:* PC1 = top-utility common-task feature ⇒ its AUROC should collapse on the hard/rare stratum while residual directions carry the signal there. *Falsifiable experiment:* stratify MATH-500 by difficulty level (proxy for task rarity/complexity); if F-2's PC1-only AUROC (0.7731 pooled) drops sharply on the hardest stratum *and* the PC1-residualized cov-spectrum recovers it, "single direction is enough" is refuted for the tail. Note this is *already weakly corroborated by our own FE881*: PC1-residualized cov-spectrum lifts 0.7731→**0.7928**, i.e. the residual directions demonstrably carry extra signal — exactly what the paper predicts and what a strict reading of F-9's "redundant" claim denies. (See P11-FE130602.)

**R3 — Refutes the structural interpretation of F-3 ("prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal *structurally, not positionally*", cos 0.046).**
The capacity/interference lens offers a deflationary alternative: a width-limited model is *forced* to park weakly-related signals in orthogonal low-utility subspaces because it lacks capacity to co-align them; reduced interference at larger width should let the two directions partially align. *Mechanism that fires:* if F-3's orthogonality is a small-model capacity artifact rather than a structural property, `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)` should rise materially from ~0.046 at 1.5B toward a non-trivial value at 7B. *Falsifiable experiment:* recompute prefill and final-token DoM on the cached 7B states (`math500_7b/*.npz` carry full `states`) and measure their cosine; if 7B alignment ≫ 0.046, F-3's "structural orthogonality" is downgraded to a capacity artifact. (See P11-FE130603.) This is the sharpest refutation because we hold the 7B activations already.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Utility ranking `u_{k,j}=π_k·λ_{k,j}`** — ranks every (task, feature) pair by frequency × per-task covariance eigenvalue; the width-N optimum keeps the top-N by utility.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min CPU — needs a stratification of MATH-500 into "tasks" (difficulty levels join from HF MATH metadata) and per-stratum L19 covariances.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly maps our PC1/DoM onto a frequency-weighted utility coordinate and tests R2.
- **Projection-captured signal `s_k(U)=Tr(P_U C_k)/Tr(C_k)`, normalized `s̃_k=(s_k−N/d)/(1−N/d)`** — fraction of task-k variance captured by an N-dim subspace, baselined against a random Stiefel projection.
  *Replication cost*: ~20min CPU on cached covariances.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a principled "how much of the correct-vs-incorrect covariance does the top-N DoM/PC subspace explain" metric, sharper than raw PR.
- **Residual signal `δ_F(U)=Tr((I−P_U)M_F)` ("residual controls learning")** — unexplained variance after projecting out the top-N subspace; a residual-vs-N curve.
  *Replication cost*: ~15min CPU — reuses `cov_spectrum/eigval_cache.npz`.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a residual-decay curve for the correct vs incorrect groups is a cheap new view on F-7.
- **Participation ratio / effective rank of mixture covariance `M=Σ π_k C_k`** — counts effective dimensions; the paper's core "width buys low-utility tail" claim.
  *Replication cost*: ~10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the central 1.5B-vs-7B test for R1.
- **Distributed Alignment Search (DAS)** — learns a rotation to localize a *causal* low-dim subspace encoding a target feature (used to find the 1-D comparison-order direction and Fourier modes).
  *Replication cost*: needs gradient-based subspace fitting on cached states + a labelled causal target — ~1–2h CPU per target, more setup than the others.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no (this pass) — heavier, and our correctness label isn't a clean causal intervention target like modular addition; park as a possible H100/causal follow-up.
- **Gradient-interference cosine `cos(g, g_r)` decomposed into task-token vs non-task-token gradients** — measures whether language-modeling gradients overwrite task gradients.
  *Replication cost*: requires fresh backward passes (training-time quantity).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training-dynamics quantity, out of scope for our frozen-inference regime.

## Approaches & framings

- **Utility = frequency × eigenvalue as the master ordering of feature learning.** Reframes "what does width buy" as "which low-utility directions get capacity." Intersects F-2/F-6/F-7: invites us to coordinate the DoM/PC1 not just by variance (eigenvalue) but by a frequency-weighted utility — a lens we have not applied.
- **"Residual controls learning" (gradient norm bounded by unexplained variance).** Conceptual move: once the bulk is explained, the remaining signal lives in the tail. Even at inference this reframes our PC1-residualization (FE881) as "reading the residual," giving a principled reason the residualized probe beats raw PC1.
- **Memorization-as-substrate-for-abstraction (retention enables rare-task generalization).** A reframing of memorization as *beneficial*; weakly intersects our D-bucket / "problems no amount of self-consistency rescues" framing (H-913 family) — the hardest problems may be exactly the low-frequency tail a 1.5B cannot retain.
- **Learnable-via-data-scaling vs learnable-via-model-scaling (Defs 1–2).** Sharp distinction between "undertrained" and "fundamentally capacity-bounded." If our 1.5B's D-bucket is the latter, no decoding-time trick (more K, steering) closes it — directly relevant to selective-prediction ceiling claims (F-8).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Utility metric is purely spectral: build per-stratum covariance `C_k`, weight by stratum frequency `π_k`, form `M=Σ π_k C_k`, eigendecompose, rank by `π_k λ_{k,j}`. No training needed — applies to any cached activation matrix.
- Normalization baseline `N/d` (expected captured fraction of a random N-dim Stiefel projection in d dims) is the right null for "is the top-N subspace capturing more than chance" — cleaner than our ad-hoc PR baselines. For us d=1536; N = number of retained PCs.
- Toy setup: shared width-N encoder `U` (U^T U = I), closed-form optimal decoder `D_k* = Λ_k^{1/2} B_k^T U`, so only encoder dynamics matter — i.e. the *subspace* is the whole story, matching our DoM-as-direction framing.
- 100K Adam steps suffice in the toy setup (loss flat beyond, even at 10×).
- No public code link surfaced in the HTML; figures/appendices (App. B.4 localization, B.5 task-neuron localization, C.4 one-neuron two-task model) hold the method details.
