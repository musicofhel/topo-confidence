# Triage brief — 2306.04488 — Rewarded soups: towards Pareto-optimal alignment by interpolating weights fine-tuned on diverse rewards

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04488
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

1. **Against F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core finding is that *multiple* independently-trained objectives remain linearly connected in weight space from a shared initialization. If the same linear-connectivity property holds in *activation direction space* — i.e., if one could train separate DoM directions for different correctness sub-objectives (e.g., arithmetic vs. reasoning steps) and interpolate them — it would refute the implicit assumption that a *single* DoM direction is the optimal readout. Mechanism: train 2+ DoM variants on stratified problem types, interpolate, check if interpolated AUROC > 0.7731. Falsifiable by: if interpolated direction never exceeds single-DoM AUROC, F-2's single-direction framing holds.

2. **Against H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy):** Rewarded Soups shows that weight interpolation achieves Pareto-optimal trade-offs across *competing* rewards. If steering with the DoM direction implicitly trades off multiple latent objectives (fluency vs. correctness vs. calibration), then H-1's single-vector steering assumption may be inadequate — you'd need a *soup of steering directions* interpolated at inference. Mechanism: if adding the DoM steering vector improves correctness but degrades calibration (ECE), it would indicate the Pareto multi-direction formulation is needed. Falsifiable by: measuring ECE before/after DoM steering; if ECE unchanged or improved, single-vector suffices.

3. **Against F-8 (selective prediction via prefill DoM works at 50% coverage / 71.6% accuracy):** The paper demonstrates that averaging across reward-specialized models improves over any single model. Applied to our pipeline: if one trains separate abstention predictors on different sub-populations (easy/hard, short/long, topic-split) and interpolates their predictions, the resulting selective predictor might dominate F-8's single-threshold approach. Mechanism: train K fold-stratified abstention classifiers, interpolate their logits, check if coverage-accuracy curve dominates F-8's. Falsifiable by: if interpolated ensemble ≤ single-DoM selective curve at all coverage levels.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The DoM direction is trained on a single binary reward (correct/incorrect). Rewarded Soups suggests that *decomposing* this binary signal into finer-grained reward components (partial correctness, reasoning quality, numeric accuracy) and training separate directions could yield better Pareto coverage of the correctness space. The key question: does LMC hold for directions in activation space as it does in weight space?

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** The near-orthogonality of prefill and final-token directions is reminiscent of directions trained on *antagonistic* rewards in the paper (Figure 15). The paper shows LMC breaks down for antagonistic objectives — this provides a theoretical lens for why prefill/final directions don't compose additively (they optimized different "rewards" in different computational regimes).

- **F-10 (PH adds negative signal beyond covariance):** Tangential. The paper operates entirely in weight space, not topology space.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs. decomposability):** If prefill DoM conflates two latent rewards (familiarity and decomposability), the Rewarded Soups framework predicts that training separate probes and interpolating would yield a tighter Pareto front than the single merged signal.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Linear weight interpolation for multi-objective alignment** — Fine-tune N models on N rewards from shared init, then compute θ_final = Σᵢ λᵢ·θᵢ where λ ∈ simplex. Proves Pareto optimality under linear mode connectivity.
  *Replication cost*: Not directly applicable (requires RL fine-tuning), but the *interpolation principle* applied to cached probe directions costs ~10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — not weight interpolation per se, but interpolation of trained DoM/probe directions in activation space is a direct 10-min CPU analogue.

- **Hypervolume metric for Pareto front evaluation** — Standard multi-objective optimization metric quantifying dominated volume. Used to compare Pareto fronts of different methods.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (scipy or pymoo implementation on cached probe outputs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could use hypervolume to evaluate multi-probe direction interpolation against single-DoM baseline.

- **Simplex uniform sampling for coefficient search** — Sample M points from N-dimensional simplex, evaluate each, pick best. Lightweight alternative to grid search for interpolation coefficients.
  *Replication cost*: trivial, <5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct utility if we interpolate multiple directions.

## Approaches & framings

- **Multi-policy over single-policy alignment:** Instead of optimizing one objective and accepting trade-offs, train independent specialists and interpolate. This reframes our single-DoM approach: correctness prediction might benefit from decomposition into sub-objectives.

- **Linear mode connectivity as a geometric guarantee:** The finding that fine-tuned weights remain linearly connected from a shared initialization provides a theoretical framework for asking: do directions in activation space (DoM, PC1, spectral features) remain linearly connected when trained on sub-populations? This is a transferable geometric insight.

- **Pareto Coverage Set (PCS) as evaluation criterion:** Rather than optimizing a single AUROC number, we could evaluate our probe's coverage of the full correctness-confidence Pareto front. This shifts the question from "how high is AUROC?" to "how much of the preference space do we cover?"

## Replicable intermediates

- **Direction interpolation sanity check:** We have cached DoM direction (L19, 1536-d) and PC1 direction. Compute interpolated direction d(λ) = λ·DoM + (1-λ)·PC1 (normalized), sweep λ ∈ [0,1], evaluate AUROC at each step. If AUROC is concave in λ (i.e., some interpolant beats both endpoints), it validates the LMC analogy in direction space. Uses: `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` cached NPZs.
- **Multi-fold direction interpolation:** Train DoM on 5 cross-validation folds (already done for OOF AUROC). Instead of averaging predictions, *average the directions* and evaluate. If averaged-direction AUROC ≈ OOF AUROC, it confirms direction-space LMC.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2306.04488 — Rewarded soups: towards Pareto-optimal alignment by interpolating weights fine-tuned on diverse rewards (Ramé, Couairon, Shukor, Dancette, Gaya, Soulier, Cord, 2023)

**Relevance:** Introduces linear mode connectivity (LMC) in weight space as a geometric property of fine-tuned models from shared initialization. While the paper operates in weight space (not activation/direction space), the core principle — that independently-trained objectives remain linearly connected from a shared starting point — directly motivates testing whether our DoM directions, PC1, and spectral features (all derived from the same L19 activations) exhibit analogous LMC in direction space. The hypervolume Pareto metric and simplex sampling are directly applicable to our multi-probe evaluation.

**Key claim we tested:** Weights fine-tuned on diverse rewards from a shared pre-trained initialization remain linearly connected (LMC), enabling Pareto-optimal interpolation.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — The weight-space LMC claim is not directly testable with our pipeline, but the analogous direction-space LMC hypothesis (P11-FE130488) can be tested on cached NPZs. If DoM and PC1 interpolation yields concave AUROC curves, it would validate the transfer of LMC from weight space to activation direction space.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE130488, P11-FE130489, P11-FE130490, H-860, H-861
