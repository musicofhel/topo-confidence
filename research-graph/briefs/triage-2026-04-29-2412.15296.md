# Triage brief — 2412.15296 — Confidence in the Reasoning of Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15296
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Yudi Pawitan, Chris Holmes (December 2024).
Models: GPT-4o, GPT-4-turbo, Mistral (closed-source / API).
Benchmarks: BIG-Bench causal-judgment, BIG-Bench formal-fallacies, plus a
custom probability/statistical-puzzles set.
Probes: behavioral only — *no* hidden-state, residual-stream, or attention
probing. Confidence is measured (i) qualitatively as persistence under a
"please reconsider" reprompt, and (ii) quantitatively as a self-reported
0–100 score. Headline conclusion: "current LLMs do not have any
internally coherent sense of confidence" — second-pass accuracy is often
*worse* than first-pass; verbal confidence is overstated; verbal
confidence is only partially explained by token-level probability.

## Refutations

1. **F-8 selective-prediction-at-50%-coverage premise is challenged by the
   "second answer often worse than first" effect.**
   F-8 reports 71.6% accuracy on the answered half (K=2.5 average) by
   gating low-prefill-DoM problems for additional sampling. Pawitan &
   Holmes find that prompted second passes *reduce* accuracy on multiple
   reasoning benchmarks. If the same effect holds on MATH-500's
   prefill-DoM-flagged low-confidence half, the F-8 gain is at most a
   K-budget effect (more samples), not genuine *recoverability*.
   *Mechanism*: the model's low-confidence problems are not low-confidence
   because of fixable noise — they are low-confidence because the model
   actually doesn't know, so neither reprompting nor extra sampling
   recovers the answer; F-8's 71.6% would then be inflated by the K=1
   already-correct mass propagating through.
   *Falsifiable test*: run their persistence prompt ("Are you sure?
   Please reconsider.") on the bottom-50% prefill-DoM half of MATH-500
   for Qwen-2.5-1.5B. If reconsidered-answer accuracy on that subset is
   ≤ first-pass accuracy on the same subset, F-8's recoverability
   premise weakens; the gain must then be re-attributed to sampling
   variance under K=2.5.

2. **F-2 cross-domain generality is challenged.**
   F-2 establishes prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731 on MATH-500 only.
   Pawitan & Holmes evaluate qualitatively *different* reasoning tasks
   (causal judgment, formal fallacies, probability puzzles) where token
   probability — and by extension residual-stream "knows-the-answer"
   geometry — may not align with correctness the way it does in
   arithmetic/algebra.
   *Mechanism*: residual streams in math reasoning carry a
   computation-trace whose terminal state is a near-deterministic
   numerical answer (high token-probability when correct). On
   causal-judgment / fallacy tasks, "correct" is less often a
   high-probability completion of a tightly-constrained sequence — so
   the prefill-DoM "I know this" geometry decouples from accuracy.
   *Falsifiable test*: extract Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill L19 activations on
   BIG-Bench causal-judgment + formal-fallacies (HF-available).
   Train 5-fold OOF DoM. If AUROC < 0.65 on either, F-2's framing as a
   *general* correctness predictor (not just MATH-500-specific) is
   refuted.

3. **H-12 (label-free semantic-entropy probes can replace supervised DoM)
   is challenged if internal confidence inherits the verbal-confidence
   incoherence.**
   The paper's central conclusion — "no internally coherent sense of
   confidence" — if extended from verbal to internal state, predicts
   that label-free probes (SEPs) reading out the model's
   self-knowledge circuit will show the *same* miscalibration that
   verbal confidence shows. H-12 implicitly assumes there is a coherent
   internal "I know this" signal that SEPs can extract without labels;
   Pawitan & Holmes argue against the existence of such a coherent
   signal at all.
   *Mechanism*: if the broken verbal-confidence circuit and the residual
   "knows-the-answer" geometry share the same underlying representation,
   any unsupervised probe of the latter will inherit the broken
   calibration of the former — including the systematic overstatement.
   *Falsifiable test*: train SEP probe on Qwen 1.5B at L19 (label-free,
   per Farquhar et al.). Compare AUROC to supervised prefill DoM
   (0.7731). If SEP AUROC ≤ 0.65 on MATH-500 *and* fails on
   causal-judgment, H-12's premise that label-free internal coherence
   exists is contradicted.

4. **H-1 per-position DoM steering is challenged by the
   prompted-intervention-worsens-accuracy effect.**
   Pawitan & Holmes show that the simplest model-internal intervention —
   prompting reconsideration — *degrades* performance. By analogy,
   pushing residual streams along the prefill-DoM direction (H-1's
   proposed steering) may degrade rather than improve, especially on
   borderline-confidence problems where the steering crosses the
   decision boundary in the wrong direction.
   *Mechanism*: H-1 implicitly assumes that "more along the
   correctness-direction = more correct." Pawitan & Holmes' finding is
   evidence that confidence-coupled interventions are non-monotone:
   high-confidence-already problems are unaffected, low-confidence
   problems get pushed past the cliff into wrongness.
   *Falsifiable test*: H-1's planned steering experiment must include
   (a) +DoM steering, (b) −DoM steering, (c) random-direction same-norm
   perturbation, all at three magnitudes. If +DoM and random show
   indistinguishable Δ-accuracy, H-1's claim that the DoM direction is
   *causally* correctness-relevant collapses.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** — the paper's "confidence is
  only partially explained by token-level probability" finding bears
  directly: if prefill DoM is itself partially captured by token-prob,
  the AUROC inherits the calibration ceiling Pawitan & Holmes identify.
  This sets a concrete quantitative ceiling to investigate (do
  token-prob baselines reach 0.6X? What's the residual signal?).
- **F-8 (selective-prediction acc = 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** — the
  "second answer often worse" effect motivates a control: F-8's gain
  must be decomposed into "K=2.5 sampling variance" vs "actually-recovered
  answers under reconsideration." Without that decomposition, F-8 is
  consistent with the paper's pessimistic view.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free for refusal)** — the
  paper's "no internally coherent confidence" claim *directly opposes*
  the implicit assumption of label-free internal coherence in H-12.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — the paper's evidence that
  prompted self-intervention degrades performance is an existence proof
  worth pre-registering against in H-1's design.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Persistence reprompt** — after the model's first answer, send a
  follow-up prompt of the form "Are you sure? Please reconsider." Record
  whether the answer changes. Use change-rate as a behavioral
  confidence proxy.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min H100 for Qwen 1.5B on existing 500
  MATH-500 K=1 greedy problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of refutation #1, gives
  a label-free behavioral confidence signal we can correlate with
  prefill DoM.

- **Self-reported confidence elicitation** — append "On a scale of
  0–100, how confident are you?" to the prompt; parse the integer.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min H100 on the same 500 MATH-500 problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a "verbal confidence"
  baseline to contrast with prefill DoM AUROC at problem level. Should
  reveal whether the residual-stream signal captures information not
  present in verbal confidence.

- **Token-level probability vs confidence correlation** — they compute
  the underlying token probability of the model's answer and regress
  self-reported confidence on it. We can do the same with prefill DoM
  score in place of self-reported confidence.
  *Replication cost*: <10min CPU on cached logprobs (we already have
  K=1 greedy generations on H100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — concrete way to ask "is prefill
  DoM essentially a re-skinning of token-prob, or does it carry
  independent signal?"

## Approaches & framings

- **"Internally coherent sense of confidence" framing.** Decomposes
  confidence into multiple measurable channels (verbal, token-prob,
  reconsideration-stability) and asks whether they cohere. Useful for
  our project: it suggests adding "prefill DoM" as a fourth channel
  and asking whether it coheres with the other three or stands apart
  as a label-required supervised signal. Sharpens H-12's framing —
  label-free coherence is something the paper *denies*.
- **Refutation-by-intervention.** Treats prompted reconsideration as a
  probe of confidence robustness. Analogue for our work: per-position
  DoM steering as a probe of whether the residual-stream confidence
  representation is causal vs correlational (this is exactly P11-FE3,
  but the framing — "intervention-worsens-accuracy is evidence
  *against* a coherent internal signal" — is reusable).

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **BIG-Bench causal-judgment** — ~200 multi-choice causal-reasoning
  questions. HF-available. *Applicable*: yes — primary cross-domain
  test for refutation #2 (does prefill DoM AUROC hold off-MATH-500?).
- **BIG-Bench formal-fallacies** — ~250 logical-fallacy multi-choice
  questions. HF-available. *Applicable*: yes — second cross-domain
  test for refutation #2.
- **Probability/statistical puzzles set** — custom curation by the
  authors; not clearly released as a dataset. *Applicable*: no
  unless they release it; even then, small n and mostly numeric so
  too close to MATH-500 to be a strong cross-domain probe.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- All three evaluated models (GPT-4o, GPT-4-turbo, Mistral) are accessed
  via API or are hosted; the paper does not run open-weights with
  internal-state extraction. Therefore *no* hidden-state activations,
  attention patterns, or residual streams are reported — none of their
  numerical results are directly comparable to our Qwen 1.5B/7B
  L19-based numbers.
- Persistence-prompt format is a single follow-up turn; they do not
  run multi-round Socratic dialogue. Replicating it is a one-extra-call
  experiment.
- They check correlation against token-level probability of the answer
  — implies they have model-supplied logprobs (so Mistral was likely
  run open-weights or via a logprob-returning API). For our pipeline,
  we already have logprobs cached on H100 generations.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Persistence reprompt on Qwen 1.5B MATH-500.** Use cached K=1
  greedy answers from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`. Send
  one extra turn ("Are you sure? Please reconsider.") per problem,
  parse second answer. Compute per-problem persistence flag and
  correlate with prefill DoM score. Direct replication of paper's
  central qualitative-confidence experiment, with the addition of our
  internal-state correlate.
- **Token-prob baseline for prefill DoM.** From cached K=1 generations,
  compute mean log-prob of the answer span per problem (already in
  output JSONs). Train a simple logreg on token-prob alone, compute
  AUROC, compare to F-2's 0.7731 prefill DoM AUROC. Quantifies
  "partially explained by token-prob" for *our* internal signal.
- **Verbal-confidence elicitation on same.** Append confidence question
  to a fresh K=1 pass. Compare verbal-confidence AUROC vs prefill DoM
  AUROC at problem level. Numbers we can run without new GPU time
  (~30min H100 once a pod is up).

## Proposed HYPOTHESES.md additions

### H-96: Prefill DoM AUROC drops below 0.65 on non-math reasoning benchmarks
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2412.15296 + F-2
**Test:** Generate K=1 Qwen-2.5-1.5B answers on BIG-Bench causal-judgment
(~200 q) and formal-fallacies (~250 q). Extract prefill L19 activations
through the existing P11 pipeline. Train 5-fold OOF DoM. Compare AUROC
to F-2's MATH-500 baseline 0.7731.
**Requires:** H100 (~1h generation) + CPU (~30min probe training).
Open-weights only — closed-source models in the source paper are not
required for this test.
**Would change:** If AUROC < 0.65 on either, F-2 should be re-scoped
from "prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor" to "...on
math reasoning specifically." If AUROC ≥ 0.7 on both, F-2's
generality is *strengthened* against the paper's pessimism — a
positive-result outcome that materially extends F-2.
**Blocks:** H-12 (cross-domain SEP comparison meaningful only after
this baseline lands).

### H-97: Prefill DoM residual-after-token-prob AUROC > 0.7 (i.e. token-prob does not subsume prefill DoM)
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2412.15296 + F-2 + EXP that establishes 0.7731
**Test:** From cached K=1 MATH-500 greedy generations, compute mean
log-prob of the predicted answer span per problem. Fit single-feature
logreg on token-prob alone, AUROC. Then orthogonalize prefill-DoM
features against token-prob (residualize via OLS), retrain DoM,
recompute AUROC. Three numbers: token-prob-alone AUROC,
prefill-DoM-alone AUROC (= 0.7731), residualized-prefill-DoM AUROC.
**Requires:** CPU only (~20min). All inputs cached.
**Would change:** If residualized AUROC < 0.6, prefill DoM is largely
a re-skin of token-prob and F-2's significance is overstated — would
reframe the entire pathway-2-vs-pathway-11 story. If residualized
AUROC ≥ 0.7, prefill DoM carries genuinely new structural information
beyond logprobs, sharpening the case for residual-stream geometry as
an independent correctness channel.
**Blocks:** any future framing of prefill DoM as "more than logprobs."

### H-98: Reconsidered-answer accuracy on prefill-DoM-flagged low-confidence MATH-500 is ≤ first-pass accuracy
**Priority:** MEDIUM
**Motivated by:** 2412.15296 + F-8
**Test:** Run the persistence reprompt ("Are you sure? Please
reconsider.") on each of 500 MATH-500 K=1 problems for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
Compute first-pass and second-pass accuracy on (a) bottom-50% prefill
DoM subset, (b) top-50% prefill DoM subset, (c) overall.
**Requires:** H100 (~30min) on the existing K=1 pipeline.
**Would change:** If second-pass accuracy on the bottom-50% subset is
≤ first-pass, F-8's recoverability premise is undermined and the
71.6%-at-coverage-0.5 gain must be re-attributed to K=2.5 sampling
variance rather than recovered answers. If second-pass accuracy is
*strictly higher* on the bottom-50% subset than on the top-50%
subset, this *strengthens* F-8 with a behavioral analogue.
**Blocks:** any selective-prediction writeup that claims
"recoverability" without this control.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2412.15296 — Confidence in the Reasoning of Large Language Models (Pawitan & Holmes, 2024)

**Relevance:** Pessimistic empirical study of LLM self-confidence on
non-math reasoning tasks (causal judgment, formal fallacies,
probability puzzles) using closed-source models (GPT-4o, GPT-4-turbo,
Mistral). Stays purely at the prompt-output level — no hidden-state
or residual-stream probing. Headline conclusion: "current LLMs do not
have any internally coherent sense of confidence" — second-pass
accuracy is often worse than first-pass, verbal confidence is
overstated, and verbal confidence is only partially explained by
token-level probability. Bears on F-2 (cross-domain generality of
prefill DoM AUROC), F-8 (selective-prediction recoverability premise),
and H-12 (label-free internal coherence assumption).

**Key claim we tested:** That LLM confidence — verbal, behavioral, and
token-probability — is incoherent across tasks; reconsideration
worsens accuracy; calibration is poor.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** (CITED ONLY pending H-96/H-97/H-98). The
paper does not directly evaluate residual-stream-based confidence
signals (which is our project's only confidence channel). Our prefill
DoM (F-2) is a *different* probe than verbal/persistence/token-prob
confidence and has only been measured on MATH-500. Whether the
paper's pessimism extends to prefill DoM is an open empirical
question we plan to settle with P11-FE96 (persistence reprompt),
P11-FE97 (cross-domain AUROC), and P11-FE98 (token-prob residual
AUROC).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE96, P11-FE97, P11-FE98, H-96, H-97,
H-98.

**Status:** TO TEST
