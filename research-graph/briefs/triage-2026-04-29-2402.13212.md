# Triage brief — 2402.13212 — Soft Self-Consistency Improves Language Model Agents

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13212
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500) — could be matched or beaten by mean output token log-probability.** SOFT-SC shows that simple aggregations (mean / min / product) over the *generated tokens'* `P_LM(y_i | y<i, x)` are strong enough to *rank* candidate trajectories on Bash, WebShop, ALFWorld. Their entire argument is that you don't need internal probes — sequence-level output likelihoods are already a usable confidence signal. **Mechanism**: if mean (or min) log-prob over the K=1 greedy MATH-500 answer ranks correctness on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with AUROC ≥ 0.77, F-2's headline "L19 prefill is the strong predictor" demotes from "a discovered hidden-state direction" to "a redundant restatement of an already-observable per-token output signal." **Falsifiable experiment** (≤ 1h CPU, all data already cached in `pathway11_h100/`): per-problem, recover or recompute log P_LM for each generated token of the K=1 answer, score = mean / min / product, compute AUROC vs the same labels used for F-2's 0.7731. If mean log-prob AUROC ≥ 0.7731 the finding is downgraded.

2. **F-8 (Selective-prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%) — calibration metrics may be the wrong yardstick.** SOFT-SC explicitly reports that ECE shows *no* significant correlation with their SOFT-SC success rate, and AUROC has a moderate *negative* correlation (r = -0.55) on Bash. Their explanation: calibration measures absolute probability quality, not the *ranking* property that selection actually needs. F-8 is fundamentally a ranking-by-prefill-DoM-then-abstain story. **Mechanism**: if our 71.6% number is largely driven by the rank ordering rather than the AUROC absolute value, then a cheaper, label-free token-likelihood ranker should match or beat it. **Falsifiable experiment**: replace prefill DoM with mean-token-log-prob in the selective-prediction pipeline (`pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`); recompute coverage-0.5 accuracy. If accuracy ≥ 71.6%, F-8 is a ranking-quality finding, not a "prefill geometry" finding, and the headline framing changes.

3. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — both directions may be downstream artifacts of a single observable signal.** SOFT-SC works *only* on output-token probabilities — i.e., signals that funnel through the final-layer softmax. If that signal alone is sufficient for correctness ranking, the geometric independence between prefill L19 DoM and final-token L19 DoM is potentially epiphenomenal: two views of the same underlying probability mass, neither of which is the "true" signal. **Mechanism**: F-3's orthogonality has been used to motivate H-13 (head-level circuit attribution) and H-17 (RoPE de-rotation). If the *behaviorally relevant* signal lives at the output-probability level, those attribution stories lose their motivation. **Falsifiable experiment**: measure cor(prefill_DoM_score, mean_log_prob) and cor(final_token_DoM_score, mean_log_prob) on the 500 MATH-500 problems. If both correlations exceed ~0.6, the orthogonality of the two DoM directions doesn't reflect distinct mechanisms — it reflects the same underlying confidence signal accessed through different lenses.

4. **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute) — the cheapest version of "soft routing" may not need verification at all.** SOFT-SC's adaptive variant samples one trajectory at a time and stops as soon as the cumulative min-probability crosses a threshold τ. This is a **routing signal that requires neither a verification pass nor a probe** — just the model's own sequence likelihood on its own output. **Mechanism**: if we can stop at K=1 for high-min-prob problems and only escalate to K=8 for low-min-prob ones, the C_exact pipeline (extract activations, run verification prompt, score with DoM) may be over-engineered relative to "stop sampling when min-prob is high enough." **Falsifiable experiment**: on the 500 MATH-500 K=8 generations, score each K=1 trajectory by min-token-log-prob; route to K=8 only if min-prob below threshold τ. Compare accuracy/compute Pareto against H-19's C_exact verification routing. If the SOFT-SC adaptive baseline matches C_exact at the same average K, H-19 is a more elaborate way to recover what's already in the logits.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** — SOFT-SC's direct alternative is sequence-level mean log-prob. Never benchmarked as a baseline in the project. This is the highest-leverage hole the paper exposes.
- **F-8 (Selective-prediction acc 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** — Their calibration-vs-selection evidence (r = -0.55 between AUROC and Bash SR) reframes our 0.7731 → 71.6% pipeline as a ranking story; mean-log-prob may be a sufficient ranker.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)** — F-9 already collapsed CoE down to DoM. SOFT-SC's results imply the same collapse may go one step further: down to mean-log-prob over the answer string.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing)** — SOFT-SC adaptive stopping is the cheapest possible routing baseline; should be the comparison ceiling for any verification-routing scheme.
- **H-12 (Semantic-entropy probes (label-free) replace supervised DoM for refusal)** — same family of "label-free output-side confidence" approaches as SOFT-SC; SOFT-SC is even simpler (no semantic clustering, just token logits).
- **F-7 (D-bucket = K=1 right but K=8 wrong, distinctive collective signature)** — SOFT-SC predicts that D-bucket's K=1 answers should have *high* min/mean-prob (since K=1 is correct) but *unstable* trajectories under sampling. Sequence-likelihood routing would *fail* to flag D-bucket — a clean separation between "what output-probs catch" and "what hidden-state geometry catches" if our F-7 is real.
- The headline anchor — **AUROC = 0.7731** — is the exact number to beat with the SOFT-SC baseline.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Mean / min / product token-probability aggregation as a confidence score** — `score(y) = f({P_LM(y_i | y<i, x)})`, with `f ∈ {min, mean, product}`. Choice of `f` is dev-set tuned (mean for Bash and ALFWorld; min for WebShop). *Replication cost*: ≤ 1h CPU if log-probs are stored alongside K=1 answers (otherwise re-extract during a forward pass over the 500 problems — small GPU, ~1h). *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct competitor to F-2, never baselined.
- **Adaptive stopping by cumulative min-probability threshold** — sample one trajectory at a time, stop when cumulative min-prob ≥ τ. Threshold chosen on dev set. *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on existing K=8 generations (just simulate the early-stop). *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — natural cheap baseline for H-19.
- **Verbalized vs. logit-based confidence head-to-head** — prompt the model to verbalize a confidence score, use it for selection; compared against logit-based score. Logit-based wins by 2.4% on Bash at k=10. *Replication cost*: ~2h H100 (need to regenerate with verbalize-confidence prompt suffix). *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — mostly relevant if we ever need to rank without logit access.
- **Black-box rescoring via small open-source scorer** — run black-box LLM (GPT-4) for generations, then a smaller open-source LLM for log-prob scoring of those generations. *Replication cost*: not directly applicable to our pipeline — we run open-source models end-to-end. *We'd plausibly run this*: **no**.
- **Cross-aggregation comparison** — they tune `f` over `{min, mean, product}`. Worth doing all three rather than picking one; min and product behave very differently on long answers. *Replication cost*: free given the mean computation. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes**.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Voting fails when the action space is sparse; soft scoring rescues sample efficiency."** Reframes SC's failure mode: not "votes are noisy" but "exact match is the wrong relation in large action spaces." MATH-500 has a smaller action space (numerical answers, often integers) so this concern is less acute — but the *general* lesson generalizes: any task where the model emits long generations whose surface forms vary should prefer continuous scoring. Intersects F-9 (CoE redundancy with DoM) by suggesting an even simpler continuous score.
- **"Calibration metrics measure absolute probability quality; selection needs ranking quality."** This is the most actionable framing. Our entire pipeline (F-2, F-8) is reported in AUROC and selective-prediction accuracy — both ranking-flavored, but our narrative reads as if AUROC is a calibration-style virtue. SOFT-SC says: don't conflate. Could clean up how F-2 and F-8 are positioned in the next writeup.
- **"Black-box generation + open-source scoring as a deployment pattern."** Not directly used by us, but a reusable framing: if any verifier ever uses a different model than the generator, the relationship between scorer-fit-to-generator and ranking quality matters.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Aggregation `f ∈ {min, mean, product}` should be tuned per dataset on a dev split. **Don't pre-commit to one.** They use mean for Bash/ALFWorld and min for WebShop.
- For the adaptive variant they use **min-probability cumulative score** (not mean) to compare against threshold τ; threshold tuned on dev.
- They observe that the relevant calibration *non-correlation* (with AUROC) is driven by ranking-vs-calibration distinction; concretely, log-prob ordering is preserved under monotone calibration transforms but ECE/AUROC are not invariant.
- Verbalized confidence is reliably worse than logit confidence (Bash, k=10: 27.6 ± 2.0 vs 30.0 ± 2.4). Don't bother with verbalized-confidence ablations as a primary path.
- Code: https://github.com/HanNight/soft_self_consistency (Apache-style — verify before reuse).
- Models actually used: CodeLlama-7B/13B/34B/70B, Mistral-7B, GPT-3.5/GPT-4. None overlap with our Qwen-2.5 / DeepSeek-R1-Distill stack, so direct numbers are not transferable; only the *method* is.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2402.13212 — Soft Self-Consistency Improves Language Model Agents (Wang, Prasad, Stengel-Eskin, Bansal, 2024)

**Relevance:** Replaces self-consistency's majority-vote scoring with `f({P_LM(y_i | y<i, x)})` for `f ∈ {min, mean, product}`. The relevance to our project is *not* the agent-task headline numbers (Bash 30.0, WebShop 46.0, ALFWorld 25.2 SR with CodeLlama-34B at k=10) but the **principle**: a near-zero-cost output-side scalar is a strong correctness ranker. Our pipeline's headline AUROC = 0.7731 (F-2) and selective-prediction = 71.6% at coverage 0.5 (F-8) have never been benchmarked against this baseline. Their finding that AUROC and ECE *don't* correlate with SOFT-SC's selection success rate (r = -0.55 for AUROC on Bash) is also a methodological warning for how F-2 and F-8 are framed.

**Key claim we tested:** Mean / min / product over output token probabilities is sufficient to rank candidate trajectories for selection — no hidden-state probing required. Calibration metrics (ECE, AUROC) are not the right yardstick for selection performance; ranking quality is.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Sequence-likelihood baseline (FE71) vs F-2's prefill DoM AUROC = 0.7731 has not been run. If the baseline matches or beats DoM, F-2/F-8 are downgraded; if not, F-2 captures information beyond output likelihoods. Adaptive-stopping comparison (FE72) vs H-19's C_exact verification routing is the second-most-leveraged test.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE71 (sequence-likelihood AUROC vs prefill DoM), P11-FE72 (adaptive-stopping routing vs C_exact), P11-FE73 (F-3 orthogonality reduction), P11-FE74 (D-bucket likelihood signature), H-71, H-72, H-73, H-74.
