# Triage brief — 2604.22709 — Thinking Without Words: Efficient Latent Reasoning with Abstract Chain-of-Thought

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22709
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics) would be refuted if breathing emerges from a 64-token abstract codebook with no natural-language content.** Mechanism: Abstract-CoT generates 144-token sequences from a reserved vocabulary `<TOKEN_A>...<TOKEN_BB>` with random initial embeddings, post-trained via SFT+GRPO. The tokens have no semantic content at init and reach a Zipf-like distribution after RL (Fig. 4) — the meaning is invented by training, not pre-existing. If a Qwen3-8B Abstract-CoT model exhibits the same per-token PR inflation/collapse curve we measured on verbalized CoT (F-1/F-5), then the breathing signal is driven by (i) AR position, (ii) GRPO-induced trajectory shape, or (iii) constrained-decoding bottleneck — not by content. Falsifiable experiment: extract per-position L19 activations on MATH-500 from a public Abstract-CoT checkpoint (or fine-tune our Qwen2.5-1.5B with the released training pipeline at M=64), compute temporal PR; if peak PR ≥ 90% of verbalized-CoT peak, F-5 fails.

2. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) would be weakened or refuted if the prefill signal vanishes when the model is trained to compress reasoning 11.6× into post-prompt abstract tokens.** Mechanism: in our F-2 setup the prefill carries a "I will or won't get this right" prior because the verbal CoT is unconstrained. Under Abstract-CoT, the model knows it must finish in ≤128 abstract tokens and the answer attends *only* to those tokens (block-structured attention mask, Eq. 3); the prefill no longer needs to pre-commit, since the abstract trace is the only channel by data-processing-inequality (their Eq. 2). If prefill DoM AUROC drops below 0.6 on Abstract-CoT runs while final-token DoM stays > 0.7, F-2 was a property of unconstrained verbalization, not "the model knows before generating".

3. **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046) would be refuted if Abstract-CoT compresses both signals into a single direction.** Mechanism: the paper's conditional Markov structure C → H_Z_abs → Y collapses verbal-CoT scaffolding into one bottleneck. If both prefill DoM and final-token DoM are forced to read/write through this 128-token bottleneck, they may align (cos > 0.4) because there is no "long verbalized scratchpad" to keep them geometrically distinct. Falsifiable: cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) computed on Qwen3-8B Abstract-CoT runs > 0.3 → F-3 was an artifact of long verbal CoT.

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) would be refuted if Abstract-CoT trajectories — same model, same problem set — give CoE-60 a strict lift over single-layer DoM that's larger than what we measured under verbalized CoT.** Mechanism: under aggressive token compression, the per-step layer-wise embedding trajectory may carry information that single-layer DoM cannot, because the model is forced to do more computation per token. Falsifiable: train CoE-60 vs single-layer DoM probes on cached Abstract-CoT activations; if the gap widens > 0.02 AUROC, F-9 was specific to the verbalized regime.

5. **F-1 (breathing universal across scales) would be refuted by the paper's own Table 1 if Abstract-CoT's behavioral parity holds across model families.** The paper achieves matched accuracy on Qwen3-8B (90.8% vs 89.8% SFT-CoT), Qwen3-4B (89.8% vs 88.4%), and Granite-4.0-Micro-3B (74.4% vs 72.2%) at 8.4–10.4× fewer tokens. If "the same task is solved" with an order-of-magnitude shorter trajectory, then the temporal PR curve as a function of token-position is *not* a universal property of "doing the task" — it's a property of "doing the task in many tokens". F-1's framing as "universal across architectures and scales" should be tightened to "universal at fixed verbalization budget".

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT shows compressed or qualitatively different breathing)** — directly motivated by this paper. The brief upgrades H-22 from "blocked, awaiting code release" to "actionable now": the paper specifies M=64, m_max=128, T=3, 1M GRPO episodes, gpt-oss-20b reward model, Algorithm 1 pseudocode, and the public Dolci-Think-SFT/RL datasets. The training pipeline can be reproduced from §3 + §4.1 without waiting for an explicit code drop.
- **F-1 (universal dimensional breathing)** — the headline scale-collapse claim is directly tested by replacing 1205 verbal tokens with 144 abstract tokens at near-identical accuracy (82.4 → 90.8 on Qwen3-8B). Breathing-amplitude vs token-count regression becomes feasible.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold) and F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%)** — both depend on the prefill signal being usable. The bottleneck attention mask (answer attends only to abstract tokens, not verbal CoT) gives a clean ablation: train one variant where prefill cannot leak through, and another where it can.
- **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics)** — the paper's cold-start RL ablation (RL-only fails: 82.0 vs 90.8 with warm-up) suggests the abstract-token *embeddings* must carry content for the trajectory to work. This is a finer-grained version of "content-dependent": the content lives in the codebook embeddings rather than in surface tokens.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Abstract-CoT compresses the trajectory horizontally; CoE redundancy may not hold under that compression.
- **F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature)** — D-bucket is "K=1 right, K=8 wrong". Under 11.6× token compression, sample-to-sample variance shrinks. The D-bucket population may collapse, providing an indirect mechanism check.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Bottlenecked SFT with block-structured attention mask (§3.2, Eq. 3)** — concatenated sequence [x; c; ẑ; y] where the answer block masks out the verbal CoT block while keeping prompt + abstract tokens. Implements the C → H_Z_abs → Y conditional Markov structure.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (mask construction); 8×H100 days for actual training run.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — even without retraining, the masking trick gives a clean way to ablate "what does the prefill see vs the final-token see" on our existing Qwen2.5-1.5B by re-running prefills with masked context.

- **Constrained decoding via codebook restriction** — at each generation step, normalize over `A = V_abs ∪ {<endabstract>}` only; hard cap at m_max=128.
  *Replication cost*: 1h to add to our generation harness.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful as a cheap "force the model to use a small token set" baseline for any of our future-token probes.

- **GRPO with KL regularization over both abstract trace and answer (Eq. 5, DeepSeekMath formulation)** — group-relative reward normalization with K rollouts; advantage A_k = (R̂_k − mean) / (std + ε); KL applied jointly over π^abs(z|x) and π(y|x,z).
  *Replication cost*: 32×H100 days for 1M episodes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — too expensive for our refutation-oriented use, but the formulation is our reference for any future RL-shaping of probe targets.

- **Generative reward model (gpt-oss-20b, OpenAI 2025)** — used to score response quality on non-verifiable tasks; recipe extends to AlpacaEval and HotpotQA.
  *Replication cost*: API/inference cost only (no training).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for grading our K=8 sampling outputs without ground-truth labels.

- **Token-level permutation analysis on the trace (Table 3a)** — randomly permute reasoning tokens (turn-level for verbal CoT, token-level for abstract) and measure response accuracy delta. Tests compositional vs bag-of-tokens reasoning.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our 1024-tok MATH-500 generations as a cheap test of whether reasoning order matters at our scale.

- **Truncation ablation at fixed k=32 thinking tokens (Table 3b)** — cap CoT at k tokens, append end-delimiter, measure response. Verbal-CoT lost 11.0 pts; Abstract-CoT lost 6.0 pts.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the structural cousin of our 256-tok-truncation incident (PROJECT_RECORD §1d). Their "graceful degradation" framing for short trace is a useful refutation pattern.

- **Codebook-size scaling sweep (M ∈ {1, 2, 4, …, 512}, Fig. 5–7)** — performance saturates around M=64 for all three benchmarks; cold-start RL collapses for M ≥ 16 on MATH-500.
  *Replication cost*: 9× warm-up runs, ~2 weeks H100 each. High.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — but we can use the M=1 → 512 trend as a hypothesis prior for our own probing dimensionality sweeps.

- **Token frequency tracking across RL training (Fig. 4, Fig. 8–14)** — log abstract-token use per RL step; observe Zipf-like distribution emergence.
  *Replication cost*: trivial logging hook during any RL run.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we ever do RL — a direct analog for tracking which probe directions get used during selective-prediction routing.

## Approaches & framings

- **Discrete latent bottleneck as an information channel (§3.2, Eq. 2)**: I(C; Y | X, Z) ≤ I(C; H_Z_abs | X, Z). Reframes "how much can a short reasoning trace carry" as a channel-capacity problem. Useful framing for our F-2 prefill-DoM channel: instead of asking "what direction predicts correctness", ask "what's the mutual information between the prefill's L19 activation and the answer correctness, bounded by the rest of the trajectory".

- **Reasoning is separable from human-readability (§5)** — explicit endorsement of DeepSeek-R1-Zero's finding that strong performance survives non-verbalizable rationales. This is the same conceptual move that lets us treat L19 DoM as a correctness signal independent of the surface CoT semantic content.

- **The cold-start vs warm-started gap (4.2)** — cold-start RL underperforms the base model at all M values. This is mechanistically the same lesson as our F-7: "the geometry must already be there for the probe/RL signal to land". Warm-up shapes the embedding manifold so RL has something to optimize. Maps to our finding that DoM is detectable in pre-trained models without training a probe.

- **Bounded reasoning budget as a first-class object (§2.4)** — the paper situates Abstract-CoT alongside L1, AdaCoT, e1, ThinkPrune in the "RL for budget control" line. We currently have no explicit budget-controlled framing for our selective-prediction work (F-8). Treating coverage K as an RL-optimizable budget might extend F-8 from "fixed coverage 0.5" to a learnable policy.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Abstract codebook design**: M=64 reserved tokens labelled `<TOKEN_A>` … `<TOKEN_BB>` (alphabetical, two-letter for M > 26). Two delimiters `<beginabstract>`, `<endabstract>`. Random embedding init. Uniform random selection during initial warm-up — alphabetical cycling and explicit power-law-init were both tried and underperformed uniform.
- **Hyperparameters**: m_max = 128 (max abstract tokens); T = 3 policy iteration rounds; SFT = 3 epochs per round; RL = 1M episodes. SFT on 8×H100, RL on up to 32×H100.
- **Block-structured attention mask** (§3.2): abstract tokens attend to prompt + verbal CoT + earlier abstract tokens; the answer attends to prompt + abstract tokens + earlier answer tokens but **not** to the verbal CoT (mask entry A_{i,j}=0 for i ∈ Y, j ∈ C).
- **GRPO defaults** (Eq. 5): K rollouts per prompt; reward = generative score from gpt-oss-20b; advantage = group-normalized; KL applied to the joint π^abs · π over (z, y); reference policy = warm-started checkpoint.
- **Constrained-decoding mechanic**: at each step within `<beginabstract>...<endabstract>`, restrict softmax to A = V_abs ∪ {<endabstract>}; if m = m_max, force `<endabstract>` and resume unconstrained.
- **Compression-ratio definition** (§4.2): E[|c_verbal|] / E[|m|], averaged over evaluation prompts. Gives 11.6× MATH-500, 1.9–2.2× AlpacaEval, 4.0–4.3× HotpotQA on Qwen3-8B.
- **Power-law dynamics** (Fig. 4, A.1.1): emerge purely from RL post-training — warm-up alone keeps the distribution roughly uniform; RL shapes the Zipf-like tail. One token (`<TOKEN_F>`) becomes dominant for Qwen3-8B at M=64.
- Public training data: `https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/Dolci-Think-SFT-7B`, `…/Dolci-Think-RL-7B`. Granite-4.0-Micro at `https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.0-micro`.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Permutation analysis on cached MATH-500 verbal CoT** (their Table 3a, Verbal CoT SFT row showed −8.0 pts under turn-level permutation): use existing `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` traces — turn-level shuffle of `\n\n`-separated steps, regenerate answer with greedy decode, measure delta. ~20 min CPU. Probes whether step order is load-bearing in our regime.
- **Truncation-at-32-tokens analysis on cached generations** (their Table 3b, Verbal CoT SFT lost 7.0 pts at k=32): truncate every cached MATH-500 trajectory at 32 tokens, append `</think>` (or model-specific end), regenerate answer, measure delta. ~30 min CPU. Maps directly onto our 256-tok / 1024-tok comparison and gives a continuous truncation-degradation curve.
- **Compression-ratio measurement on our K=1 generations**: compute E[|verbal CoT|] from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` on the 1.5B and 7B runs. Sets the "what would 11.6× look like for us" baseline. ~5 min CPU.
- **Bottleneck-mask ablation on prefill DoM (no retraining)**: re-run our prefill-DoM AUROC computation with the verbal-CoT tokens *zeroed in attention* (or replaced with PAD) at L19. Tests whether the 0.7731 AUROC depends on prefill seeing verbal context vs prompt-only context. ~1h CPU on cached prefill activations. Cheap surrogate for what F-2 looks like under Abstract-CoT.
