# Triage brief — 2305.00586 — How does GPT-2 compute greater-than?: Interpreting mathematical abilities in a pre-trained language model

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00586
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor, AUROC 0.7731)** — Hanna et al. show that the GPT-2 greater-than computation is *distributed*: not a single direction, not a single layer, not even a single neuron. Path-patching reveals 7 attention heads + 4 MLPs (8–11) cooperating, and inside MLP 10 the top-10 neurons sum to produce greater-than while no individual neuron does. **Mechanism**: a single-direction L19 DoM probe on Qwen residuals would necessarily *aggregate* the contributions of multiple competing components plus irrelevant variance — AUROC 0.7731 is consistent with a coarse linear projector of a multi-component circuit, not evidence that "L19 DoM is the correctness signal." **Falsifier**: refit correctness predictors using *per-head* L19 outputs (12 heads × hidden_dim/12) instead of the full residual; if the union AUROC > 0.85 while single-direction DoM caps at 0.77, F-2's "single-direction" framing is wrong and DoM is a lossy aggregator. (Reinforces P11-FE7 with a sharper prediction.)

2. **F-2 + linear-probe causality** — Hanna et al. apply LEACE [Belrose et al. 2306.03819] to remove linearly-extractable `YY` information from attention head outputs and observe only a small drop (81.7% → 64.7% probability difference). They explicitly cite this as a warning that linear extractability ≠ functional importance ([13, 40, 22]). **Mechanism**: if we LEACE the L19 prefill DoM direction out of the residual stream and the model's MATH-500 accuracy stays roughly intact while a refit DoM probe collapses to chance, then F-2's predictive power is a *probing artifact* — extractable info that the model itself doesn't use causally. **Falsifier**: LEACE-ablate prefill DoM at L19 → run K=1 generation. If MATH-500 1.5B accuracy stays ≥ 45% (vs. baseline 48.6%) while a refit probe AUROC drops to ≤ 0.55, F-2 is non-causal correlation. This is a cheap, definitive test and changes the entire selective-prediction story (F-8) if it fires.

3. **F-3 / F-9 (prefill ⊥ final-token; CoE redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Hanna et al. show the greater-than operation is implemented across *four consecutive MLPs* (8, 9, 10, 11) with progressive sharpening: MLP 9's logit-lens output is upper-triangular but coarse, MLP 10 sharpens it, MLP 11 truncates the maximum span. Each layer contributes meaningfully (MLP 8 indirect, MLP 9 mixed, MLP 10 mostly direct, MLP 11 only direct). **Mechanism**: if MATH correctness has a similar staged-MLP architecture in Qwen (likely, since Qwen has 28 layers and L17–L23 spans a comparable late-MLP block), then a *single-layer* L19 DoM must be missing the staged contributions of L17, L18, L20, L21. F-9 says CoE-60 is redundant with L19 — but Hanna et al. predict the staged-multilayer view should *win* on harder problems where multi-step refinement matters. **Falsifier**: re-run CoE on 1024-tok labels with restricted layer sets {L19}, {L17–L21}, {L15–L23}; if 5-layer CoE outperforms single-layer DoM by ≥ 3 AUROC points on the bottom-quartile-difficulty subset, F-9's "redundancy" overgeneralizes the easy cases.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective geometric signature)** — Hanna et al.'s PCA on MLP-input residual streams reveals a clean clockwise ordering by start year — but they show this structure *already exists in the static year embeddings* (modulo a clustering of 02–09). The attention heads merely "unify and move" pre-existing input structure to the end position. **Mechanism**: if D-bucket's geometric signature (F-7) is similarly an artifact of pre-existing input structure (problem token length, math-symbol density, surface-feature embeddings) rather than a *correctness/difficulty* signal, then F-7's "collective signature" reflects input statistics that propagate forward, not a meaningful difficulty manifold. **Falsifier**: regress D-bucket geometric features (top PCA components) against (problem-token length, math-token count, GPT-2 surprisal of question text, embedding-norm) on the cached 1024-tok activations. If R² > 0.6 on these surface-feature regressors, F-7 is mostly capturing input statistics, and the "geometric signature" framing collapses. (Distinct from H-21's attention-entropy test: this targets *static input* confounds, not attention dynamics.)

5. **F-8 (selective prediction via prefill DoM @ 50% coverage = 71.6%)** — Hanna et al. demonstrate that GPT-2 *over-activates* the greater-than circuit on adversarial prompts where the correct answer is *less* than `YY` ("ended in the year 17`YY` and started in the year 17", "lasted from year 7`YY` BC to year 7"). The same circuit fires on syntactically-similar but semantically-opposite prompts, producing the *wrong* sign of inequality with high confidence. **Mechanism**: if MATH-500 selective prediction relies on a similar surface-pattern circuit, then problems that match the syntactic shape of "easy" problems but require different semantics (operator swap, sign flip, modular vs. real arithmetic) will produce *high prefill-DoM signal* but *wrong answers* — exactly the failure mode that defeats selective prediction. **Falsifier**: construct adversarial MATH-500 variants by minimal operator swaps (e.g., "find the largest" → "find the smallest" with otherwise identical setup); measure prefill DoM AUROC and selective-prediction accuracy. If AUROC drops by > 10 points on these distractors while selective-prediction confidence stays high, F-8 has a critical robustness gap that the headline 71.6% obscures.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)**: this paper *is* the methodological template. Path patching + iterative path-patching (IPP) heatmaps + logit lens at head/MLP/neuron resolution + circuit-faithfulness evaluation via counterfactual datasets — the entire H-13 protocol is laid out in §3 of Hanna et al., extending Wang et al.'s IOI work (already in graph as `2211.00593`) to a task with structured numerical output. Direct anchor: F-2's 0.7731 AUROC and F-3's cos = 0.046 are exactly the kind of summary statistics that H-13 should *decompose* into per-head, per-MLP contributions.

- **F-2 / F-3 (prefill DoM vs final-token DoM, AUROC 0.7731 vs 0.7186, cos = 0.046)**: Hanna et al.'s finding that *attention heads transmit `YY` from the YY position to the end* while *MLPs at the end position do the actual computation* corroborates a stage division. Their attention-pattern analysis (Fig 6) shows in-circuit heads at the end position attend to `YY`. This predicts the prefill direction encodes "what to verify" while the final direction encodes "answer of the verification" — orthogonality is geometrically expected if the two directions live in disjoint sub-circuits. Anchor: cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 supports a clean prefill-encoding / final-computation factorization rather than two separate "correctness signals."

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)**: Hanna et al. find MLPs 8–11 each contribute partially (MLP 8: indirect 39% / direct 14%; MLP 9: 28% / 32%; MLP 10: 16% / 56%; MLP 11: 0% / direct only). Anchor: if MATH correctness in Qwen mirrors this structure across L17–L21, the F-9 "single-layer L19 dominates" result needs to be tested against bottom-quartile-difficulty MATH problems where multi-layer composition matters most.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Path patching** — causal intervention along *edges* (paths) of the computation graph; replaces a component's input with a counterfactual ("01-dataset") and observes downstream change in probability difference. Core circuit-discovery primitive.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for full Qwen-1.5B sweep; 2h H100 for L17–L21 only.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct H-13 enabler, sharpens F-2/F-3.

- **Iterative path-patching (IPP) heatmaps** — recursive path patching from logits backward through MLPs, then through MLPs to attention heads, building (layer × head)/(layer × MLP) heatmaps of contribution.
  *Replication cost*: included in path-patching cost above.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — produces the visual circuit map H-13 needs.

- **Logit lens at head/MLP/neuron granularity** — multiply component output by unembedding matrix to get vocabulary-space "boost"; reveals upper-triangular pattern indicating greater-than computation. Generalizes to: project L19 prefill DoM through Qwen's unembedding to see if it's a "correctness boost" in vocabulary space.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached residuals.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap, immediately interpretable, doesn't need pod.

- **Counterfactual minimal-edit datasets (01-dataset)** — replace a single token (start-year `YY` → `01`) to construct a counterfactual that targets one specific computation. Analog: replace MATH problem's numerical answer with a fixed wrong number, or swap operator symbol.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU to generate, 2h H100 to re-extract.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — minimal-edit counterfactuals are cheaper than full retraining and isolate a specific circuit.

- **Per-neuron path patching at MLP scale** — patch each of MLP_l's 3072 neurons individually; ranks neuron contribution; finds top-10 neurons sum to greater-than approximation.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day per MLP layer (Qwen 1.5B has ~5500 neurons per MLP); subset (top-100 candidates) feasible in 4h.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for top-N candidate set; full sweep no.

- **LEACE concept-erasure ablation** — surgically remove linearly-extractable concept (here `YY`) from a representation; tests whether linear extractability is causally required.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached residuals (concept-erasure library is `EleutherAI/concept-erasure`).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the *single most diagnostic* test for whether F-2's DoM is causal or probing artifact.

- **Circuit faithfulness/completeness/minimality evaluation** — give the network only-circuit input vs all-but-circuit input; measure recovery percentage. Reports here: 89.5% recovery from circuit-only input; -36.6% probability difference from all-but-circuit input.
  *Replication cost*: subsumed by path-patching infra.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required for any H-13 circuit claim.

- **PCA on residual stream / head outputs** — 2D PCA over per-problem MLP-input residuals and head outputs; reveals geometric structure (clockwise year ordering). Maps to F-7's geometric-signature analysis with a different lens.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached residuals.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to D-bucket vs A-bucket residual structure.

- **Cross-prompt circuit similarity (loss-recovery %)** — re-run circuit ablations on prompt variants; report percentage of original performance recovered. Quantifies generalization vs prompt-format dependence.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 per prompt variant.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests the "correctness signal" robustness against prompt rephrasing.

## Approaches & framings

- **Memorization–generalization continuum** — Hanna et al. argue greater-than lies *between* memorization and generalization: a sophisticated retrieval mechanism (lookup table indexed by `YY`) that "imperfectly identifies greater-than scenarios" rather than a fully general operation. Reframes our DoM direction: prefill DoM may be a *retrieval index* into memorized correctness patterns, not a genuine "this answer is right" oracle. Sharpens H-5 (problem familiarity vs decomposability) — familiar problems may activate a retrieval circuit while decomposable-but-novel problems route differently.

- **Circuit overgeneralization (firing in wrong contexts)** — the same circuit activates inappropriately on prompts that share surface form but require a different operation. Reframes selective prediction: high prefill-DoM signal may indicate "circuit fired" rather than "answer is correct." Aligns with the Ríos-García `2604.18805` confirmation-bias warning baked into the triage skill.

- **Edge-level vs node-level interpretability** — circuits perspective treats *interactions* between components as primary, not just individual nodes. Reframes our F-2/F-3 single-layer single-direction probing as a node-level shortcut that may miss the actual edge structure.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Year-span synthetic dataset** — 10,000 examples; template "The `<noun>` lasted from the year `XXYY` to the year `XX` "; 120 nouns × 768 valid years (1000–1899); 97 valid two-digit `YY` values; license: research code released. Applicable? **no for headline AUROC tests** (English greater-than not MATH-500), **yes as a methodological starter** — the template-based counterfactual approach is what we'd reuse, not the dataset itself.

- **01-dataset (counterfactual)** — sister set where `YY` is replaced with `01` to construct minimal-edit counterfactual. Applicable? **yes as a pattern**: construct counterfactual MATH-500 examples by minimal edits (operator swap, single-digit perturbation in a numerical answer) to enable path-patching on Qwen.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Code released: `https://github.com/hannamw/gpt2-greater-than` (GPT-2 small, year-span task).
- Built on `rust_circuit` (Redwood Research) — superseded; modern equivalent is `TransformerLens` + `transformer_lens.HookedTransformer`. Path-patching support is in `transformer_lens.utils.patching` and `transformer_lens.HookedTransformer.run_with_hooks`.
- LEACE reference: `EleutherAI/concept-erasure` (paper `2306.03819`, already in graph as pending_triage).
- IPP heatmap normalization: change in *probability difference* (vs. unpatched) — not raw logit change. For our setup the analog is change in the prefill-DoM-projected scalar or change in selective-prediction accuracy.
- Circuit completeness threshold used: 89.5% recovery is treated as "mostly sufficient." This is a *low* bar by current standards (ACDC and follow-ups expect ≥ 95%); we should set our own threshold deliberately.
- Faithfulness via *both* directions: circuit-only input recovers performance, AND all-but-circuit input destroys it. We need both for an H-13 circuit claim.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2305.00586 — How does GPT-2 compute greater-than?: Interpreting mathematical abilities in a pre-trained language model (Hanna, Liu, Variengien, NeurIPS 2023)

**Relevance:** Methodological foundation for H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits). The paper identifies a 7-attention-head + 4-MLP circuit in GPT-2 small that computes greater-than on year-span prediction (81.7% probability difference baseline; 89.5% recovery from circuit-only input). It demonstrates path patching, iterative path-patching heatmaps, logit lens at head/MLP/neuron resolution, LEACE concept-erasure controls, and circuit faithfulness/completeness tests — the entire H-13 protocol toolkit. Particularly valuable for F-2/F-3 mechanistic interpretation: the heads-transmit-`YY` / MLPs-compute-greater-than split predicts a clean stage division between prefill (input encoding, where DoM = 0.7731) and final (output computation, where DoM = 0.7186) consistent with cos(prefill, final) ≈ 0.046.

**Key claim we tested:** Mathematical operations in pretrained transformers are implemented by structured circuits combining attention heads (information transport) and late MLPs (computation), distributed across multiple components and neurons rather than concentrated in a single direction or layer.
