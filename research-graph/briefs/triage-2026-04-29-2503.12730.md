# Triage brief — 2503.12730 — TinySQL: A Progressive Text-to-SQL Dataset for Mechanistic Interpretability Research

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12730
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "L19 is *the* correctness probe site" framing on cross-architecture grounds.** TinySQL's logit-lens result (§6) shows SQL generation is *layer-staged*: early layers commit to intent (SQL keywords), middle layers ground schema names, late layers assemble structure. If MATH-500 generation follows the same staging on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers), L19 (depth-fraction 0.68) is mid-stack — i.e. the *grounding* phase, not the *capability* phase. The prefill DoM AUROC=0.7731 may then be reading "schema-resolved problem representation" rather than "model knows the answer." **Mechanism:** if intent (≈ "I will/won't get this right") localizes earlier, a layer-3-to-L19 sweep of prefill DoM AUROC should be monotonic up to a peak well before L19. **Falsifiable by:** layerwise prefill DoM AUROC on cached Stage 2 NPZs (28 layers × 500 problems, all available).

2. **Refutes F-3's "two orthogonal circuits" interpretation in favor of "one fragmented signal."** §7.2 explicitly says "EAP often reveals fragmented mechanisms scattered across layers, especially in larger models, highlighting the difficulty of isolating clean circuits." cos(prefill, final) = 0.046 is currently read as evidence for two distinct circuits (F-3). But TinySQL's evidence on BM2/BM3 (24/16 layers, comparable scale to Qwen-1.5B) suggests fragmentation, not orthogonality, is the default. Two scattered fragments of the same signal projected onto disjoint partial bases would yield ≈0 cosine without implying separate circuits. **Mechanism:** if prefill and final-token DoMs are projections of one underlying correctness signal through distinct layer-local readouts, summing them should boost AUROC; if they're truly separate circuits, summing yields no gain. **Falsifiable by:** AUROC of (a) prefill DoM, (b) final DoM, (c) prefill+final concatenated/summed, on cached Stage 2 NPZs.

3. **Refutes H-13's premise that head-level attribution will resolve prefill-vs-final mechanism.** Table 2 shows that on BM2 (24L, 14 heads/layer) and BM3 (16L, 32 heads/layer) — both close to Qwen-1.5B's scale — "top edges often involved MLPs, leading to few head selections and low recovered accuracy (5–10%)." The authors had to retain *entire layers* to recover accuracy, sacrificing granularity. If MATH-500 prefill/final-token DoM circuits live in MLPs on Qwen-1.5B, head-disjointness analysis is ill-defined: H-13 will return null because the relevant computation isn't head-local. **Mechanism:** apply EAP-style edge attribution to the L19 DoM probe target on Qwen-1.5B; check whether top-K edges are attention-head edges or MLP edges. **Falsifiable by:** EAP run on cached forward graph (~1d CPU with attribution-patching backend).

4. **Refutes F-7's "distinctive collective signature" for D-bucket via within-class variance.** §7.1 reports that "across prompts within all classes, activation patching results vary significantly, even among prompts in the same class." If MATH-500 D-bucket's apparent geometric signature is driven by 2–3 outliers within the 36 D-bucket problems, the "collective" framing collapses into a per-problem effect. TinySQL's CS3-trained → CS1-tested case has the same kind of within-class variability that could mask outlier-driven F-7 results. **Mechanism:** leave-one-out test — does removing the 2 most-extreme D-bucket problems destroy the geometric signature? **Falsifiable by:** permutation test on the 36-problem D-bucket subset, recompute the F-7 metric with each problem dropped (~30min CPU on cached arrays).

5. **Refutes F-8's "prefill encodes difficulty" interpretation in favor of "prefill encodes problem-template-class."** TinySQL's input-corruption analysis (§4.2) found BM1_CS1 relies on schema for table names and instructions for field names — i.e., the model leverages *surface template* rather than semantic content. If MATH-500's prefill DoM is similarly reading template-shape (e.g., word-problem vs. equation-solving format) rather than problem-difficulty, then 71.6% selective-prediction at 50% coverage is a *category* sorting, not a *difficulty* sorting — and would fail under prompt-rephrasing perturbations. **Mechanism:** rewrite 100 MATH-500 prompts in equivalent surface forms (e.g., paraphrase, reorder), recompute prefill DoM AUROC. If AUROC drops sharply, F-8 is template-bound. **Falsifiable by:** ~2h H100 for re-extraction on 100 paraphrased prompts.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Edge Attribution Patching (EAP)** — gradient-based linear approximation of activation patching, ranks edges by `|Δe·∇L|` to identify minimal circuits supporting a task. *Replication cost*: 1–2 days CPU on cached forward graph for Qwen-1.5B. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's the natural tool for H-13 and the F-3 fragmentation refutation; no GPU needed if we cache the gradient pass.
- **Top-k Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)** — k=16, expansion factor 4, lr 8e-4, 6 epochs, Belrose's `Sparsify` package; trained on attention-head outputs and MLP activations from decoder blocks; FVU < 0.05 confirms reconstruction quality. *Replication cost*: ~1–2h A100 per SAE per layer; ~4h H100 for L19 prefill SAE on Qwen-1.5B. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — already proposed in P10-FE5 and aligns with Algorithm 1's similarity-guided feature selection.
- **Logit Lens (Nostalgebraist 2020)** — early-exit projection of residual stream through unembedding; reveals layer-staged computation. *Replication cost*: trivial (~30min CPU on cached residuals). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of the two-phase refutation against F-2.
- **SAE-EAP overlap as identifiability measure** — quantify percentage overlap between SAE-derived and EAP-derived circuits; compare to random-circuit baseline. *Replication cost*: trivial once both circuits are computed. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — provides an "identifiability" guarantee for any circuit claim we make about prefill DoM.
- **Algorithm 1: Similarity-Guided Feature Selection + AUC Attribution** — iterative ablation of lowest-ranked SAE features until output similarity ≥ threshold; then map to model components by decoder-row-norm AUC. *Replication cost*: ~1d CPU per analysis run. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if we adopt SAE-based mechanism analysis for L19; otherwise probe-direction analysis is simpler.
- **Zero-ablation vs mean-ablation Recovered Accuracy** — zero-ablation more robust (recovers more); mean-ablation gives smaller circuits but performance no better than random. *Replication cost*: free, just an experimental-design choice. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct lesson: any future ablation work in P10/P11 should default to zero-ablation, not mean-ablation.
- **Input corruption (clean vs. corrupted pairs)** — swap table/field names between instruction and schema to localize information flow. *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — analog: paraphrase MATH-500 prompts to test F-8 template-vs-difficulty refutation.
- **Selective Node Retention** — keep only EAP-selected attention heads, ablate the rest, measure recovered accuracy. *Replication cost*: ~1d CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct minimal-circuit construction for L19 DoM probe.

## Approaches & framings

- **Two-phase generation: intent → grounding → assembly.** Maps onto our prefill (= intent? or grounding?) vs final-token (= assembly) distinction. The framing reframes F-3 (orthogonal circuits) as potentially "different layers reading the same staged computation," not "two independent circuits." This is the single most-load-bearing reconceptualization in the paper for our program.
- **Synthetic-progression complexity testbed.** CS1→CS5 controlled progression isolates which subskill activates which circuit. Analog for us: a parallel MATH-500 difficulty-stratified study where we isolate whether D-bucket geometry comes from a specific subskill (algebra vs. geometry vs. word-problems).
- **Identifiability via cross-method overlap.** "Trust a circuit only if SAE-EAP overlap > random baseline" is a methodological standard we should adopt before publishing any prefill-DoM mechanism claim. Currently F-2 is purely AUROC-driven; we have no causal-mechanism circuit story for it.
- **Robustness–granularity tradeoff (zero vs mean ablation).** Reframes ablation choice as task-dependent — for circuit *identification* prefer zero, for circuit *minimality* prefer mean (with reduced trust).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Top-k SAE training: k=16, expansion factor 4, lr 8×10⁻⁴, 6 epochs, FVU < 0.05.
- SAE training time: ~1h on A100 for BM1 (33M, 2L), ~2h for BM2 (0.5B, 24L). Linear in layer count × params.
- EAP procedure: 15 batches × 100 paired (clean, corrupted) prompts; retain top-10 edges per batch; cross-batch consistency requirement (100% for CS1/CS2, 80% for CS3) before keeping an edge.
- For larger models, EAP edges go through MLPs → keep entire layers, not just heads, to recover accuracy.
- Alpaca instruction-tuning template; AdamW with weight decay.

- Project page: abirharrasse.github.io/tinysql/
