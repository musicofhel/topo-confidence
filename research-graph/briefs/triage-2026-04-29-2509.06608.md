# Triage brief — 2509.06608 — Small Vectors, Big Effects: A Mechanistic Study of RL-Induced Reasoning via Steering Vectors

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06608
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1 (per-position DoM steering on MATH-500) as a "geometric" mechanism.**
   Sinii et al. report that ~75% of the last-layer steering effect on Qwen2.5-Math-7B
   is recoverable by simply prefixing the literal token "To" (Qwen) or "Step" (Llama)
   to each prompt; their last-layer steering vector has cosine 0.37 with the
   unembedding row of "To". *Mechanism:* a 1D additive intervention at a late layer
   acts as a token-substitution bias on the first generated token, not as a
   geometric reasoning controller. *Falsifiable:* run H-1 (DoM steering at L19 on
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B) and in parallel a control where every MATH-500 prompt is prefixed
   with `Step 1:` or `To `. If ΔK=1 from prefix prompting is within ~2 pp of ΔK=1
   from DoM steering, H-1 is not a geometric mechanism — it is a token-bias
   reformulation of prefix prompting.

2. **Refutes the "different circuits" reading of F-3 (cos = 0.046).**
   F-3 interprets the orthogonality of L19 prefill DoM and L19 final-token DoM as
   evidence of distinct circuits. Sinii et al. show that on Qwen2.5-Math-7B the
   last-layer steering effect is identical pre- vs post-attention — the final-block
   attention is computationally inert for these directions, the residual just writes
   through. *Mechanism:* the cos = 0.046 may be a positional artifact of where the
   *same* underlying direction lands after RoPE rotation across very different
   token positions, not an independent circuit. *Falsifiable:* RoPE-rotate the L19
   prefill DoM forward to the final-token position (or counter-rotate the
   final-token DoM back to the prefill position) and recompute cosine. If
   post-rotation cos ≫ 0.046, F-3's "orthogonal circuits" framing collapses into
   H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed).

3. **Refutes F-9's "redundant" claim along the steering axis.**
   F-9 says CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM (on
   the *probing* axis: AUROC 0.7731 vs CoE-60's 0.811 collapses to ≈ tie under
   matched calibration). Sinii et al. show all-layer steering on Qwen2.5-Math-7B
   reaches 42.9 (essentially matching full FT 43.8), while the best single-layer
   steering — layer 27 alone — caps at 29.4 across 6 math benchmarks. *Mechanism:*
   per-layer additive directions are not collinear; layers carry complementary
   steering signal even when their probing AUROCs look similar. *Falsifiable:*
   train multi-layer prefill DoM steering (one DoM per layer, simultaneously
   added) and L19-only DoM steering on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500. If multi-layer
   ΔK=1 ≥ L19-only ΔK=1 + 5 pp, F-9's "redundant" framing is too strong — at
   minimum it must be qualified to "redundant for *probing*, not for *steering*."

4. **Refutes the geometric framing of F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731).**
   Sinii et al. find their layer-27 steering vector has the highest cosine with the
   unembedding row of a single token ("To", cos 0.37). If our L19 prefill DoM is
   similarly aligned with a small set of unembedding rows (e.g. process-words
   "Step", "First", "We"), the AUROC 0.7731 might just be measuring "does this
   prompt elicit a process-style continuation?" — a surface lexical cue, not
   geometric decomposability. *Mechanism:* the DoM direction is a token-substitution
   axis at the unembedding lattice; the AUROC reflects which prompts that
   substitution helps. *Falsifiable:* compute `DoM @ W_U.T` on Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L19
   prefill, report the top-10 tokens. If they are reasoning-process words, F-2's
   "geometric correctness signal" framing is partly a token-bias signal and the
   description in FINDINGS.md needs to add a token-level mechanism note.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — the graph already records this
  paper as `CORROBORATED_BY F-3` with note "SV effects concentrate on first
  generated token — consistent with our cos<0.2 finding past position 1." Their
  position-resolved analysis ("induced probability changes were concentrated at the
  first generated position") is the exact pattern F-3 predicts: a direction trained
  to act at *one* boundary (prefill→generation) will be ~orthogonal to anything
  measured *past* that boundary.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — direct ancestor /
  test methodology. RLOO-trained additive residual vectors take Qwen2.5-Math-7B
  from 14.3 → 42.9 across a 6-benchmark math suite, essentially closing the gap
  to full FT (43.8). This is the strongest existing data point on the
  "single-direction steering can move math accuracy" axis. Our F-2 prefill DoM
  AUROC 0.7731 sits *higher* than the AUROC implied by their layer-27 steering
  recoverability, so the prediction is that DoM-style steering at L19 prefill on
  Qwen-2.5-1.5B should move our 48.6% (243/500) MATH-500 baseline non-trivially —
  but possibly at the expected ~75%-explained-by-prefix ceiling.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** —
  contradicts the assumption that final-block heads matter for these directions.
  Their pre/post-attention equivalence at the last layer means head-level
  attribution at layer 27/28 will fail by construction. We should attribute at
  layers 19–24 instead, where the steering effect actually has attention-routed
  structure.

- **H-1 / F-7 collateral** — their layer-27 → 29.4 vs all-layer → 42.9 gap is
  within 1pp of the matched-compute selective-prediction acc 71.6% on answered at
  K=2.5 avg from F-8: a coincidence, but it suggests a useful upper-bound check.
  If a multi-layer DoM steerer can hit 71.6% K=1 acc on the answered set without
  any sampling, the "selective prediction at coverage 0.5" framing becomes a
  *sample-free* protocol.

## Methodologies extracted

- **RLOO additive residual-stream steering** — train one additive vector per layer
  via RL (RLOO objective, per-prompt baseline) with verifiable math rewards;
  insert at the residual stream pre-attention.
  *Replication cost*: H100·day to train on DeepScaleR + Open-s1; 6-benchmark
  math eval after.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly adversarial baseline against H-1
  (DoM-supervised vs RL-trained directions on the same MATH-500).

- **Pre-vs-post-attention insertion** — apply the steering vector before or after
  the attention block of the same layer.
  *Replication cost*: cheap once steering vectors exist; ~20 min CPU per
  configuration with a cached extractor.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether final-layer attention
  is computationally inert (refines H-13).

- **Cross-layer cosine decay measurement** — for layers i, j compute
  cos(steer_i, steer_j); show shifts become "nearly orthogonal" as |i−j| grows.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 hr CPU on cached DoM vectors.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives F-3 a multi-layer context: is the
  prefill/final orthogonality (cos = 0.046) atypical, or is it the generic
  cross-position decay rate?

- **First-position concentration measurement** — measure fraction of steering
  effect on the KL/probability shift at position 0 vs > 0.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU with cached prefill/generation logits.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct numerical sharpening of F-3.

- **Token-substitution ablation (prefix-prompting baseline)** — test whether
  prefixing a 1-token "To"/"Step" prefix to the prompt recovers most of the
  steering gain.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — no GPU; 500 prompts × greedy K=1 on the same
  Qwen-2.5-1.5B/7B that produced our P11 baselines.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheapest, highest-leverage refutation test.

- **Within-family vector transfer** — train steering vector on one family member,
  apply to another.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 if training; ~1 hr if just transferring.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — bears on F-1 universality but is downstream
  of having any RL-trained vector at all.

- **Unembedding-projection / nearest-token analysis** — project the steering /
  DoM vector onto W_U^T and report top-K nearest token rows.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct mechanism check for F-2.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Token-substitution bias" framing.** Last-layer 1D residual interventions
  reduce to discrete prompt prefixes at the unembedding lattice. Reframes any
  "geometric" or "continuous" steering claim as potentially a token-bias claim,
  intersecting H-1 and F-2 directly.

- **Layer-wise mechanistic decomposition.** Last-layer (token-substitution),
  penultimate-layer (MLP-mediated process-word boost), early-layer (attention-
  pattern shifts) have qualitatively different mechanisms. This refines the P11
  "stage marker" framing by predicting that L19 (the `prefill_correctness_emerges`
  stage marker) sits in a *different mechanistic regime* than L26/L27, so trying
  to cite a "single direction explains it all" story at L19 is still fine.

- **RL-trained vs supervised-DoM directions.** Sets up an explicit comparison: do
  RL-trained additive vectors converge to the DoM direction, or to something
  different (e.g. token-substitution biases)? Reframes DoM as one of several
  functionally distinct candidate directions.

- **Bypass-attention final-block effect.** At the final transformer block,
  attention is computationally inert for these steering vectors — refines our
  attribution targets toward middle layers and reframes any final-token analysis
  as residual-stream-only (not head-routed) at the top.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2509.06608 — Small Vectors, Big Effects: A Mechanistic Study of RL-Induced Reasoning via Steering Vectors (Sinii et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** Most directly adversarial paper to the H-1 / F-2 axis we have so
far. Trains additive residual-stream vectors via RLOO on math reasoning
benchmarks and shows: (a) all-layer steering recovers nearly all of full-FT
performance on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (42.9 vs 43.8 on a 6-benchmark math suite, base
14.3); (b) ~75% of *last-layer* steering effect is recoverable by 1-token
prompt prefixes ("To" for Qwen, "Step" for Llama); (c) last-layer steering
vectors have cosine 0.37 with the unembedding row of "To"; (d) effects
concentrate at the first generated token; (e) post-attention steering at the
last layer matches pre-attention steering, so the final-block attention is
computationally inert for these directions; (f) early-layer steering vectors
become near-orthogonal to late-layer steering vectors. This paper is the
sharpest existing evidence that "1D additive steering can move math accuracy"
*and* that the mechanism is largely token-substitution at the unembedding
lattice, which is exactly the framing-overturn risk for F-2 and H-1.

**Key claim we tested:** A single additive residual-stream direction at a
single layer can move MATH-style accuracy substantially, and the mechanism for
the last-layer version reduces to a 1-token surface-form bias.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** Triage-stage. Three replicable intermediates queued
(P11-FE6608: prefix-prompting baseline + W_U projection on Qwen-2.5-1.5B;
P11-FE6609: cross-layer cosine matrix; P10-FE6608: multi-layer vs L19-only DoM
steering). The graph already records this paper as `CORROBORATED_BY F-3` for
the first-token-only effect, but the F-2 / H-1 / F-9 implications are
adversarial and untested.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE6608, P11-FE6609, P10-FE6608, H-2609, H-2610,
P10-FE1, EXP-018.

**Status:** TO TEST

## New claims

This brief introduces three quantitative external claims (Sinii et al.'s, not
ours) that get cited as motivation: 14.3 → 42.9 (all-layer steering on
Qwen2.5-Math-7B math suite, vs 43.8 full FT), layer-27-only 29.4, and ~75% of
last-layer steering recoverable by `Step`/`To` prefix prompting. None of these
