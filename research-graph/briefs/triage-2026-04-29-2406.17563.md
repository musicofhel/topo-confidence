# Triage brief — 2406.17563 — Multi-property Steering of Large Language Models with Dynamic Activation Composition

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17563
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token DoM, cos = 0.046) is partially an artefact of "already-conditioned" suffixes, not orthogonal subspaces.** Scalena et al. show that the optimal α for activation steering drops to ≈0 within the first 4–6 generated tokens because the prefix has *moved into the conditioned region* — i.e. the residual stream is already aligned with the steering direction by mid-generation, so adding more along that direction produces no probability shift. If the same effect holds for the project's correctness direction, the L19 DoM at the final token is not "in a different subspace" — it is in *the same* subspace, but the activation has already advanced along it, leaving the directional axis collinear with the activation rather than orthogonal to displacement. Mechanism: KL(f, f_Δ) at high α should fall to ≈0 across generation steps even when the underlying direction is constant. Falsifiable: take the project's L19 prefill DoM, inject at α=2 at every position during 1.5B greedy decoding on MATH-500, plot KL(f(y_<i) ‖ f_Δ(y_<i)) per token. If KL collapses to ≈0 within ~10 tokens, the orthogonality framing in F-3 is rederived as "saturation," not "different signals" — and the steering vector at the *final* token is uninformative not because the direction has rotated, but because the activation has already saturated.
2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is at risk of being mis-tested with constant α.** The paper demonstrates conclusively that constant α (Fixed) produces large ΔPPL_ICL even when conditioning works, *and* that diminishing α (Dim) fails to reach optimal trade-off across properties. If the project runs H-1 with a single-α sweep (e.g. P2-FE1 or P10-FE1's α-sweep arm) and reports "steering destroys accuracy," that conclusion may reflect over-steering at later positions, not signal absence at L19. Mechanism: at high α, the model produces fluency-degraded MATH outputs (mis-formatted answers, premature \boxed{} closures), which the K=1 grader marks wrong even if the correctness direction was nudged correctly. Falsifiable: pair every constant-α arm of H-1 with a Dyn-equivalent KL-bounded α schedule (α_i = min(KL(f‖f_Δ@α=2), 2)); if Dyn-α recovers a positive accuracy delta where Fixed-α produced negative, H-1's "doesn't work" verdict (if reached) is a false negative driven by the wrong injection schedule.
3. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 in *residual stream*) may understate signal because the strongest property direction lives in *attention-head output space*.** Scalena et al. extract steering vectors from per-attention-head outputs at the last query token (their v_i is shape H × L × d_h, *not* residual stream of dim d), and show steering is "stronger from the middle layers onwards" with strongly head-localised L2 norms (their Fig 3). If the project's residual-stream L19 DoM is averaging over heads that disagree, AUROC 0.7731 is a lower bound on what's recoverable from the same activation tensor at the same layer. Mechanism: head-localised correctness directions are diluted by the residual sum. Falsifiable: re-extract the prefill DoM as 28-head separate vectors at L19 (using attention-head outputs, not the post-MLP residual), score per-head DoM AUROC on MATH-500 1.5B labels. If max_head AUROC > 0.78 by a margin (≥ 0.01), F-2's residual-stream framing is suboptimal and the headline number can be raised.
4. **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes are the path to label-free correctness signal) is challenged by KL-divergence-guided steering as a label-free alternative.** The paper's α_i = KL(f̃_i ‖ f̃_Δ_i) modulator is *fully label-free*: it requires only an unsteered model and a high-α steered counterpart, both of which the project already has (1.5B + a frozen prefill DoM). If KL-modulated steering achieves positive selective-prediction accuracy without ground-truth labels for direction discovery, H-12's framing of semantic-entropy probes as the canonical label-free route is not unique. Falsifiable: derive a label-free correctness score by inverting Dyn — compute KL(f(prefill) ‖ f_Δ(prefill)) using a *random* contrastive direction at L19, rank MATH-500 problems by KL, compare AUROC against semantic-entropy probes (2406.15927) on the same 500 problems. If KL-rank AUROC ≥ SEP AUROC, H-12 needs a comparator arm.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-3 (cos(prefill, final) = 0.046)** — The paper's "α drops to 0 once the prefix is conditioned" is the most parsimonious mechanism for *why* the cosine is near-zero: not because the directions are independent signals, but because the activation has already advanced along the constant direction. This connection is the strongest in the brief.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering on MATH-500)** — The paper provides a ready-made injection schedule (Dyn) that the project should adopt as the *default* for any future steering arm; the methodology section captures this. P2-FE1 / P10-FE1 should be re-specified to include Dyn.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Their Fig 3 (per-head L2 norm of Δ_i across L,H) is a direct template: the project can produce the equivalent figure for the L19 prefill DoM and identify which heads carry the correctness signal. Norm anchor: AUROC 0.7731 is residual-stream; per-head AUROC is uncomputed.
- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical, not computed)** — The paper shows steering vectors are property-stable but their *required intensity* drops sharply across positions. This is *consistent with* H-17's rotation hypothesis (vector stable, application non-stable) and *also* consistent with saturation (vector and target both stable, activation moves into the cone). Distinguishing the two needs the KL-trace experiment in refutation 1.
- **F-8 (selective prediction at coverage 0.5 → 71.6%)** — Their KL(f‖f_Δ) is a label-free uncertainty signal at *every position*. The project currently uses prefill DoM threshold; a Dyn-style KL-trace on the prefill DoM gives a richer label-free signal that could improve coverage/accuracy on the same K=2.5 budget.

## Methodologies extracted

- **KL-divergence-bounded dynamic α modulation** — α_i = min(KL(f̃(·) ‖ f̃_Δ@α=2(·)), 2) where f̃ is top-p truncated softmax over the union Q_i = Q^∅_i ∪ Q^+_i. Per-token, label-free, fully online.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached prefill activations + ~2h GPU on a 1.5B forward pass with vector injection (we have the model + the P2 vector). H100 not needed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in replacement for any constant-α steering arm in the project; resolves a known failure mode (over-steering at high α).
- **Per-head per-layer activation extraction at last-query-token** — v_i^+ is shape (H, L, d_h) extracted from attention head outputs at the position of q_{n+1}, averaged over K=30 contrastive prompt pairs.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU using cached Stage 2 trajectories; the project already extracts L19 residual-stream activations at prefill but not per-head attention outputs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly addresses H-13 head attribution and refutation #3 above.
- **Top-p truncated KL** — Restricting KL to the union of top-p_top=0.4 nuclei from both distributions before normalisation. Reduces noise from low-probability tail divergence.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (NumPy add-on) once the per-token logits are cached.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if running Dyn at all; this is the trick that makes the KL signal usable.
- **Contrastive ICL prompt-pair construction with positive vs negative property polarity** — n=4 in-context demonstrations, K=30 averaged pairs, identical query strings (only answer polarity differs for safety/formality).
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day to construct correct/incorrect MATH demonstration pairs (using existing 1024-tok labels) and re-derive a contrastive DoM; cheaper than the project's current label-driven DoM extraction.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for steering experiments where we want to compare against the canonical CAA/Rimsky setup; orthogonal to the existing supervised DoM extraction.
- **Negative-α reversal test (α = −1)** — Inject the steering vector with negated sign to test reversibility of the conditioning effect.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — same setup as positive-α steering, just sign flip.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — strong sanity check for any direction we steer with; "negative α flips correctness" is a much harder bar than "positive α improves correctness."
- **ΔPPL_ICL fluency metric** — Per-example perplexity computed under the steered model vs the ICL-baseline model on the *same* generated continuation, restricted to ICL-success cases. Decouples conditioning effect from generation collapse.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on existing K=1 generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if any steering experiment is run — necessary control to distinguish "wrong answer because direction is wrong" from "wrong answer because output collapsed."

## Approaches & framings

- **Property conditioning has a fluency / strength trade-off that is property-dependent.** Their "language steering" (high signal in residual subspace) needs only α≈1; "formality steering" (more diffuse) needs α>1 with rapid fluency loss. Reframes the project's steering arms: "correctness" is more like "formality" (diffuse) than "language" (concentrated), so single-α won't work.
- **Steering vectors are stable across generation; the *required intensity* is what changes.** This is a different framing than H-17 (vector rotates) — the paper instead treats the *vector* as a fixed property axis and the *coefficient* as the dynamical quantity. This is a cleaner null model than rotation: it predicts H-17's rotation might be α-noise, not actual direction change.
- **Multi-property steering as additive linear composition.** They simply sum independent property vectors (each with its own α schedule). For the project, this suggests "correctness steering + length-control steering" or "correctness + verification" composition could be explored without re-deriving directions.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Mistral 7B Instruct v0.2 (single model — limitation explicitly acknowledged).
- n=4 ICL examples per contrastive prompt; K=30 averaged prompt pairs.
- α range explored: [-1, 4]; α=2 used as the "high-intensity" reference for KL computation.
- p_top: 0.4 default in Section 7; 0.5 reported optimal across tasks; p_top ∈ [0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.9] swept in Appendix F.
- α bounded to [0, 2] in Dyn (lower bound 0 — never negative; upper bound 2 prevents over-steering).
- Per-head per-layer injection: Attn^{l,h}_i ← Attn^{l,h}_i + α · Δ^{l,h}_i. *Not* a residual-stream addition.
- Activation extraction at *last query token* of the n+1th query, after the n ICL demonstrations.
- Code: https://github.com/DanielSc4/Dynamic-Activation-Composition (MIT-equivalent, public).
- Evaluation tools: langdetect (language), Llama-Guard-2-8B (safety), s-nlp/xlmr_formality_classifier (formality).
- ΔPPL_ICL: PPL_ICL(f_Δ, q_{n+1}) − PPL_ICL(f, p^+_icl) — **restricted to examples where ICL produced high-conditioning output** (otherwise the baseline PPL is meaningless).

**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2406.17563 + F-3

## 2406.17563 — Multi-property Steering of Large Language Models with Dynamic Activation Composition (Scalena, Sarti, Nissim, 2024)

**Relevance:** Direct methodological contribution to the project's steering line (P2, P10, H-1). The paper's core insight — that constant-α contrastive activation steering causes property-dependent fluency collapse and that a KL-divergence-bounded dynamic α schedule recovers conditioning without fluency loss — is a drop-in improvement for any steering arm in the program. More importantly, their observation that α drops to ≈0 within the first 4–6 tokens because the prefix is "already conditioned" provides a parsimonious mechanism for F-3's prefill–final orthogonality (cos = 0.046): the directions may not be in different subspaces, but on the same axis at different progress stages. Their per-head per-layer extraction recipe also provides a template for H-13 head attribution.

**Key claim we tested:** Activation steering with a fixed direction is effective when α is dynamically modulated by KL(unsteered ‖ high-α-steered), and most properties only need strong steering at the first few tokens.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — we have not yet tested KL-modulated α on the project's L19 DoM. Three direct experiments are now queued: P11-FE73 (KL-trace cross-check, tests F-3 saturation vs subspace), P11-FE74 (per-head L19 AUROC, tests F-2 head-localisation), P10-FE63 (Dyn-α replacement for P10-FE1's constant-α arms), P2-FE52 (negative-α reversal sanity check). Predicted outcomes: KL-collapse confirmed within 10 tokens; per-head AUROC modestly exceeds residual AUROC; Dyn-α recovers positive accuracy delta where constant-α plateaus; α=-1 produces measurable negative effect if the P2 vector is causal.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P10-FE63, P2-FE52, H-73, H-74, H-75.
