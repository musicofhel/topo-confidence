# Triage brief — 2504.05419 — Reasoning Models Know When They're Right: Probing Hidden States for Self-Verification

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05419
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the implicit "Qwen-2.5-1.5B base is the right substrate" framing of F-2.**
   Zhang et al. probe R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (the long-CoT-distilled sibling of our model) and report ROC-AUC ≥ 0.85 on MATH-derived chunks, vs. only 0.66 AUC when the same protocol is run on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The control is striking: the **non-reasoning** probe loses ~25 AUROC points on identical data. F-2's headline number (0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill at L19) sits exactly in the middle of that gap. Mechanism: long-CoT supervised fine-tuning installs the linear correctness direction; non-reasoning bases only carry a degraded version. Falsifiable test: run the F-2 prefill DoM extraction pipeline on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and on Qwen-2.5-1.5B base, side by side on the same MATH-500 problems. If R1-Distill ≫ base, F-2 understates achievable AUROC by 10–15 points and "DoM is what the model knows" should be reframed as "DoM is what *long-CoT-trained* models know."

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill ⊥ final orthogonality, cos = 0.046) as a structural property.**
   Their §4.5 "look-ahead" result is that probe accuracy grows *monotonically smoothly* from ~65% at 10% chunk position to 79% at 95% — a continuous trajectory through the chunk, not a phase-discontinuous prefill-vs-final dichotomy. F-3 interprets cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0 as evidence the two encode different signals. Zhang's smooth interpolation contradicts that: if the same correctness direction is being progressively refined chunk-internally, then prefill and final-token DoM are endpoints of one continuous rotation, not orthogonal directions. Mechanism: orthogonality may be a Procrustes artifact of comparing the two extremes of a smoothly rotating direction, in which case interior layers / interior positions should yield directions whose cosine to prefill_DoM and final_DoM both fall on a smooth interpolation curve. Falsifiable test: at each of {10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, 100%} positions in the CoT, fit a logistic probe at L19 of Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 1024-tok labels, report cos(probe_t, prefill_DoM) and cos(probe_t, final_DoM). If the curve is monotone, F-3's "orthogonal" framing is misleading.

3. **Refutes the F-2 "L19 is special" implicit selection.**
   Zhang et al. use the **last-layer** hidden state of the reasoning model (not L19, not any intermediate layer) and obtain ROC-AUC up to 0.92 on AIME with R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and ≥ 0.80 on MATH with R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. Their grid search converged to *d* = 0 (linear probe) for most datasets. F-2's L19 selection was based on cross-layer sweep on Qwen-2.5-1.5B base — they show last-layer is competitive on the long-CoT-trained sibling. Mechanism: layer choice may be confounded with reasoning-vs-base, not with depth. Falsifiable test: run the per-layer DoM AUROC sweep (F-2 protocol) on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B prefill activations across all 28 layers; if the AUROC profile peaks at a different layer than 19, "L19 is the right layer" is model-specific, not architecture-general.

4. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer DoM) at the chunk-level granularity.**
   F-9 says CoE's 60-dimensional cross-layer trajectory features add nothing over a single-layer L19 DoM. Zhang et al.'s look-ahead probe at chunk-internal positions extracts predictive signal from positions where no answer has yet been generated (10% position → 65% accuracy). That signal is *temporal* (where in the chunk) not *cross-layer*. If chunk-position-temporal features at a single layer carry independent signal beyond final-token L19, then a 60-dim "temporal trajectory at L19" feature should beat single-position L19 DoM — the failure mode F-9 ruled out via CoE-60's particular cross-layer construction may not have ruled out a temporal trajectory variant. Mechanism: F-9's redundancy claim was tested against cross-layer aggregation, not against within-layer temporal aggregation. Falsifiable test: on the existing pathway11_h100 NPZs, build a 60-D "temporal-DoM-at-L19" feature (DoM projection at 60 evenly-spaced CoT positions), train logistic on top, compare against single-layer single-position L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Zhang's R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B-on-MATH probe achieves ECE ≤ 0.1 with linear probe (d=0). Suggests F-2's 0.7731 may be a substrate-limited ceiling, not a method ceiling. Direct experiment: F-2 on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B is the missing control.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Their look-ahead curve is monotone in chunk position, not bimodal. The "0.046" cosine may be the integral over a smooth rotation rather than an orthogonality.
- **F-8 (selective-prediction acc 71.6% at 50% coverage, K=2.5)** — Zhang's confidence-based early-exit at Thr=0.85 reduces tokens by 24% at no accuracy loss (88.2% vs 88.6% baseline), and beats static early-exit by 5pp at matched compute. Direct comparison: their 24% token reduction is the natural baseline F-8's selective-prediction-at-coverage-0.5 should be benchmarked against.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — They use 2-layer MLP not single-layer DoM, but most converge to d=0 (linear). Supports the "linear probe sufficient" prong of F-9 while refuting the "single layer / single position is enough" prong (see refutation 4).
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering for MATH-500)** — Their "probe as on-policy verifier" with early-exit is the steer-by-not-steering version of H-1. If their early-exit gain comes from probe, ours from DoM steer, the two should be combinable.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free)** — Zhang's pipeline is supervised (Gemini 2.0 Flash labels intermediate answers). H-12 would build the same probe without labels.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Reasoning-trace chunking by switch-keywords** — Split CoT on `\n\n`, then merge paragraphs into "chunks" delimited by keywords {"wait", "double-check", "alternatively", "make sure", "another way", "verify", "to confirm"}. Each chunk gets one intermediate-answer label.
  *Replication cost*: ~2 hr CPU to re-process the existing pathway11_h100 1024-tok CoT outputs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the keyword-based chunker is a useful preprocessing primitive for testing F-3 chunk-internal trajectory.
- **Gemini-2.0-Flash-as-judge for intermediate answers** — Use Gemini to extract the intermediate answer from each chunk and score it against ground truth. Output JSON per chunk with `{result, correctness}`.
  *Replication cost*: ~$5 in Gemini API on MATH-500 × 5 reasoning models.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — necessary for chunk-level labels we don't have. Also testable via Gemini-2.5 Pro for higher reliability.
- **Last-token-of-chunk hidden state as representation** — For each chunk, take the last-layer hidden state at the last token. Train 2-layer MLP (`σ(ReLU(eW₁+b₁)W₂+b₂)`).
  *Replication cost*: trivial on cached Hugging Face hidden states.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the natural extension of F-2 from "prefill end-of-prompt" to "end-of-each-chunk".
- **Weighted BCE for class imbalance** — `L = -1/N Σ (w·α·yᵢ log pᵢ + (1-yᵢ) log(1-pᵢ))`, w = neg/pos ratio, α grid-searched.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (config change).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — A/B/C/D-bucket data is also imbalanced; this is a better loss than the unweighted CE we use.
- **Mid-chunk look-ahead probing at paragraph boundaries** — Probe at {10, 25, 50, 75, 90, 95}% paragraph positions within a chunk to test how early correctness is encoded.
  *Replication cost*: needs paragraph-position annotations, ~1 day to add to pipeline + ~2h H100 to re-extract activations at additional positions.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is exactly the F-3 orthogonality refutation experiment.
- **Confidence-based early-exit verifier** — During inference, evaluate each chunk's probe output `pᵢ`, truncate when `pᵢ > Thr` (e.g. 0.85). Compare to static early-exit at fixed `m` chunks.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day to re-run MATH-500 with on-policy probe-driven truncation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct comparison to F-8 selective prediction.
- **Non-reasoning-model control** — Run the same probing pipeline on the non-reasoning instruct counterpart (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct vs R1-Distill-Llama-8B). Difference quantifies the "long-CoT contribution" to encoded correctness.
  *Replication cost*: ~half H100 day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct vs R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B is the obvious control for F-2.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Reasoning models *encode* correctness but *fail to use* it"** — gap between probe-recoverable signal and what the model's own generation policy actually exploits. Maps directly onto F-2's "DoM exists in residual stream" + the open question of why MATH-500 K=1 stops at 48.6% on 1.5B. The framing predicts H-1 (steering) and H-19 (verifier-routing) should both yield gains because the substrate is there but not deployed.
- **"On-policy hidden verifier"** — internal probe = lightweight verifier integrated with the model's own representations, vs external LLM-as-judge. Reframes the F-N findings as a hidden-verifier-extraction program rather than a geometry program.
- **Chunk = unit of analysis** — replaces the F-2 implicit unit "prompt + everything before final token" with a finer "intermediate-answer-bearing chunk." This is an intermediate-grain alternative to F-3's "prefill-end vs final-token" binary that the F-3 chunk-internal trajectory experiment (refutation 2) needs.
- **Calibration as a first-class signal alongside AUROC** — Brier and ECE quoted alongside AUROC as primary metrics. F-2/F-8 currently report only AUROC; calibration may be where DoM falls down (or not) and we don't know.

- **MATH (Hendrycks et al. 2021) train + MATH-500 test** — Same 500-example test set we use. Hugging Face. Applicable? **yes** — direct overlap with pathway11_h100.
- **GSM8K** — 1000 train / 1316 test downsample. HF. Applicable? yes — natural OOD test for our pipeline (we currently only test on MATH-500).
- **AIME 1983–2024 train / AIME 2025 test** — di-zhang-fdu/AIME_1983_2024 + yentinglin/aime_2025 on HF. ~30 test problems. Applicable? yes — much harder distribution shift; would let us probe the F-2 generalization frontier.
- **KnowLogic (Zhan et al. 2025)** — 5.4K MCQ logical reasoning, knowledge-driven synthesis. HF (arxiv 2503.06218). Applicable? yes — single-correct-answer subset is a logical-reasoning OOD test we currently lack.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Hidden size grid: `d ∈ {0, 16, 32}`. Most converged to `d=0` (linear), explicitly noted in §4.1.
- Learning rate grid: `{1e-3, 1e-4, 1e-5}`. Loss-weight `α ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}`. Weight decay `{0.001, 0.01, 0.1}`.
- 200 epochs cap, batch size 64, Adam, early-stop on val loss.
- Top-10-by-val-acc ranking, then pick smallest hidden size as the report model (Occam).
- vLLM for inference with PagedAttention (Kwon et al. 2023).
- Max output 30K tokens; truncated completions discarded (this is consequential for AIME — only 25–30 test examples survive!).
- Inference prompt subtly different from Qwen native template; small perplexity hit accepted to keep representation extraction on-policy.
- Code/repo: not linked in arxiv abstract. (Prior admission record points to `github.com/AngelaZZZ-611/reasoning_models_probing` — verify before relying.)

**Relevance:** Linear probe over **last-layer hidden states at chunk boundaries** of long-CoT-trained reasoning models (R1-Distill-{Llama-8B, Llama-70B, Qwen-1.5B, Qwen-7B, Qwen-32B}, QwQ-32B) recovers intermediate-answer correctness at ROC-AUC 0.80–0.92 with ECE < 0.1, on GSM8K, MATH, AIME, KnowLogic. Repository: `github.com/AngelaZZZ-611/reasoning_models_probing`.

**Key claim we tested:** Final-token last-layer hidden state of long-CoT-trained reasoning models linearly encodes intermediate-answer correctness, well-calibrated, and the same direction is recoverable mid-chunk before the answer is articulated.
