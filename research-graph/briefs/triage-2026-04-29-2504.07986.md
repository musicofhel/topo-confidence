# Triage brief — 2504.07986 — SEAL: Steerable Reasoning Calibration of Large Language Models for Free

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07986
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 ⇒ direction rotates) is in tension with SEAL's single static vector working across an entire long-CoT trace.** SEAL extracts one vector S = H_E − H_RT at layer 20 (in DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) and re-applies it at every "\n\n" boundary across a trace of thousands of tokens, getting +11% on MATH-500. If the project-relevant direction in residual space rotates as much as F-3 claims (near-orthogonal between prefill and final), a single fixed vector should *not* uniformly help across all thought boundaries. Mechanism for refutation: either (a) the relevant axis is not the DoM but the thought-type axis (which is rotation-stable), or (b) F-3's measured rotation is specific to base Qwen 2.5 1.5B and disappears in R1-Distill (long-CoT distilled) variants. **Falsifiable test:** run the SEAL extraction on cached pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute Stage 2 NPZs for base Qwen 2.5 1.5B — compute H_E − H_RT at L19 and check (i) cos to prefill DoM, (ii) cos drift across token positions. If the thought-type axis is rotation-stable while DoM rotates, F-3 is real but is *not* the axis you should be steering on.

2. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is "the" correctness signal at AUROC 0.7731) may be a confound with thought-type structure rather than a pure correctness direction.** SEAL shows execution vs reflection/transition is the dominant separable axis in deep layers (Figure 3, layer 20 t-SNE shows almost no overlap), and excessive reflection/transition strongly correlates with failure. If the L19 prefill DoM partially encodes "this prompt will trigger many reflection/transition thoughts" rather than "this answer will be wrong," then the AUROC is parasitic on the thought-pattern signal that SEAL identified. Mechanism: prefill L19 activations include features predictive of downstream thought-type composition. **Falsifiable test:** project prefill L19 activations onto a thought-type axis (computed by labeling cached MATH-500 generations with SEAL's keyword classifier and taking class-mean diff); compute residual AUROC for correctness *after orthogonalizing out the thought-type axis*. If residual AUROC drops materially below 0.65, F-2's interpretation as a correctness signal is incorrect — it's a verbosity/confusion signal.

3. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is partially pre-empted, and an alternative cheaper recipe pre-empts the steering direction we'd choose.** SEAL is essentially a *successful* test of "latent-space steering at a single mid-layer can move MATH-500 accuracy by 11pp," which is the success-bar H-1 sets out to clear (≥ 3pp). If H-1's DoM-based steering yields less than SEAL's +11pp on the *same* benchmark with a *different* vector, that would refute H-1's premise that the DoM is the right direction to steer. Mechanism: thought-type contrast (E − RT) is a stronger steering axis than correct − incorrect contrast at L19/L20. **Falsifiable test:** run H-1's per-position DoM injection at α∈{−4,−2,0,2,4} *and* run SEAL-style H_E − H_RT injection at every "\n\n" on the same 100 held-out MATH-500 problems with base Qwen 2.5 1.5B. If SEAL beats DoM-steering at matched compute, retire H-1's DoM-based recipe and rewrite the steering line of attack.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) may be a thought-pattern artifact — SEAL provides a non-geometric explanation.** SEAL shows that in DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on MATH-500, incorrect responses contain significantly more reflection/transition thoughts than correct ones. If our 1.5B D-bucket samples (K=1 right, K=8 wrong) systematically have more reflection/transition thoughts than other buckets, the "geometric signature" is a downstream consequence of reasoning-pattern instability, not an intrinsic geometric property. Mechanism: more reflection/transition steps → more long-trace residual-stream variance → distinctive PR signature. **Falsifiable test:** apply SEAL's keyword classifier to cached pathway11_h100 K=1 and K=8 generations, count reflection+transition thoughts per A/B/C/D bucket. If D-bucket has a thought-count distribution distinct from A-bucket at p < 0.01 (Mann-Whitney), F-7's "geometric signature" should be reframed as "behavioral signature with a geometric shadow."

5. **F-2's "prefill predicts correctness" framing is mechanism-blind — SEAL's mid-layer intervention shows the *causal* lever is at the thought-boundary token, not the prefill.** SEAL's intervention is at the "\n\n" boundary token *during* generation, not at prefill. The +11pp gain comes from on-the-fly editing, not from prefill-time prediction. If F-2's prefill signal is purely diagnostic but the actionable causal axis is at thought boundaries, then F-2 should be downgraded from "the project's headline" to "an early-warning byproduct of a downstream thought-pattern axis." Mechanism: prefill L19 reads off priors that get committed downstream at thought boundaries; intervening at thought boundaries is what moves the trajectory. **Falsifiable test:** activation patching at prefill L19 vs. at first 5 "\n\n" tokens — which intervention site moves K=1 accuracy more on matched problems? If thought-boundary patching dominates, the project's narrative needs a structural revision.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering, biggest swing, ~$200/3 H100-days)** ↔ SEAL is a working proof that single-layer mid-stack steering at "\n\n" tokens moves MATH-500 accuracy by +11.0pp on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (67.0 → 78.0). Our H-1 success bar is ≥ 3pp; SEAL has cleared it with a *different* vector. This forces an A/B comparison: DoM steering vs SEAL steering, on the same model + same 100 held-out MATH-500 problems. - **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** ↔ SEAL operates at layer 20 of R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (which has 28 layers like base Qwen 2.5 1.5B), one layer above our L19. Both findings independently identify mid-late layers as the conceptual layer; SEAL via t-SNE separability of thought types, ours via DoM AUROC peaking at L19. Either we're triangulating the same layer for the same reason, or two different signals coincidentally peak there.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ SEAL implicitly assumes the steering direction is rotation-stable enough to apply repeatedly. The two findings disagree about how stable the relevant direction is. Resolution requires running SEAL's vector on our cached 1.5B trace and measuring its rotation, OR running our DoM extraction on R1-Distill and measuring its rotation.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** ↔ SEAL also wins at single-layer; corroborates F-9's "one layer is enough" conclusion. But SEAL's single-layer axis is *thought-type*, not *correctness*, so F-9's redundancy claim should be re-stated: "single-layer is enough *if* you pick the right axis."

- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT shows compressed breathing, "is breathing computation or verbalization?")** ↔ SEAL *suppresses* reflection/transition without removing CoT; this is a partial intermediate point on the verbalization↔computation axis. If breathing magnitude under SEAL's intervention is closer to base than to no-CoT, breathing is more about verbalization volume than reasoning structure.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Thought-segmentation by "\n\n" tokens.** Decompose generation O = (T1, …, T_N) by double-newline. *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU on cached generations. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives us a free per-thought structure on top of cached pathway11 traces.
- **Keyword-based thought-type classifier (E / R / T).** Appendix B keywords (e.g., "Alternatively" → transition, "Wait/let me double-check" → reflection); rest = execution. *Replication cost*: 10 min once we extract the appendix. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed for any of the refutation tests above.
- **Class-mean difference steering vector S = H_E − H_RT at "\n\n" tokens, layer i.** Compute at one transformer block on ~1000 MATH-500 training samples. *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached residuals; or ~30 min H100 to extract residuals at "\n\n" boundaries on R1-Distill. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the core refutation lever for Refutations 1–3.
- **On-the-fly representation intervention H = H + α · S at every "\n\n" boundary.** α = 1.0 default, layer 20 for R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B, layer 55 for QwQ-32B-Preview. *Replication cost*: ~30 min H100 per α value on MATH-500. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct A/B against H-1's DoM steering recipe.
- **Logits-penalty baseline (token-level "wait"/"alternatively" suppression with shift values from {−1,−3,−10,−20}).** Wang et al. 2025 method. *Replication cost*: 30 min H100. *We'd plausibly run this*: probably not — already shown weaker than SEAL, but useful as a control if we replicate.
- **Layer-sweep ablation for steering site.** Apply S at different layers, measure MATH-500 accuracy. Mid-to-late best, generalizes across model sizes. *Replication cost*: ~3 h H100 on 1.5B. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct check on whether L19 (our DoM peak) coincides with steering optimum.
- **t-SNE of per-thought-class representations layer-by-layer.** Diagnostic visualization of separability. *Replication cost*: 10 min CPU on cached residuals. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap visual cross-check that thought-type axis exists in our base Qwen 2.5 1.5B residuals (not just in R1-Distill).

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning-pattern decomposition (Execution / Reflection / Transition).** Reframes "long CoT" not as a single dial but as a 3-component composition. Intersects with H-22 (Abstract-CoT) and our breathing analysis: breathing amplitude could be largely driven by the reflection/transition fraction rather than total length.
- **"Concept-level" steering > "token-level" steering.** Argues that reflection/transition can be expressed in many surface forms ("let me double-check", "another approach is"), so logits penalty fails while latent-space steering succeeds. Reframes our DoM as a candidate concept-level axis but suggests thought-type is a stronger candidate concept.
- **Win-win efficiency-and-accuracy claim.** SEAL improves accuracy *and* reduces tokens; this is the inverse of the test-time-scaling narrative (more tokens → better). Intersects directly with our 1.5B vs 7B headline numbers (48.6% / 73.2%): if SEAL applies to base Qwen 2.5 1.5B it could narrow the 1.5B → 7B gap without parameter scaling.
- **Steering-vector transferability.** Math-extracted vector works on GSM8K and LiveCodeBench. Aligns with our suspicion (NEXT_EXPERIMENTS / breathing replicates on code) that a math-derived axis generalizes; gives a concrete recipe to test.
