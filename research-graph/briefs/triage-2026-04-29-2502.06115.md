# Triage brief — 2502.06115 — Task-driven Layerwise Additive Activation Intervention

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06115
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1's heuristic-DoM-as-steering-vector premise.** H-1 plans to inject `α · w_t_norm`, where `w_t_norm` is the position-conditioned mean-difference (DoM) direction — a *passive* contrast extracted from a probe, never optimized for steering. This paper shows (Table 6, Llama3-8B, N=10) that an optimization-based Δ minimizing the actual generation loss with lasso + group-lasso regularization beats every heuristic and PEFT baseline (Function Vectors, ICV, IA³, Soft Prompt, LoRA) across Eng-Fr, Eng-Ger, Antonym. Mechanism: the DoM averages over noisy correct/incorrect prompt-pair contrasts; optimization extracts the steering-actionable component while sparsity regularization controls the small-N regime. Falsifiable: implement Eq. 2 of the paper at L19 last-token on N=10 MATH-500 problems with NLL of the gold answer as target loss, sweep α, compare ΔAcc vs heuristic-DoM steering on the held-out 490 problems. If the optimized Δ produces ≥1pp larger MATH-500 K=1 lift at the same coverage, H-1 should be rewritten to *use the optimized vector*, and the heuristic DoM falls back to a probing-only artifact.

2. **Refutes F-2's privileged-layer (L19) framing as architecture-universal.** F-2 reports L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers — L19 is at depth 0.68). This paper's optimization picks ℓ=4 on Llama3-8B (32 layers — depth 0.125) for rule-understanding tasks. The paper explicitly argues (§3 introduction, §3.1) that *late-layer interventions are degraded by representation shifts accumulated from earlier layers*, motivating a shallow-layer optimum. If the correctness-prediction signal also lives shallower than L19 once we move from passive probe to active steering objective, F-2's L19-specialness is a probe-objective artifact, not a layer where correctness is "really" encoded. Falsifiable: run the paper's optimization (Eq. 2, with binary-correct/incorrect target) at every layer ℓ ∈ {1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28} on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with N=10 MATH-500 pairs. If the optimum is at ℓ ≪ 19, F-2's L19 status is downgraded to "probe-objective optimum, not generation-objective optimum".

3. **Refutes F-3's "two distinct circuits" reading of cos(prefill, final) = 0.046.** F-3 interprets the near-orthogonality as evidence for two *separate* mechanisms (prefill encodes a problem-difficulty signal, final encodes an answer-confidence signal). This paper's results show that *last-token activations alone*, intervened with a single optimized Δ at one layer, suffice to steer Llama3-8B across heterogeneous tasks (translation, antonyms, opinion distributions). If a single last-token vector can carry a task-signal that subsumes the work of a probe over the prefill, then prefill and final-token DoMs may be two different *optimum-of-different-loss* projections of one underlying task-success subspace — not two circuits. Mechanism: the heuristic DoM finds the maximum-variance contrast within each pool (prefill vs final); these can be approximately orthogonal even when both project onto the same 2-D "task-success" plane. Falsifiable: optimize Eq. 2-style Δ at L19 last-token on MATH-500 correct/incorrect targets; measure cos(Δ_optimized, prefill_DoM) and cos(Δ_optimized, final_DoM). If Δ_optimized has cos > 0.3 with both prefill and final DoMs, F-3 must be rewritten — the 0.046 number is between two suboptimal projections, not between two circuits.

4. **Refutes H-1's per-position elaborate design.** H-1 plans 28 position buckets × dimension-sized direction extraction. This paper achieves task-steering with a single Δ injected at a single position (last token) at a single layer — and demonstrates additive composition of task-specific Δ vectors (Table 2). If single-position last-token intervention is already strong, H-1's per-position complexity is unnecessary engineering. Falsifiable: ablate H-1 to single-position intervention at L19 last-token; if the lift is ≥80% of the per-position version's lift, the per-position design is over-engineered and should be simplified before run.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Layerwise additive activation intervention with lasso + group-lasso (Eq. 2)** — single vector Δ ∈ ℝ^D added to last-token activations at one layer ℓ, optimized to minimize task-loss with `γ‖Δ‖₁ + λ Σ_h ‖Δ_h‖₂` regularization (group = attention head). *Replication cost*: ~2h H100 once code is wired into our pipeline (loss is autograd; N=10 trivial). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct candidate replacement for H-1's heuristic DoM.
- **Additive task-vector composition (Δ_τ + Δ_τ')** — sum of independently-optimized task vectors used as a no-extra-training intervention for the composed task. *Replication cost*: trivial once Δ vectors exist (~minutes). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to combining "be correct" Δ with "use scratchpad" Δ if H-1 splits MATH-500 task into subtasks.
- **NLL task loss for next-token prediction tasks; KL task loss for distribution-matching tasks** — two concrete loss families for the same intervention framework. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — NLL is the natural choice for MATH-500 final-answer steering.
- **Group-lasso-by-attention-head sparsity** — penalizes whole-head usage rather than individual coordinates, motivated by Hernandez et al. and Li et al. attention-head sparsity findings. *Replication cost*: trivial (one extra loss term). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — also gives an interpretable head-importance map for free that we could compare against H-13 (head-level attribution).
- **N=10 demonstration small-data protocol** — paper deliberately operates in low-data regime to show the optimization is sample-efficient; 10 input/output pairs per task. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — matches our typical "we have 500 MATH-500 problems but want to validate from few".

## Approaches & framings

- **Single-layer intervention principle ("avoid representation shift")** — paper's central reframe: rather than scatter Δ across layers (Function Vectors, ITI), focus on one layer because later-layer activations are downstream of earlier-layer interventions, which contaminates effects. Intersects F-9 (single-layer DoM ≈ CoE-60) — gives the F-9 redundancy a *mechanistic explanation*.
- **Task vectors as algebraic objects** — interventions form a (linear) algebra under additive composition. Intersects H-1 (steering vector design) — suggests H-1 should be designed as a vector-space-of-task-fixes rather than a single fix.
- **Optimization vs heuristic ICL signal extraction** — reframe of the entire steering literature: heuristics (mean-difference, FV) are unconditionally improvable by gradient-based optimization with regularization. Intersects every steering hypothesis we have (H-1, H-12).

- Regularization: λ = γ = 0.01 (group lasso = lasso parameter, both small).
- Optimizer details unspecified; assume Adam with default lr from PEFT defaults.
- Hardware: 4× NVIDIA A5000 (sufficient for 8B; for our 1.5B/7B Qwen runs we have H100 oversupply).
- Code: https://github.com/HieuNT91/LayerwiseIntervention.git (PyTorch, public).
- N=10 train pairs per task; metric = Exact Match + GPT-4-as-judge ("GPT-Eval").
- Layer scan in Figure 1 is for *unregularized* losses; with regularization the layer-sensitivity may differ — caveat for any replication.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2502.06115 — Task-driven Layerwise Additive Activation Intervention (Nguyen, Nguyen, Nguyen, Nguyen, 2025)

**Relevance:** Optimization-based replacement for the heuristic mean-difference (DoM) steering vector that H-1 plans to use. The paper's central claim is that a single Δ vector at one layer, optimized via NLL or KL task-loss with lasso + group-lasso regularization, beats every heuristic intervention baseline (Function Vectors, ICV, ITI) and even PEFT baselines (LoRA, IA³, Soft Prompt) in the N=10 small-data regime on rule-understanding and opinion-distribution tasks. The single-layer principle is justified mechanistically: scattering Δ across layers is degraded by accumulated representation shift in later layers — which independently corroborates F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).

**Key claim we tested:** A single-layer optimized Δ outperforms heuristic activation-extraction interventions in the small-N regime; the optimal intervention layer is shallow (ℓ=4 on Llama3-8B with 32 layers), not late.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — the paper's optimization framework is directly applicable to H-1, F-2, F-3. P11-FE73 / FE74 / FE75 will test (a) whether optimized L19 Δ beats heuristic L19 DoM on MATH-500, (b) whether the generation-objective optimal layer matches our probe-objective L19 or is shallower, and (c) whether the optimized Δ overlaps with both prefill and final DoMs (which would refute F-3's "two distinct circuits" reading of cos = 0.046). The paper's single-layer-vs-scattered argument *corroborates* F-9 with a mechanistic explanation (representation drift) we should annotate.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73 (heuristic-vs-optimized Δ on MATH-500), P11-FE74 (layer scan), P11-FE75 (Δ-vs-DoM cosine), H-71, H-72, and modification to H-1.
