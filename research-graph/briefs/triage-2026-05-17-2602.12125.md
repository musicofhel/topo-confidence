# Triage brief — 2602.12125 — Learning beyond Teacher: Generalized On-Policy Distillation with Reward Extrapolation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12125
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

1. **F-2 practical relevance via implicit-reward dominance.** The paper demonstrates that the token-level implicit reward log(π*/π_ref) provides dense per-token supervision that captures "quality" of each generated token. If this logit-space signal (which requires no hidden-state extraction, only forward-pass logits) is a better correctness predictor than L19 prefill DoM (AUROC 0.7731), it would undercut the practical value of F-2 — why probe 1536-dim residual streams when a simple log-probability ratio suffices? **Mechanism:** compute per-problem average implicit reward from a teacher-student pair on MATH-500 and compare AUROC against DoM. **Falsifiable by:** running the implicit-reward predictor on MATH-500 with Qwen-2.5-1.5B as student and 7B as teacher; if its AUROC < 0.77, F-2's practical case holds.

2. **F-2/F-3 refutation via student-surpasses-teacher distillation.** ExOPD with λ=1.25 produces 1.5B students that surpass 30B teachers on math reasoning (Table 3: 25.4 avg vs 23.1 OPD baseline). If we distill a 1.5B model via ExOPD to achieve accuracy > 48.6% on MATH-500, extract L19 prefill activations, and find that DoM AUROC drops or stays flat despite higher accuracy, this would refute the premise that DoM tracks a causal mechanism for correctness — it would be a correlate of the *untrained* model's geometry, not of the correctness-generating computation itself. **Mechanism:** ExOPD-distilled 1.5B achieves higher accuracy but DoM AUROC doesn't improve proportionally. **Falsifiable by:** running H-366 with ExOPD instead of RevKD and measuring both accuracy and DoM AUROC on the distilled checkpoint.

3. **F-8 refutation via reward-interpolation calibration.** The paper shows that reward interpolation (λ < 1) produces students whose behavior lies smoothly between reference and teacher. This means the distillation process itself creates an *inherently calibrated* model — one that "knows" how much to commit to the teacher's strategy. If such a model's built-in calibration (measured via ECE or selective-prediction accuracy) outperforms our external DoM-gated selective prediction (71.6% at 50% coverage, F-8), it would suggest that training-time calibration dominates representation-probing-based abstention. **Mechanism:** λ < 1 student has lower coverage but higher accuracy on uncertain problems, beating F-8's coverage-accuracy frontier. **Falsifiable by:** training a λ=0.75 OPD variant and comparing its self-calibrated selective-prediction curve against DoM-gated prediction.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-11 (Big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B):** Directly addressed. ExOPD provides a principled framework with reward scaling λ and flexible reference model choice for the 7B→1.5B distillation experiment. However, H-11 is LOW priority with its motivator eliminated by EXP-031 (7B vs 1.5B transfer asymmetry was not observed at 1024 tokens). The paper uses Qwen3 models rather than our Qwen-2.5, adding a family-gap complication.

- **H-366 (Distillation transfers correctness behavior but not the prefill DoM direction):** HIGH priority and closely connected. The paper's key finding that ExOPD students can *surpass* teachers means the student's internal representations must diverge from the teacher's in some meaningful way. This makes H-366's prediction (accuracy transfers, DoM doesn't) more plausible — the student may develop its own correctness pathway rather than inheriting the teacher's geometric signature. ExOPD would be a stronger test case than RevKD for H-366 because the student explicitly *extrapolates beyond* the teacher's reward surface.

- **H-367 (Prefill direction stability under fine-tuning):** OPD is yet another fine-tuning regime to test alongside SFT and RevKD. The paper's interpolation results (λ < 1 produces intermediate behavior) would predict a smooth rotation of the DoM direction as λ increases — testable directly.

- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The implicit reward log(π*/π_ref) is a competing correctness signal. At cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, the prefill and final-token directions are orthogonal — the implicit reward operates at the token-generation level (closer to final-token), raising the question of whether two orthogonal correctness signals coexist or one dominates.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Implicit reward computation (log π*/π_ref per token)** — Token-level dense reward from log-probability ratio between teacher and reference model.
  *Replication cost*: Requires forward passes through both teacher and student on MATH-500; ~2h on H100 for Qwen-2.5-7B teacher + 1.5B student.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires GPU forward passes with two models, not applicable to cached NPZs.

- **Reward scaling (G-OPD with λ parameter)** — Multiplicative factor on the implicit reward vs KL regularization weight. Optimal at λ=1.25 across their experiments.
  *Replication cost*: Full training run, multiple days H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train models, we probe cached representations.

- **Reward correction (swapping reference model to teacher's pre-RL base)** — Replacing student's base as reference with teacher's pre-RL base for more accurate implicit reward in cross-scale distillation.
  *Replication cost*: Additional model inference cost (~1.5x per training step).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — same as above, training infrastructure required.

- **Token-level rollout correction** — Mitigates training-inference mismatch in on-policy sampling (citing Liu et al. 2025b).
  *Replication cost*: Implementation detail for GRPO/OPD training.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training methodology, not probing.

## Approaches & framings

- **OPD = dense KL-constrained RL:** The paper reframes on-policy distillation as a special case of RL where the reward is the implicit reward (log-probability ratio) and the KL coefficient is fixed at 1. This lens connects distillation and RL through the same objective, just with different reward densities (sparse outcome vs dense token-level). Intersects our framings tangentially: both projects use per-token signals to assess quality, but we probe hidden states while they optimize logits.

- **Reward extrapolation as "learning beyond teacher":** The key conceptual move — setting λ > 1 pushes the student past the teacher's capability boundary. This implies that internal representations in the distilled model are not simply compressed copies of the teacher's; the student develops novel computation. Relevant to H-366's question about whether DoM transfers through distillation.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- ExOPD uses fixed λ=1.25 across all experiments after initial tuning — no per-task tuning needed.
- GRPO training: batch 128, rollout n=8, max response 16,384 tokens, LR 1e-6, 500 steps for math RL.
- G-OPD training: batch 1024, rollout n=1, LR 1e-5, 50 steps (same-size) or 100 steps (strong-to-weak).
- Evaluation: temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, 32 solutions per math problem, Math-Verify for answer checking.
- Built on verl framework (Sheng et al. 2024).
- Code available: https://github.com/RUCBM/G-OPD
