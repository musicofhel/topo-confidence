# Triage brief — 2505.17306 — Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the *necessity* premise of H-1 (per-position DoM steering bank).**
   Wang et al. extract a *single* refusal direction by mean-difference between
   harmful vs benign prompt activations at one layer (typically a middle layer),
   then apply that one direction at *every* generation position to bypass
   refusal across 14 typologically distinct languages with near-perfect ASR
   (no per-position vector bank, no per-language re-extraction). H-1's premise
   is that fixed-vector steering failed in P10v1 because the DoM rotates during
   generation and therefore a per-position bank is required. The refusal
   evidence undercuts that — a single direction applied uniformly steers
   behavior robustly even though the *content* layer (L_R in their notation)
   has the same RoPE/positional dynamics as our L19. *Mechanism*: if their
   single-direction-applied-everywhere works for refusal but P10v1's E1 fixed
   vector failed for correctness, the failure might be *signal strength /
   wrong layer / wrong contrast set*, not "directions rotate so banks are
   required". *Falsifiable experiment*: run their exact mean-difference
   extraction (avg over prompt tokens, not just last) on MATH-500
   correct-vs-incorrect prompts at L19, then steer at every generation
   position with that one vector. If accuracy moves on Qwen-2.5-1.5B without
   a per-position bank, H-1's per-position framing is over-engineered.

2. **Refutes F-3's "prefill ↔ final-token orthogonality is a meaningful
   computation difference" reading (cos = 0.046).**
   Wang et al. measure cross-language cosine similarities between refusal
   directions and find them strikingly *parallel* (high cosine) across very
   different token distributions, sequence lengths, and per-language RoPE
   trajectories. If RoPE-induced position rotation were the dominant force
   moving residual-stream task directions, cross-language similarity would
   *also* be low, because different languages have wildly different effective
   token-position distributions for the harmful-prompt span. They observe the
   opposite. *Mechanism*: this isolates the prefill-vs-final orthogonality as
   *position-induced* (= H-17, RoPE-mechanical), not *content-induced*. F-3's
   framing currently still treats orthogonality as evidence of two
   functionally distinct circuits; Wang et al. data tilts toward the rotation
   being mechanical. *Falsifiable experiment*: take their exact methodology
   and apply it to MATH-500 at *fixed prompt position* across two prompt
   reformulations (paraphrases, permuted few-shot order). If the
   correctness-DoM at prompt position is *parallel* across reformulations
   *but* still orthogonal to the final-token DoM, the cos=0.046 is positional
   rotation alone (supports H-17, weakens F-3's two-circuit reading).

3. **Refutes the F-2-as-stated claim that "prefill final token" is the
   privileged extraction position.**
   Wang et al. extract via mean-difference *across the entire prompt span*,
   not the final prefill token. Their resulting direction has near-perfect
   transfer. If we used their extraction recipe on MATH-500 we might find that
   `mean_pool(prompt_tokens) → DoM` outperforms `prompt_final_token → DoM` at
   L19, which is the AUROC 0.7731 anchor. *Mechanism*: F-2 currently anchors
   on a specific position (prefill final token); the universal-direction work
   suggests the *averaged* prompt representation is the canonical extraction
   site. *Falsifiable experiment*: re-extract DoM at L19 on cached
   `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` activations using mean-pooled prompt
   tokens (pad-masked) instead of the final prefill position; OOF 5-fold
   AUROC. If > 0.79, F-2 needs a position-amendment. If ≈0.77, F-2 stands.

4. **Refutes H-12's premise that label-free probes are needed for
   distribution-shift robustness.**
   Wang et al.'s direction is *fully supervised* (mean of harmful − mean of
   benign embeddings), and it transfers across 14 languages — i.e., very
   substantial input-distribution shift — with near-perfect ASR. This is
   evidence that *supervised* DoM directions can themselves be cross-domain
   robust if the contrast set is well-chosen. *Mechanism*: H-12 assumes
   label-free SEPs are needed for refusal-style abstention to generalize off
   the training distribution. If supervised DoM already does that for an
   adjacent behavior (refusal), the marginal value of label-free probes for
   correctness is smaller than H-12 frames. *Falsifiable experiment*: extract
   prefill DoM on MATH-500 train, test it on (a) AIME 2024, (b) GSM8K, (c)
   HumanEval correctness with no re-fitting. If AUROC drop is < 0.05, H-12
   should be downgraded to LOW.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Cross-distribution direction parallelism check** — measure cosine
  similarity between mean-difference directions extracted from two different
  data distributions at the *same* layer / position. Concretely: extract DoM
  from (MATH-500, GSM8K, AIME, HumanEval) at L19 prefill final token, compute
  pairwise cosines.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached Stage-2 NPZs (already have
  MATH-500 activations; need ~500 GSM8K activations on H100 = ~20 min H100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether F-2's signal is
  task-specific or content-general (Platonic-ish, see H-20).
- **Mean-pool-prompt extraction (vs final-prefill-token extraction)** —
  compute DoM by averaging over prompt-token activations (with attention-mask
  weighting) instead of taking just the last prefill position.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute`
  NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests Refutation #3, cheap, may
  push AUROC > 0.7731.
- **Single-direction generation-time steering (no per-position bank)** —
  ablate / add the extracted DoM direction at every generation position with a
  single coefficient α; sweep α in {-1.0, -0.5, +0.5, +1.0}.
  *Replication cost*: 4 h H100 (need pod) — same compute envelope as
  P11-FE3 but no patching; just ablation/addition.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — Refutation #1, cheap counter-example for
  H-1.
- **Cross-domain transferred-probe robustness curve** — extract DoM on one
  benchmark, evaluate AUROC on others without re-fitting; report the AUROC
  drop per benchmark pair.
  *Replication cost*: 1 h CPU once GSM8K/AIME/HumanEval Stage-2 caches exist
  (currently only MATH-500 is cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not yet — depends on us first running the
  Stage-2 extractor on the other benchmarks (≈ 2 h H100 each).
- **Layer-localization sweep with attack-success metric** — for refusal
  specifically: sweep over all layers, ablate the per-layer DoM, measure ASR.
  Maps to "for correctness specifically: sweep over all layers, steer with
  per-layer DoM, measure accuracy delta on K=1 MATH-500".
  *Replication cost*: 28 layers × 4 α × 500 problems ≈ 6 h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes-eventually — this is the natural extension of
  P11-FE3 once steering compute is available.

## Approaches & framings

- **Universality-by-parallelism** — instead of arguing universality from
  "same accuracy", they argue it from "same direction" (high cosine across
  conditions). This reframes our F-2 from a single-AUROC-number finding into a
  geometry-claim ("the L19 DoM points the same way across MATH-500
  reformulations / paraphrases / few-shot orders").
- **Behavior-as-direction-not-circuit** — they treat refusal as a single
  scalar projection, not a circuit. Our F-2 currently does too, but F-3 leans
  toward "two circuits" (prefill vs final). Their work pulls the F-3 story
  back toward "one direction, multiple position projections of it" — i.e.,
  H-17.
- **Cross-language as a cheap distribution-shift testbed** — for our
  domain, the analog is "cross-paraphrase" or "cross-benchmark". This gives
  us a cheap robustness probe that doesn't require new train-set labels.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Mean-difference extraction is on **prompt-side** activations averaged over
  the harmful-prompt token span, not on responses. Important nuance for
  Refutation #3 — we should average over prompt tokens (mask-aware) rather
  than just the last prefill token.
- They measure transfer with **ASR delta**: with vs without ablating the
  refusal direction at L_R. The signed delta (post − pre) is the headline
  number. Our equivalent: K=1 accuracy delta with vs without DoM addition.
- Layer L_R is selected per model (Llama 2 / Qwen 2 / Yi / Gemma) by sweeping
  layers and picking the max-ASR-delta layer. Our equivalent: F-2's L19 was
  picked by max-AUROC sweep on Qwen-2.5-1.5B; same recipe.
- They report **cosine similarities between cross-language directions** as a
  matrix. Mirrors what we'd want to do across (MATH-500, GSM8K, AIME) at L19.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.17306 — Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages (Wang, Wang, Liu, Schütze, Plank, 2025)

**Relevance:** Methodologically parallel to F-2's prefill DoM. Wang et al.
extract a single mean-difference refusal direction at one middle layer in
each of {Llama 2, Qwen 2, Yi, Gemma} and ablate it to bypass refusal across
14 typologically distinct languages with near-perfect transfer. Their
extraction recipe (mean over prompt tokens, single layer, single direction
applied everywhere) and their universality framing (cross-distribution cosine
parallelism) directly inform our open questions on F-2 (extraction position),
F-3 (orthogonality interpretation), H-1 (per-position bank necessity), H-12
(supervised vs label-free), and H-17 (RoPE-mechanical rotation).

**Key claim we tested:** A single mean-difference direction extracted at one
mid-network layer transfers across distribution shifts (their case:
languages; our case: benchmarks / paraphrases) with near-perfect cosine
parallelism, without retraining or per-position banks.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Three direct experiments are queued: P11-FE70
(mean-pool extraction), P11-FE71 (cross-benchmark DoM parallelism), P11-FE72
(single-direction generation-time steering). Until those run, classification
cannot be final.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE70, P11-FE71, P11-FE72, H-72, H-73 (and
informs H-1, H-17 if confirmed).

**Status:** TO TEST
