# Triage brief — 2605.07407 — Emergent Symbolic Structure in Health Foundation Models: Extraction, Alignment, and Cross-Modal Transfer

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07407
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

**R1 — ICA may outperform DoM/PC1 for correctness prediction (refutes F-2)**

F-2 establishes that a single supervised direction (DoM ≈ PC1, cos 0.9216) captures the dominant correctness signal at AUROC 0.7731. This paper shows ICA extracts sparse, selective directions from frozen embeddings that concentrate target-specific information into individual components — their ICA symbols have higher selectivity than PCA directions. If ICA applied to our cached L19 prefill activations produces a correctness-predicting direction with AUROC significantly above 0.7731 AND low cosine similarity with DoM/PC1, it would refute F-2's implicit sufficiency claim. Falsification experiment: run ICA on cached 500×1536 L19 prefill activations, project correct/incorrect labels onto each IC, compute per-component AUROC. If max IC AUROC > 0.7731 and cos(best_IC, DoM) < 0.5, F-2 is refuted.

**R2 — CCA alignment between prefill and final-token may find shared structure (refutes F-3)**

F-3 establishes prefill/final-token DoM directions are orthogonal (cos = 0.046). This paper demonstrates that CCA between independently-trained modality-specific embeddings recovers shared subspaces with correlations approaching r = 0.99 — even when raw embedding transfer is near-chance (AUC ≈ 0.50). Their method explicitly solves for maximal shared structure under linear projection. If CCA applied to our prefill vs final-token L19 activations (same 500 samples, two 1536-d views) finds canonical correlations significantly above 0 in dimensions beyond PC1, it would show that the cos = 0.046 orthogonality is superficial — the views share substantial structure in a rotated subspace. Falsification: run CCA on prefill vs final-token L19 NPZs; if top-5 canonical correlations average > 0.3 and the shared subspace carries correctness-predictive variance, F-3 is weakened.

**R3 — Selectivity Index shows correctness signal is distributed, not concentrated (refutes F-2 + cov-spectrum ceiling)**

The paper's Selectivity Index (SI = Hoyer_sparsity × L1_norm) quantifies whether a direction's predictive power is concentrated or distributed across targets. If we compute SI for our PCA/ICA directions w.r.t. correctness labels and find HIGH selectivity (concentrated in one component) — that confirms F-2. But if SI is LOW (distributed across many sparse components each contributing weakly), it challenges the 0.7928 cov-spectrum ceiling as artificially capped by the linear-probe paradigm, suggesting nonlinear combinations of sparse symbols could exceed it. Falsification: compute SI for each of 20 top PCA components and all ICA components; if median SI < 0.3 and an ensemble of sparse symbols achieves AUROC > 0.80, F-2's single-direction sufficiency is refuted.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: Paper's core method — PCA/ICA/NMF decomposition of frozen embeddings — is directly applicable to our L19 prefill activations. Their finding that ICA produces sparser, more selective directions than PCA motivates testing whether ICA decomposition of our 500×1536 activation matrix produces a direction that beats DoM at correctness prediction.

- **F-1 (breathing universality)**: Their cross-modal alignment (PPG ViT ↔ Accel ViT) recovering shared symbolic structure across independently-trained encoders parallels our cross-architecture breathing claim. Their "95% transfer retention" metric provides a quantitative benchmark for what "shared geometry" means across models.

- **F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality, cos = 0.046)**: Their CCA + Hungarian alignment methods are designed precisely for the case where two embedding spaces encode overlapping information in non-aligned bases. The cos = 0.046 raw orthogonality may coexist with high CCA correlation in a rotated subspace.

- **F-10 (PH null)**: Their W1 (Wasserstein-1) distance between condition-positive/negative groups is a distribution-level metric that doesn't require topological machinery. It provides a non-PH geometric test for group separability on our cached activations.

- **H-4 (Sharpness Dimension ↔ Breathing)**: Their finding that a low-dimensional shared subspace captures most predictive information across modalities parallels the hypothesis that breathing and the Sharpness Dimension are formal correspondents — both describe effective dimensionality fluctuations in embeddings.

## Methodologies extracted

- **ICA decomposition of frozen embeddings** — Independent Component Analysis applied post-hoc to cached activation matrices; produces statistically independent, sparse directions from the same data PCA would act on.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (scikit-learn FastICA on 500×1536 matrix).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct drop-in on cached NPZs, immediate comparison to DoM/PC1.

- **NMF decomposition of frozen embeddings** — Non-negative Matrix Factorization producing parts-based additive components; requires non-negative input (would need shift or apply to squared/abs activations).
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (sklearn NMF + preprocessing).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — requires non-negativity preprocessing that might distort signal, lower priority than ICA.

- **Selectivity Index (SI = Hoyer_sparsity × L1_norm)** — Quantifies how concentrated a direction's association is across multiple targets.
  *Replication cost*: 2min CPU (trivial to compute once we have ICA/PCA directions + correctness labels).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap diagnostic of whether correctness signal is sparse or distributed.

- **W1 (Wasserstein-1) distance for group separability** — Earth-mover's distance between the distribution of a given symbol's activations in correct vs incorrect groups, z-scored to unit variance.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (scipy.stats.wasserstein_distance per component).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — non-parametric separability metric, complements AUROC.

- **CCA cross-view alignment** — Canonical Correlation Analysis finding shared subspaces between two representations of the same subjects. Sparse variant adds L1 penalty.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (sklearn CCA or custom sparse CCA).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to prefill vs final-token views (same 500 problems, different token positions).

- **Hungarian bijective symbol matching** — Optimal assignment on a correlation matrix between two sets of directions; maximizes sum of matched correlations.
  *Replication cost*: 2min CPU (scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — for 1.5B vs 7B direction alignment if both NPZs available.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Symbols" as named interpretable directions** — Reframes PCA/ICA components as a "symbol dictionary" with selectivity profiles per target. Intersects F-2: our DoM is a "correctness symbol" in their language; the question is whether additional symbols exist with complementary predictive power.

- **Transfer retention as alignment metric** — AUC_transfer / AUC_in-domain quantifies how much structure survives a domain shift. Could formalize cross-scale (1.5B→7B) or cross-benchmark (MATH→code) transfer of our DoM direction.

- **Near-symmetry of alignment** — Forward and reverse alignment produce almost identical results (r = 0.995, |ΔAUC| = 0.009). Tests whether our prefill→final vs final→prefill alignment is symmetric — asymmetry would imply causal directionality.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Embedding dimension: 256 per 1-min window, aggregated by averaging per subject.
- ICA via FastICA; NMF via standard sklearn; PCA via eigendecomposition of covariance.
- CCA sparse variant: L1 regularization with λ grid [8e-2, 4e-2, 2e-2, 1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4]; saturates at λ ≤ 2e-2 with >95% alignment retained.
- Data efficiency: cross-modal transfer saturates at ~7,500 paired samples (relevant: our N=500 is well below this).
- Selectivity Index: SI_i = H(w_i) · ||w_i||_1 where H is Hoyer sparsity measure.
- All methods operate on frozen embeddings with no model retraining.
