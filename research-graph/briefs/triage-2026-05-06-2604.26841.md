# Triage brief — 2604.26841 — Language Diffusion Models are Associative Memories Capable of Retrieving Unseen Data

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26841
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature):** The paper shows that "collective" basin geometry in the memorization regime is an artifact of dataset size—when models are in the memorization regime (small training set relative to capacity), clusters form around training points that appear as collective signatures but dissolve entirely once the model transitions to generalization. If D-bucket problems in Qwen-2.5-1.5B sit in a memorization regime (the model has seen few examples of that difficulty class), F-7's "distinctive collective geometric signature" could be a memorization attractor rather than a meaningful correctness signal. **Falsifiable:** Compute per-problem conditional entropy (from logit-lens on cached L19 activations) for D-bucket vs. non-D-bucket problems; if D-bucket problems show systematically lower conditional entropy (sharper basins = memorization regime), the collective signature is explained by memorization proximity rather than difficulty-specific geometry.

2. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731):** The paper demonstrates that token-level conditional entropy alone separates "recovered" (correctly handled) from "unrecovered" tokens with near-zero entropy for stable tokens. This suggests the DoM direction may simply be a proxy for a memorization/generalization phase boundary—problems the model has memorized (low conditional entropy basin) will be answered correctly, and the L19 prefill direction merely detects proximity to a memorization attractor. The predictive power would then be explained by memorization detection, not a meaningful "correctness geometry." **Falsifiable:** Compute conditional entropy from logit outputs on the same 500 MATH-500 problems; if conditional entropy achieves ≥ AUROC 0.77 for correctness prediction and cos(entropy_ranking, DoM_ranking) > 0.85, the DoM direction is largely explained as a memorization detector.

3. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):** The paper's framework predicts that "familiarity" and "decomposability" are not separate dimensions—they are both manifestations of where a problem sits relative to the memorization-to-generalization transition. Familiar problems are near memorized attractors (low conditional entropy), while decomposable problems may lie near novel attractors that formed in the generalization regime. The distinction H-5 posits would collapse into a single memorization-distance axis. **Falsifiable:** Extract per-problem conditional entropy and regress against both the DoM score and a decomposability proxy (e.g., number of reasoning steps required); if conditional entropy fully mediates the DoM-correctness relationship (partial correlation DoM→correctness | entropy ≈ 0), H-5's two-axis framing is unnecessary.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's conditional entropy metric is a direct competitor/explanation for the prefill DoM direction. If per-token conditional entropy from logit-lens can match the AUROC, DoM may be a learned proxy for memorization proximity rather than a geometric correctness signal.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature):** The memorization-to-generalization phase transition framework predicts that problems the model hasn't fully generalized on (D-bucket) will cluster in a distinct basin region—this is an alternative explanation for F-7's collective signature that doesn't require a difficulty-specific geometric structure.

- **F-10 (PH adds negative signal beyond covariance):** The paper's connection between conditional entropy and energy landscape curvature (Appendix C: H(x|z_t) ∝ −½ log det H(z_t)) provides a principled reason why PH would be uninformative—if the residual stream landscape is well-described by a single quadratic basin (Gaussian null), PH captures only noise around that basin. The paper's framework predicts F-10's null result.

- **F-4 (correct trajectories collapse harder):** The asymmetric collapse finding maps onto the paper's asymmetric basin structure: memorized (correct) sequences have sharper basins (lower conditional entropy → more deterministic attractors), while incorrect sequences lie in flatter landscape regions. The collapse = basin sharpness correspondence.

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS):** The paper's Appendix C formally relates conditional entropy to Hessian curvature. "Dimensional breathing" (expansion then collapse) could map to the model traversing from flat generalization-regime landscape during prefill to sharp memorization-regime attractors at the final token—providing a formal energy-landscape interpretation of H-4.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Token recovery rate as basin-of-attraction probe** — Corrupt tokens at varying noise levels and measure fraction recovered after denoising, as a function of dataset size. Quantifies basin size around specific inputs.
  *Replication cost*: Requires forward passes through the model at multiple noise levels; ~2-4h H100 for 500 problems at 10 noise levels.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires diffusion model machinery not applicable to autoregressive Qwen.

- **Conditional entropy as memorization/generalization discriminator** — Compute H(x^ℓ | z^{1:L}_t) = −Σ_k p_θ(x^ℓ|z)_k · log p_θ(x^ℓ|z)_k per token, sum across sequence. Memorized sequences have near-zero entropy; generalized sequences have finite entropy.
  *Replication cost*: 20-40min CPU if using cached logit outputs; ~1h H100 if extracting fresh logits from Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we can compute per-token entropy from Qwen's output logits on the 500 MATH-500 problems and correlate with DoM scores / correctness labels. This is the most directly testable methodology.

- **Classification margin analysis via pseudo-likelihood gradient** — Decompose the learning signal into Hebbian term × margin penalty (Eq. 11). The gradient dL/dW ∝ −(1/P)Σ x^ℓ x^m [1 − tanh(M^ℓ(x))] identifies which patterns have the smallest margins.
  *Replication cost*: Conceptual/analytical only—no direct computation path on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires access to model weights' gradient structure, not applicable to frozen-extractor pipeline.

- **Greedy (deterministic) vs stochastic sampling comparison** — Replace stochastic sampling with argmax to isolate basin geometry from sampling noise.
  *Replication cost*: N/A for autoregressive models.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — specific to diffusion model reverse process.

## Approaches & framings

- **Memorization-to-generalization as phase transition, detected by conditional entropy convergence:** The framing that memorization and generalization are sharp phases (not a smooth continuum) governed by dataset-size-to-model-capacity ratio. Intersects H-5/F-7: D-bucket problems may be in a different phase than A/B/C problems.

- **Basins of attraction without explicit energy (conditional likelihood suffices):** Dropping the requirement for an explicit energy function extends AM theory to feed-forward architectures (transformers). Intersects F-10: explains why PH (which implicitly assumes energy-landscape structure) is uninformative—the relevant attractor structure exists in conditional probability space, not geometric space.

- **Entropy gap as confidence calibration:** The "entropy gap" between memorized vs generated sequences narrows with model scale. Intersects F-2/F-8: the DoM-based selective prediction may be detecting the entropy gap—problems where the model is in its memorization regime (low entropy gap) are answered correctly with high confidence.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- UDDMs trained with diffusion transformer backbone (DiT architecture from Peebles & Xie 2023)
- Three model scales tested: Tiny (~24M), Small (~135M), Medium (~384M)
- Sequence length 128, GPT-2 tokenizer, 1M training iterations
- Training fractions sampled from 10^-4 to 1.0 (54 models per scale, 162 total)
- Greedy sampling = replace Cat(·) with argmax(·) in reverse process
- Conditional entropy computed at t = 10^-5 (near-clean) for practical deployment metric
- Code appears to build on Sahoo et al. 2024 [44] codebase
