# Triage brief — 2605.03327 — DGPO: Distribution Guided Policy Optimization for Fine Grained Credit Assignment

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03327
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — prefill DoM may be a GRPO training artifact, not an intrinsic geometric property.** DGPO demonstrates that GRPO's uniform sequence-level advantage broadcasting creates a qualitatively different training signal from DGPO's entropy-gated token-level credit. If a DGPO-trained Qwen-2.5-1.5B checkpoint has a substantially different L19 prefill geometry (e.g. DoM direction rotates, AUROC drops), then F-2's prefill signal is an artifact of the specific RL objective, not an architecture-intrinsic correctness encoding. *Mechanism:* DGPO reshapes which tokens receive gradient credit during training; tokens that GRPO treats identically get radically different gradients under DGPO. This should alter the internal representations, potentially disrupting the L19 prefill direction that F-2 depends on. *Falsifiable test:* Train Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Base on DAPO-17K with DGPO (matching their 7B recipe), extract L19 prefill activations on MATH-500, compute DoM AUROC, and compare to 0.7731. If cos(DoM_GRPO, DoM_DGPO) < 0.5 or AUROC_DGPO < 0.70, F-2 is partly GRPO-specific. Directly tests H-707.

2. **F-8 (selective prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage, 71.6% answered accuracy) — DGPO's entropy gating implies a strictly better abstention signal exists within the generation itself.** DGPO's entropy-gated scores identify "pivotal reasoning tokens" in real time. If these per-token scores (available during generation, no separate probe needed) predict correctness better than the static prefill DoM direction, then F-8's selective prediction is suboptimal and the prefill-based approach is dominated by a generation-time alternative. *Mechanism:* DGPO computes w_{i,t} = Softmax(d_{i,t} · H(π_θ) / τ) per token; aggregating these scores (e.g. mean, variance, max of w_{i,t} over the CoT) provides a generation-time confidence signal. If this signal's AUROC > 0.7731 on the same MATH-500 population, the prefill direction is superseded. *Falsifiable test:* On a DGPO-trained checkpoint, compute per-token w_{i,t} scores during MATH-500 generation, aggregate to a scalar per problem, evaluate selective-prediction accuracy at 50% coverage. Compare to 71.6%. Directly tests H-282.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective geometric signature) — D-bucket may be the "confident hallucination" mode that DGPO's entropy gate explicitly filters.** DGPO identifies tokens where deviation from reference is high but entropy is low as "confident hallucinations" — exactly the pattern we'd expect from D-bucket problems (wrong but model is confident). If D-bucket membership corresponds to problems where the mean per-token entropy during CoT is systematically low while Hellinger deviation is high, then F-7's "collective geometric signature" is just the manifestation of GRPO failing to differentiate these hallucination-mode problems during training. *Mechanism:* Under DGPO training, D-bucket problems would receive suppressed gradients (low entropy → low w_{i,t}), potentially dissolving the distinct geometric cluster. *Falsifiable test:* On a DGPO-trained checkpoint, extract D-bucket assignments on MATH-500, compute L19 prefill geometry. If D-bucket's distinctive geometric signature (cosine concentration, PR profile) weakens relative to the GRPO-trained baseline, F-7 is GRPO-specific. Tests H-708.

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — DGPO-style importance weighting of trajectory tokens could break the redundancy.** F-9 established redundancy by treating all 60 tokens in the CoE window uniformly. DGPO shows that tokens within a sequence have radically different importance (w_{i,t} varies by orders of magnitude via softmax). If CoE features are recomputed with DGPO-style entropy-gated weighting (upweighting high-deviation high-entropy tokens, downweighting syntactic tokens), the weighted-CoE features might separate from L19 DoM. *Mechanism:* Uniform token averaging in CoE-60 dilutes the signal from pivotal tokens; importance-weighted averaging concentrates it. *Falsifiable test:* Using cached generation traces with per-token logits, compute entropy-gated importance weights, recompute CoE-60 with weighted averaging, and test correlation with L19 DoM. If R² drops below 0.8, F-9's redundancy claim needs qualification. Tests H-6.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** DGPO operates on Qwen-2.5 family (7B, 32B); the same architecture family as our 1.5B. Their token-level credit assignment implies that the internal representations shaped by RL training are objective-dependent. The L19 prefill direction that achieves 0.7731 was learned under whatever RL alignment Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct received — DGPO suggests this direction would shift under different RL. Connects to H-707 (DoM rotation under different RL objectives) and H-282 (GRPO variance-prioritization artifact).

- **F-8 (71.6% at 50% coverage):** DGPO's entropy gating offers a competing abstention mechanism — the generation-time w_{i,t} scores are a "free" confidence signal that doesn't require a separately trained probe. If DGPO-style scores match or exceed F-8's selective prediction performance, the entire prefill-extraction pipeline becomes unnecessary for deployment. Tests H-682 (F-8 RL-fragility).

- **F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature):** The "confident hallucination" filtering via entropy gating maps directly onto D-bucket's operational definition (problems where the model is wrong but appears confident). DGPO's framework predicts that D-bucket problems should be identifiable via low-entropy high-deviation token profiles during generation. Tests H-708.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder):** DGPO's credit reallocation implicitly creates different gradient landscapes for correct vs incorrect trajectories. Under DGPO, correct trajectories' pivotal tokens get amplified gradients while incorrect ones get suppressed (low reward → low advantage). This training dynamic could either amplify or reduce the asymmetric collapse at the final token.

- **H-10 (breathing pattern changes after fine-tuning):** DGPO is a qualitatively different fine-tuning objective from GRPO/DPO. If breathing patterns (F-1) are measured on DGPO-trained vs GRPO-trained checkpoints, any difference directly confirms H-10 and reveals whether the breathing phenomenon is RL-objective-sensitive.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Hellinger distance as token-level divergence metric** — Bounded [0,1] distance between current policy and reference policy logit distributions at each token position, replacing unbounded KL divergence: d_{i,t} = 1 - Σ_a √(π_θ(a) · π_ref(a)).
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU if we have per-token logits cached; H100 hours if we need fresh forward passes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — Hellinger distance can be computed on any two probability vectors; we could compute it on cached activation vectors (after softmax normalization) as a geometry metric, or on logit distributions during generation.

- **Entropy gating mechanism** — Scales deviation by normalized Shannon entropy of the policy distribution to filter confident hallucinations: s_{i,t} = d_{i,t} · H̃^κ_{i,t}. The entropy acts as an "epistemic uncertainty" gate.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached logits; requires per-token vocabulary-level logits.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if per-token logits are cached — the gating formula is trivial to compute. No if we only have hidden states.

- **Temperature-scaled softmax credit reallocation** — Converts per-token scores into importance weights via w_{i,t} = T_i · exp(s_{i,t}/τ) / Σ_j exp(s_{i,j}/τ). Redistributes coarse sequence-level advantage to tokens.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (5min CPU) once scores are computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the softmax reallocation is architecture-agnostic; could be applied to any per-token metric.

- **Bounded divergence as exploration signal rather than penalty** — Conceptual reframing: instead of penalizing deviation from reference policy, DGPO uses deviation magnitude as a positive signal for where the model is "thinking hard."
  *Replication cost*: 0 (conceptual).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this framing can be applied to our cached per-token hidden-state analysis: high activation-space deviation from a reference direction could be treated as "high cognitive effort" rather than noise.

## Approaches & framings

- **Distribution deviation as credit signal, not penalty.** Standard RL penalizes policy drift; DGPO inverts this — large deviation + high uncertainty = genuine exploration. This intersects F-2/F-8: our prefill DoM direction might be capturing exactly the tokens where the model's distribution deviates most from its default. If DoM ≈ "aggregate deviation direction," the DGPO framing provides a mechanistic explanation for why it works.

- **Entropy as epistemic uncertainty gate.** Shannon entropy of the output distribution at each token serves as a proxy for "how uncertain the model is." This connects to H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability): high prefill entropy → unfamiliar problem → high deviation justified; low prefill entropy → familiar problem → deviation is hallucination. The prefill DoM signal might be an aggregate of this token-level entropy pattern.

- **Coarse-to-fine credit reallocation without auxiliary networks.** DGPO achieves process-level supervision from outcome-level rewards by redistribution, not by training a separate PRM. This is methodologically analogous to how our L19 DoM probe achieves per-problem prediction from a single-layer extraction — both avoid training expensive auxiliary models.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Hellinger distance on cached L19 prefill activations:** Treat each 1536-dim activation vector as an empirical distribution (after L1-normalization or softmax), compute Hellinger distance between correct-mean and incorrect-mean activation profiles. Compare the resulting distance to the cosine-based DoM direction. Uses `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/phase2_prefill_dom.npz`.
- **Entropy of L19 prefill activations as correctness predictor:** Compute Shannon entropy of each sample's 1536-dim activation vector (after softmax normalization). Test whether per-sample entropy correlates with correctness (AUROC comparison to 0.7731). Uses same cached NPZ.
- **Softmax-temperature sensitivity of DoM:** Apply temperature-scaled softmax to the L19 activation vectors at various τ values before computing DoM, testing whether the temperature sensitivity DGPO reports (Table 4) has an analogue in activation-space correctness prediction.

## 2605.03327 — DGPO: Distribution Guided Policy Optimization for Fine Grained Credit Assignment (Jin, Zhu, Du, Jiang, Tian, Zhang, Ding, 2026)

**Relevance:** DGPO introduces a qualitatively different RL training objective for the Qwen-2.5 architecture family, using Hellinger-distance-based token-level credit reallocation with entropy gating instead of GRPO's uniform sequence-level advantage. The core question for our project is whether F-2's prefill DoM direction (AUROC 0.7731) and F-8's selective prediction are stable across RL objectives or GRPO-specific artifacts. DGPO's entropy gating also offers a competing generation-time confidence signal that could supersede prefill-based abstention.

**Key claim we tested:** Token-level credit reallocation via bounded divergence + entropy gating outperforms uniform sequence-level advantage (GRPO) on math reasoning benchmarks.
