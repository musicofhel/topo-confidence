# Triage brief — 2604.18805 — AI scientists produce results without reasoning scientifically

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18805
Triaged: 2026-04-29

> Authors: Ríos-García, Alampara, Gupta, Mandal, Mannan, Aghajani, Krishnan, Jablonka (2026).
> Eight chemistry/materials domains, 25,000+ agent runs, three frontier models
> (GPT-4o `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`, Claude Sonnet 4.5 `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`,
> GPT-OSS-120B), two scaffolds (ReAct, structured tool-calling). Headline: base
> model accounts for 41.4% of explained variance vs 1.5% for scaffold; evidence
> ignored in 68% of traces, refutation-driven belief revision in 26%, convergent
> multi-test evidence rare (7%). Token-level mean log-prob separates workflow
> (-0.20) from hypothesis-driven (-0.27) domains. Note: this paper is **purely
> black-box** — no hidden-state / residual-stream / probing analysis. Focus is
> evaluation methodology and epistemological trace analysis.

## Refutations

1. **Mean token-level log-probability may match L19 DoM AUROC, collapsing F-2 / F-9.**
   The paper's mean-log-prob signal (Section 4.10, GPT-OSS) cleanly separates
   "easy" domains (-0.20) from "hard" domains (-0.27) and predicts which
   environments require near-complete trace injection to succeed. We have
   *never* benchmarked the L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731 against the cheapest
   possible black-box baseline: mean log-probability of the generated answer
   span over MATH-500 trials. **Mechanism**: if the prefill direction is just
   reading off the same uncertainty signal that lives in the next-token
   distribution, mean-log-prob should give comparable AUROC at zero extraction
   cost. **Falsifiable**: compute per-problem mean token log-prob from the
   cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` generation logs and compute
   AUROC against the same correctness labels used for the DoM probe. If
   AUROC ≥ 0.75, F-2's "DoM-as-probe" framing collapses into "DoM ≈ smoothed
   logit confidence" and F-9's redundancy chain extends to logits.

2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) is predicted null
   by the paper's "scaffold engineering alone cannot repair" finding.**
   The paper documents that even injecting *near-complete successful prior
   trajectories* (steps n-2, n-1) into the conversation history fails to
   recover hypothesis-driven performance — Pass^k still falls below 0.05 by
   k = 4–6 with success-trace interventions at early steps (Figure 5B). A
   per-position DoM steering vector is dramatically *less* invasive than
   injecting hundreds of tokens of prior reasoning. **Mechanism**: if the
   "reasoning failure" lives in the base-model policy and not in the
   conversational context, an L19 activation push will be insufficient to
   change what the next-token distribution does. **Falsifiable**: the H-1
   experiment as already designed — apply the prefill DoM at L19 across
   positions, measure 1.5B MATH-500 K=1 accuracy, compare to 48.6% baseline.
   If lift is < 1 percentage point, this paper's framing is right and H-1 is
   PARKED.

3. **F-2's "prefill better than final-token" (0.7731 vs 0.7186) is consistent
   with the paper's finding that base-model behavior is invariant to context
   length, which removes one of the proposed *mechanistic* readings of F-3
   (orthogonality, cos 0.046).**
   The paper finds that adding more reasoning context (workflow vs
   hypothesis-driven, near-complete trace injection vs not) does **not** shift
   the topology of agent reasoning — "the topology of agent reasoning is the
   same across all three" domain groups. **Mechanism**: if the model's
   reasoning topology is invariant, the apparent orthogonality between prefill
   and final-token DoM is more likely to reflect *measurement-position
   confounds* (the prefill is always before the answer span, the final token is
   always after) than two genuinely distinct decision processes. **Falsifiable**:
   measure the cos similarity between L19 DoM extracted at the prefill position
   vs at a *second* prefill position (e.g., 5 tokens before the original prefill,
   in a setup where the question prompt is identical). If the cos similarity is
   also ≈ 0, position-not-content drives orthogonality and the F-3 framing
   needs rewriting.

4. **F-7's "D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature" may be
   confounded with knowledge vs reasoning ability per the IRT decomposition.**
   The paper's two-stage IRT framework separates latent knowledge ability
   from latent reasoning ability and shows the gap between strongest and
   weakest model is 5.3 standard units in *Inorganic Qualitative Analysis*
   (reasoning-heavy) but 0.62 standard units in *ML-based Property Prediction*
   (knowledge-heavy). **Mechanism**: D-bucket problems (K=1 right, K=8 wrong —
   pathological) might be problems where knowledge is high but reasoning
   discipline is low, and our "geometric collective signature" is just the
   hidden-state correlate of high-knowledge-low-reasoning. **Falsifiable**:
   re-tag MATH-500 problems with a coarse knowledge / reasoning split (e.g.,
   AMC-2022 retrieval-bound vs novel-derivation), then check whether the
   F-7 D-bucket signature appears uniformly across both splits or
   concentrates in one. If it concentrates, F-7 is a sub-claim of the
   IRT framework and not a novel geometric finding.

5. **F-8's "selective-prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%" is a Pass@1 metric;
   the paper's Pass^k decay suggests F-8 will not generalize to multi-trial
   reliability.**
   The paper introduces Pass^k (probability all k trials succeed) explicitly
   to expose unreliable agents that pass at k=1 but compound failures across
   trials. We have never computed Pass^k for the prefill-DoM-selected subset
   of MATH-500 problems. **Mechanism**: if the DoM probe is selecting
   problems on the basis of *easy-to-classify* correctness (reading
   smoothed logit confidence) rather than *intrinsically reliable* problems,
   the same 50%-coverage subset will exhibit the same Pass^k decay as the
   unfiltered set. **Falsifiable**: run K=5 sampling on the DoM-top-50%
   subset and compute Pass^k. If Pass^5 (5/5 correct) on the selected
   subset is not substantially higher than Pass^5 on the unselected
   bottom-50% subset, F-8's "selective prediction works" claim only holds
   for single-trial pipelines.

6. **(Meta) The paper's 68% evidence-non-uptake finding is direct evidence
   that the per-paper triage isolation discipline is load-bearing for the
   integrity of F-N — this is a constraint on the *researcher*, not on F-N.**
   This is not a refutation of any F-N. It is a refutation of the workflow
   that *generated* F-N if the workflow was confirmation-biased. The fact
   that 68% of LLM agent traces ignore retrieved evidence (us — when
   reading papers) is the precise reason `/paper-triage` runs each paper in
   a fresh `claude -p` subagent without F-N bodies in default context.
   **Falsifiable** by checking whether prior, non-isolated triage briefs
   in `briefs/` show systematically higher F-N corroboration rates and
   lower refutation rates than the post-2026-04-28 isolated briefs.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Item Response Theory (2PL) for latent ability decomposition** —
  estimates separate knowledge and reasoning latent abilities per
  model-environment pair from a diagnostic question bank with binary
  correct/incorrect outcomes. Could be applied to MATH-500: split
  problems into "domain knowledge" diagnostic items vs "novel reasoning"
  diagnostic items, fit IRT, recover per-model latent abilities.
  *Replication cost*: 2 days CPU + new diagnostic-item curation by hand.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — if the IRT-decomposition collapses
  F-7's geometric signature into a knowledge/reasoning factor it would
  significantly re-frame Pathway 11.

- **Manual epistemic-graph annotation (H/E/T/J/U/C nodes + edges)** —
  every step in an agent trace tagged with hypothesis / evidence /
  test / judgment / update / commit; dependencies become directed edges.
  Pattern templates (untested claim, evidence non-uptake, etc.) are
  matched as subgraph motifs. Browsable annotated traces at
  https://lamalab-org.github.io/corral/#explainers.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day per 50 traces with the published taxonomy
  (Section H.1, Tables H.14–H.15).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — chain-of-thought correctness on
  MATH-500 is a single-shot completion, not a multi-step agent loop;
  the H/E/T/J/U/C taxonomy would degenerate.

- **Trace intervention experiment (inject n−2 / n−1 prior successful or
  failed trajectories into history)** — measures whether contextual
  priming can recover performance. The Pass@k recovery threshold gives
  a *behavioral* read on which step in a trace is load-bearing.
  *Replication cost*: not applicable — we don't have agent loops.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not directly. But the *idea* is reusable
  for our prefill setup: inject a successful CoT trajectory from
  problem A into the prefill of problem B and see whether the prefill
  L19 DoM moves toward "predicted correct" — a counterfactual probe of
  whether the prefill direction encodes problem-specific or
  general-confidence content.

- **Mean token-level log-probability as confidence baseline** — for each
  generated token, save the top-1 log-prob; pool over all assistant
  messages per environment; report the mean. Provides a near-zero-cost
  black-box confidence proxy. (Section 4.10, G.7.)
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached generation logs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes — high priority**. This is the
  baseline we should already have for F-2 / F-9.

- **Bayesian latent-factor model with PSIS-LOO model selection (M1–M8)** —
  predicts task success from latent abilities + environment + scope +
  scaffold + verbosity, selects best specification by leave-one-out
  cross-validation. Yields the 41.4% / 1.5% variance decomposition.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day CPU once the IRT factors are in place
  (depends on first method).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: only if IRT framework yields useful latents.

- **Pass^k metric (all k trials succeed)** — alternative to Pass@k (any
  of k trials succeeds). Exposes compounding unreliability that Pass@k
  hides.
  *Replication cost*: 4 h H100 to generate K=5 samples on 500
  MATH-500 problems for Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests F-8 generalization.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning vs scaffolding as orthogonal training targets.** The paper
  argues that the dominant lever for fixing agent reliability is at the
  base-model training stage, not at orchestration / prompting. This
  reframes "interpretability of hidden states" (our project) as one of
  the few interventions left that *does* operate on the base model
  rather than on the scaffold — but only if the hidden-state signals
  feed back into training (RLHF / process reward / probe-shaped loss).
  Our project currently only uses hidden states diagnostically, not as
  a training target.

- **"Outcome-based evaluation cannot detect these failures."** The
  paper's central methodological claim. Pathway 11's evaluation is
  outcome-based (correct vs incorrect at K=1). The paper would predict
  that two probes giving the same MATH-500 AUROC may still differ in
  whether they are reading off principled correctness signals vs
  surface-statistical confidence. This intersects F-2 directly: a probe
  that reads "logit-smoothness" can score 0.77 AUROC on MATH-500
  without measuring anything we'd call "the model knowing the answer."

- **Language Decision Process (LDP) factorization
  π_overall = f(π_LLM, π_scaffold).** A clean formal statement that
  separates base-model policy from scaffold policy. Our framing of the
  "prefill direction" is implicitly in the π_LLM term; the paper makes
  the algebra explicit and would argue that any geometric finding at
  the base-model level should be measured *holding scaffold constant*
  — which we do, but not formally documented.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- All runs at temperature **0.0**; intervention controls (failed-trace
  baseline) use temperature **0.7**.
- Three frontier models: GPT-4o (`gpt-4o-2024-08-06`),
  Claude Sonnet 4.5 (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`), GPT-OSS-120B
  via Blablador.

  vs structured tool-calling (API-parsed JSON tool calls).

  comprehensive — controlled by `[BRIEF]` / `[DETAILED]` / `[EXAMPLES]`
  tags in docstrings. Useful trick we don't currently use.
- Log-probabilities only available for GPT-OSS-120B; closed-source
  models do not expose token-level log-probs (caveat noted in the paper).
- Variance-decomposition model selection: 8 candidates (M1–M8) with
  increasing structural complexity; selection by PSIS-LOO; M7 chosen.
- Code: github.com/lamalab-org/corral (repo URL inferred from the
  paper's environment URL).

  near baseline Pass^5, F-8 is single-trial-only.
  Cost: 4 h H100 (K=5 × 250 problems × ~512 tokens).

(a) a probe reading principled correctness signals or (b) a probe
