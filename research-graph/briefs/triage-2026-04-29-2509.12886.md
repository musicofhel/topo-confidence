# Triage brief — 2509.12886 — The LLM Already Knows: Estimating LLM-Perceived Question Difficulty via Hidden Representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12886
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Yubo Zhu, Dongrui Liu, Zecheng Lin, Wei Tong, Sheng Zhong, Jing Shao
Affiliations: Nanjing U., Shanghai AI Lab, Xidian U.
Year: 2025

One-line: Trains a two-layer FC value function `V̂(s_t)` over the LLM's last-layer hidden states via TD learning; at test time uses `V̂(s_0)` (initial state from the input question alone, no generation) as a difficulty / correctness predictor. Reports ROC-AUC up to 94.15% on MMBench (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 67.28% on gsm8k (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), with cross-domain transfer holding ~89% AUROC ScienceQA → MMBench but cross-family transfer collapsing to ~47–50% AUROC Qwen → LLaMA. Higher-order Markov state (k=2,3) yields no improvement over k=1 (within ±1 pt).

## Refutations

**R1 — Last-layer prefill probe could equal or beat L19 mid-layer prefill DoM, refuting F-2's layer-specificity claim.**
- F-N cited: F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500).
- Mechanism: the paper uses the **final transformer layer** hidden state `h_T` (`§5.1 Implementation Details`: "we use the last layer hidden state h_t as the state s_t") and reaches 94.15% ROC-AUC on MMBench, 67.28% on gsm8k. F-2 specifically asserts L19 is the *unique best* layer for prefill DoM in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers total). If the final layer (~L27) carries comparable correctness signal, F-2's "L19 specifically" framing is incomplete and the layer-sweep argument is overstated.
- Falsifiable experiment: extract last-layer prefill activation from cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` NPZ for the same 500 MATH-500 problems, fit linear DoM, recompute OOF 5-fold AUROC. Refutation fires if AUROC ≥ 0.7731 ± 0.02.

**R2 — A TD-trained value function over hidden states could exceed F-2's static linear probe, showing the prefill-correctness map is non-linear.**
- F-N cited: F-2 (linear DoM at L19, AUROC 0.7731) and F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).
- Mechanism: their `F̂_φ` is a **two-layer fully connected NN** trained via squared TD error δ_t over rollout sequences (`§3.3`), not a linear projection. On gsm8k (closest to MATH-500) they reach 67.28% — *lower* than our 77.31% — so for math the gain is unclear, but their MMBench / ScienceQA AUROCs (94.15% / 93.09%) are far above what a linear probe would yield on those datasets. If a TD-trained V̂ on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill h_0 beats 0.7731 AUROC on MATH-500, F-2's linear-separability framing of the prefill signal is suboptimal — there is non-linear structure F-2 is leaving on the table.
- Falsifiable experiment: train two-layer FC `F̂_φ` (lr 1e-4, k=1 Markov order, OOF 5-fold) on cached prefill activations + ground-truth correctness labels; compare AUROC to 0.7731.

**R3 — Cross-family value-function transfer fails (47–50% AUROC), refuting H-11's implicit assumption that prefill DoM transfers across model scales/families.**
- H-N cited: H-11 (Big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B).
- Mechanism: Table 5 — value function trained on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, evaluated on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct: 47.29% (commonsenseQA), 49.74% (StrategyQA). Both at chance. The paper's authors conclude transfer requires a separate value function per target model. H-11 plans to use Qwen-7B's prefill DoM (or a learned distillation thereof) as a teacher signal for Qwen-1.5B — **same family, smaller scale**, but if cross-model representations are this poorly aligned even within the open-source ecosystem, the within-family-cross-scale case is the one experiment that would either save or kill H-11.
- Falsifiable experiment: project cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill activations onto Qwen-2.5-7B prefill DoM direction (already extractable from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute`), compute correctness-prediction AUROC on Qwen-1.5B's MATH-500 labels. Refutation of H-11 if AUROC < 0.60.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Direct corroboration of the *core* claim ("initial hidden state alone predicts correctness"), with two caveats: (a) their last-layer probe replaces L19, raising R1; (b) their gsm8k AUROC 67.28% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B is **below** our 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500, suggesting either ground-truth label difference (their easy/hard from 3-rollout consistency vs our K=1 correctness) or that math is harder for the prefill-only signal in their pipeline.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — Strong corroboration. Their Table 8 (k-order Markov ablation, k ∈ {1,2,3}) reports ROC-AUC within ±1 pt across orders. This is direct external evidence that *temporal-trajectory features add no signal over the initial state* — which is exactly F-9's claim restated in TD-learning language.
- **F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonal, cos 0.046)** — Soft corroboration. Their TD bootstrap couples `V(s_0)` to `V(s_T)` through the Bellman fixed point, but at test time only `V(s_0)` is used and performance is preserved — consistent with prefill being the dominant signal independently of final-token dynamics.
- **H-5 (prefill encodes familiarity vs decomposability)** — Their cross-domain generalization (Table 3: 89.03% AUROC ScienceQA→MMBench) is consistent with prefill encoding *domain-general* difficulty rather than domain-specific features. Strengthens H-5's framing.
- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels)** — *Threatened*: their k-order ablation (k=1≈k=3 within ±1 pt) is fresh external evidence that trajectory features should not beat single-state probes. If H-6's CoE-60 advantage at 1024-tok appeared, this paper says the gain is unlikely to come from temporal richness.
- **H-11 (Big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B)** — *Directly threatened* by R3.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free replace supervised DoM)** — Methodologically adjacent: their TD-bootstrap mechanism generates pseudo-labels via reward at EOS, similar in spirit to label-free probe training.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute)** — Their difficulty-aware Self-Consistency / Best-of-N (`§4`) is a *softer* version of H-19: gate K=1 vs K=N on `V̂(s_0)` rather than on a verifier pass. Their results (Figure 3) — accuracy preserved or higher with fewer total tokens — are consistent with H-19's expected outcome.

## Methodologies extracted

- **TD-learning value function over hidden-state Markov chain** — model `s_t = (H_t, y_t)`, fit `F̂_φ(s_t) ≈ V(s_t)` via squared TD error `δ_t = γR(y) - F̂_φ(s_t)` at EOS and `δ_t = γF̂_φ(s_{t+1}) - F̂_φ(s_t)` otherwise. Two-layer FC, lr 1e-4, A100. *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU on cached MATH-500 prefill + final-state NPZs in `pathway11_h100`. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct ceiling probe for F-2 and crisp comparison to F-9.

- **Higher-order Markov state (concatenation of last k hidden states)** — re-define `s_t = (h_{t-k+1},…,h_t,y_t)`, retrain. *Replication cost*: 30min CPU, only needs cached final-token + intermediate states. *We'd plausibly run this*: **probably not** — F-9 already establishes this is redundant; running it again only adds external corroboration, marginal value.

- **Threshold-tuned binary classification of difficulty** — pick `τ` on 5% validation split, treat `V̂(s_0) > τ` as "easy". *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — natural extension of F-8 (selective prediction); already implicit in P11 selective-prediction pipeline.

- **Difficulty-aware adaptive Self-Consistency** — easy questions → K=1 direct; hard → K=10 majority vote. *Replication cost*: 30min H100 with K=8 cache already extant. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — natural cousin of FE-19 (C_exact verification routing).

- **Difficulty-aware adaptive Best-of-N** — easy → K=1; hard → pick highest `P(true | x, y_k)` among K candidates. Requires the model's self-judgement probability, which we have for K=1 but not yet K=8 in cached form. *Replication cost*: 1h H100 generation pass. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes**.

- **Difficulty-aware adaptive Self-Refine** — easy → K=1; hard → iterative T-step refinement. *Replication cost*: 2h H100 (no cache exists). *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — Self-Refine is orthogonal to current Pathway 11 focus and the underlying F-7/F-8 findings.

- **Last-layer hidden-state probing instead of mid-layer** — they use `h_T` not `h_19`. *Replication cost*: 20min CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct R1 refutation test.

- **Cross-domain probe transfer evaluation** — train probe on dataset A, eval on B, no fine-tune. *Replication cost*: trivial CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** when comparing MATH-500 → AIME or MATH-500 → BBH.

- **Cross-model probe transfer evaluation** — train on model A, eval on B same family or different family. *Replication cost*: needs LLaMA cache (we don't have); within-Qwen-family is feasible. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** for Qwen-1.5B ↔ Qwen-7B (R3 refutation).

## Approaches & framings

- **Token generation as a Markov chain over hidden representations.** Brings Bellman / TD-learning machinery into hidden-state probing. Intersects F-2 (we use a static linear probe; this is a dynamics-aware replacement) and F-9 (their k-order ablation directly corroborates F-9's "temporal redundancy"). Useful framing to import: lets us frame prefill DoM as a degenerate value function with γ=0.

- **Value-function = expected output quality at a state.** Operationalizes "model knows what it knows" as a Bellman fixed point. Touches H-19 (verification gating) — both are flavors of "use internal signal to decide compute allocation."

- **"Difficulty" defined via 3-rollout consistency rather than K=1 correctness.** A *softer* label than F-2 / F-8 use. Worth flagging: their AUROC numbers are not directly comparable to ours, since their easy/hard label collapses K=1, K=2, K=3 votes into a single bit. This is closer to our K=8 majority-correctness label than K=1 greedy-correctness.

- **Fine-tuned probe vs frozen target LLM.** They explicitly argue against fine-tuning the target LLM (general-capability degradation, ref. Qi et al. 2024). This is an alignment-with-current-pathway argument: F-1…F-10 all preserve the frozen target, and HYPOTHESES that propose intervention should respect this constraint.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Two-layer fully-connected NN as the value function head (architecture beyond layer count not specified; treat as an "MLP" with default hidden dim, likely 512 or 1024 — paper does not say).
- Adam-style optimizer, lr **1e-4**.
- Sampling temperature **T = 0.5** for ground-truth rollout collection.
- **3 independent rollouts per question** to define easy (all match GT) vs hard (otherwise). Ablation (Table 7) shows 3 rollouts ≈ 6 rollouts (Macro-F1 within 0.4 pts on MMBench).
- Reward function: scalar Reward(y) at EOS only, 0 otherwise (`§3.2`, Eq. 1).
- Bellman discount factor γ used but value not specified (likely γ=1 since EOS terminates).
- Markov state by default `s_t = (H_t, y_t)` with H_t the *full prefix* of hidden representations. Higher-order ablation found k=1 sufficient.
- Threshold τ tuned on 5% validation split per dataset.
- Single A100 GPU; PyTorch 2.5.1, Python 3.9.21.
- No code link in the abstract / paper text we extracted.

## 2509.12886 — The LLM Already Knows: Estimating LLM-Perceived Question Difficulty via Hidden Representations (Zhu, Liu, Lin, Tong, Zhong, Shao, 2025)

**Relevance:** Directly corroborates F-2's "initial hidden state predicts correctness" thesis on a different model family (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B) and a different task suite (MMBench, ScienceQA, MathVista, gsm8k, commonsenseQA, StrategyQA). Their probe is a TD-trained two-layer FC value function over the **last-layer** hidden state, which is structurally richer than F-2's static linear DoM at L19. Three implications: (a) their k-order Markov ablation (k ∈ {1,2,3} within ±1 pt) provides external corroboration of F-9's temporal-redundancy claim; (b) their last-layer choice raises a layer-specificity refutation question for F-2 (FE-70); (c) their cross-family transfer collapse (~47-50% AUROC Qwen→LLaMA) directly threatens H-11's distillation plan and motivates a within-family transfer pre-flight check (FE-71).

**Key claim we tested:** The initial hidden state of an LLM, prior to generating any output token, contains enough information to classify question correctness above chance.

**Our result:** **REPLICATED** with the following caveats. Their AUROC numbers are higher than F-2's (94.15% MMBench vs our 0.7731 MATH-500), but their ground-truth label is softer (3-rollout consistency, not K=1 correctness), their probe is non-linear (two-layer FC vs our linear DoM), and they use the last layer (not L19). On gsm8k — the closest to our MATH-500 regime — their AUROC drops to 67.28%, *below* our 0.7731, suggesting math reasoning is harder for the prefill-only signal regardless of probe family. The paper's k-order ablation independently corroborates F-9. The cross-family transfer result is a useful warning shot for H-11.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE69 (TD-learned V̂ vs linear DoM), P11-FE70 (last-layer vs L19), P11-FE71 (within-family DoM transfer); H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** REPLICATED.
