# Triage brief — 2410.04707 — Learning How Hard to Think: Input-Adaptive Allocation of LM Computation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM = strong correctness predictor at AUROC 0.7731) may be far from the ceiling for the same hidden-state family.** Damani et al. train an MLP on the *last hidden state* (top layer, last token) of a frozen base LM as a marginal-reward / single-sample-success-probability (λ) predictor on a Math benchmark (Numina-COT + Mathstral-7B + oracle verifier) and report 84% median-threshold accuracy and a calibration-curve fit to the empirical λ. F-2 fixed the probe at L19 (mid-late) and used a linear logistic probe — Damani's MLP+top-hidden-state and a LoRA variant suggest the choice of layer and probe family leaves substantial signal on the table. If a Mathstral-7B-style MLP/LoRA probe on Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill state beats 0.7731 AUROC for K=1 correctness on MATH-500 by >0.05, F-2's "L19 is special" reading needs revising.
   *Falsifiable test*: train a 2-layer MLP on the L19 prefill activations cached for `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`, plus separately on the model's *top-layer* prefill activations (Qwen-2.5-1.5B has 28 layers). If top-layer MLP AUROC ≥ L19 linear-probe AUROC, F-2 is layer-imprecise.

2. **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% answered-accuracy) is dominated by a Damani-style marginal-reward allocator at matched compute.** F-8 is a binary answer/abstain rule. Damani's binary-reward special case (Eq. 7) predicts λ_i directly and pushes 25–50% compute reduction at matched accuracy on Math by shifting samples from low-λ̂ to medium-λ̂ problems — a strictly richer decision than abstain. If, on MATH-500 cached K=8 generations, an offline Ada-BoK allocator using prefill DoM as λ̂ beats F-8's 71.6% / K=2.5 trade-off at the same average K, F-8's framing is wrong: the right primitive isn't selective prediction, it's adaptive best-of-k allocation.
   *Falsifiable test*: take cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` λ-equivalent scores (logistic-probe outputs), run Damani's offline-binning algorithm on K=1..8 to compute Ada-BoK at average K=2.5, compare to F-8's 71.6% answered-accuracy at coverage 0.5.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective signature; "K=1-right, K=8-wrong" pathology) predicts that Damani's online allocator should *catastrophically over-allocate* to D-bucket problems and the offline binning fix won't fully save it.** Damani explicitly diagnoses this: their online variant on Code falls below baseline because predictor errors push budget to truly impossible problems; offline binning regularizes it. F-7 says the project has a structurally analogous bucket on Math (36/500 problems). If we run Damani's online Ada-BoK on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 cached generations, we expect to reproduce the over-allocation pathology specifically on D-bucket problems. If the pathology is *absent* (online ≈ offline at all budgets), F-7's claim that D-bucket has a distinctive collective signature is weakened — D-bucket would be no different from generic high-λ̂ problems with stochastic K=8 luck.
   *Falsifiable test*: bucket-stratified compute-allocation analysis on Damani's online variant applied to F-7's labeled D-bucket subset.

4. **H-19 (C_exact verification routing > C_infer scaling at matched compute) implicitly assumes verification is the right secondary signal.** Damani's marginal-reward predictor is a competing primary signal: λ̂ alone, with no verification step, gives 25–50% Math compute savings. If λ̂(prefill) at fixed K = 1 already routes as well as C_exact (verify-then-recompute) at average K = 3–4, the verification pass in H-19 / FE19 is buying nothing — the prefill signal contains the routing information natively. Damani's Math accuracy/loss table (84% / 0.48 vs 0.69 baseline) is the comparator.
   *Falsifiable test*: cross-validate FE19's planned C_exact routing against an ablation that uses *only* prefill λ̂ (no verification call) for the same MATH-500 K=8 cache.

5. **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token, cos=0.046) loses interpretability if Damani's probe target shifts from "K=1 correct" to "Δ marginal benefit of one more sample".** F-3's orthogonality is computed against the *K=1 correctness* DoM. Damani's probe targets a different functional: marginal reward. If a probe trained to predict Δ_i,1 = q(x,1) - q(x,0) (i.e., λ_i) produces a direction that aligns *with* the final-token DoM (cos > 0.5), F-3 is an artifact of probe target choice — the residual stream encodes "will this sample pass" as a single linear direction across both prefill and final-token positions, but querying it as "is this output correct" only surfaces in prefill.
   *Falsifiable test*: train two probes on the same L19 cache — one targeting K=1 correctness (replicates F-2/F-3), one targeting Bernoulli λ̂ over K=8 (Damani-style). Compare cosine of the two probe directions, and the cosine of each to final-token DoM.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold)**: Damani's MLP-on-last-hidden-state for Math is the same probe family — frozen LM, light probe on encoder hidden state. Their 84% median-threshold accuracy on Numina-COT/Mathstral is not directly comparable to 0.7731 AUROC, but the existence claim ("LMs encode usable difficulty information in prefill") is fully aligned. Strongly corroborates F-2; pushes for a probe-architecture sweep (linear vs. MLP vs. LoRA).
- **F-8 (selective prediction acc 71.6% at coverage 0.5, K=2.5 avg)**: Damani's adaptive best-of-k with offline binning is the correct generalization of F-8 from binary abstain → continuous K allocation. - **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive geometric signature)**: Damani's offline-binning fix specifically regularizes against pathological over-allocation to "looks easy but is impossible" queries (Code: 50% of problems have 0% success). This is structurally similar to F-7's D-bucket. Test of whether D-bucket survives Ada-BoK regularization is a direct stress test of F-7.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)**: Damani uses a single hidden state (top layer last token) — *not* a multi-layer chain. If their probe matches CoE/DoM performance with one layer, F-9's "single-layer suffices" is corroborated.
- **H-5 (prefill encodes familiarity vs decomposability)**: Damani frames the same signal as "marginal reward" / task-completion probability — a behavioral framing, not a structural one. Either framing predicts the same probe outputs; H-5 needs a discriminating experiment beyond what Damani provides.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing)**: Damani's adaptive best-of-k is the *infer-time-only* competitor to C_exact verification routing. Their 25–50% compute reduction on Math is the bar FE19 must clear to justify the verification pass.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes / label-free)**: Damani's probe is fully supervised (requires λ_i estimates from B_max samples per training query). Orthogonal direction; H-12 stays open.

## Methodologies extracted

- **MLP-on-last-hidden-state difficulty probe** — 2-layer MLP on the LM's top-layer last-token hidden state (prefill encoding); cross-entropy loss against empirical Bernoulli λ_i estimated by sampling B_max=∼16–32 generations per training query.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached MATH-500 prefill activations (already have L19 + arguably top-layer cached for Qwen-2.5-1.5B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct probe-family ablation against F-2's linear logistic probe.

- **LoRA difficulty predictor variant** — instead of frozen-LM + probe, parameter-efficient fine-tune the base LM with a small LoRA head predicting λ̂ (used for the Math experiments specifically).
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B + LoRA on MATH-500 with K=8 cache as labels.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if FE19/FE7 results suggest the linear probe is leaving signal on the table. Worth pairing with FE7's SVD analysis.

- **Marginal-reward vector regression Δ̂(x; θ)** — predict full vector [Δ_i,1, ..., Δ_i,B_max] of per-budget marginal rewards via squared-loss regression. For binary rewards, collapses to the analytic ΔΔ_i,b = λ_i (1-λ_i)^b form, so only λ̂ needs to be learned.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU once K=8 generations + λ_i estimates are in hand (already cached in pathway11_h100/).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly compares to F-8 selective-prediction framing.

- **Greedy matroid allocation algorithm** — O(B_max·n) algorithm that incrementally turns on c_ij = 1 (allocate j-th unit to query i) for the largest predicted Δ_ij. Provably optimal because feasible sets form a matroid (Edmonds 1971).
  *Replication cost*: 30min — trivial to implement once Δ̂ values exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required to produce an Ada-BoK curve comparable to F-8.

- **Offline binning allocation** — quantile-bin queries by predicted λ̂ on a held-out set, solve allocation per bin, store fixed bin → budget map. Regularizes against per-query predictor error (the Code pathology).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed for the F-7 stress test.

- **Bernoulli cross-entropy training (Eq. 7)** — for binary-reward domains, train λ̂(x;θ) directly via binary cross-entropy on empirical λ_i estimates rather than squared-error on Δ-vectors.
  *Replication cost*: trivial; same as standard logistic probe loss but with soft targets.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — distinct from F-2's binary-target logistic probe; soft-target training may push AUROC up.

- **Calibration-curve diagnostic** — predicted-vs-empirical λ scatter + reliability diagram, used as the primary intrinsic evaluation of the probe (alongside loss and median-threshold accuracy).
  *Replication cost*: 5min once λ̂ values are computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the project currently reports AUROC but no calibration plot for F-2; this is a cheap addition with diagnostic value.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Difficulty" as marginal benefit of one additional unit of compute** — reframes correctness prediction from a binary classification target to a vector of per-budget benefits Δ_ij. Intersects with H-5: the project currently reads prefill DoM as "encodes familiarity vs. decomposability"; Damani reads the same signal as "encodes the probability that one more sample succeeds." Behavioral-vs-structural framings make different predictions for cross-budget extrapolation.
- **Adaptive compute allocation as the right test-time decision rule** — generalizes F-8's selective prediction (binary answer/abstain) to a continuous K-allocation. The decision rule, not the underlying signal, is what produces 25–50% compute savings on Math.
- **LMs already encode reward distributions in pre-training** — explicit framing. Aligns with the project's broader claim that pre-training instills correctness-aware geometry.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Probe input is the *last token* of the *encoded query* hidden state — i.e., the prefill final-position activation. This is exactly what F-2 calls "prefill DoM" but extracted from the *top* layer rather than L19. Layer-of-choice is therefore the cleanest probe-architecture variable to vary in replication.
- For binary-reward Math/Code, λ̂ is trained via Bernoulli cross-entropy on soft λ_i targets (Eq. 7), not on hard 0/1 K=1 outcomes. This soft-target training is a candidate explanation for any AUROC gap between Damani-MLP and F-2's linear probe.
- B_max for empirical λ_i estimation: not stated explicitly, but compute-budget plots go up to B≈32, suggesting ≥32 generations per training query during label collection.
- LoRA variant used specifically on Math (not Code). Small bias toward LoRA on continuous-difficulty domains.
- Greedy matroid allocator: O(B_max · n) — trivial overhead vs. generation cost.
- Offline binning: empirically found to regularize *better* than online allocation when the predictor has Bernoulli noise, especially when the dataset has a heavy tail of zero-success problems.
- Notable framing: "easy/medium/hard" tertile binning of predicted λ̂ shifts compute distribution from easy/medium-heavy at low B to hard-heavy at high B (Fig. 6). Useful diagnostic for Ada-BoK on D-bucket.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2410.04707 — Learning How Hard to Think: Input-Adaptive Allocation of LM Computation (Damani, Shenfeld, Peng, Bobu, Andreas, 2024)

**Relevance:** Trains a lightweight probe (2-layer MLP or LoRA) on a frozen LM's prefill hidden state to predict per-budget marginal reward Δ̂(x; θ), then uses a greedy matroid allocator to assign compute budgets across queries (online) or quantile-bin → fixed-budget (offline). On Numina-COT/Mathstral-7B, achieves 25–50% Math compute reduction at matched accuracy with 84% median-threshold λ-prediction accuracy. Direct intersection with F-2 (prefill DoM as correctness predictor) and F-8 (selective prediction at coverage 0.5).

**Key claim we tested:** Prefill hidden states encode usable difficulty information that can be turned into adaptive compute-allocation policies — the same factual claim as F-2, but framed as "predict marginal reward" rather than "predict K=1 correctness".

**Our result:** **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED** — F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) is the project's instantiation of the same encoding claim. Damani's MLP/LoRA probe family and offline-binning allocator are direct generalizations of (a) F-2's linear logistic probe and (b) F-8's binary selective prediction. We have not yet replicated Damani's allocator on our cached MATH-500 generations; FE73 is the planned head-to-head. If FE73 reproduces 25–50% compute savings at matched K=2.5 accuracy, the framing of F-8 should shift from selective prediction to adaptive best-of-k.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72, P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, P11-FE77, H-72, H-73, H-74
