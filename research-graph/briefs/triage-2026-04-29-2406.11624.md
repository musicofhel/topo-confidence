# Triage brief — 2406.11624 — Words in Motion: Extracting Interpretable Control Vectors for Motion Transformers

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11624
Triaged: 2026-04-29

The paper (Tas & Wagner, ICLR 2025) does linear probing on motion-forecasting
transformer hidden states, fits difference-of-means style control vectors along
the direction between hidden-state populations of opposing features, then
*refines* those control vectors via Sparse Autoencoders (FC, conv, MLPMixer
backbones; activations include JumpReLU). Headline qualitative claim:
SAE-enforced sparsity in hidden states yields a **more linear relationship
between control-vector temperature (scale) and forecast change** than raw DoM.
Datasets are Argoverse 2 Forecasting and Waymo Open Motion. Domain is
motion-trajectory prediction, not LLM math, but the recipe (linear probe →
DoM → SAE refinement) is exactly the recipe sitting under our P10/H-1 program.

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill / final-token DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) is potentially a
   raw-DoM artifact.** Mechanism: raw difference-of-means produces *dense*
   directions that mix the relevant correctness axis with whatever surface
   covariates dominate the residual stream at that layer (length, format,
   topic). Tas/Wagner's SAE refinement projects hidden states onto a sparse
   feature basis and isolates the actual interpretable axis — at which point
   the previously-orthogonal raw vectors can collapse onto a *shared* sparse
   feature. Falsifiable test: train a small SAE on cached L19 prefill and
   final-token residuals (P11 NPZs, no new generation), refit the
   correctness-axis control vector in the SAE feature space, recompute
   cos(prefill, final). If the SAE-feature cosine is ≥ 0.3, the F-3
   "orthogonality" finding is a dense-DoM artifact and the F-3 → "two distinct
   circuits" framing fails.

2. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) likely fails
   with raw DoM and only succeeds after SAE refinement.** Mechanism: the
   paper's central empirical claim is that raw control vectors produce
   *non-linear* (sub-linear or reversing) scaling responses, which read as
   "steering doesn't work" past small alpha. Refining via SAE makes the alpha
   sweep monotonic. If we run H-1 on the cached L19 DoM at the planned alpha
   sweep and find a hump-and-collapse curve, that does *not* refute steering
   as a mechanism — only the chosen extraction. Falsifiable test: P10-FE1's
   three arms (probe weight, mass-mean DoM, trained per-layer bias) should
   include an SAE-refined fourth arm. If the SAE arm beats all three at any
   alpha while the others plateau, the H-1 framing of "DoM is the steerable
   object" is wrong.

3. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) is plausibly *not* the
   correctness-direction ceiling.** Mechanism: linear probes hit high
   accuracy on multiple feature axes simultaneously when the residual stream
   is polysemantic. Tas/Wagner show that SAEs decompose hidden states into
   sparse features that probe better *individually* than the dense direction.
   If the prefill correctness signal is one of several entangled features,
   the SAE-refined sparse correctness feature can exceed 0.7731. Falsifiable
   test: train a JumpReLU-SAE on cached L19 prefill activations from the
   pathway11_h100 NPZs, identify the feature(s) with highest correctness
   AUROC, compare to 0.7731. If any single sparse feature ≥ 0.80, the
   "0.7731 = the prefill correctness signal" reading is too strong — F-2's
   numerical anchor demotes to "0.7731 is the *dense-DoM* ceiling, sparse
   features carry more."

4. **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) is plausibly false
   under sparse decomposition.** Mechanism: F-9's redundancy claim is built
   on dense per-layer DoMs and a 60-dim trajectory feature aggregate. If
   per-layer SAEs find sparse features at *non-L19* layers that are
   correctness-predictive but orthogonal in SAE-feature space to L19's
   feature, then CoE-style aggregation is recovering signal the single-layer
   L19 DoM misses — even though both look "matched" on AUROC. Falsifiable
   test: per-layer JumpReLU-SAEs on L0…L27 cached residuals; for each layer
   identify the top-5 correctness-AUROC features; check whether ensembling
   them via simple logistic regression beats single-layer L19 DoM by ≥ 0.02
   AUROC. If yes, F-9's "redundant" framing collapses.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — paper is methodologically the most
  important external evidence on the steering side: it shows that the
  difference-of-means recipe works as a control-vector extractor on
  transformer hidden states *but* needs SAE refinement to scale linearly.
  Numerical anchor: the H-1 plan uses raw L19 DoM (cos with prefill DoM 0.046,
  AUROC 0.7731) and expects monotonic alpha-sweep behavior. This paper
  predicts non-monotonic raw-DoM scaling.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper's linear-probe finding
  ("high probing accuracy implies meaningful directions") corroborates the
  *probe* finding. But the SAE refinement angle suggests 0.7731 may be a
  dense-DoM ceiling, not the correctness-signal ceiling.
- **F-3 (cos=0.046, prefill-final orthogonality)** — paper provides the
  methodology to test whether 0.046 is dense-DoM noise; SAE refinement
  predicts non-trivial alignment at the sparse feature level.
- **P10-FE1** (the existing TRIGGERED, CRITICAL E1 steering experiment) —
  this paper directly motivates adding a fourth arm: SAE-refined DoM. The
  three currently-planned arms are all dense-vector approaches and may
  uniformly under-perform the SAE-refined version.
- **F-7 (D-bucket geometric signature)** — paper shows linear probes
  recover *multiple* simultaneous interpretable axes. The D-bucket signature
  may be a sparse SAE feature, in which case "collective geometric"
  signature is a coarser description of a sparse-feature effect.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Linear probing for "neural collapse toward interpretable features"** —
  fit a linear classifier on hidden states for each candidate categorical
  feature; high probing accuracy is the criterion for picking which axes to
  steer along. *Replication cost*: ~2 h CPU per layer per feature on
  pathway11_h100 cached NPZs. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly
  feeds an axis-confound check (length tier, problem topic, formula
  density) on top of the L19 DoM.

- **Difference-of-means control vector along opposing-feature populations**
  — exactly the mass-mean direction we already use, but applied per
  *categorical feature axis* (their motion features) rather than only per
  *correctness label*. *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on cached residuals.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the H-1 / P10-FE1 plan should run DoM
  per-axis (correctness, length-tier, topic) to disambiguate confounds in
  the L19 direction.

- **Sparse Autoencoder refinement of control vectors** — train a small SAE
  (their variants: FC, 1D-conv, MLPMixer; activations TopK / JumpReLU /
  ReLU); decode the refined vector by selecting the SAE feature(s) most
  aligned with the raw DoM. *Replication cost*: ~4-8 h H100 to train
  per-layer JumpReLU SAEs on cached prefill/final residuals; ~1 h CPU to
  refit the DoM in feature space. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is
  the single highest-value methodological add to the H-1 / P10-FE1 program.

- **Control-vector temperature sweep (alpha scaling) with monotonicity
  check** — sweep scalar α applied to (refined) control vector, plot
  prediction-change-vs-α; the paper's headline empirical observation is
  that SAE refinement converts a non-monotone curve to a monotone one.
  *Replication cost*: bundled into P10-FE1's α sweep. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes.

- **SAE backbone ablation (FC vs conv vs MLPMixer)** — they vary
  autoencoder architecture and find different sparsity / linearity
  trade-offs. *Replication cost*: 2× the SAE training cost above.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: probably no in v1 — pick JumpReLU-FC as a
  default; revisit only if v1 shows promise.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Interpretability via control" framing** — instead of asking "what
  feature does this direction encode?", ask "what does the model do when we
  scale the direction?". Maps directly onto our `correct vs incorrect` DoM
  steering question and suggests reporting alpha-sweep curves rather than
  scalar effect sizes.

- **Sparsity as the reason DoM steering "works linearly"** — recasts the
  DoM-steering question from "is this direction causal?" to "is this
  direction *isolated* from surface confounds?" SAE sparsity is the
  isolation mechanism. Intersects F-3 (orthogonality) and H-1 (steering)
  directly: orthogonality may be evidence of dense-direction confound mass,
  not of mechanism separation.

- **Probe accuracy as a *gating* signal for which axes are worth
  steering** — in their pipeline, you only fit a control vector along axes
  that probe well. We don't gate this way; we steer along the correctness
  DoM regardless of whether other axes (length, topic) probe better. Suggests
  a "steerability budget" reframing of our axis selection.

  check.
- They compare against a Koopman autoencoder as a baseline; not relevant to
  our pipeline.

## Replicable intermediates

- **JumpReLU-SAE training on cached pathway11_h100 L19 prefill residuals**
  — single-layer first; can use sparseautoencoder/sae_lens or implement
  ~150 LoC ourselves. Inputs already cached; ~4 h H100 to train, ~1 h CPU
  to identify the correctness-aligned feature and recompute AUROC. Gates
  refutations 1, 3, and 4.
- **Per-feature-axis DoM probing** — fit linear probes for length-tier,
  topic-cluster, and formula-density on the same L19 prefill residuals;
  compare AUROC against 0.7731 correctness AUROC. ~20 min CPU. Gates
  refutation 3 even without SAE training. If length-tier AUROC ≥ 0.7,
  F-2's "correctness signal" reading is partially confounded.
- **Alpha-sweep monotonicity audit on existing P10 steering vector** —
  cos(P2 cached steering vector, current L19 DoM) = 0.05 already known.
  Plot the cached P2 alpha-sweep response (if archived) for monotonicity;
  if non-monotone, that is consistent with the paper's "raw DoM is
  non-linear in α" prediction. ~10 min if data archived; otherwise blocked
  on H100 regen (P2-FE1).
