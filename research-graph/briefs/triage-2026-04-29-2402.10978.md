# Triage brief — 2402.10978 — Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10978
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 / F-8 framing that supervised DoM is the strongest available
   correctness signal** — Mohri & Hashimoto report on MATH that GPT-4 base
   correctness rises from 75% → 95% by removing ~10% of sub-claims using
   *frequency scoring* (count, via GPT-4, how often each sub-claim appears
   across 5 alt-samples at T=1.0). That is a label-free, black-box scorer
   operating at sub-claim granularity. Mechanism of the refutation: a
   self-consistency proxy at the sub-claim level can plausibly outscore our
   supervised L19 DoM (AUROC 0.7731) because (a) it sees 5 stochastic
   completions instead of one frozen activation, and (b) its grain is a single
   reasoning step, not the whole answer. Falsifiable in our setup: regenerate
   K=5 completions for the 500 MATH-500 problems (1.5B), run GPT-4 sub-claim
   separator + frequency counter, compute sub-claim-level AUROC. If frequency
   scoring AUROC ≥ 0.78 at the sub-claim grain, F-2's "DoM is the strong
   predictor" headline becomes "DoM is the *best supervised* predictor" — a
   weaker claim.

2. **Refutes F-8 selective-prediction headline (71.6% on answered at 50%
   coverage)** — the paper's MATH result corresponds to ~95% accuracy on
   *retained* content while keeping ~90% of the original output. Our F-8 keeps
   50% of *problems* and reaches only 71.6%. The two settings differ
   (whole-answer abstention vs sub-claim deletion), but the paper's regime
   dominates ours on both axes (much higher correctness at much higher
   retention) using only ~50 manually-annotated calibration examples and zero
   activation extraction. Mechanism: their back-off operates inside the answer
   (removing wrong steps but keeping right ones), so per-token usefulness is
   preserved. Falsifiable: build a hybrid that uses prefill DoM as the
   per-problem gate and frequency scoring as the per-step gate on the answered
   half; if hybrid retention/correctness beats the DoM-only number on
   1024-tok 1.5B MATH-500, F-8's framing ("DoM gating is the right unit") is
   refuted in favor of finer-grained gating.

3. **Refutes the implicit assumption behind F-2's calibration regime that we
   need supervised labels at scale** — F-2 is fit with 5-fold OOF on hundreds
   of MATH-500 examples. The paper attains its guarantee with a *50-example*
   manually annotated calibration set. Mechanism: split conformal trades
   scoring-function quality for distribution-free coverage at trivial
   calibration cost. Falsifiable: subsample our MATH-500 labels to 50, refit
   the DoM probe vs. wrap an unfit DoM as a conformal score; if the
   conformal-wrapped scorer attains valid 1−α coverage where the
   small-sample-fit probe collapses, F-2's "we need 5-fold OOF over 500
   labels" engineering choice is over-specified.

4. **Refutes (implicitly) F-9's redundancy story** — F-9 says CoE-60
   (geometry-based) ≈ single-layer DoM. The paper introduces a *non*-geometric
   black-box scorer (sampling agreement) that may dominate both. If frequency
   scoring AUROC > both 0.7731 (DoM) and 0.716 (CoE-60) at sub-claim grain on
   MATH-500, F-9's framing — "trajectory features are redundant with DoM" —
   misses the larger story: the whole residual-stream geometry family may be
   redundant with simple sample-agreement. Mechanism: sample agreement
   directly observes the model's own output distribution, which the
   activation-based geometry only approximates. Falsifiable as in (1).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) — direct comparator: the paper's
  scoring functions (frequency, GPT-4 confidence) are alternative correctness
  predictors that need no hidden states and no supervised probe fit. Frequency
  scoring is the strongest non-oracle scorer in their evaluation.
- **F-8** (selective prediction 71.6% at coverage 0.5 on Qwen-1.5B) — the
  paper offers a *probabilistic-guarantee* version of selective prediction.
  Coverage 0.5 → α-conformal-factuality with α = 0.5 in their language. Their
  guarantee is marginal over inputs and over draws of the calibration set,
  matching F-8's own marginal regime.
- **F-9** (CoE-60 ≈ DoM) — the paper opens a new comparator family
  (sampling-based agreement) outside of the residual-stream-geometry family
  that F-9 lives in. Whether sample-agreement dominates the geometric family
  is an open question their setup makes immediately testable.
- **H-12** (label-free semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM) —
  *confirmed in spirit*. Frequency scoring is a coarse SC-style sibling of
  semantic-entropy probes (paper itself flags Manakul SelfCheckGPT and Tian
  confidence elicitation as siblings). If frequency scoring works at our
  pipeline, H-12's hypothesized direction (label-free wins) gets a positive
  data point even before the SEP-specific test runs.
- **H-19** (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched
  compute) — the paper's frequency scorer is essentially a verification
  signal computed *during* generation rather than after. A strong result here
  would suggest C_exact's compute should be spent on diverse samples + a GPT-4
  judge rather than on a self-verification prompt.
- **prior_admission_note linkage to F-11** — admission filter routed this
  paper as "EXTENDED_BY F-11 (conformal wrapper for our DoM probe)". That
  reading is correct but narrow: the paper's actual contribution is a
  *meta-algorithm* wrapping any score, of which DoM is one option.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Sub-claim decomposition (S)** — GPT-4 with a structured prompt that
  splits an LM output into atomic propositions. Replication cost: ~$0.03 per
  500-token answer at GPT-4 prices, negligible compute. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — needed for any sub-claim grain experiment on MATH-500 traces.
- **Sub-claim merger (M)** — GPT-4 with a prompt that re-stitches a kept
  subset back into prose. Replication cost: same as above. *We'd plausibly
  run this*: yes — same.
- **Frequency scoring (best practical scorer)** — sample 5 alternates at
  T=1.0; ask GPT-4 how many of the 5 contain each sub-claim. Replication
  cost: 5× generation (~30 min H100 for 500 MATH-500 problems on 1.5B; ~1 h
  on 7B). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — high ROI as direct comparator to
  F-2/F-9.
- **GPT-4 confidence scoring** — direct prompt asking GPT-4 to score each
  sub-claim. Replication cost: ~$1 / 500 problems. *We'd plausibly run this*:
  yes — cheap baseline for any scorer benchmark.
- **Ordinal / random scoring baselines** — `n−j` and `N(0,1)`. Replication
  cost: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed to ground any AUROC
  table.
- **Oracle scoring** — uses 1{y* ⇒ c_j}; upper bound on any scorer.
  Replication cost: trivial given annotated MATH-500. *We'd plausibly run
  this*: yes — gives a ceiling for any DoM/frequency comparison.
- **Split conformal calibration over a custom score** — `r(x,y*) := inf{t :
  ∀j≥t, y* ∈ E(F_j(x))}`, then take ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/(n+1) quantile of n calibration
  scores. Tie-break with N(0, 0.001). Replication cost: trivial Python. *We'd
  plausibly run this*: yes — direct path to wrapping our DoM probe in a
  distribution-free guarantee (F-11 EXTENDED_BY edge).
- **Partial-entailment relaxation (Corollary 5.3)** — switch from "all
  sub-claims entailed" to "≥a fraction entailed". Replication cost: trivial
  once entailment oracle exists. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — paper
  itself reports gains "small" empirically; lower priority.
- **4-way manual annotation (Factual / Subjective / Unverifiable / False)**
  — first two count as entailed. Replication cost: ~1 h human time per 50
  examples. *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if we extend to
  open-ended QA; for MATH-500 we already have ground-truth answers.
- **Nestedness-relaxed score (Eq. 4)** — strict-safe threshold infimum;
  matters only when F_t isn't nested. Replication cost: trivial. *We'd
  plausibly run this*: probably no — our F_t (sub-claim threshold) is nested
  by construction.

## Approaches & framings

- **Correctness ≡ coverage** of an entailment set. Reframes
  factuality/correctness as classical conformal uncertainty quantification.
  Intersects F-8's selective-prediction framing (which is implicitly
  Bernoulli abstention rather than coverage of an entailment set).
- **Implicit confidence sets via natural language** — the paper never
  enumerates the uncertainty set; it represents it by a natural-language
  output that all elements of the set must entail. Bypasses the
  "exponentially-large sequence space" obstacle that has blocked sequence-level
  conformal LM work (Quach et al. 2023). Intersects our pipeline only loosely
  — we live in activation space, not output space — but the trick that "the
  *output itself* represents the confidence set" could be reused if we ever
  publish a calibrated abstention API around DoM.
- **Back-off as specificity reduction** — instead of refusing, progressively
  drop the least-confident sub-claims. Closest tie to our work: this is a
  *scaffolded* version of selective prediction that interleaves answering and
  refusing within one response. F-8 is hard refuse-or-answer; the paper's
  framework opens a softer middle ground we haven't explored.
- **Scoring-function-agnostic guarantee** — Theorem 4.1 holds for *any*
  sound F_t. This decouples "calibration math" from "score quality", letting
  us iterate on scorers without re-deriving guarantees. Useful framing for
  H-12 / SEP comparisons.

  problems from MATH test split). The paper's per-step entailment annotation
  on its 50 examples is a candidate seed if we want to bootstrap step-level
  labels for our cached traces.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- All three model roles (L base, S separator, M merger) are GPT-4 with
  prompts in Appendix A. We'd substitute Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct for L (we
  already have its outputs); S and M can stay GPT-4 since they're called
  per-output, not per-token.
- Frequency scoring uses **5** samples at temperature 1.0. With our existing
  K=1 greedy pass, we'd need 5 fresh samples; pathway11_h100 already has K=8
  data for selective prediction (per F-8 description), so we may be able to
  reuse those if temperature/seed match.
- Calibration set size **n=50**; quantile ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉. For α=0.2 on n=50,
  quantile index = 41. Trivially small, but coverage variance is large at
  this n (paper reports stdev ≈ 0.09 over the calibration draw).
- Tie-break with `N(0, 0.001)` noise; "noise terms consistent across scoring
  functions" — important detail for fair scorer comparison.
- Annotation: 4-way label, *Factual* and *Subjective* count as entailed.
  Crowd-worker labels were noisy; authors did annotation in-house.
- Code: `https://github.com/tatsu-lab/conformal-factual-lm` (Stanford Tatsu
  group, repo URL also stored in the graph migration record for this paper).

  *first 50 MATH-500 problems on 1.5B*: requires regenerating K=5 at T=1.0
  for 50 problems (~3 min H100), GPT-4 sub-claim separator + counter (~$2,
  ~10 min API). Returns AUROC of frequency scoring per sub-claim. Compare
  against per-problem prefill DoM (already have). Establishes whether sample
  agreement at sub-claim grain dominates whole-answer DoM on a 50-problem
  scoping run.
- **Conformal factuality on 1.5B MATH-500 with DoM-as-score** (no new
  scoring function, just the calibration wrapper). Existing
  pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json supplies the per-problem
  DoM; we need ground-truth correctness (already have) and a partition into
  calibration / test. Trivial.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2402.10978 — Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024)

**Relevance:** Provides a meta-algorithm — split conformal prediction over an entailment set — that wraps any black-box correctness scorer to deliver marginal 1−α factuality guarantees with very small calibration sets (~50 examples). Their best practical scorer is *frequency scoring*: count sub-claim appearances across 5 alternate samples at T=1.0. They report MATH lifts from 75% → 95% correctness at ~90% sub-claim retention. Directly relevant to F-2 (DoM as scorer), F-8 (selective prediction with guarantees), and H-12 (label-free alternatives to supervised DoM).

**Key claim we tested:** With n=50 calibration examples, any sound scorer can be wrapped in split conformal prediction to give 1−α marginal correctness coverage on LM outputs. Their frequency scorer dominates GPT-4-confidence and ordinal/random scorers on MATH.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — we have not yet (a) wrapped our DoM probe in their conformal calibration (P11-FE73 / H-73), (b) compared frequency scoring against DoM at sub-claim grain on MATH-500 (P11-FE74 / H-74), or (c) built the hybrid gate (P11-FE75). All three experiments are ROI ≥ 7 and use mostly cached data.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** TO TEST.
