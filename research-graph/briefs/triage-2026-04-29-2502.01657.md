# Triage brief — 2502.01657 — Improving Rule-based Reasoning in LLMs using Neurosymbolic Representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01657
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "L19 is the special prefill correctness layer" universality claim** if their layer-17 result holds across architectures. Mechanism: Dhanraj & Eliasmith pick layer 17 in LLaMA-3.1 8B (32 layers, ~53% depth) by minimising encoder RMSE for problem-input reconstruction. F-2 picks L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers, ~68% depth) by maximising correctness AUROC. If the *same* family of "best layer for hidden-state probing" tracks depth-fraction (e.g. ~55–70%) and the *kind* of information differs (problem identity vs. correctness), the L19 finding is a generic mid-late phenomenon, not a Qwen-specific correctness layer. Falsifier: train a linear problem-type classifier on every Qwen 1.5B layer using the cached MATH-500 Stage-2 NPZs; if classification peaks ≥3 layers from L19, F-2's "L19 is privileged for prefill correctness" weakens to "L19 happens to be the best layer for *this* signal, in *this* model".

2. **Refutes the H-1 abandonment rationale** (fixed-vector steering parked because the DoM direction rotates during generation, P10 v2 finding). Mechanism: this paper performs *single-layer* fixed-mix steering (50/50 linear blend at L17) and gets +25× accuracy gain on trained problem types. Their decoder vector is *per-prompt computed* (encoder reads the current hidden state, generates a problem-specific solution vector, decoder writes back), but the *insertion mechanism* — single layer, fixed-α blend — is exactly what P10 v2 declared insufficient. Falsifier: if we re-run the P10 fixed-α intervention but feed a *per-prompt* vector (e.g. predicted DoM displacement from a small probe rather than the global DoM mean), and accuracy moves measurably, the "direction rotates therefore steering is dead" framing is wrong — the bug was *vector source*, not insertion mechanics. This would un-park H-1 and reopen P10-FE1.

3. **Refutes F-9's "CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM" generalisation** when extended off-domain. Mechanism: F-9 says trajectory information is dispensable because a single layer carries enough signal for correctness on MATH-500. This paper shows a *single layer* (L17) can carry not just correctness but full *digit-level* problem inputs and operation type, with linear decodability error <2% per digit. If the same is true at L19 of Qwen for MATH-500 problem identifiers (e.g. problem-type clusters, difficulty bands), then CoE/temporal information may indeed be superfluous *for in-distribution problems* — but the threshold-gating result (Section 4.4, dot-product 0.8 cutoff) shows their method explicitly *fails* on out-of-distribution problem types. Falsifier: F-9's redundancy claim should fail on cross-domain transfer (MATH-500 → ABC-44 or → code generation): if CoE features add measurable AUROC over single-layer L19 DoM on transfer, the redundancy is in-distribution only, and F-9 needs a domain qualifier.

4. **Refutes the "DoM-as-mass-mean" framing implicit in F-2 / F-7** if their VSA encoder out-performs simple linear probes. Mechanism: our DoM is `mean(h_correct) − mean(h_incorrect)` — a single direction. The paper's encoder is also linear, but trained against a *structured* target (HRR vectors with `binding`/`bundling` algebraic operations), and recovers digit-level information with 98% accuracy that a naive linear probe per Elhage et al. cannot. Falsifier: if a structured (VSA-style) probe trained against problem-type targets at L19 prefill achieves higher correctness AUROC than DoM, then F-2's headline 0.7731 AUROC is leaving signal on the table — DoM is the right direction but wrong representation.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: paper picks L17/32 in LLaMA-3.1 8B as the encoder-optimal layer; depth fraction (~53%) is comparable to L19/28 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (~68%). Different criterion (input reconstruction vs. correctness AUROC) but converges on a mid-late layer. Suggests L19 is privileged because *all* problem-relevant information is linearly available there, not specifically correctness.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)**: paper notes that residual-stream content drifts at deeper layers because of accumulated residual+RMS contributions (their eq. 9). Same mechanism we cite for prefill/final orthogonality; this is a corroborating mechanistic picture, not a refutation.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM)**: see refutation #3 — paper supports the in-distribution version, but threshold-gating mechanism warns it fails out-of-distribution. F-9 may need a domain caveat.
- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)**: paper's success at single-layer per-prompt intervention with 50/50 mix is the strongest published prior for un-parking H-1. See refutation #2 and proposed FE.
- **H-5 (Prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)**: paper's gating mechanism (dot-product threshold 0.8 against trained problem-type vectors) is essentially a *familiarity classifier*. If we replicate their encoder on MATH-500 problem-type clusters, "familiarity score" becomes a directly-measurable confound for F-2 / F-8.
- **H-12 (Semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM for refusal)**: paper's threshold gating (Appendix A) is conceptually similar — refuse to intervene when the encoded representation is far from any trained class. Useful prior for H-12's refusal-via-OOD-detection direction.
- **H-13 (Head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)**: paper's encoder weights at L17 give a direct view of which residual-stream directions carry digit/op information — a complementary handle to attention-head attribution.
- **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null)**: paper's success with a *purely linear* encoder corroborates F-10's conclusion that the geometry of the residual stream is, after centring, near-Gaussian — a linear map suffices, no manifold structure needed.

## Methodologies extracted

- **HRR-VSA encoding of hidden states** — train a linear encoder `h → v ∈ R^2048` whose target is a structured Holographic Reduced Representation `v = bind(n1, hundreds⊗d2 + tens⊗d1 + ones⊗d0) + bind(n2, …) + bind(problem_type, op)`, then a linear decoder `v → h` that maps a *solution* VSA vector back to a hidden state. Encoder/decoder both linear, RMSE loss, 1000 epochs, then 1 epoch of CE fine-tune on LLM logits.
  *Replication cost*: ~4h on a single 8B-class GPU per layer; encoder/decoder are tiny linear maps so negligible compute. The harder part is choosing what symbolic targets to bind for *non-arithmetic* problems (MATH-500 is not just digit arithmetic).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, but only the linear-probe-with-structured-targets variant. Full HRR over MATH-500 isn't worth the targets-design cost. The 0.8 dot-product gating mechanism is portable.

- **Linear-encoder layer sweep with reconstruction RMSE** — train an encoder per layer, plot RMSE vs layer, pick the minimum. Their Figure 2 shows RMSE bottoms out at L17 then rises with depth.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached Qwen 1.5B Stage-2 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests the F-2 layer-specificity claim (proposed FE below).

- **50/50 hidden-state intervention with similarity gating** — at the chosen layer, replace `h ← 0.5·h + 0.5·decoder(VSA_solution)` only when `dot(encoded_VSA, problem_type_proto) > 0.8`. Otherwise pass through unchanged. This is a *learned-vector* version of P10 fixed-α steering with an explicit refusal mechanism on OOD inputs.
  *Replication cost*: tiny; the inference loop modification is trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly relevant to H-1 un-parking (proposed FE below).

- **Procedurally-generated single-token-answer arithmetic benchmark** — Symbolic-Math: 20k train / 200 val / 2000 test, 10 problem types (mod, ×, gcd, lcm, x²mod, AND, XOR, OR, +, //), all answers truncated to single LLaMA tokens via mod-1000.
  *Replication cost*: trivial generation, MIT-licensed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful as a *toy domain* for testing whether F-2 / F-7 generalise off MATH-500 onto a fully synthetic single-step rule-following benchmark.

## Approaches & framings

- **Hidden states carry compositionally decodable problem inputs at a specific mid-late layer.** This reframes the L19/L17 phenomenon: it's not about *correctness* per se, it's about *problem features being maximally linearly available* before deep mixing scrambles them. This is a *decoupling* of "where is the problem state most legible" from "where does the model decide whether to be right".
- **Symbolic-space offload as steering.** Recasts intervention not as "push along a direction" but as "round-trip through a structured external representation, edit there, write back". Suggests our P10 framework may have been too narrow — the right intervention may be a per-prompt-computed *displacement vector* rather than a global direction.
- **OOD refusal via similarity gating.** Their threshold mechanism makes the steering operation *self-suspending* on out-of-distribution inputs. This is a constructive design pattern for any steering method we might revive — gate on probe confidence, refuse intervention otherwise.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Symbolic-Math Dataset** (introduced by paper) — 22.2k examples, 10 arithmetic operations on 3-digit ints, single-token answers, MIT licensed, fully procedurally regenerable, GitHub at vdhanraj/Symbolic-Math-Dataset. Applicable? yes (limited) — useful as a *controlled* toy domain to ask whether F-2 / F-9 are MATH-500-specific or generalise to pure arithmetic.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- LLaMA-3.1 8B, 32 layers, 4096-dim hidden states; intervention at layer 17.
- VSA dimensionality: 2048 (chosen to match the LoRA rank baseline so trainable-param count is comparable).
- Encoder + decoder: linear maps. RMSE loss. 1000 epochs of pre-training + 1 epoch of CE fine-tune on LLM logits.
- Mixing ratio: 50/50 between decoder output and original hidden state. Justified empirically in Appendix D (not read).
- Similarity gating threshold: 0.8 dot product against problem-type prototypes.
- Few-shot prompting: 2 in-context exemplars per query. Greedy decoding (T=0).
- HRR binding = circular convolution; bundling = vector addition; similarity = dot product.
- Pseudo-inverse for unbinding: `y† = (y_1, y_d, y_{d-1}, …, y_2)`.
- Code: github.com/vdhanraj/Neurosymbolic-LLM and github.com/vdhanraj/Symbolic-Math-Dataset.
- Compute overhead: Θ(d·v + v·log v) per forward pass, dominated by the standard transformer cost — negligible.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2502.01657 — Improving Rule-based Reasoning in LLMs using Neurosymbolic Representations (Dhanraj & Eliasmith, 2025)

**Relevance:** Paper trains a linear encoder/decoder pair at LLaMA-3.1-8B layer 17 to map hidden states into a Holographic Reduced Representation (HRR) vector-symbolic algebra space, performs symbolic computation in that space, and writes the solution back into the residual stream via 50/50 mixing — gated by a 0.8 dot-product similarity threshold for OOD refusal. They report 88.6% lower CE loss and 15.4× more correct answers on a procedurally generated single-token arithmetic benchmark vs. CoT and LoRA baselines. The work is directly relevant to F-2 (single-layer privileged for hidden-state probing), F-3 (deeper-layer scrambling via residual+RMS), F-9 (single-layer sufficiency), and re-opens H-1 by demonstrating that single-layer fixed-α intervention works when the inserted vector is per-prompt-computed.

**Key claim we tested:** A linear encoder at a specific mid-late layer can recover structured problem-input information (digit-level, operation-type) with <2% per-digit error, and a linear decoder with 50/50 mixing at the same layer can write back a *solution* that the rest of the LLM completes correctly.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — paper's results not yet replicated on Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Their L17/32 layer choice in LLaMA-3.1-8B is at a comparable depth fraction to our L19/28 in Qwen, but their criterion (problem-input reconstruction RMSE) differs from ours (correctness AUROC). The direct cross-checks are H-71 (does the layer choice transfer when target changes from problem-input to problem-type), H-72 (does per-prompt single-layer steering work when the source vector is correctness-direction rather than VSA-solution), and H-73 (do structured probes beat scalar DoM).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE57, P11-FE58, P11-FE59, P11-FE60, H-71, H-72, H-73.

**Status:** TO TEST
