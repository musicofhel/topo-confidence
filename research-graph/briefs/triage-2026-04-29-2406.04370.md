# Triage brief — 2406.04370 — Large Language Model Confidence Estimation via Black-Box Access

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04370
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 / F-8 — black-box engineered features may match or exceed white-box prefill DoM.** Pedapati et al. report AUROC 0.81–0.96 on QA tasks (and AUARC up to 0.96 on SQuAD/Mistral) using six prompt perturbations + logistic regression *with no internal access*. Our F-2 / F-8 narrative leans on the value of L19 prefill DoM (AUROC 0.7731, selective-pred 71.6% at coverage 0.5) precisely because it is a single forward-pass internal signal. Mechanism for refutation: if a 5-perturbation × 5-generation black-box pipeline run on Qwen-2.5-1.5B + MATH-500 reaches AUROC ≥ 0.7731, then white-box-ness is not pulling weight relative to plausible-cost black-box baselines and F-2's "internal geometry uniquely predicts correctness" framing weakens. Falsifiable test: replicate their Algorithm 7 (SD+PP+SP+EFA+SR+SRC, semantic-set count + rouge similarity + NLI contradiction features, logistic regression) on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500 K=1 generations using majority-of-K=8 as label, compute AUROC — compare to 0.7731.

2. **H-12 (label-free SE) — supervised LR on engineered black-box features beats SE baseline by 5–15 pp AUROC.** Their Table 2 shows SE consistently underperforms their LR-on-perturbations across all four QA datasets and four LLMs (e.g. SQuAD/Llama: SE=0.61 vs Ours=0.83; CoQA/Llama: SE=0.64 vs 0.92). This means the H-12 path of "label-free semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM" is *bracketed above* by a label-using baseline they already beat. Mechanism: engineered perturbation diversity carries strictly more signal than within-prompt sampling diversity. Falsifiable test: on our cached MATH-500 K=8 generations, compute SE (their formulation) and their LR-on-features pipeline; if the LR pipeline's AUROC > SE's by ≥5pp on the same Qwen runs, H-12 should be downgraded from "promising replacement" to "lossy distillation of a label-using probe."

3. **F-2 transferability claim — confidence model trained on one LLM transfers zero-shot across LLMs on a dataset.** Their Tables 4–5 show LR-built-on-Llama recovers 0.78–0.94 AUROC on Mistral/Flan-ul2/GPT-4 (roughly within 5pp of native). This is a transferability claim our F-2 has *never* been tested for: we only have AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B; we have not extracted prefill activations + DoM probe for Qwen-7B and tested cross-direction. If their transferability finding generalizes, we'd predict that a DoM probe trained on Qwen-1.5B prefill should, when applied via shared features (semantic-set + rouge + NLI from the *same prompt*), recover comparable AUROC for Qwen-7B — i.e. the *features* are dataset-driven, not model-driven. Mechanism: the predictive signal is dataset-level (which problems are hard) more than model-internal-state. Falsifiable test: run their black-box pipeline on Qwen-7B MATH-500 generations using LR weights fit on Qwen-1.5B; if AUROC stays ≥ Qwen-7B-native-LR − 5pp, the dataset-driven hypothesis dominates and F-2's "L19 DoM is a model-specific direction" wording needs hedging.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on Qwen-1.5B/MATH-500)** ↔ their headline AUROC range 0.81–0.96. Different task domain (short QA vs MATH-500 CoT) but same numeric instrument — direct head-to-head once Qwen-1.5B/MATH-500 is run through their Algorithm 7.
- **F-8 (selective-pred 71.6% on answered, K=2.5 avg, coverage 0.5)** ↔ their AUARC tables (0.45–0.96 across LLMs/datasets). AUARC is a continuous version of selective-prediction accuracy across rejection thresholds; their numbers can be cross-walked once we re-express F-8 as AUARC.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer DoM)** ↔ their interpretability finding that SD (single-direction sampling diversity) plus one or two perturbation features dominate the LR. Both points support the "ensemble or trajectory features add little to a well-chosen 1-D summary" thesis. The shared idea is that response-level diversity, however measured, is largely 1-D for confidence.
- **H-12 (SEPs as label-free replacement)** ↔ they use SE as a baseline and beat it by 5–15pp. Tightens the scope of H-12 from "replace supervised DoM" to "replace *one specific* perturbation feature."
- **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token DoM, cos 0.046)** is *not* directly addressed — they have no notion of internal direction, so F-3's orthogonality has no analogue here.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing, ROI=10 P11-FE19)** is orthogonal — their black-box pipeline could provide the routing signal *without* internal access, which is a cheaper alternative formulation worth flagging.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Six prompt perturbations: SD, PP, SP, EFA, SR, SRC** — Stochastic Decoding (greedy + beam + 3× nucleus), Paraphrasing via back-translation through Helsinki-NLP MT, Sentence Permutation via NER reordering, Entity Frequency Amplification via repetition, Stopword Removal preserving negations, Split-Response Consistency via NLI contradiction over random splits of the response.
  *Replication cost*: ~1h CPU once Qwen-1.5B MATH-500 K=1 generations are cached (already are); back-translation needs Helsinki-NLP MT models (~500MB), spaCy NER, NLTK, deberta-large-nli (~430MB).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed for refutation 1 and is the highest-signal one-shot comparison against F-2. The pieces all fit our existing CPU-only post-processing pipeline.

- **Two featurizations per perturbation: semantic-set count + lexical similarity (rouge).** Semantic sets formed via NLI bidirectional entailment grouping; rouge averaged over generation pairs.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached MATH-500 outputs once perturbation generations exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — same reason as above, can't be skipped.

- **SRC feature (NLI contradiction over random splits of a *single* generation).** Different from the rest — perturbs the *response*, not the prompt; single-pass cheap.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached MATH-500 generations — only requires the one already-emitted CoT trace per problem.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — even cheaper than F-9's CoE-60 trajectory and lets us test if intra-CoT contradiction predicts MATH-500 correctness without any internal state. Standalone hypothesis-worthy.

- **Logistic regression as the confidence model with feature-importance interpretation.** Top features are recorded per dataset/LLM in their Table 10; trend is "for a given dataset, top features are shared across LLMs." Drives their transferability claim.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU after features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — interpretability is needed to test transferability claim across Qwen-1.5B/7B.

- **Cross-LLM transfer test of confidence model.** Train LR on source-LLM features+labels, evaluate on target-LLM features (same dataset). Reports both AUROC and AUARC.
  *Replication cost*: same cost as features — once 1.5B and 7B both have features, transfer is free.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests refutation 3, novel for our DoM work.

- **Rouge>0.3 threshold for binary correctness label.** Allows continuous similarity → binary label conversion when ground-truth answers can vary in surface form.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — MATH-500 has exact-match-comparable boxed answers; rouge thresholding adds noise we don't need. But worth knowing as a fallback for free-form generation tasks.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Prompt-perturbation diversity > sampling-only diversity for black-box confidence."** Reframes the Kuhn-style semantic-entropy framing (perturb the *output* via sampling) by perturbing the *input*, then aggregating output diversity. Intersects F-2/F-9 framing because it shows orthogonal axes of "what should diversity be measured over"; our prefill-DoM and CoE-60 work measures internal-state diversity over the *same* input.
- **"A confidence model can be feature-shared across LLMs on a given dataset."** Frames confidence prediction as predominantly dataset-structure-bound rather than model-internal-state-bound. Direct tension with our F-2 framing where the L19 DoM direction is model-specific (different per Qwen scale). If their framing is right, F-2's specific layer-and-direction is a re-encoding of dataset-level features any black-box pipeline can recover.
- **"Interpretability via logistic regression coefficients enables feature transfer."** Lightweight alternative to mech-interp circuit work; coefficient signs and magnitudes give a 1-pp story for free. Could be borrowed when reporting F-2 to provide a dataset-level interpretability axis alongside the L19 direction.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Five generations per perturbation × six perturbations + greedy + beam + 3 nucleus for SD = ~28 generations per problem. For 500 MATH-500 problems × ~1024 tok each, this is ~14 GPU-hours on H100 if generations are not already cached for variants.
- Rouge>0.3 threshold for label creation (training only) — they use this not the dataset's gold answer matching, citing Lin et al. ([24]).
- 1000 datapoints for hyperparameter tuning, rest for evaluation; results averaged over 5 runs with stdev reported.
- NLI model: deberta-large-nli for both semantic-set entailment grouping and SRC contradiction probability. Required for two of the six features.
- Optional input-prompt-as-tokens feature (256-tok max) gave minimal improvement and sometimes hurt — they explicitly drop it. Important: argues against just feeding tokenized prompt to a probe.
- Open-source baseline code: `https://github.com/zlin7/UQ-NLG/` (Lin et al. 2024). Useful for replicating their SE / EigenValue / Eccentricity / Degree baselines too.
- Their feature table for SD (their Table 10, appendix) names the dominant-feature-per-LLM-dataset. Anchor this in any replication on Qwen.
- Their core public-facing claim is on AUROC; AUARC is given in the appendix; ECE in Table 6 is also reported but loses to baselines in some cases — calibration is *not* their headline.
- TMLR 2025 venue (preprint v3 of June 2024 arxiv).

## Replicable intermediates

- **SRC-feature-only sanity check (single-CoT NLI-contradiction).** For each of the 500 MATH-500 K=1 greedy generations already on disk in `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`, run deberta-large-nli over 5 random splits of the CoT, take max contradiction probability, and compute AUROC against the existing labels. ~10–20min CPU. If this single feature (no perturbation queries) reaches ≥0.65 AUROC, it's a competitive cheap signal worth folding into F-8 selective-pred.
- **Cross-model probe-transfer feasibility check.** Both Qwen-1.5B and Qwen-7B prefill activations exist (`pathway11_h100/`). Train DoM on 1.5B, score 7B, compare to 7B native-DoM AUROC. ~30min CPU. Tests refutation 3's frame even before running the full black-box pipeline.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2406.04370 — Large Language Model Confidence Estimation via Black-Box Access (Pedapati et al., 2024 / TMLR 2025)

**Relevance:** Black-box confidence estimation as a direct alternative/baseline to F-2/F-8's white-box prefill L19 DoM selective prediction. Pedapati et al. design six prompt perturbations (SD, PP, SP, EFA, SR, SRC), compute semantic-set count and rouge similarity per perturbation plus NLI contradiction for SRC, and train logistic regression to predict correctness. Reach AUROC 0.81–0.96 across CoQA/SQuAD/TriviaQA/NQ × Llama-13b/Mistral-7B/Flan-ul2/GPT-4 with no internal access — bracketing our F-2 number (0.7731) from above on QA. Their interpretability + cross-LLM transfer findings (a confidence model trained on one LLM transfers zero-shot to others on the same dataset) supply a refutable framing for whether F-2's L19 DoM is dataset-driven or model-specific.

**Key claim we tested:** A label-using LR on engineered black-box features (perturbation-diversity + NLI contradiction) achieves selective-prediction AUROC competitive with or exceeding white-box internal-state probes — and transfers across LLMs on a fixed dataset.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — H-73 (full pipeline replication on Qwen-1.5B / MATH-500), H-74 (SRC-only feature), H-75 (cross-model DoM transferability) operationalize their three load-bearing claims for our 1024-tok CoT regime. Their headline AUROC values are on short QA (different domain), so a domain-shift floor is plausible; we have no direct number yet to file as REPLICATED or CONTRADICTED. Adjacent baseline papers (Kuhn 2302.09664, Lin 2305.19187, Tian 2305.14975) should be admitted to the graph alongside.
