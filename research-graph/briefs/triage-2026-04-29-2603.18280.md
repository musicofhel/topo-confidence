# Triage brief — 2603.18280 — Detection Is Cheap, Routing Is Learned: Why Refusal-Based Alignment Evaluation Fails

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18280
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Probe accuracy alone is non-diagnostic — F-2's AUROC 0.7731 may not be a correctness signal.**
   The paper shows that null controls (food-vs-technology, science-vs-history, geography-vs-music) and permutation baselines all reach 100% LOCO-CV accuracy on the same hidden-state probe pipeline used for "real" concepts. Our F-2 result (prefill L19 DoM, OOF 5-fold AUROC = 0.7731) uses random-split CV, not held-out *category* CV. Mechanism: if MATH-500 problems cluster by subject (algebra/geometry/number-theory/etc.) and per-subject base correctness rates differ, a 5-fold random split lets the probe memorise subject↔accuracy correlation. Falsifiable test: leave-one-subject-out CV across MATH-500's 7 subjects — if F-2 AUROC collapses toward 0.55–0.6 on held-out subjects, the probe is detecting *which subject* not *correctness routing*. (See FE block below.)

2. **Single-direction story may be insufficient — single-layer L19 DoM understates the mechanism.**
   The paper's effective ablation uses 6–12 layers per model with α ∈ {5,8} (40–65% of model depth) and cites Wollschlager et al "concept cones" arguing refusal is multi-direction. Mechanism: if correctness routing is genuinely distributed across a band of layers, our L19-only F-2 captures a *projection* of a richer multi-layer subspace and the residual variance attributed to "noise" is actually load-bearing geometry. Falsifiable test: replicate F-2 with concept-cone (multi-direction) instead of single mean-diff vector, sweep cone rank 1..8 — if AUROC monotonically rises to 0.83+ at rank≥4, single-direction framing of F-2/F-3 is incomplete.

3. **Detection ≠ routing — F-3's prefill/final cos=0.046 may not reflect a correctness mechanism.**
   The paper's "detect, route, generate" framework would re-interpret our orthogonality finding: prefill could be *detection* of difficulty/topic, final-token could be *generation*-stage signal, and neither is the *routing* layer that determines correctness. Mechanism: cosine of 0.046 doesn't establish that *either* direction causally drives the answer; it could mean both are downstream of an upstream routing decision we haven't probed. Falsifiable test: activation-patching swap of *only* the prefill direction between matched correct/incorrect pairs (P11-FE3) — if outcome doesn't flip, F-3's framing as "two correctness directions" is wrong; both are spectator signals.

4. **Capability entanglement may make H-1 (DoM steering on MATH-500) destructive.**
   In the paper, Qwen3-8B's political-sensitivity direction was *entangled* with factual knowledge, so ablation produced 72% wrong-event/wrong-date confabulation. Mechanism: if Qwen-2.5-1.5B's L19 DoM correlates with mathematical-formula-recall or arithmetic circuits, then steering against the "incorrect" pole could damage the very capability the steering is supposed to preserve, and H-1 will fail not because the direction is wrong but because it's load-bearing. Falsifiable test: ridge-residualize DoM against a capability basis (math-token logits, latex density, problem length, subject one-hot) before steering — if residualized direction's AUROC drops below 0.6, F-2 was largely a capability/topic detector and H-1's steering should be expected to hurt accuracy.

5. **F-8 (71.6% selective-prediction at 50% coverage) inherits the non-diagnosticity critique.**
   If the underlying probe is contaminated by problem-structure features (length, vocabulary, latex density, subject), the abstention curve looks the same as a probe that genuinely abstains on *uncertain* problems would — but it would be effectively "abstain on hard-looking problems", not "abstain on problems the model is wrong about". Mechanism: K=2.5 average compute at coverage 0.5 could also be achieved by a length-only baseline if longer problems are wrong more often. Falsifiable test: compute selective-prediction curve using a length-only logistic baseline as null control — if it reaches 65%+ at coverage 0.5, F-8's headline is reduced from "geometric routing" to "length effect plus a few points".

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper's single-direction CAA ablation `h' = h − α·(h·v̂)·v̂` with mean-diff direction from 24+24 prompts is *exactly* our DoM construction. Establishes independent precedent for the single-direction ansatz, but also delivers the LOCO-CV diagnostic test we haven't run.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — paper's "detect → route → generate" three-stage framework is a clean theoretical home for our orthogonality finding. Detection (prefill) and generation (final) being geometrically decoupled is *predicted* by their framework, not surprising. This is corroborative for F-3's existence but reframes its interpretation.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive geometric signature)** — paper's entangled-Qwen3-8B example (political direction tangled with factual knowledge causing confabulation) gives a concrete *mechanism* for how D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong) could arise: a single direction encoding both correctness and a competing capability, where stochastic decoding flips behavior across the entanglement boundary.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500)** — paper shows cross-model transfer of routing directions *fails*; this directly corroborates the per-model-direction premise of H-1 and adds urgency to the ridge-residualization step before any steering trial.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final circuits)** — paper's surgical-ablation methodology is the residual-stream analogue of head-attribution; if prefill ablation removes the prediction signal, that's a stronger test than head-level patching.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM)** — paper's null-control critique applies: any unsupervised replacement still needs LOCO-CV diagnosis before being trusted.

## Methodologies extracted

- **CAA-style rank-one projection ablation** — `h' = h − α·(h·v̂)·v̂` applied across a band of layers with sweep α ∈ {2,5,8,12,20}.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min H100 on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B (just need forward pass with hooks).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's the causal complement to F-2 we've been deferring (P11-FE3-style activation patching).

- **Ridge residualization against capability axes** — Tikhonov-regularised projection that removes capability-aligned components (math, coding, reasoning, writing, safety) from the candidate direction *before* probing/ablating, λᵣ=0.01.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached prefill activations + need to define a capability basis (problem length, latex density, subject one-hot, math-token logit norm).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly addresses Refutation #4. Cheapest step toward "is F-2 actually correctness or capability/topic?"

- **Leave-one-category-out cross-validation (LOCO-CV)** as probe diagnostic — train on 5 of 6 categories, test on held-out; demand >>50% accuracy on held-out as the only meaningful generalization test.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached prefill NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — leave-one-subject-out across MATH-500's 7 subjects is the obvious analogue. Highest-leverage cheap refutation test in this brief.

- **Null/permutation probe controls** (food-vs-tech, shuffled labels) to establish the noise floor of the probe pipeline.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — shuffle-label baseline is one we should have already run for F-2.

- **Multi-judge LLM scoring** (Gemini 2.5 Flash + 3 AI judges + 1 human, 1–5 scale, 8-category taxonomy) for behavioral evaluation.
  *Replication cost*: minimal API.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — our outcome variable is correctness (binary, MATH-500 has gold answers), not narrative compliance.

- **Cross-model direction transfer test** — extract direction from model A, ablate in model B, measure behavioral change.
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B → Qwen-2.5-7B pair.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether F-2's DoM is universal or model-specific (paper says model-specific, would constrain H-11 distillation).

## Approaches & framings

- **Three-stage descriptive framework: detect → route → generate.** Reframes our prefill/final orthogonality (F-3) as a detection/generation decoupling with an unobserved routing layer between them. Suggests a missing third probe target ("which layer/circuit *routes* detection to behavior?") that we haven't searched for.

- **"Probe accuracy is necessary but not sufficient" as a normative principle.** Reframes every AUROC we've reported as a *candidate* signal that needs (a) null-control floor, (b) held-out category test, (c) capability-residualization before being treated as evidence of a correctness mechanism.

- **Mechanism shift / behavioral substitution** — refusal can collapse while narrative steering compensates. Analogue for us: if we steer against the "incorrect" pole on MATH-500 and accuracy stays flat, the model may have substituted a different failure mode (e.g., refused to answer, hedged, returned trivially-wrong format) rather than become more correct. Argues for richer outcome metrics than binary correctness in any future H-1 trial.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Ridge probe hyperparameter: λ=1.0 (fixed, not swept). For our DoM probes that's a defensible default rather than the sklearn default.
- Effective ablation depth band: 40–65% of model depth (their finding across 5 model families). For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers), this maps to layers ~11–18 — brackets our L19 DoM, modestly above mid-depth.
- α sweep range: {2, 5, 8, 12, 20} with effective range α=5–8. Useful prior for any DoM-steering trial under H-1.
- Capability basis for ridge residualization: math, coding, reasoning, writing, safety. Direct template for our analogue (math-formula recall, latex density, problem length, subject one-hot).
- Sample-set size: 48 prompts (24+24) sufficient for direction extraction. Suggests our 1024-tok DoM extracted from 500 problems is generously over-specified; we could compute LOCO-CV on tiny per-subject splits without sample-size concerns.
- Code/data availability: paper mentions GitHub repo (not URL-extracted from PDF); worth `pip` checking before any replication.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Leave-one-subject-out CV on F-2.** MATH-500 has 7 subjects (Prealgebra, Algebra, Number Theory, Counting & Probability, Geometry, Intermediate Algebra, Precalculus). Train DoM on 6 subjects' prefill activations from `pathway11_h100/.../prefill_*.npz`, evaluate AUROC on the held-out subject. Repeat 7 times, report mean and worst-case. **This is the single highest-leverage cheap test in the brief — ~20 min CPU on already-cached data.** Script: extend `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/run.py` with subject-stratified split or write `scripts/loco_subject_cv.py`.
- **Shuffle-label null control.** Same prefill NPZ, randomly permute correctness labels, retrain DoM, report AUROC. Should land at ~0.5; if higher, we have leakage. ~10 min CPU.
- **Length/latex-density null baseline for F-8 selective prediction.** Logistic regression on `len(problem_text)`, `latex_token_count`, `digit_count` only (no hidden states), compute selective-prediction curve, compare to F-8's 71.6% at coverage 0.5. ~15 min CPU.
- **Ridge-residualised DoM AUROC.** Project capability basis (length, latex density, subject one-hot, math-token logit norm) out of L19 prefill activations, recompute DoM AUROC. If it stays ≥0.74, F-2 survives the capability-control test; if it drops below 0.65, F-2 is mostly capability/topic. ~1 h CPU.

Each of these directly stresses F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) and F-8 (selective prediction at 71.6%).
