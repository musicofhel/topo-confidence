# Triage brief — 2512.16602 — Refusal Steering: Fine-grained Control over LLM Refusal Behaviour for Sensitive Topics

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16602
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Ridge-regularized mass-mean (WRMD) would refute F-2's choice of plain DoM as the optimal residual-stream summary.**
   The paper's WRMD vector is `ṽ = (Σ_N + λI)⁻¹ Δ` with `λ = 10⁻²` (eq. in §3.1). This is exactly the same construction as our prefill L19 DoM but with covariance whitening — a closed-form generalization of LDA. If WRMD-on-prefill-L19 beats plain-DoM-on-prefill-L19 on MATH-500 OOF AUROC by more than the ±0.01 noise floor, F-2's "AUROC 0.7731 plain DoM is the strong correctness predictor" headline is downgraded — there's a strictly better single-layer linear summary we never tested.
   *Mechanism*: covariance whitening projects out high-variance content directions (subject domain, problem length) that contaminate the raw mean difference. Their RMD/WRMD ablation (Tables in §4) showed WRMD = 23.82% CCP refusal vs plain WMD = 33.73% — a 10pp improvement on the same data with the same labels.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: refit WRMD at L19 using cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` activations + 5-fold OOF, sweep `λ ∈ {10⁻⁴…10⁰}`, report AUROC. P11-FE162 below.

2. **The paper's "refusal signals are distributed across many dimensions" finding refutes the spirit of F-2 / F-3 if it extends to correctness.**
   They report (§4.3) that refusal in Qwen3-80B is *distributed across many dimensions* — the steering vector is not approximately rank-1 in the residual stream. F-2/F-3 hinges on a single L19 direction carrying correctness; F-3 specifically claims prefill-DoM ⊥ final-DoM (cos = 0.046), which is consistent with two rank-1 axes. If correctness in Qwen-2.5-1.5B is in fact spread across `r ≫ 1` directions like refusal in Qwen3-80B, then DoM is capturing only one component and an SVD/PCA over the contrast set would find higher-AUROC subspaces.
   *Mechanism*: refusal/correctness are cognitive judgments, not low-level features; the paper's evidence is that judgement-style behaviors live in low-rank subspaces, not single directions, in larger models.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: do top-k SVD of `(activations[correct] - μ_incorrect)` at L19, report cumulative AUROC explained by k=1, 2, 4, 8 components vs the DoM baseline. If k=4 explains ≥+0.02 AUROC over k=1, F-2 is the leading-component story, not the whole story.

3. **Their layer-fraction finding (peak at layer 42 / 80) refutes our absolute-L19 anchor in F-2.**
   The paper identifies layer 42 in the Qwen3-80B-A3B-Thinking architecture (~80 layers total) as max-correlation, i.e. ~52% depth, and explicitly argues "refusal mechanisms operate primarily in the upper transformer layers." Our F-2 picks L19 of 28 layers in Qwen-2.5-1.5B, i.e. ~68% depth. If correctness, like refusal, lives at a *fixed fraction* of network depth, then naively transferring "L19" to 7B (28 layers) or to other architectures will systematically miss the optimal layer — and the cross-scale Pathway 5 transfer story already known to be confounded becomes architecture-fraction-confounded too.
   *Mechanism*: residual streams normalize activation magnitudes across layers; the meaningful coordinate is fractional depth, not absolute index.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: re-extract DoM AUROC for every layer of Qwen-2.5-7B (28 layers), check whether peak layer ≈ L19 (absolute) or ≈ L19 (rescaled to 28L = same fraction). Cached 7B Stage 2 NPZs cover this. P11-FE163 below.

4. **Their bidirectional-control degradation refutes H-1's optimism about per-position DoM steering.**
   Inducing refusal at α > 0 via WRMD: at 50% target political refusal, general benchmarks held; at 72% target, "general prompt refusal increased noticeably"; at 100% target, "model largely unusable" (§4.4). H-1 ("per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy") implicitly assumes you can move the targeted behavior without collateral damage. If correctness DoM behaves like refusal DoM, then the α large enough to move accuracy by an interesting margin (≥3pp) likely also degrades unrelated benchmarks (MMLU-Pro Eng dropped 76.88 → 67.70 even at WRMD with 23.82% CCP refusal — an 8pp collateral hit at moderate α).
   *Falsifiable experiment*: when running H-1 / P10-FE1, report MMLU and HumanEval scores at the same α as the MATH-500 evaluation. If MATH-500 +1 ⇒ MMLU −5, the steering is *not* a clean accuracy lever.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731)** — same mathematical object as the paper's RMD up to ridge regularization. Direct methodology cousin: confirms the "mass-mean direction is the right linear summary" intuition (paper picks Δ = μ_P − μ_N, just like our DoM = μ_correct − μ_incorrect), and offers ridge variant as a strict superset.
- **F-3 (prefill ⊥ final-token, cos = 0.046)** — paper does not test direction-rotation across positions; their refusal vector is computed at a single layer per token-position, but they do not check whether the "induce-refusal" direction is orthogonal to the "remove-refusal" direction (it should be antipodal, not orthogonal). Tangentially relevant.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — paper's finding that refusal is "distributed across many dimensions" raises the inverse possibility for correctness: if WRMD at L19 captures more than DoM, then CoE-60 may not be redundant — it may be the *trajectory* version of the multidimensional subspace. Worth retesting CoE vs WRMD-at-L19 (not vs DoM-at-L19).
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — paper provides a real-world precedent that aggressive steering breaks unrelated capabilities. Strong predictor for what H-1 will find on MATH-500.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM for refusal)** — paper *replaces* pattern-matching with an LLM-as-judge (GPT-OSS-20B); this is a label-source upgrade that's directly portable to H-12 (use GPT-OSS as the refusal judge instead of pattern strings).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Ridge-regularized mass-mean (RMD) and weighted variant (WRMD)** — `ṽ = (Σ_N + λI)⁻¹ Δ`, `λ = 10⁻²`, normalized to unit L2. Closed-form Tikhonov-regularized LDA-style projection.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached prefill L19 NPZs (covariance is 1536×1536 for 1.5B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — strict superset of plain DoM, free upgrade if it works.
- **LLM-as-a-judge for behavior labeling (GPT-OSS-20B)** — replaces brittle pattern-match refusal detection with a judge model that emits scores `z_k ∈ [−1, 1]`; aggregated across K=5 candidates with neutral threshold |c| ≤ 0.15.
  *Replication cost*: 4 h H100 + GPT-OSS-20B inference for 500 problems × K candidates.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes for B-bucket / D-bucket relabeling** — current correctness labels are a single binary; a soft confidence label from a stronger judge could sharpen H-15 length controls. **No for primary correctness** — MATH-500 has ground truth.
- **Augmented contrast set with conversational/code/math distractors** — extends |N| (negative set) with 500 code, 500 math, 500 arithmetic, 2000 SmolTalk samples to prevent the steering vector from learning topical signal instead of refusal/compliance.
  *Replication cost*: 1 h CPU to assemble + cache.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly applicable to F-7 D-bucket: augment the "incorrect" pool with diverse non-MATH problems to check whether the D-bucket signature is genuine fragility vs MATH-domain leakage in the activations.
- **Bidirectional steering with α-sweep + collateral evaluation** — same vector, sign-flip α, evaluate target and off-target benchmarks at each α.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 if integrated with H-1 / P10-FE1.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes**, mandatory addition to H-1's protocol — would prevent a "MATH-500 +1, MMLU −5" headline.
- **Layer-correlation scan with deepest-5%-excluded** — scan all layers, pick max-correlation, drop the deepest 5% to avoid output corruption.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached per-layer activations (we already have all 28 layers from P8).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — gives a principled re-justification of L19 vs the current "we just used L19."

## Approaches & framings

- **"Behavior axes are subspaces, not directions"** — the paper's central reframe vs Arditi 2406.11717's single-direction view. Bears directly on whether F-2/F-3's rank-1 story is sufficient or whether we should be doing rank-r subspace probes.
- **"Fragile pattern-detection ⇒ continuous judge"** — moving from binary pattern match to a continuous judge score is a labeling-pipeline upgrade we could mirror for B-bucket/D-bucket characterization.
- **"Negative set augmentation as a confound shield"** — explicitly aim the negative class at the orthogonal-to-target axes by mixing diverse distractors. We have not done this for our correct/incorrect contrast.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Regularization `λ = 10⁻²` empirically chosen across all experiments — a single hyperparameter, no per-layer tuning.
- Covariance computed from the *negative* class only (`Σ_N`), not pooled. Matters for our use: would mean computing covariance of incorrect-only activations, which is the smaller class on our 1.5B (243 correct / 257 incorrect) — fine, but worth noting we should choose the larger class for stable Σ.
- K=5 candidates per prompt with greedy decoding for the prompts, judge uses nucleus τ=0.6 / top-p=0.95 / top-k=20.
- Deepest 5% of layers excluded from intervention — for 28L this means skipping L26/L27 in any layer scan we run for steering (not for probing).
- Layer 42 of ~80 in Qwen3-80B-A3B-Thinking is the peak-correlation layer for refusal — fractional depth ~52%, vs our F-2 L19/28 = ~68%.
- 12-category refusal rubric (Appendix B); has an explicit "neutral" |c| ≤ 0.15 dead-band.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2512.16602 — Refusal Steering: Fine-grained Control over LLM Refusal Behaviour for Sensitive Topics (García-Ferrero, Montero, Orús, 2025/LREC 2026)

**Relevance:** Direct methodology cousin to F-2/F-3. The paper's RMD/WRMD construction is plain DoM + Tikhonov-regularized covariance whitening; it's the strict generalization of our prefill L19 DoM that we never tested. Three findings transfer directly to our pipeline: (1) ridge-regularized mass-mean beats plain mass-mean by ~10pp on the same labels (H-72); (2) refusal signals are distributed across many residual-stream dimensions, not a single direction — challenges F-2/F-3's implicit rank-1 framing (H-73); (3) bidirectional steering induces large collateral damage on off-target benchmarks at moderate α (MMLU-Pro Eng 76.88→67.70), forcing H-1 / P10-FE1 to add an off-target evaluation column (H-74).

**Key claim we tested:** Mass-mean steering directions become substantially better when whitened by the negative-class covariance (`(Σ_N + λI)⁻¹ Δ`, λ = 10⁻²) and benefit from negative-set augmentation with diverse distractors. Behavior axes are low-rank subspaces, not single directions.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — the WRMD upgrade is cheap (~20min CPU, cached data) and falsifiable against our F-2 0.7731 headline. The multidimensional-subspace claim is similarly cheap (top-k SVD, ~10min). Both H-72 and H-73 should be run before any external write-up of F-2/F-3 to determine whether the rank-1 plain-DoM story survives a stronger linear baseline. The collateral-damage protocol (H-74) is mandatory addition to H-1 / P10-FE1 regardless of the other tests.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE162, P11-FE163, P11-FE164, P10-FE162, H-72, H-73, H-74, H-75.
