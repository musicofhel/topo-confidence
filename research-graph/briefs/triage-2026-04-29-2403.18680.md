# Triage brief — 2403.18680 — Non-Linear Inference Time Intervention: Improving LLM Truthfulness

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18680
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Multi-token averaging refutes the sharp prefill-vs-final orthogonality picture (F-3).**
   F-3 reports cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 — i.e., the two probe directions live in nearly-orthogonal subspaces. NL-ITI shows that averaging activations across the **last 4 answer tokens** (probing) and the **last 6 answer tokens** (intervention) is the optimum, giving large MC1 gains (36.35% → 50.19%). If the truthful direction rotated quickly through orthogonal axes from token to token, averaging should *destroy* signal, not improve it. The fact that ρ=6, τ=4 is the joint optimum implies a **slowly-rotating** or **quasi-stationary** truthful direction across the trailing answer-token window. Mechanism: if F-3's orthogonality is a between-prompts-and-answer phenomenon (rather than a within-answer phenomenon), then the answer-side direction lives in a low-curvature manifold and our two-vector picture undercounts an entire smooth subspace of the answer-side. Falsifiable: take cached pathway11 per-token L19 activations, compute DoM at each of the last 8 tokens, then compute the pairwise cosine matrix of those 8 directions. If all off-diagonal entries are <0.1, F-3 generalizes to a per-token rotation; if off-diagonals are >0.6, F-3 is specifically a prefill-vs-answer cleavage and our DoM probe is single-point-sampling a smooth answer-side manifold.

2. **Non-linear probing shifts the "best layer" claim implicit in F-2.**
   F-2 picks **L19** as the best DoM layer in Qwen-2.5-1.5B at AUROC 0.7731, using a *linear* probe. NL-ITI explicitly demonstrates (Figure 2) that non-linear (MLP) probing finds the strongest probe-accuracy gains in the **first six layers** of LLaMA-2-7B — layers where linear probing reports near-chance. They argue that linear probing systematically *underestimates* concept presence in early layers because the encoding is non-linear there. If this transfers to Qwen-2.5-1.5B, our "L19 is the magic layer" claim is a property of our linear probe, not of the model. Mechanism: early-layer concept information may be encoded in non-linear features (e.g., XOR-like gates on token-position pairs) that the LR probe in F-2 can't see. Falsifiable: re-fit a 1-hidden-layer MLP probe at every layer 0..27 on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill activations (we have all 28 layers cached); if any layer < L19 reaches MLP-AUROC ≥ 0.78 while LR-AUROC < 0.7 there, F-2's layer choice is a probe artifact.

3. **Per-attention-head intervention (NL-ITI) outperforms residual-stream addition (the H-1 paradigm).**
   H-1 proposes per-position residual-stream DoM steering on MATH-500. NL-ITI gets +13.84 absolute MC1 (33.54 → 50.19) by adding bias *only to a Top-K subset of attention heads* (with α scaling per-head std), not by writing to the residual stream as a whole. Their head-selection is driven by per-head probe accuracy and an α intervention strength tuned against KL-divergence on OpenWebText. If head-selective intervention is materially stronger than residual-stream-level intervention, H-1's likely null result (residual-stream add) won't refute the *idea* of DoM steering — it would only refute the wrong granularity. Mechanism: residual-stream additions are diluted by the unrelated heads at the same layer; per-head additions only modify the heads carrying truthful information. Falsifiable: run H-1 with two arms — (a) residual-stream prefill-DoM add, (b) Top-K=48 head-level DoM add (computed per (l,h) pair using NL-ITI methodology) — both at matched α-on-KL budget. If (b) moves MATH-500 K=1 accuracy by ≥3pp while (a) moves <1pp, the H-1 protocol is wrong, not the hypothesis.

4. **OOD probe transfer claims (TruthfulQA→ARC/MMLU/OBQA) push back on F-5 content-dependence.**
   F-5 says breathing/DoM signal is content-dependent. NL-ITI trains its probe on TruthfulQA (~800 samples, factual-recall domain) and reports MC1 *gains* on ARC (+3pp), MMLU (+1.8pp), OpenBookQA (+3.4pp). This is the classic "the probe learned a domain-general truthfulness direction." If our prefill-DoM is also domain-general, F-5 is too pessimistic; if it is genuinely MATH-500-specific, F-5 stands but predicts a probe-transfer failure NL-ITI doesn't see. Falsifiable: train prefill DoM on MATH-500 train, evaluate AUROC on MMLU-stem questions or ARC-Challenge using the same Qwen-2.5-1.5B extractor. Drop of ≤0.05 AUROC = F-5 too strong; drop of ≥0.15 = F-5 confirmed and the NL-ITI generalization is a property of factual-recall tasks, not reasoning tasks.

## Methodologies extracted

- **MLP probe (1 hidden layer)** — Replace logistic regression with single-hidden-layer MLP per (layer, head). They report deeper MLPs overfit on TruthfulQA's ~800 samples.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached pathway11 prefill activations (Qwen-2.5-1.5B has 28 layers × ~16 heads = 448 head-positions; trivially cheap).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct head-to-head with our LR probe; potential to lift F-2's 0.7731 if non-linearity carries signal.

- **Multi-token probing (τ-window average)** — Average activations across the last τ tokens (τ=4 optimum for LLaMA-2-7B + TruthfulQA) before training the probe.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU; pathway11 has per-token activations for many problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests Refutation 1; cheap.

- **Multi-token intervention (ρ-window bias)** — At inference, add the mean direction (computed across the last ρ tokens of training Q+A pairs) to attention head activations during the last ρ tokens of generation. ρ=6 optimum for LLaMA-2-7B.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (requires online generation with interventions; same envelope as H-1).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — combine with H-1 head-selection arm.

- **Top-K attention head selection by probe accuracy** — Rank all (layer, head) pairs by MLP probe accuracy; intervene only on Top-K (K is a hyperparameter). NL-ITI sweeps K, picks the (α, K) that minimizes KL-vs-MC1 trade-off.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — fixes H-13.

- **KL-divergence-vs-task-metric Pareto curves** — Sanity-check intervention strength by tracking KL(intervened || original) on a neutral corpus (OpenWebText). Plot MC1 vs KL across (α, K) sweeps; an MC1 maximum near KL ≈ 0.5 is normal. KL is a "sanity check" only — generalization should be assessed on held-out benchmarks, not KL alone.
  *Replication cost*: trivial — we have a pile of unrelated MATH problems for the KL reference distribution.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would discipline the H-1 sweep.

- **Cross-benchmark generalization protocol** — Train probe on dataset A (TruthfulQA), evaluate intervention on B (ARC, MMLU, OBQA) without re-tuning. Reports MC1 + CE + KL per benchmark.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU per held-out benchmark on cached activations + 1h H100 if re-extracting on B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gold-standard test for F-5 (Refutation 4).

## Approaches & framings

- **"Truthful knowledge is distributed across answer tokens, not concentrated at the final token."** Direct contradiction of the implicit final-token-as-summary assumption baked into ITI. For us: a smooth-direction-across-answer-tokens framing reframes F-3's orthogonality as prefill-vs-answer-window (singleton vs subspace), not prefill-vs-final-token.

- **"Linear probes systematically underestimate non-linearly encoded concepts in early layers."** Direct push-back on F-2's "L19 is the layer" claim, which uses LR. This intersects our methodology, not our findings — but if a non-linear probe at L4 beats LR at L19, the finding's frame collapses.

- **"KL-divergence on a neutral corpus is a sanity check, not a primary metric."** Useful framing for H-1: sweep α-on-KL not α-on-task-loss to avoid overfitting the steering direction to the eval distribution.

- **Head-level rather than residual-stream-level steering granularity.** NL-ITI's biggest implicit framing shift: the residual stream is too coarse a target. Different heads at the same layer carry different concepts; steer the right ones, not all of them.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Model: LLaMA-2-7B (32 layers, 32 heads/layer = 1024 head-positions). Our work uses Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers, 12 heads/layer = 336 head-positions) — comparable scale.
- MLP probe: 1 hidden layer was best; deeper overfit on ~800 samples. Hidden width not specified in paper but their GitHub (Samsung/NL-ITI) has the spec.
- (ρ, τ) joint optimum = (6, 4) for LLaMA-2-7B + TruthfulQA. Asymmetric (ρ > τ): more tokens used at intervention than at probe training.
- Intervention: `x_l ← x_l + α · σ_l^h · θ_l^h` only at Top-K heads, where σ is the per-head per-layer standard deviation across Q+A pairs.
- α swept over a range that produces KL ∈ [0, ~1.5] vs OpenWebText. MC1 maximum sits near KL ≈ 0.5; pushing α further degrades.
- Code: github.com/Samsung/NL-ITI (MIT license, full replication).
- Few-shot prompting was used for evaluation (consistent with their ITI baseline reproduction at MC1 = 36.35%).
- Probe training set is small (~800 TruthfulQA pairs) — be careful about probe variance.

## Replicable intermediates

- **MLP-probe vs LR-probe at L19**: train a 1-hidden-layer MLP on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill L19 activations from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`. If MLP-AUROC > 0.7731 by ≥0.02 OOF, F-2's 0.7731 is a linear-probe artifact. (~30min CPU.)
- **Per-token DoM cosines on the last 8 answer tokens**: from cached per-token activations in pathway11, compute DoM at positions [-1, -2, ..., -8] and the 8×8 cosine matrix. Settles Refutation 1 directly. (~20min CPU.)
- **Per-(layer, head) MLP-probe heatmap on MATH-500**: 28×12 = 336 head-positions; train MLP probes on cached per-head activations (if cached at head granularity; otherwise use residual-stream as proxy). Identifies Top-K heads à la NL-ITI. (~1h CPU if per-head cached, else needs re-extraction.)
- **OOD transfer of prefill DoM**: take F-2's L19 LR probe, evaluate it on MMLU-stem and ARC-Challenge (we have a Qwen-2.5-1.5B extractor pipeline). AUROC drop directly tests F-5. (~2h CPU on cached activations + 30min H100 if re-extracting.)
