# Triage brief — 2604.27169 — Semantic Structure of Feature Space in Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27169
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1. F-2's DoM direction may be a semantic-axis composite, not a dedicated correctness signal.**
F-2 establishes that a single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731, and cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 shows this direction is nearly aligned with CAST PC1. Kozlowski & Boutyline demonstrate that semantic axes (e.g. true-false, certain-uncertain, wise-foolish) are substantially non-orthogonal in LLM hidden states, with the first 3 PCA components of 32 semantic axes explaining >45% of variance in a 3B model. If DoM/PC1 is primarily capturing projection onto this low-dimensional semantic Evaluation–Potency–Activity subspace — specifically the Evaluation axis (good-bad / true-false) — then the 0.7731 AUROC may reflect semantic content of MATH-500 prompts (harder problems may use language that projects differently on evaluative axes) rather than an internal correctness computation. This would reframe F-2 from "the model computes a correctness signal" to "the model's semantic representation of the prompt happens to correlate with difficulty."
*Mechanism*: Construct 32 semantic axes in Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L19 using their contrastive-pair protocol; project cached L19 prefill activations onto these axes; test whether semantic-axis projection AUROC matches or exceeds 0.7731.
*Falsifiable by*: If DoM has significant orthogonal residual after projecting out the 3-d semantic subspace AND that residual still predicts correctness, the semantic-confound hypothesis is rejected.

**R2. F-3's prefill/final DoM orthogonality (cos = 0.046) may be a semantic subspace rotation artifact.**
F-3 establishes that prefill and final-token DoM directions are structurally orthogonal. The paper shows that the optimal layer for semantic structure extraction differs by model size (30% depth for 3B, 50% depth for 70B), and that individual PCA components of the semantic subspace are not consistently aligned between datasets — only the spanned subspace is similar (requiring CCA to detect alignment). This demonstrates that semantic subspaces rotate across processing contexts. If the L19 semantic subspace rotates between the prefill position and the final token position within the same forward pass, prefill DoM and final-token DoM could both be semantic-Evaluation directions but at different orientations of the same underlying subspace. The cos = 0.046 would then reflect subspace rotation rather than genuinely distinct computational processes.
*Mechanism*: Compute the semantic PCA subspace at L19 separately for prefill-position and final-token-position activations; measure subspace angle. If both DoM directions lie within their respective semantic subspaces, orthogonality is explained by rotation.
*Falsifiable by*: If DoM has large components outside the semantic subspace at both prefill and final positions, the rotation explanation fails.

**R3. H-1's DoM steering prediction is undermined by predictable semantic spillover and scale-dependent resilience.**
H-1 hypothesizes that per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy. The paper demonstrates that steering along one semantic axis produces spillover effects on all geometrically aligned axes proportional to their cosine similarity (r = 0.681 for 3B, r = 0.652 for 70B). If DoM is entangled with the semantic subspace (per R1), steering on DoM will produce uncontrolled interference on "good-bad", "true-false", "safe-unsafe", and other evaluative axes — potentially degrading reasoning coherence even while pushing the correctness-correlated direction. Critically, the paper shows 70B models are ~10× more resilient to steering than 3B (Figure 13: on-target effects drop from ~20–40 percentage points to ~2–5), suggesting that any DoM steering effect found on Qwen-2.5-1.5B may vanish on larger models, limiting practical utility and cross-scale generalizability of H-1.
*Mechanism*: During DoM steering experiments on 1.5B, measure spillover on off-target semantic axes; test whether coherence degrades proportionally to semantic-axis alignment.
*Falsifiable by*: If DoM steering improves accuracy without measurable spillover on semantic axes, the entanglement concern is refuted. If steering effects persist at 7B scale, the resilience concern is refuted.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: The paper's core finding that semantic features form a low-d subspace (3 PCs >45% variance) directly challenges the interpretation of DoM as a dedicated correctness direction. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 already shows DoM is near-identical to the unsupervised leading direction — Kozlowski & Boutyline provide the framework to test whether that leading direction is simply the semantic Evaluation axis.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal)**: Their finding that semantic structure is consistent across Llama 3B/70B and Qwen 1.7B/32B provides a potential alternative explanation for breathing universality — breathing may reflect traversal of the universal semantic Evaluation–Potency–Activity subspace rather than a model-specific computational phenomenon.

- **F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046)**: The paper's demonstration that semantic subspace orientation varies by layer depth and model size provides a mechanism for the orthogonality that doesn't require distinct computational processes.

- **F-10 (PH adds no signal beyond covariance)**: Their semantic axes are explicitly linear directions, and their PCA analysis confirms the semantic subspace is well-described by linear structure. This is consistent with F-10: if the meaningful structure in residual streams is linear/semantic, PH would add nothing beyond what covariance captures.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)**: If DoM is a semantic-Evaluation axis projection, "problem familiarity" may reduce to "evaluative semantic content of the prompt" — MATH problems the model has seen more training data for may simply have richer evaluative-semantic associations.

- **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence)**: The Evaluation axis (good-bad) could be the conceptual bridge between breathing amplitude and the Sharpness Dimension — both may be measuring the same evaluative-semantic structure.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contrastive-pair semantic axis construction** — Mean-of-10-antonym-pair difference vectors (e.g. good/bad, true/false) computed from 4 prompt templates. Each axis is a direction in activation space representing a semantic scale.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (need to define antonym pairs relevant to mathematical reasoning + run 4 prompts × ~64 words through Qwen-2.5-1.5B to get feature vectors; but we have cached activations so only need the axis construction step with fresh prompts).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to characterize the semantic content of our L19 prefill DoM direction.

- **Norm-relative steering with spillover measurement** — Steering as w' = w + α‖w‖f̂ with α = 0.33, measuring off-target effects on all 31 non-target axes. Spillover correlated with cosine similarity between axes.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h GPU (need forward passes with hooks on Qwen-2.5-1.5B for each word × axis × direction).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes but lower priority — relevant only if H-1 steering experiments are pursued.

- **CCA subspace alignment** — Canonical Correlation Analysis to compare 3-d PCA subspaces between different data sources (human ratings vs LLM features). Finds rotations that maximally align two low-d subspaces.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (sklearn CCA on cached PCA components).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to measure alignment between semantic subspace and the correctness-relevant subspace defined by DoM + cov-spectrum eigenvectors.

- **Mean-pooled + multi-prompt feature extraction** — Average activations across all token positions for 4 semantically varied prompts, then average across prompts. More robust than single-token extraction.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h GPU (need new forward passes on prompt variations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for now — we already have last-token and mean-pooled prefill activations; adding 3 more prompt variations is low priority unless single-prompt results are noisy.

## Approaches & framings

- **Features as relational geometry, not isolated directions**: The paper argues against treating linear features as a catalogue of independent knobs. Instead, angles between feature directions encode meaningful associations, and the subspace they span is as important as individual directions. This reframes our DoM direction — rather than asking "what single concept does DoM encode?" we should ask "where does DoM sit in the relational geometry of all feature directions?"

- **Evaluation–Potency–Activity (EPA) as universal semantic backbone**: The psychological EPA model (Osgood 1957) maps onto the first 3 PCs of LLM semantic features. If our correctness-relevant subspace (DoM, cov-spectrum eigenvectors) overlaps with EPA, this provides a theoretically grounded characterization of what the model "knows" at prefill time.

- **Superposition-as-meaningful-entanglement**: Contra the "superposition = interference to be resolved" framing of Elhage et al. 2022, Kozlowski & Boutyline (citing Hinton et al. 1986) argue that non-orthogonality between features is *functional* — it enables generalization. This reframes F-10's PH-null result: perhaps the "structure" in residual streams is *supposed* to be low-rank/linear/Gaussian-like because that's the geometry of meaningful semantic entanglement.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Semantic axis construction uses 10 antonym pairs per axis, averaged; single pairs are noisier but still work.
- Feature vectors are extracted from layer at ~30% depth for small models, ~50% depth for large — L19 is 67% depth in 28-layer Qwen-2.5-1.5B, which is deeper than their optimal. This means if we construct semantic axes at L19, they may be less clean than at an earlier layer.
- Steering coefficient α = 0.33 (as fraction of residual stream norm) was selected by trial to avoid incoherence — relevant calibration for H-1 steering experiments.
- Qwen3 1.7B and 32B results in appendix closely match Llama results, confirming cross-architecture generality.
- Code/data: no repository link provided in the paper.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Project DoM onto semantic-evaluative axis**: Using cached L19 prefill activations (500 × 1536), construct a crude "true-false" axis from the model's own token embeddings for antonym pairs (no new forward passes needed — just embed "true" vs "false" etc. through the embedding layer, take differences). Compute cos(DoM, true-false-axis). If high, supports the semantic-composite interpretation of F-2. Script: ad-hoc numpy, ~15min. Cached artifact: `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` activations + DoM vector.

- **CCA between correctness subspace and random 3-d subspace**: Take the top 3 PCs of our 500 × 1536 activation matrix and run CCA against the correctness label vector. Compare canonical correlations to those from the top 20 cov-spectrum eigenvectors (FE881 AUROC 0.7928). This tests whether the low-d semantic subspace framing adds anything beyond what we already know from PCA. Script: sklearn CCA, ~5min CPU.
