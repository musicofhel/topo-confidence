# Triage brief — 2604.09780 — The Myth of Expert Specialization in MoEs: Why Routing Reflects Geometry, Not Necessarily Domain Expertise

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09780
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) interpretability claim**: The paper proves that linear projections of hidden states (like our DoM = difference-of-means direction) inherit the same "triangle" geometry as MoE routers — similar hidden states → similar projections, but dissimilar hidden states → unpredictable projections. Their Fig. 6 shows ~60% overlap between different models solving the *same* math questions, meaning that the L19 representation geometry encodes model-specific computation rather than problem semantics. If the DoM direction is similarly model-specific (not transferring across architectures), then F-2's AUROC 0.7731 may reflect an idiosyncratic Qwen-2.5-1.5B training artifact rather than a universal correctness signal. **Falsifiable experiment:** Compute DoM on a second 1.5B model (e.g., Phi-2, Gemma-2B) on the same MATH-500 and check cos(DoM_Qwen, DoM_other) — if < 0.3, the direction is model-specific and the correctness "signal" is architectural, not semantic.

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality, cos = 0.046)**: The paper's Section 5.2 demonstrates that prefill-phase hidden states and generation-phase hidden states are qualitatively different regimes — prefill collapses to near-identical representations in deeper layers (router collapse, Fig. 9), while generation diversifies. This means the cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 may be a trivial consequence of the prefill-vs-generation phase transition documented here (not a discovery about "two distinct confidence signals"). Their finding that "prompt-level routing does not predict rollout-level routing" directly parallels our observation that prefill and final-token directions are orthogonal — but reframes it as an already-known regime shift rather than a novel geometric finding. **Falsifiable experiment:** Compute DoM at intermediate generation tokens (e.g., token 50, 100, 200 of CoT) and track cos(DoM_prefill, DoM_t). If the transition is sharp at the prefill→generation boundary (as their router collapse data predicts), F-3 is explained by regime change, not two independent confidence signals.

3. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature)**: The paper's Proposition 1 shows that hidden-state similarity is *sufficient* to explain routing similarity — but that *dissimilar* hidden states can still activate *similar* routing patterns (the "triangle" shape). This many-to-one mapping means that geometric clustering (like D-bucket's collective signature) could be an artifact of shared syntactic structure rather than a genuine problem-difficulty signal. Their finding that "deeper layers exhibit near-identical expert activation across semantically unrelated inputs" (Section 5.3) specifically challenges whether geometric signals in deep layers (L19 is deep for Qwen-2.5-1.5B) reflect input semantics at all. **Falsifiable experiment:** Shuffle token order within D-bucket problems (destroying semantics, preserving token distribution) and re-extract L19 activations. If D-bucket clustering persists, it's driven by token statistics, not difficulty semantics.

4. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)**: The paper explicitly shows that during prefilling, "semantically different prompts activate nearly identical experts" (Section 5.2, Fig. 7). If the L19 prefill activations converge across semantically different math problems (as demonstrated for MoEs), then the prefill signal cannot encode problem familiarity or decomposability — it's encoding something about the model's anticipated output format rather than the input problem's properties. **Falsifiable experiment:** Present the same MATH-500 problem with trivially different framing (verbatim vs. paraphrased) and check if prefill DoM projections are identical. If yes, the prefill signal is format-encoding, not problem-encoding.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core theorem (Prop. 1) proves that linear projections of hidden states produce a "triangle" distance pattern — close inputs → close projections, but far inputs → unpredictable. The DoM direction is itself a linear projection (difference of class centroids projected onto activations). The 0.7731 AUROC lives in the regime where correct/incorrect means are "close enough" in the principal subspace to produce predictable separation. Their `‖PΠ_r‖₂` term (router-data alignment) directly corresponds to how much variance our DoM captures along the top PCA directions — relevant since cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** The paper documents that prefilling and generation are structurally distinct regimes with different hidden-state correlation patterns. In reasoning models specifically, later layers during prefilling collapse to similar representations across inputs (Fig. 9), while generation diverges. This is a mechanistic account for why prefill and final-token directions would be orthogonal.

- **F-10 (PH null — topology not distinguishable from Gaussian):** The paper's Prop. 2 shows that load-balancing suppresses shared directions, effectively projecting hidden states onto a lower-dimensional subspace. If similar rank-collapse / direction-suppression dynamics occur in dense models (which they reference via Noci et al. 2022), the resulting low-effective-rank representations would be well-approximated by Gaussians, consistent with F-10.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing):** Their observation that hidden-state correlation increases with depth (left Fig. 2) and that energy concentrates in the first singular vector at later layers is precisely what dimensional breathing captures — the dimensionality compresses at certain layers. The paper's SVD-based energy metric (Eq. 7) is closely related to participation ratio.

- **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension):** The paper's layer-wise analysis of hidden-state correlation growth maps directly to the SD framework — their "rank collapse" phenomenon is the MoE manifestation of what we call breathing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **RMS distance in principal subspace (Prop. 1 bound)** — Upper bound on projection distance using data-aware Lipschitz constant `‖PΠ_r‖₂`. Projects hidden states onto top-r SVD directions and measures distance there, giving a tighter bound than naive operator norm.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (SVD of 500×1536 matrix is trivial).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 activations to quantify how much of DoM's discriminative power lives in the top-r principal subspace.

- **Directional energy (Eq. 7) and retained energy (Eq. 8)** — Measures fraction of total SVD energy captured by direction ρ, and how much a linear projector (router/DoM) retains of each singular direction. Decomposition of a linear projection's discriminative power by PCA component.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly quantifies how DoM's AUROC 0.7731 decomposes across PCA components, complementing the existing cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 result.

- **Router confidence as OOD detector (max softmax probability)** — Average maximum softmax probability over tokens at each layer as a confidence/OOD signal. Applied to shuffled/reversed sequences as OOD probe.
  *Replication cost*: N/A (requires MoE model).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — Qwen-2.5-1.5B is dense, not MoE.

- **Sequence-level pooled hidden-state cosine similarity (Eq. 15-17)** — Normalize per-token hidden states, mean-pool across token positions, compute cosine similarity between sequences. Used to compare representation similarity at the sequence level.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could compare pooled L19 representations of correct vs incorrect to see if the DoM signal is better captured by pooled vs. last-token representations (relevant to H-780).

- **Overlap under top-P (Eq. 18-19, Jaccard on frequency-thresholded expert sets)** — Measures how much two activation patterns share their "heavy hitter" components by thresholding at cumulative mass P and computing Jaccard overlap.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (adapted to PCA loadings rather than expert frequencies).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could be adapted to measure overlap of "heavy" PCA dimensions between correct/incorrect groups.

## Approaches & framings

- **Specialization-as-geometry:** The central reframing — any apparent "specialization" in linear projections of hidden states is emergent from the representation geometry, not a property of the projector. Directly challenges interpreting DoM as a "correctness direction" — it might just be a "representation-geometry direction" that happens to correlate with correctness.

- **Prefill vs. generation as distinct geometric regimes:** Formalizes the intuition that prefill and generation tokens live in different representation subspaces. Intersects with F-3's orthogonality finding by providing a mechanistic account (depth-driven rank collapse during prefill, diversity during generation).

- **Load-balancing as direction suppression:** Training objectives can force models to suppress shared directions. Analogous framing: if the model's training objective implicitly suppresses the "correctness" direction (because balanced performance requires not overfitting to easy problems), this could explain why the DoM signal saturates at 0.7731 rather than reaching higher.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- SVD decomposition of token hidden states at each layer to get directional energy: `H = UΣV^T`, energy_ρ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵖ sᵢ² / Σⱼ sⱼ²
- Router-data alignment measured as `‖PΠ_r‖₂` (operator norm of router projected onto top-r data subspace)
- Retained energy of projector P on direction vᵢ: `(1/T) Σ_t ‖Pvᵢ ⊙ mₜ‖² / ‖P‖_F²`
- Models studied: DeepSeek-V2-Lite, GPT-OSS-20B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, ERNIE-4.5-21B-A3B, Moonlight-16B-A3B, Trinity-Mini-Base, Ling-mini
- All analysis done on sequences from OpenWebText (16 sequences for token-level, 100 for sequence-level)
