# Triage brief — 2605.04230 — Layerwise LQR for Geometry-Aware Optimization of Deep Networks

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04230
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

No genuine refutations identified after careful full-paper read — paper is likely off-topic for inference-time correctness prediction. Recommend weak CITED ONLY status.

**Rationale for this assessment:** This paper addresses *training-time optimization* — how to compute geometry-aware parameter updates more efficiently via an LQR control-theoretic reformulation. Our F-N findings and H-N hypotheses concern *inference-time* hidden-state geometry as a predictor of correctness. The mathematical vocabulary overlaps (Jacobians, curvature, spectral structure, cross-layer dynamics) but the objects being analyzed are fundamentally different:

- LLQR operates on the parameter-space curvature matrix H and layer Jacobians ∂f_i/∂θ_i during *training*.
- Our findings concern activation-space geometry (DoM directions, covariance spectra, PH) during *inference*.

The admission filter's suggested connections (H-600 A-LQR steering, H-542/H-636 multi-layer probe ensembles, H-733 why-L19-is-peak) rely on an analogy between training-time parameter perturbation dynamics and inference-time activation geometry. While the A_i = ∂f_i/∂x_i Jacobians do appear in LLQR and do describe how activation perturbations propagate, the paper never analyzes these from an inference perspective. No claim in the paper touches residual-stream correctness signals, probing, selective prediction, or hidden-state geometry.

Candidate refutations considered and rejected:

1. *F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer DoM)*: LLQR shows block-diagonal approximations discard cross-layer coupling. By analogy, single-layer L19 DoM might miss inter-layer structure that trajectory features should capture. However, this is a training-optimization result about parameter curvature, not about inference activations. The block-diagonal → full-curvature gap in LLQR says nothing about whether CoE-60's summary statistics capture cross-layer activation coupling. **Not falsifiable without importing a training-optimization theorem into an inference-probing setting.**

2. *F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 ceiling)*: LLQR implies single-layer views are information-lossy. But the 0.7731 ceiling is about supervised probe accuracy on cached activations, not about curvature approximation quality. **No mechanism maps parameter-curvature coupling loss to probe-accuracy saturation.**

3. *H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension)*: LLQR's Riccati gain matrices K_i could provide an alternative layer-importance ranking. But the paper never computes K_i spectra across layers or relates them to activation geometry. **Speculative only.**

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

Connections are weak and analogical rather than direct:

- **F-1 (Dimensional breathing)**: The layer Jacobian A_i = ∂f_i/∂x_i that defines LLQR's dynamics is the same operator that governs how breathing perturbations propagate across layers. The spectral radius of A_i controls perturbation amplification — this could explain *why* breathing amplitude varies by layer. However, the paper computes A_i only for training-time update derivation, never for analyzing inference activation dynamics. No numerical anchor applies.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ L19 DoM)**: LLQR's core claim (Theorem 4.1) is that cross-layer couplings encoded by M_i = ∇²_{θ_i, x_i} H carry information that block-diagonal approximations lose. If this principle transferred to probing (replacing parameter blocks with activation snapshots at each layer), it would motivate a coupling-aware multi-layer probe. Our F-9 result (CoE-60 is redundant with L19 DoM) might be an artifact of CoE-60's summary statistics not capturing the M_i-encoded coupling. No numerical anchor applies.

- **H-13 (Head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)**: LLQR's per-layer Jacobian factorization could in principle be applied to decompose attention-head contributions. But the paper treats layers as monolithic, not heads.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Learned structured inverse preconditioner (LLQR relaxation)** — Learn a block-diagonal (or Kronecker-factored) inverse preconditioner U by minimizing an LQR objective that retains cross-layer coupling, then apply as a gradient preconditioner. The "learn the inverse" trick avoids expensive matrix inversion.
  *Replication cost*: Needs full training loop with autograd for Jacobian/Hessian-vector products. H100 days for meaningful scale.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train models, we probe frozen ones.

- **Riccati backward recursion for layerwise curvature propagation** — Backward sweep computing gain matrices K_i that encode how curvature at later layers influences optimal updates at earlier layers. Exact solution recovers Newton/NGD steps.
  *Replication cost*: Needs per-layer Jacobians (autograd + forward pass). ~1h H100 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B per minibatch.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — designed for optimizer updates, not activation analysis. Would need substantial re-derivation to apply to probing.

- **EMA-smoothed periodic preconditioner refitting** — Recompute preconditioner every n steps, blend with previous via exponential moving average. Stabilizes optimization with minimal tuning.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — applicable to training loops, not to our inference probing pipeline.

## Approaches & framings

- **Neural network as discrete-time dynamical system**: Layers are time steps, weights are controls, forward pass is trajectory. This is a well-established framing (LeCun 1988, E 2017) but LLQR extends it to cover natural gradient descent and other geometries, not just Newton. This framing is orthogonal to our approach — we analyze activation *states* at fixed time steps (layers), not the *dynamics* of parameter optimization.

- **Structure on the inverse, not the geometry**: Instead of approximating the curvature matrix H with a structured surrogate (as K-FAC does), approximate the inverse H^{-1} directly under the full-curvature objective. This conceptual move — pushing structure to the output rather than the input of an optimization — might inspire analogous moves in our probing pipeline (e.g., learning a structured projection of activations under a correctness-prediction objective rather than applying PCA and then probing).
