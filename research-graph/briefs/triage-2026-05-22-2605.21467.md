# Triage brief — 2605.21467 — DelTA: Discriminative Token Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21467
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

**R1 — The "single direction" in F-2 may be an artifact of centroid-dominated averaging, not a genuine geometric signal.**
DelTA shows that standard advantage-weighted centroids are dominated by shared high-frequency patterns (formatting tokens) that dilute sparse discriminative directions. Our DoM direction (F-2, AUROC 0.7731) is itself a mean-difference direction — essentially a centroid discriminator in activation space. If the same shared-pattern domination occurs in our hidden-state centroids (correct vs incorrect prefill activations), then the DoM direction may be inflated by shared problem-encoding patterns rather than a true "can I solve this?" signal. The refutation would fire if applying DelTA-style discriminative reweighting to our 500 cached L19 prefill activations produces a *different* dominant direction with substantially higher AUROC than 0.7731.
*Falsifiable by*: compute DelTA-style α-coefficients on cached L19 prefill activations, extract a discriminative DoM, compare AUROC to standard DoM 0.7731.

**R2 — The prefill/final orthogonality (F-3, cos 0.046) could be an artifact of shared-pattern cancellation rather than genuinely distinct circuits.**
DelTA demonstrates that positive-side and negative-side centroids can look superficially different (low cosine similarity) while being driven by the same shared subspace dominating both. If most of the variance in both prefill and final-token activations lives in a shared formatting/problem-encoding subspace, the residual discriminative directions — which is what DoM captures — could be orthogonal simply because the shared pattern cancels differently at prefill vs final position, not because the underlying circuits are geometrically unrelated. The Song-Zhong residualization (F-3 control) addresses position/context confounds but not within-side shared-pattern domination.
*Falsifiable by*: apply the DelTA centroid-refinement procedure (iterative α reweighting) to both prefill and final-token correct/incorrect centroids, then measure cos between the *refined* discriminative directions. If cos increases substantially (>0.3), F-3's orthogonality claim is weakened.

**R3 — The D-bucket geometric signature (F-7) may reflect shared-pattern concentration rather than a genuine collective geometry.**
DelTA's core insight is that per-token (or per-sample) reweighting can suppress shared patterns to reveal discriminative structure. F-7's "distinctive collective signature" for the 36 pathological problems (group prefill PR 14.49, lowest of any bucket) could be an artifact of these problems sharing more formatting/structural tokens than other buckets — their "collective" signature may be shared-pattern-dominated rather than geometrically distinctive. If reweighting samples by their discriminative contribution (analogous to DelTA's λ coefficients) collapses the inter-bucket PR differences, the "collective geometric signature" dissolves.
*Falsifiable by*: compute per-sample discriminative scores (distance-to-opposite-centroid margin) for all 500 problems, then re-estimate bucket-level PR using only high-discriminativeness samples. If D-bucket's distinctive PR disappears, F-7 is undermined.

**R4 — The cov-spectrum probe (AUROC 0.7928) may be fitting shared covariance structure rather than discriminative covariance shifts.**
The cov-spectrum finding (top-20 log-eigenvalues of PC1-residualized covariance → AUROC 0.7928) extracts features from the covariance matrix of all samples. DelTA's framework suggests that covariance-based features may be dominated by shared patterns. If the eigenvalue spectrum is driven primarily by formatting/structural tokens that are common to both correct and incorrect completions, the probe may be fitting noise structure rather than a genuine correctness signal. The surplus over DoM (0.7928 vs 0.7731) could come from shared-pattern overfitting rather than genuine additional discriminative information.
*Falsifiable by*: apply DelTA-style sample reweighting before computing the covariance matrix (upweight discriminative samples, downweight shared-pattern-dominated ones), then re-extract eigenvalues. If AUROC drops below DoM (0.7731), the cov-spectrum surplus is shared-pattern overfitting.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** DelTA's discriminator view directly reinterprets our DoM direction. Our DoM = mean(correct activations) − mean(incorrect activations), which is exactly the "centroid-based discriminator" DelTA analyzes. DelTA's Equation 3 decomposes the RLVR update into μ̄₊ − μ̄₋; our DoM is the activation-space analogue. The 0.7731 AUROC is the classification accuracy of this centroid discriminator. DelTA's core claim is that this centroid can be *improved* by reweighting — directly suggesting our DoM may be suboptimal.

- **F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality, cos 0.046):** The "side-wise centroid" formalism provides a new lens on this. Both prefill and final DoMs are centroid discriminators; their orthogonality means the discriminative directions in activation space at these two positions are linearly independent. DelTA's framework predicts this could happen if the shared patterns that dominate each centroid differ at prefill vs final position.

- **F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics):** DelTA's token-level decomposition identifies which tokens carry the learning signal — analogous to our question of which token positions carry the correctness-prediction signal. Their finding that formatting tokens dominate centroids mirrors our need to understand whether the prefill DoM is driven by early-position structural tokens or later content tokens.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy):** DelTA's discriminative reweighting would predict that a *reweighted* DoM-based selector could improve the selective-prediction frontier. If the DoM is currently diluted by shared patterns, cleaning it up should move the accuracy-at-coverage curve.

- **F-10 (PH null — topology adds negative signal):** DelTA's framework explains *why* covariance-based features might capture what PH does not: the discriminator operates on mean directions and distances in gradient/activation space, which are inherently first/second-moment quantities. PH captures higher-order shape features that may be dominated by shared topological structure common to both correct and incorrect trajectories.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):** DelTA's per-token credit assignment could test this. If the discriminative tokens cluster at early-prompt positions (problem encoding), the signal is familiarity. If they cluster at later CoT-initiation positions, it's decomposability.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Centroid-discriminator decomposition** — Decomposes a mean-difference direction into advantage-weighted positive/negative centroids (Equations 2-3) and reframes the resulting vector as an implicit linear discriminator.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (just linear algebra on cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our 500×1536 L19 prefill activation matrix with correct/incorrect labels.

- **Entropy-regularized discriminative soft assignment (α-coefficients)** — Iterative procedure: compute per-sample distance margins to opposite-side centroids, apply sigmoid-softmax to get discriminativeness scores α ∈ [0,1], refine centroids using α-weighted averages (Equations 6-7). K=1 refinement iteration suffices.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (iterative but converges in 1 step on 500 samples).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the core technique to test whether our DoM can be improved.

- **Self-normalized reweighted surrogate** — Maps α-scores to bounded coefficients λ ∈ [λ_min, λ_max] and normalizes the loss by Σλ (Equation 8). Prevents magnitude drift during training.
  *Replication cost*: N/A (training-time technique, not applicable to cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train, we only probe.

- **Top-K hidden-gradient proxy** — Instead of full token-gradient vectors, uses LM-head output rows with frozen representations as a cheaper proxy. Table 4 shows this even slightly outperforms full gradients (24.29 vs 23.27 avg).
  *Replication cost*: N/A (requires training infrastructure).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training method, not a probe.

## Approaches & framings

- **Centroid-as-discriminator**: Reframes any mean-difference direction as an implicit linear discriminator whose quality depends on how well it separates the two sides. This is directly relevant to how we think about DoM — our DoM *is* a centroid discriminator, and its AUROC (0.7731) is the discriminator's accuracy. This framing opens the door to asking: is 0.7731 a ceiling or a floor?

- **Shared vs discriminative decomposition**: The observation that "higher- and lower-reward responses often share substantial common structure" maps exactly to our correct/incorrect activations sharing problem-encoding structure. The question of whether DoM is contaminated by shared structure is a new framing for F-2.

- **Within-side summarization vs between-side discrimination mismatch**: Our DoM minimizes within-group variance weighting implicitly (it's a centroid), but F-2 asks a between-group question (classify correct vs incorrect). This mismatch is exactly what DelTA identifies and addresses.
