# Triage brief — 2606.12289 — The Standard Interpretable Model: A general theory of interpretable machine learning to deductively design interpretable methods using Lagrangian mechanics

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12289
Triaged: 2026-06-14

(Barbiero, De Felice, Espinosa Zarlenga, Giannini, Bonchi, Jamnik, Marra, Noris — IBM/USI/Oxford/Pisa/Cambridge/KU Leuven/Czech Acad., 2026)

## Refutations

**R1 — F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos 0.046) may be the *generic* SIM CoT-faithfulness violation, not a correctness-specific signature.**
SIM §4.3.2 prompts **Qwen2.5** (our exact family) to emit a prediction + a chain-of-thought, then measures whether the prediction Jacobian ∇f lies in the span of the CoT Jacobian ∇c via Constraint II = `1 − ||Q_cᵀQ_f||²_F / rank(∇f)`. It finds violation **consistently > 0.8** — the next-token prediction is largely *independent* of the information encoded in the CoT, for *any* prediction. F-3 frames the prefill-DoM ⟂ final-DoM orthogonality as a deep structural fact ("can I solve this" vs "did I solve this" circuits living in unrelated subspaces). Mechanism: if SIM's Jacobian-subspace independence between "reasoning trace" and "next-token prediction" is a model-wide property of Qwen2.5 that holds regardless of correctness, then F-3's orthogonality is just CoT-unfaithfulness restated in DoM coordinates, carrying no correctness-specific content. Falsifiable: compute SIM's Constraint-II violation between the prefill-Jacobian subspace and the final-Jacobian subspace *separately on the correct and incorrect subsets*; if violation is ~equally high (~0.8) in both, the orthogonality is not correctness-discriminative and F-3's "structural, not positional" framing overclaims (see P11-FE1228 / P11-FE1231).

**R2 — F-2 / H-5: the L19 prefill DoM (AUROC 0.7731) is precisely the "uninterpretable latent direction" SIM's Premise II rules out, and likely fails Symmetry I as a "difficulty concept".**
SIM Premise II states verbatim that if a prediction "depends on hidden features, latent directions, or internal variables that cannot be expressed in terms of shared concepts, then the target entity cannot fully interpret the prediction." Symmetry I makes this testable: a latent score is a genuine *concept* only if its induced ordering is **monotone** w.r.t. the human concept preorder, and SIM Fig 9 shows even strong Qwen2/CLIP routinely violate this (non-monotone, presentation-order-dependent) for a trivial concept ("red"). H-5 reads DoM as encoding "problem familiarity vs decomposability" — a difficulty concept. Mechanism: project cached L19 activations onto DoM to get a score; if the DoM-score's rank-ordering disagrees with a human/GPT difficulty preorder beyond chance, DoM is a non-semantic latent (Premise II uninterpretable), not a difficulty concept. Falsifiable: SIM Symmetry-I preorder-violation rate of DoM-score against a difficulty label — high violation refutes the H-5 "difficulty" reading and demotes F-2's signal to "predictive but not concept-aligned" (see P11-FE1229).

**R3 — F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM): AUROC-parity is the wrong redundancy metric; SIM's subspace overlap is finer.**
F-9 establishes redundancy through AUROC parity (EXP-79/FE421: L19 ridge 0.8493 ≥ CoE-60's within-domain ~0.811 from a single layer). SIM Constraint II provides a strictly finer notion of equivalence — the principal-subspace overlap `||Q_aᵀQ_b||²_F / rank` between two feature gradient subspaces. Mechanism: two readouts can have *equal AUROC while spanning nearly orthogonal gradient subspaces* — SIM's CoT result is exactly this pathology (high marginal predictiveness, near-zero subspace overlap). If CoE-60's feature subspace overlaps the L19-DoM subspace by ≪ 1 despite equal AUROC, then F-9's "redundant" overclaims: the two are predictively equivalent but geometrically distinct, and CoE-60 could carry orthogonal signal that surfaces under distribution shift. Falsifiable: compute Constraint-II overlap between the CoE-60 feature subspace and the L19-DoM subspace on cached features (see P11-FE1230).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-3 ↔ SIM §4.3.2 (CoT Jacobian independence).** Our cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = **0.046** (0.0008 residualized) is the supervised-mean-difference analogue of SIM's Jacobian-subspace violation > 0.8 on Qwen2.5. Same orthogonality phenomenon ("reasoning trace ⟂ prediction"), two different estimators — SIM's is a Grassmann-subspace metric, ours a single-direction cosine.
- **F-2 ↔ SIM Premise II.** F-2's L19 prefill DoM (AUROC **0.7731**, OOF 5-fold) is the canonical "prediction depends on a hidden latent direction not expressed in shared concepts" object that SIM's Premise II defines as *uninterpretable to the human target*. Our headline finding is, in SIM's terms, a maximally non-interpretable-but-predictive direction.
- **cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 ↔ SIM Constraint II (rank-1 case).** Our supervised DoM ≈ unsupervised PC1 is exactly a 1-D Constraint-II alignment (Q_c, Q_f both rank-1): the correctness "concept gradient" and the dominant covariance direction are SIM-Symmetry-II aligned. The cov-spectrum probe lifting PC1-residualized to **0.7928** is the residual-after-projection that SIM Eq (6) would call the un-captured output variation.
- **F-8 ↔ SIM Symmetry I.** Selective prediction (71.6% answered acc at coverage 0.5) thresholds a DoM-score — a pure rank operation. Symmetry I says the *ordering* is the meaning-bearing invariant (monotone-map-invariant), which formally justifies reporting AUROC/coverage rather than MAE/calibration; SIM Fig 5 empirically shows MAE is an inappropriate interpretability proxy.
- **F-9 ↔ SIM Constraint II (subspace overlap).** L19 ridge 0.8493 vs CoE-60 0.811 is AUROC redundancy; SIM supplies the gradient-subspace overlap that distinguishes predictive-equivalence from geometric-equivalence.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Constraint-II subspace-alignment metric** — `1 − ||Q_aᵀQ_b||²_F / rank(∇f)` where Q_a, Q_b are orthonormal bases (via SVD) of two gradient/feature subspaces; rank approximated by the 99.9%-energy effective rank. A normalized Grassmann/principal-angle overlap (0 = subspaces nested, 1 = orthogonal).
  *Replication cost*: ~20min CPU — two SVDs on cached 500×1536 matrices.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly re-tests F-3 and F-9 with a finer metric than cosine/AUROC.
- **Symmetry-I preorder-violation rate** — fraction of sample pairs whose model-score order disagrees with a target preorder; rank-based, invariant to strictly monotonic score transforms. For a binary correctness label this equals `1 − AUROC`; for a graded difficulty label it generalizes AUROC to ordinal targets.
  *Replication cost*: ~15min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — recasts our AUROC as a SIM-grounded interpretability metric and lets us test DoM against an *ordinal* difficulty preorder (H-5).
- **Jacobian-subspace CoT-faithfulness probe** — autodiff the next-token prediction Jacobian ∇f and the CoT-token Jacobian ∇c w.r.t. the residual stream, then score subspace overlap (Constraint II).
  *Replication cost*: H100 (fresh forward+backward passes per problem).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes (Tier 2)** — it is SIM's own method applied to our exact F-3 question on Qwen-2.5-1.5B/MATH-500.
- **Prototype-ordering semantic repair (Fig 10)** — sort prototypes by a target attribute, assign each test point to its nearest prototype in latent space, read off the induced ordering; recovers correct concept semantics with *no fine-tuning*.
  *Replication cost*: ~30min CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — test whether L19 activations, prototype-sorted by difficulty, recover a difficulty preorder (a constructive version of R2).
- **Top-K concept/dimension masking + effective-rank sparsification** — mask all but the top-K activated coordinates, recompute the Jacobian, measure how prediction dependency concentrates (SIM: Steerling-8B depends on ~500 of 33k concepts, sparsifiable to top-16; violation follows `√(1−K/16)`).
  *Replication cost*: ~20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **partly already** (FE291 (PC1,PC9)=0.7856 is this spirit) — extend to "how many PCs does the correctness signal structurally depend on."
- **Constraint-compilation via Deep Set / monotone architecture** — compile an invariance into the architecture (e.g. `f = f₂(Σ f₁(z))`) so the symmetry holds by construction rather than by penalty.
  *Replication cost*: training a new model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — architectural; our pipeline reads frozen activations, doesn't train interpretable heads.

## Approaches & framings

- **Interpretability as preserved invariants ("immutables") under a Lagrangian** — recasts "is f interpretable for human h" as: which properties of f remain invariant under a group of transformations, with a Lagrangian `L = T − V` whose minima are accurate-and-interpretable models. Intersects our framing by giving DoM-as-correctness-readout a precise interpretability status: it is a predictive direction that violates Premises I–III (a non-concept latent), so "the model knows but can't explain" becomes a formal statement, not a metaphor.
- **Symmetry I — concept meaning = the induced preorder, not the score scale** (invariant under strictly monotonic maps). This is the theoretical justification for our rank-based AUROC/coverage reporting over MAE/calibration, and supplies an ordinal generalization for testing DoM against graded difficulty (H-5).
- **Symmetry II — interpretable ⇔ output gradients lie in the concept-gradient span ⇔ steerable through concepts** (Eq 4–6, links explicitly to Turner et al. steerability and Vandenhirtz intervenability). Intersects our (now REFUTED) `dom-steering` / `dom-causal-lever` premises: SIM predicts steerability *only if* ∇f ⊆ span(∇c); our refutation of DoM steering (FE269) is consistent with DoM's output-gradient lying *outside* any clean concept subspace.
- **CoT explanations are not interpretable** (a prediction is interpretable only if it actually depends on the explanation) — a conceptual sharpening of F-3 and of the prefill-vs-final orthogonality story.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Constraint II approximates `rank(∇_z h̄)` by the **99.9%-energy effective rank** of the Jacobian — a usable convention for our subspace-overlap computations.
- They use **h̄ (concept hidden state) as a proxy for f** because "the two coincide up to a linear map," and compute ∇h̄ instead of ∇f for efficiency — relevant if we ever Jacobian-probe: pick a layer where the readout is linear in the residual.
- VLM pairwise semantics tested with **3-sample majority vote** for robustness.
- Symmetry-II local enforcement (DNN+L) needs **~1000 epochs** before the constraint violation drops — local optimization is slow and only locally valid; architectural compilation (DNN+A) is exact by construction. (Cautions any "train a faithfulness penalty" plan.)
- λ₃ ∈ [1e-5, 10] sweeps the concept-formula complexity knob (Constraint III).
- A PyTorch **"Concepts"** library (Section 5.2) provides core modules/functionals for compiling these constraints; potential code reference if we ever build concept heads.

## 2606.12289 — The Standard Interpretable Model (Barbiero, De Felice, Espinosa Zarlenga, Giannini, Bonchi, Jamnik, Marra, Noris, 2026)

**Relevance:** A general theory of interpretable ML grounded in Lagrangian mechanics: interpretability premises → symmetries → constraints → a Lagrangian whose minima are accurate-and-interpretable models. Three symmetries are directly load-bearing for us: Symmetry I (concept meaning = the induced preorder, invariant under monotonic maps — the formal justification for our rank-based AUROC reporting), Symmetry II (interpretable ⇔ output gradients lie in the concept-gradient span ⇔ steerable), and the empirical CoT-faithfulness result on Qwen2.5 (prediction Jacobian is independent of the CoT Jacobian, violation > 0.8) — a parallel-method echo of F-3's prefill⟂final orthogonality (cos 0.046). Their Premise II ("predictions depending on hidden latent directions are uninterpretable") names exactly what our L19 DoM is: a maximally predictive (AUROC 0.7731), maximally non-concept-aligned direction.

**Key claim we tested:** Two things in our terms — (1) the information driving Qwen2.5's next-token prediction is geometrically independent of the information in its reasoning trace; (2) a predictive latent direction is interpretable only if its score-ordering is monotone w.r.t. a human concept preorder.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — F-3 (cos 0.046) is a single-direction analogue of SIM's subspace CoT-faithfulness violation, but we have not yet measured (a) whether that orthogonality is correctness-conditional (P11-FE1228/1231) nor (b) whether DoM passes Symmetry I against difficulty (P11-FE1229). cos(DoM,PC1)=0.9216 already reads as a rank-1 Symmetry-II alignment. Their methods (Constraint-II subspace overlap, Symmetry-I preorder violation) are cheap to run on our cached activations and could either sharpen or deflate F-2/F-3/F-9.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE1228, P11-FE1229, P11-FE1230, P11-FE1231, H-1228, H-1229; bears on F-2, F-3, F-9, F-8, H-5.

**Status:** TO TEST
