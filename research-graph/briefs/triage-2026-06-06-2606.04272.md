# Triage brief — 2606.04272 — RL Excursions during Pre-Training: Re-examining Policy Optimization for LLM training

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04272
Triaged: 2026-06-06

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-463 (F-2's correctness signal disappears on pure-SFT models):** H-463 predicts that pure-SFT models (no RL) would lack the prefill DoM correctness signal. This paper shows that SFT *degrades general capabilities by 4–8pp* while RL leaves them intact (§4.2), and that SFT *sharpens* the distribution (pass@k decreases during RL-after-SFT). If distribution sharpening is the mechanism creating the DoM signal — i.e., the model concentrating probability mass along a single direction for familiar problems — then H-463's prediction inverts: SFT models should have *stronger* DoM signal than base models (because SFT sharpens more aggressively than RL), not weaker. The refutation fires if DoM AUROC on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct (which went through SFT→RL) is driven primarily by the SFT stage's sharpening, not the RL stage. **Falsifiable by:** Extract L19 prefill DoM from a Qwen base checkpoint, a pure-SFT checkpoint, a pure-RL checkpoint, and the final instruct checkpoint; compare AUROC on MATH-500.

2. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — training-stage dependence:** The paper demonstrates that RL applied directly to base checkpoints *expands* the output distribution (both pass@1 and pass@k improve), while SFT→RL *sharpens* it (pass@k drops during RL). These are qualitatively different internal representational regimes. F-2's DoM direction was measured on Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, which went through the standard SFT→RL pipeline. If the DoM direction is an artifact of the *sharpening* induced by that specific pipeline — a concentration of probability mass along one residual-stream axis — then it would not exist (or would point in a different direction) in a model trained with direct RL from a base checkpoint. The mechanism: distribution expansion creates a broader residual-stream manifold where no single direction captures correctness. **Falsifiable by:** Train a 1B model with direct RL (no SFT) on math, extract L19 prefill residuals on MATH-500, fit DoM, measure AUROC. If AUROC < 0.65, the DoM direction is pipeline-specific.

3. **Refutes H-8 (prefill direction is stable across training checkpoints):** The paper shows that RL effectiveness and behavior change dramatically across pretraining checkpoints (from 4B to 50B tokens), with qualitatively different internal dynamics at different stages (seed brittleness at early checkpoints resolving by 10B tokens, §B.4). The parallel-averaging result (§5) further shows that combining RL and SFT gradients produces yet another representational regime. If the prefill DoM direction rotates substantially across these training regimes — base → early-RL → late-RL → SFT → SFT→RL → parallel-averaged — then H-8's stability prediction is violated. The paper's Figure 13 shows that even random seed choice at early checkpoints can produce completely different training outcomes (favorable vs unfavorable), suggesting high sensitivity of internal representations to training trajectory. **Falsifiable by:** Extract L19 DoM at 5+ OLMo2 checkpoints across the pretraining trajectory (e.g. 4B, 10B, 20B, 50B tokens), compute pairwise cosine similarities. If max cos < 0.5, H-8 is refuted for this architecture.

4. **Refutes H-707 (DoM rotates under different RL algorithms):** The paper provides the first controlled comparison of internal representational effects of direct-RL vs SFT→RL on the *same* base checkpoints. H-707 predicts DoM rotation between GRPO and MaxRL. This paper's finding that direct RL *expands* while SFT→RL *sharpens* the distribution suggests the rotation H-707 predicts might be better described as a *dimensionality change* — the DoM direction may not rotate so much as become ill-defined (no single dominant direction) under distribution-expanding RL. This is a stronger form of the H-707 prediction that changes the experimental design: instead of measuring rotation angle, one should measure the explained-variance ratio of PC1 under different training pipelines. **Falsifiable by:** Compare PC1 explained-variance ratio of L19 prefill activations between direct-RL and SFT→RL trained models on matched checkpoints.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's central finding that RL vs SFT produce qualitatively different internal representations (distribution expansion vs sharpening) directly questions whether the DoM AUROC is training-pipeline-specific. The 0.7731 was measured on a model that went through the sharpening SFT→RL pipeline; a direct-RL model might have fundamentally different geometry.

- **F-5 (Breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics):** The paper's finding that "targeted pre-training data is more essential than model size for RL" (§3.3) aligns with F-5: the content of training data, not the mechanics of the training procedure, determines capability. This strengthens F-5's framing that representational geometry reflects content-level signal, not procedural artifacts.

- **F-8 (Selective-prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy):** The paper's pass@k analysis framework (evaluating at k=1,8,32) parallels our selective-prediction setup. Their finding that direct RL improves both pass@1 and pass@k (distribution expansion) suggests that a DoM-based selective predictor on a direct-RL model might have *better* coverage properties than on an SFT→RL model, because the model has broader correct-answer coverage.

- **H-853 (DoM degrades under diversity-preserving RL):** The paper's parallel-averaging result (§5) achieves the strongest pass@32 — maximal output diversity — while preserving general capabilities. H-853 predicts DoM degradation under such diversity-preserving training. The parallel-averaged model is the ideal test case.

- **F-1 (Dimensional breathing is universal):** The paper trains a 1B OLMo2 and a 4B variant from scratch. If breathing is truly universal, it should be observable at intermediate pretraining checkpoints, not just at the final checkpoint. The paper's multiple saved checkpoints offer a unique opportunity to test breathing emergence during pretraining.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Parallel averaging of RL and SFT gradients (Algorithm 1):** At each training step, compute RL and SFT gradients from the same parameter snapshot with independent Adam optimizer states, then average the parameter updates. Achieves best pass@32 across all recipes.
  *Replication cost*: Requires training from scratch; not applicable to our inference-time pipeline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires GPU training; our pipeline is inference-only on pretrained models.

- **Base pass@k as RL-effectiveness predictor (§3.4):** The base model's 8-shot pass@k on the test set monotonically predicts post-RL pass@k. A lightweight diagnostic before committing to RL training.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU to evaluate 8-shot pass@k on MATH-500 with cached model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't do RL training, but the *framing* (base capability predicts post-training outcome) maps to our DoM: prefill geometry predicts generation outcome.

- **Pretraining-checkpoint sweep for capability analysis:** Saving checkpoints every few billion tokens and applying different post-training recipes to each, measuring both target-domain and general-capability metrics.
  *Replication cost*: Days of GPU training; requires access to OLMo2 training infrastructure.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires pre-training compute.

- **Distribution expansion vs sharpening diagnostic (§4.1):** Compare pass@1 and pass@k (k=32) before/after a training intervention. If pass@k drops while pass@1 rises → sharpening. If both rise → expansion. A clean two-number diagnostic for representational regime.
  *Replication cost*: 1h GPU to run pass@32 on MATH-500 with vLLM.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we could compare pass@1 vs pass@32 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B base vs instruct to determine which regime our DoM model is in, using cached hidden states to check if DoM correlates differently with pass@1 vs pass@32 correctness labels.

## Approaches & framings

- **RL as distribution expander vs SFT as distribution sharpener:** Reframes the RL-vs-SFT debate from "which is better" to "which modifies the output distribution in which way." Intersects F-2/H-707: if DoM is a sharpening artifact, the direction exists *because of* SFT concentration, not despite it.

- **Pipeline-specificity of internal representations:** The claim that "distribution sharpening is an artifact of SFT preceding RL, not of RL itself" implies internal representational geometry (like DoM) could be pipeline-specific. This is a threat to the generality of F-2.

- **Data composition as the binding constraint on RL:** Pre-training data mix matters more than model scale for RL effectiveness. Intersects F-5 (content-dependence): the representations that our DoM probes may be downstream of data composition choices more than architecture or training procedure.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- OLMo2 1B architecture, 4096 sequence length, batch size 512; pretrained on 50B tokens (~2.5× Chinchilla optimal)
- GRPO hyperparameters: lr=1e-6, 32 rollouts/prompt, KL coefficient 1e-3, AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.999)
- SFT: lr=4e-5, 5 epochs to convergence (Fig 14), 100 epochs for interleaved mode
- Parallel averaging: independent Adam states for RL and SFT branches, equal-weight averaging of parameter updates
- Base model evaluated with 8-shot prompting; post-trained models evaluated 0-shot
- Infrastructure: A100 and H100 GPUs; pretraining takes days, GRPO days, SFT hours
- Code: uses OLMo pretraining library and VeRL library

- **pass@1 vs pass@32 sharpening diagnostic on cached activations:** We have MATH-500 L19 prefill activations for Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct. We can compute DoM projection for all 500 problems, then split by whether the problem is "easy" (correct at K=1) vs "hard" (incorrect at K=1). If DoM projection magnitude correlates more strongly with pass@1 correctness than with pass@k correctness, this is evidence that DoM is a sharpening-regime signal. Uses: `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` cached data.

- **PC1 explained-variance ratio as expansion/sharpening proxy:** On cached 500×1536 L19 prefill activation matrix, compute PCA. If PC1 explains >30% of variance, the representation is "sharp" (concentrated along one direction). Compare this to a theoretical direct-RL model where expansion would predict lower PC1 explained variance. Uses: `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/` cached results (FE291 already computed cos(DoM,PC1)=0.9216).
