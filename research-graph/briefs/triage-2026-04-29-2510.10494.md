# Triage brief — 2510.10494 — Tracing the Traces: Latent Temporal Signals for Efficient and Accurate Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10494
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-9 / H-6 refutation candidate — layer-averaged trajectory beats single-layer DoM.**
   Vilas et al. report Cumulative Change AUROC = 0.74 ± 0.09 averaged across all transformer layers on R1-D / Phi4R+ / Qwen3-14B over GPQA / AIME 2025 / TSP, and explicitly demonstrate that trajectory signals (Net/Cumulative/Aligned) beat the cross-layer "Wang 2024" baseline (LayerMag = 0.58 ± 0.17, LayerAng = 0.67 ± 0.14). Their LT signals also use **temporal aggregation across reasoning segments**, which is structurally different from CoE (cross-layer at fixed token). F-9 ("CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM") was established at 1.5B / 1024-tok. **Refutation mechanism**: if we replicate Net Change and Cumulative Change on Qwen-2.5-1.5B's 1024-tok cached residual streams using the segment-averaging recipe (k=500 tokens), and they outperform our prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, then F-9's "redundant with single-layer" claim breaks at the temporal axis they exploit. **Falsifiable test**: compute LT-Net / LT-Cumulative / LT-Aligned on `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` traces and compare to 0.7731.

2. **F-3 refutation candidate — drift direction is task-meaningful even when prefill ⊥ final.**
   F-3 says cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 (orthogonal). Vilas et al. define the **drift vector** u_l = h_l^(N) − h_l^(1) and show that AlignedChange = ⟨v_l^(n), u_l⟩ / (·) gives AUROC 0.73 ± 0.08 — i.e., the "average direction of motion across the trace" is a discriminative correctness axis. **Refutation mechanism**: if our prefill-DoM is orthogonal to the trace drift (consistent with F-3) but the trace drift itself reliably separates correct/incorrect at AUROC > 0.74 on our 1.5B 1024-tok data, then there is a *third* informative axis in residual space that F-3 ignored — and the prefill ⊥ final framing was incomplete. **Falsifiable test**: compute drift = (mean of last-segment activations) − (mean of first-segment activations) per problem on Qwen-2.5-1.5B 1024-tok cache; project final-token DoM onto this drift; if drift's correctness AUROC > final-token DoM AUROC 0.7186, F-3 needs a third axis added.

3. **F-4 refutation candidate — collapse asymmetry contradicts cumulative-path negativity.**
   F-4 ("correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at final token") implies correct traces should have *more* representational change in PR / dimensional terms. Vilas et al. report **Spearman r = −0.38 between Cumulative Change and accuracy**: longer total path → less correct. They also report Net Change correlates *positively* (r = 0.28) with correctness. **Refutation mechanism**: if F-4's "harder collapse" actually corresponds to *smaller* cumulative norm and *larger* net displacement (more directed motion, less wandering), then F-4's current framing as a final-token PR phenomenon is dimensionally incomplete — the asymmetry lives in **path geometry**, not endpoint dimensionality. **Falsifiable test**: bucket Qwen-2.5-1.5B 1024-tok traces by correctness, measure (i) cumulative L2-norm of step updates, (ii) net L2 of (final − prefill) — if signs match Vilas et al. (correct: smaller cumulative, larger net), F-4 should be reframed as "directed motion" rather than "collapse."

4. **H-6 refutation candidate — trajectory signals are model-agnostic; single-layer DoM is not.**
   Vilas et al. report consistent Net/Cumulative/Aligned AUROCs across **three different model families** (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus, Qwen3-14B) on three different domains (science MCQ, math, NP-hard TSP). Our single-layer L19 DoM is anchored to Qwen-2.5-1.5B specifically. **Refutation mechanism**: if their LT signals replicate cleanly on Qwen-2.5-1.5B and 7B but our L19 DoM fails to transfer to R1-D-14B / Phi4R+ / Qwen3-14B (which we don't have results for yet), trajectory features dominate H-6's "CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok" — single-layer DoM may not even survive at 14B reasoning-tuned scale. **Falsifiable test**: when H100 pod is up, run R1-Distill-1.5B (already in P11-FE5 queue) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B with both signals; compare per-model AUROC.

5. **F-7 refutation candidate — D-bucket lacks distinctive *trajectory* signature.**
   F-7 says D-bucket has a "collective (not per-problem) signature." Vilas et al. claim per-trace Cumulative/Net/Aligned values discriminate correct from incorrect at the **single-problem** level (their AUROC is per-problem). **Refutation mechanism**: D-bucket items (K=1 right, K=8 wrong — the F-7 pathology) should look *correct* on Vilas-style single-trace LT signals if their K=1 trace was right, but we know they're geometrically anomalous. If LT signals correctly distinguish K=1-right traces in D-bucket from K=1-right traces in A-bucket on a per-problem basis, F-7's "collective only" framing is wrong; if LT is blind to D-bucket, F-7 holds and Vilas et al.'s training-free recipe inherits the same blindspot. **Falsifiable test**: stratify the 500 MATH-500 problems by A/B/C/D bucket; compute LT-Net per problem; check whether D-bucket K=1 traces look like A-bucket K=1 traces (LT blind) or like incorrect traces (LT sees the pathology).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, 1024-tok, Qwen-2.5-1.5B)** ↔ Vilas Net Change AUROC 0.71 ± 0.09. *Their average-across-layers Net Change is the closest direct competitor.* On comparable terms, our single-layer prefill is at the upper end of their distribution (~ +0.06 above their mean), but they report on 14B reasoning models on a 30-problem AIME subset — sample-size and model-class differences matter. Connection: their Combined LT score (weighted sum of three signals) is a candidate ceiling against which 0.7731 should be benchmarked.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ Vilas Aligned Change (cosine of step-update with overall drift). Their AlignedChange = 0.73 AUROC says step-updates do align with drift in correct traces. Our prefill/final orthogonality is *not in tension* with this — drift = h_l^(N) − h_l^(1), which is a separate vector from the supervised DoM. The connection is that both lines of work agree directional structure exists in residual streams during reasoning; we disagree on which direction matters most.
- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct collapse harder)** ↔ Vilas finding that Cumulative Change is **negatively** correlated with correctness (Spearman r = −0.38). Their "less wandering" phenomenology is dimensionally consistent with our "tighter PR collapse" — but their measurement is path-length, ours is final-token dimensionality. Could be the same effect viewed through different math.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** ↔ Vilas Cross-Layer Mag/Angle baseline (Wang 2024). They report CoE-style cross-layer signals = 0.58–0.67 AUROC, beaten by their LT-temporal signals at 0.71–0.74. *Important*: Wang 2024 (their cross-layer baseline) is the same Wang 2024 / Chain-of-Embedding paper (2410.13640) we anchor F-9 to. Vilas et al. essentially confirm that the cross-layer score is weak — but propose a **temporal** alternative we have not tested.
- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok)** ↔ direct contradiction with our F-9 if Vilas-style LT signals beat single-layer DoM on our 1024-tok cache.
- **H-15 (length-band PR control)** ↔ Vilas observation that Cumulative Change ~ trace length. Our concern is that the "trace length confounds correctness" pattern is documented in their data too. They normalize per-segment length but average across all layers; we should re-check H-15's design accordingly.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** ↔ Vilas claim of per-problem discriminability; tension if LT signals work per-problem but our prefill DoM is collective.
- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%)** ↔ Vilas early-answer-selection achieving 70% token reduction + 2.6% accuracy gain. Different mechanism (multi-sample MV-replacement vs. single-trace abstention) but same downstream goal. Their early-stopping recipe could plug into F-8's coverage framework.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Segment-averaged trajectory states** — divide the reasoning trace into k=500-token segments; compute segment-level state h̃_l^(n) = mean of token activations within segment n at layer l.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on existing 1024-tok cache (need to re-extract because cached pipeline is per-token, not per-segment).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's a direct extension of our pipeline and the most threatening competitor to single-layer DoM.

- **Net Change** = (1/L) Σ_l ‖u_l‖_2 / N where u_l = h̃_l^(N) − h̃_l^(1). Layer-averaged norm of overall drift, normalized by segment count.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU once segments are computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.

- **Cumulative Change** = (1/L) Σ_l Σ_{n=2..N} ‖v_l^(n)‖_2 where v_l^(n) = h̃_l^(n) − h̃_l^(n−1). Sum of step-update norms across the whole trace.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.

- **Aligned Change** = (1/L) Σ_l (1/(N−1)) Σ_n ⟨v_l^(n), u_l⟩ / (‖v_l^(n)‖ ‖u_l‖). Layer-averaged cosine of step-updates with overall drift.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the most novel signal we don't currently compute.

- **Combined LT score** — weighted sum of Net + Cumulative + Aligned, weights chosen on a calibration set so signals more correlated with accuracy contribute more.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.

- **Step-wise early-exit evaluation** — at each k=500 token checkpoint, recompute LT signals on the partial trace and measure ROC-AUC vs. final correctness. Yields an AUROC-vs-tokens curve.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to F-8 selective prediction.

- **Random forest on LT signals at 2k tokens for early path selection** — sample 5 traces in parallel; at 2k tokens, compute LT signals on each partial trace; classifier picks one to continue, others terminate.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (need to generate 5 samples per problem).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we want to extend F-8 from selective abstention to selective compute allocation.

- **Threshold calibration via cross-validation** — choose τ such that fraction of accepted-but-wrong traces is bounded; on calibration set, simulate full decision rule and pick τ that maximizes accuracy then evaluate on held-out.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.

- **Cross-layer baselines** (LayerMag = mean magnitude of layer-to-layer changes; LayerAng = mean angle of layer-to-layer changes, from Wang 2024).
  *Replication cost*: already implemented for our CoE-60 pipeline; trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a clean apples-to-apples comparison with our F-9 setup.

## Approaches & framings

- **Temporal axis as the primary correctness signal**, separate from cross-layer geometry. This decouples the "depth" axis (Wang 2024 / CoE / our F-9) from the "time" axis (segments along the generation). Our pipeline currently treats prefill (single position) and final-token (single position) as endpoints; Vilas et al. coarse-grain the in-between into segment-averaged states. Intersection: F-2 (prefill) and F-4 (final-token collapse) are both endpoint phenomena; the trajectory between them has not been probed at our 1024-tok scale.
- **Drift-vs-update decomposition** — separating overall direction (drift) from step-by-step motion (update) is a different geometric primitive than per-position DoM probing. Intersection: H-6 should be re-examined under this decomposition; CoE-60 is a per-layer summary at fixed token, which doesn't capture the drift-update split.
- **Reasoning trace as a coarse-grained random-walk-vs-directed-walk** — the negative correlation of Cumulative Change with accuracy (more wandering → less correct) reframes correctness as a path-geometry property. Intersection: F-4's asymmetric collapse may be a special case of this directed-walk hypothesis evaluated at the endpoint.
- **Training-free, model-agnostic recipe** — they explicitly pitch LT as needing no probe training, no annotations, no model-specific calibration beyond τ-tuning. This is in tension with our supervised DoM (which needs labeled correct/incorrect to compute). Could the unsupervised drift direction substitute for our supervised mass-mean?

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Segment size k=500 tokens, fixed. They tried alternative segmentations (delimiter-based) in Appendix F.
- All hidden states averaged at segment level *before* computing drift / update; this denoises token-level fluctuation.
- Net Change is normalized by N (segment count) to control for trace length; Cumulative Change is *not* normalized — by design, longer traces accumulate more.
- They average across **all** L transformer layers; no layer selection. (Ours uses L19 specifically.)
- Cumulative Change reported "sign-reversed" in their AUROC plots because the raw signal is negatively correlated with correctness — they invert for plotting comparability.
- Output-distribution baselines (Logit Margin / Entropy / Perplexity) were elicited via prompt `[…{trace_end} Final Answer:]` — interesting trick if we ever need a clean output-confidence comparison on our pipeline.
- Random forest classifier at 2k tokens uses LT signals as features; classifier is "lightweight" but no architecture details in main text.
- Models tested are all 14B-class reasoning-tuned (R1-D, Phi4R+, Qwen3-14B). Generalization to base (non-reasoning-tuned) models like our Qwen-2.5-1.5B is **not** demonstrated in this paper.
- No code link given in the abstract or main text we read; check appendix for repo URL.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2510.10494 — Tracing the Traces: Latent Temporal Signals for Efficient and Accurate Reasoning (Vilas, Yousefi, Nushi, Horvitz, Balachandran, 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct competitor in latent-space correctness prediction. Introduces a training-free family of trajectory signals (Net Change, Cumulative Change, Aligned Change) computed by averaging hidden states into k=500-token segments and measuring drift / step-update geometry, layer-averaged across all transformer layers. They test on three 14B reasoning models (R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus, Qwen3-14B) on three domains (GPQA, AIME 2025, TSP) and report mean AUROC 0.71–0.74, beating Wang 2024 cross-layer (LayerMag 0.58, LayerAng 0.67) and output-distribution baselines (Entropy 0.67, Perplexity 0.49). They also demonstrate inference-time scaling (70% token reduction, +2.6% accuracy via early answer selection). The temporal aggregation axis is **orthogonal** to our prefill-vs-final endpoint framing — and we have not tested it.

**Key claim we tested:** Layer-averaged trajectory signals over coarse-grained reasoning segments outperform both cross-layer signals (Wang 2024 / CoE-style) and output-distribution confidence at predicting final-answer correctness.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — H-72 / H-73 / H-74 set up the replication on Qwen-2.5-1.5B 1024-tok cache. Outcome will determine REPLICATED vs CONTRADICTED vs PARTIALLY CONFIRMED. Critical comparator is whether any single LT signal beats prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on our cache.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE70 (LT replication), P11-FE71 (Spearman path-length test), P11-FE72 (drift-projection axis test), P11-FE73 (D-bucket blindness test), H-72, H-73, H-74. Indirectly relevant: P11-FE5 (R1-Distill-1.5B comparison), H-6 (CoE-vs-DoM contest).
