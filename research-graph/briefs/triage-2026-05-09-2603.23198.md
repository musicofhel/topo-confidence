# Triage brief — 2603.23198 — Sparser, Faster, Lighter Transformer Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23198
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

1. **F-5 (Breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics) — FFN sparsity patterns provide a simpler mechanistic explanation.**
   The paper shows (Section 4.3, Figure 7) that per-token FFN activation counts vary by >10x based on token information content: predictable tokens (URL fragments, contractions) activate far fewer neurons, while high-information tokens (specific nouns, verbs) activate many more. Sequence-position effects are also large — first tokens have exponentially more activations. If F-5's "content-dependent breathing" is simply the residual-stream shadow of deterministic FFN sparsity routing (which the paper shows is already content-adaptive without any geometric interpretation), then the breathing phenomenon reduces to FFN activation-count variation across token positions, and the geometric framing is unnecessary. **Test:** Extract per-position FFN L0 norms for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 at L19 and correlate with the participation ratio curve. If the PR trajectory is fully predicted by FFN non-zero counts at each position, F-5's "content signal" collapses to "FFN sparsity signal."

2. **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) — DoM may be an epiphenomenon of per-problem FFN sparsity patterns.**
   The paper demonstrates that FFN output is extremely low-rank: at mild L1 reg, only ~29 out of 5632 hidden neurons are active on average per token. Even without L1 reg, only ~911/5632 activate (>80% intrinsic sparsity). If the FFN contribution to the L19 residual stream lives in a ~30-dimensional subspace that *differs* between problems where the model "knows" the answer vs. not, then DoM ≈ PC1 might just be capturing which low-rank FFN subspace the model routes through — a trivially interpretable sparsity-routing signal, not a deep geometric property. This would mean a simple L0-norm (count of active FFN neurons at L19) could match DoM's 0.7731 AUROC, refuting the geometric interpretation. **Test:** Run a forward pass extracting L19 FFN gate activations for the 500 MATH-500 problems, compute per-problem mean L0 norm, and train a logistic classifier on L0 alone. If AUROC ≥ 0.75, DoM's geometric interpretation is severely weakened.

3. **F-4 (Correct trajectories collapse harder — asymmetric collapse) — sparsity-driven rank collapse.**
   The paper shows sparsity increases with model scale and that different token types receive different activation budgets. If correct answers involve more "predictable" tokens near the final position (model is confident), they would have fewer active FFN neurons, producing a lower-rank FFN output that mechanically reduces the residual-stream participation ratio. The "asymmetric collapse" (correct PR 4.33 vs. incorrect PR 8.51 at 1.5B) could be entirely explained by correct final-token positions having higher FFN sparsity (fewer active neurons → lower-rank residual update → lower PR). **Test:** Extract final-token FFN L0 norms for correct vs. incorrect groups on MATH-500. If correct problems show significantly higher sparsity (fewer active neurons), the asymmetric collapse is explained by FFN routing without needing a geometric account.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's finding that FFN activations are extremely sparse (~29 active neurons at 1.5B with mild L1, ~911 even without regularization) means the FFN contribution to the residual stream at any layer is confined to a low-dimensional subspace. Since DoM ≈ PC1 of the L19 residual cloud (cos 0.9216), and the cov-spectrum probe lifts to 0.7928, the eigenstructure of the residual covariance may be dominated by the rank of FFN outputs. If the FFN at L19 contributes a rank-~30 update, this constrains how many eigenvalues can be large, directly affecting the cov-spectrum features used in FE881.

- **F-1 (Dimensional breathing):** The paper's layer-wise sparsity profile (Figure 6) shows a pronounced hump in activation counts in early-middle layers, with first/last layers being sparsest. This is structurally analogous to the breathing pattern (PR rises mid-generation, collapses at final token). The per-layer sparsity profile of FFN activations could be a contributing mechanism to the per-position PR profile we observe.

- **F-4 (Asymmetric collapse):** Per-token activation allocation (Figure 7b) shows exponential decrease in FFN activity with position. If the final-token position has extremely sparse FFN activations, this provides a mechanistic explanation for why PR collapses at the final token for all problems — and differential sparsity between correct/incorrect final tokens could explain the asymmetry.

- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null):** If the residual-stream covariance is dominated by a low-rank FFN contribution, the cloud would be approximately Gaussian in the FFN-output subspace (central limit theorem over many tokens each contributing sparse low-rank updates). This would provide a mechanistic explanation for why PH features match a Gaussian null — the residual stream is close to Gaussian *because* it is the sum of many sparse, approximately independent FFN contributions.

- **H-325/H-260/H-718 (per admission note):** The paper's finding that the ~1% active neuron set differs across tokens connects to hypotheses about whether the DoM signal concentrates in a sparse-surviving neuron subset.

  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether sparsity patterns explain DoM.

- **Layer-wise sparsity-to-speedup correlation** — Pearson correlation between per-layer average non-zeros and per-layer contribution to a downstream metric (they use speedup; we could use per-layer AUROC contribution).
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU on cached per-layer features (if available).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we have per-layer DoM from P8 layer-wise sweep.

- **Dead neuron tracking** — Track which FFN neurons are permanently inactive across all inputs, measuring the "alive" fraction per layer.
  *Replication cost*: 30min GPU (same forward pass as L0 profiling).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — relevant if dead neurons correlate with the DoM-aligned subspace.

- **L1-regularized sparsity induction** — Simple L1 penalty on FFN activations to control sparsity level without architectural changes.
  *Replication cost*: H100 days (full training run).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't train models.

- **TwELL sparse packing format** — Tile-wise ELLPACK format for efficient sparse FFN computation.
  *Replication cost*: Implementation effort for CUDA kernels.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — infrastructure, not analysis.

## Approaches & framings

- **FFN as dynamic key-value memory.** The paper frames FFN layers as sparse knowledge stores where the gate projection selects which "memory slots" (down-projection rows) to retrieve. This intersects our F-2 framing: if the DoM direction ≈ PC1 aligns with the dominant FFN retrieval pattern, DoM could be understood as a "knowledge retrieval mode" detector — the model activates a specific sparse neuron set when it recognizes a problem type, and DoM captures this routing signature in the residual stream.

- **Information-adaptive computation.** The finding that LLMs allocate more FFN computation (more active neurons) to high-information-content tokens suggests a natural information-theoretic framing for breathing: PR rises during reasoning because reasoning tokens carry more information and activate more neurons, then collapses at the answer because the answer token is highly predictable given the chain-of-thought.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Model architecture: "Transformer++" (Qwen/Llama family), gated FFN with ReLU replacing SiLU. Hidden FFN dim for 1.5B model: 5632. Context length: 2048.
- ReLU activation produces exact zeros enabling true sparsity; SiLU does not. Qwen-2.5 uses SiLU by default.
- At 1.5B without any L1 reg, intrinsic ReLU sparsity gives ~911 active neurons out of 5632 (83.8% sparse).
- With L1=2e-5 (recommended): ~29 active neurons out of 5632 (99.5% sparse), negligible performance loss.
- Sparsity settles within ~1000 training steps (~1B tokens).
- Dead neuron fraction exceeds 30% at recommended L1 levels.
- Per-layer sparsity: first two layers least active, hump in early-middle layers, peak ~layer 6 (with L1 reg) or ~layer 13 (without).
- Code: github.com/SakanaAI/sparser-faster-llms

## 2603.23198 — Sparser, Faster, Lighter Transformer Language Models (Cetin, Peluchetti, Castillo, Naruse, Murakami, Jones, 2026)

**Relevance:** Studies unstructured sparsity in FFN layers of transformer LLMs, finding that only ~1% of FFN neurons activate per token. The per-token and per-layer activation count patterns (content-dependent, position-dependent, layer-dependent) provide a potential alternative mechanistic explanation for the residual-stream geometry signals (DoM, breathing, asymmetric collapse) observed in our Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 experiments. If FFN sparsity routing explains the correctness signal, our geometric interpretation needs revision.

**Key claim we tested:** FFN activation sparsity is extreme (~99%+ with mild L1 reg) and content-adaptive, with different tokens receiving different computational budgets based on information content.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — We have not yet extracted FFN gate activations for our MATH-500 problems. The paper's sparsity claims are for their own trained models (with ReLU), while Qwen-2.5 uses SiLU (which produces no exact zeros). However, SiLU models still exhibit substantial *approximate* sparsity (many near-zero activations), and the qualitative pattern of content-adaptive activation allocation likely transfers. Key experiments: P11-FE124202 (L0 vs DoM AUROC), P11-FE124203 (sparsity and asymmetric collapse), P11-FE124200 (stable rank of cached activations).
