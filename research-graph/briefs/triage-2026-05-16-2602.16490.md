# Triage brief — 2602.16490 — From Growing to Looping: A Unified View of Iterative Computation in LLMs

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16490
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence):** The paper shows that residual norm growth is *slower* in looped/depth-grown models vs. baselines, and that computational progress (measured via Tuned Lens early-exit accuracy) continues improving through late layers. If our L19 DoM peak (F-2, AUROC 0.7731) corresponds to an iterative-computation fixed point rather than a "sharpening" event, then the dimensional breathing framing is misattributed — the signal would be detecting convergence of an iterative computation loop rather than a geometric sharpening phenomenon. Falsifiable by: computing depth-score and per-layer early-exit accuracy on our cached per-layer activations and testing whether L19's AUROC peak aligns with an iterative convergence signature (stabilization of cosine similarity between consecutive layers) rather than a dimensional collapse signature.

2. **Refutes F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046):** The paper demonstrates 4-layer periodic patterns in attention sublayer contributions and "aggregation layers" that depend on broad previous context. If Qwen-2.5-1.5B has implicit periodic block structure (as depth-grown models do), the orthogonality between prefill and final DoM might reflect that these two probes land on *different phases of a periodic computational cycle* rather than encoding structurally distinct geometric properties. The prefill direction captures one phase of the 4-layer cycle; final-token captures another. Falsifiable by: checking whether cos(DoM_L17, DoM_L21) and cos(DoM_L18, DoM_L22) show periodic structure — if adjacent-period layers have *high* similarity while same-layer different-position (prefill vs final) have low similarity, the periodicity explanation wins over the "structurally distinct directions" interpretation.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM):** The paper shows looped/depth-grown models benefit from *accumulation across repeated blocks* — single early-exit (analogous to single-layer DoM) misses information that only emerges from iterative refinement across multiple passes of the same layers. If the L17–L21 "flat AUROC band" (H-442) reflects repeated computation rather than a single decisive layer, then CoE-60's trajectory aggregation might capture cross-iteration dynamics that single-layer DoM structurally cannot. The F-9 finding (redundancy) would hold only because our baseline Qwen model doesn't exhibit strong looping behavior — but this is a contingent, not structural, result. Falsifiable by: testing whether models with explicit looping (e.g., a Loop(4×6) variant) show CoE-style trajectory features significantly beating single-layer DoM, establishing that F-9's redundancy is architecture-dependent.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's depth-score analysis and Tuned Lens early-exit findings directly bear on *why* L19 is special. Their convergent depth-wise signatures suggest L19 may be the layer where an iterative computation stabilizes — the "last aggregation layer" before output. The paper's SmolLM results show these signatures emerge at predictable fractional depths (~0.7–0.8 of total depth), which maps to L19/24 ≈ 0.79 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B.

- **H-489 (fractional depth universality):** The paper provides direct evidence. Both looping and depth-growing produce performance peaks at consistent fractional positions regardless of absolute depth. Their SmolLM-360M (12 layers) and SmolLM-1.7B (24 layers) show aligned behavior at relative positions. This confirms the hypothesis that L19's importance is a fractional-depth phenomenon.

- **H-733 (L19 as change-point):** The paper's "aggregation layer" analysis (via local-effect interventions) shows layers at specific periodic positions serve as information-integration points. L19 in a 24-layer model falls at exactly the position their framework predicts the final major aggregation layer.

- **H-231 (L19 as ensembling block):** The paper's finding that middle-block looping models show improved reasoning, with aggregation layers integrating distributed computation, directly supports the interpretation that L19 functions as an ensembling/aggregation layer.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universality):** The paper's residual norm analysis shows periodic expansion/compression of representation magnitude across depth in both looped and grown models. This is a parallel discovery of "breathing" at the norm level (our F-1 is about participation ratio / effective dimensionality), suggesting a shared underlying mechanism.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Depth score (Csordás et al.)** — Measures relative importance of each layer to final output by comparing full-model logits to layer-ablated logits. Quantifies "late-layer reliance."
  *Replication cost*: 30min–1hr CPU (requires forward passes with layer ablation, not just cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — need fresh forward passes on Qwen-2.5-1.5B but straightforward to implement; directly tests whether L19 is a depth-score peak.

- **Tuned Lens early-exit accuracy** — Per-layer linear probes from residual stream to vocabulary, measuring task accuracy extractable at each layer. Already in our graph (2303.08112).
  *Replication cost*: 2–4hr GPU (need to train 24 linear probes on MATH-500 hidden states).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — would show whether L19 corresponds to an accuracy plateau (convergence) or an inflection point (last big jump).

- **Residual norm growth rate analysis** — Track ||h_l|| / ||h_0|| across layers, comparing correct vs. incorrect. Decompose into attention and MLP sublayer contributions.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (can compute from cached per-layer activations if available, otherwise 30min GPU for fresh extraction).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — if we have per-layer norms cached, this is trivial; tells us whether correct/incorrect diverge in norm growth rate at L19.

- **Attention sublayer contribution ratio** — ||attention_output_l|| / ||residual_l|| per layer. Reveals periodic structure aligned with computational blocks.
  *Replication cost*: 30min GPU (need attention output norms, not typically cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires fresh forward pass with attention output hooks, lower priority than depth score.

- **Local-effect intervention** — Remove layer l's contribution from all downstream layers (not just layer l+1). Maps which layers serve as "aggregation" vs "processing" layers.
  *Replication cost*: 2–3hr GPU (24² interventions × 500 samples).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — expensive, and depth score gives a cheaper proxy for the same question.

- **Inference-time looping** — Repeat a block of layers k times at inference without training. Tests whether a model's computation benefits from additional iterative refinement.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2hr GPU (need to run Qwen-2.5-1.5B with layers 16–20 repeated 2–3×).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether L19's importance is because it's doing iterative computation that benefits from repetition. High signal-to-insight ratio.

## Approaches & framings

- **Iterative computation as fixed-point convergence:** Rather than viewing depth as "feature hierarchy," reframes deep layers as iterations converging toward a solution. Intersects F-2 by suggesting L19 DoM predicts correctness because it detects whether the iterative solver has converged — convergent = correct, divergent = incorrect.

- **Block-periodic structure as architectural primitive:** Even untied models develop implicit periodic patterns. Intersects H-442 (flat AUROC band L17–L21) by suggesting this band corresponds to one "computational block" that functions as a unit.

- **"Grow first, loop later" composability:** Depth-growing during training + inference-time looping at test time. Intersects H-1 (DoM steering) by suggesting that if L19 represents an iterative step, the correct intervention isn't "steer toward DoM direction" but "repeat the computation block until convergence."

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Loop notation: Loop(e-L×k-d) means e unique encoder layers + L-layer block repeated k times + d unique decoder layers. Total effective depth = e + L·k + d.
- MIDAS block size B=4 for depth growing; PROP-1 schedule allocates training time proportional to stage index.
- Inference-time looping works best with 1–2 additional repetitions; more repetitions degrade performance.
- Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ at 20% during final 15% of pretraining ("math cooldown") yields best reasoning gains.
- Code not linked in the abstract page; no public repository mentioned.
