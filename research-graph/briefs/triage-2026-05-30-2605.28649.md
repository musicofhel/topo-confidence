# Triage brief — 2605.28649 — Interpretability-Guided Layer Selection over Subspace Projection: SAEs as Stethoscopes, Not Scalpels, for Raw Task Vector Model Editing

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28649
Triaged: 2026-05-30

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — mechanism: SAE-diagnosed geometric misalignment between activation-space probes and weight-space interventions.**
   The paper demonstrates that projecting weight-space task vectors through activation-space SAE features retains only ≤3.5% of modification energy and produces zero statistically significant gains (Table 3). Our DoM direction lives in activation space (it's a 1536-d direction in L19 residual stream). H-1 proposes steering by *adding* a scaled DoM vector to activations during generation — but this paper's core negative result shows that activation-space directions and weight-space modifications occupy fundamentally misaligned geometric objects. If this geometric misalignment extends to activation-space steering vectors (which also attempt to modify computation by injecting activation-space directions), then H-1's DoM steering approach could suffer an analogous information bottleneck. The falsifying experiment: compare DoM steering (activation-space injection) against a weight-space equivalent (e.g., task vector from LoRA fine-tuning on DoM-high vs DoM-low problems, injected into L19 weights only). If the weight-space version substantially outperforms activation-space DoM steering, the refutation holds.

2. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — mechanism: the specificity score reveals layer-level specialization is distributed, not concentrated at a single layer.**
   Table 6 shows that on Gemma-3-4B-IT, math-relevant SAE features are distributed across 14/34 layers (SP ≥ 4.0), with high-specificity layers spanning L14–L32 and a bimodal peak at mid-network (14–17) and late-network (30–32). If a similar SAE diagnostic on Qwen-2.5-1.5B showed that math-correctness signal is distributed across many layers rather than concentrated at L19, F-2's "single L19 direction" framing would be an incomplete account — the 0.7731 AUROC ceiling might reflect looking at only one of many signal-bearing layers. The falsifying experiment: compute SAE specificity scores (or a simpler per-layer DoM AUROC sweep) across all 28 Qwen layers and test whether a multi-layer probe combining the top-k SP layers significantly exceeds 0.7731. We already have partial evidence against this (F-9 shows CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM), but that used a different multi-layer aggregation method than SAE-guided selection.

3. **Refutes H-878 (Reconstruction-only SAE on L19 prefill activations recovers correctness-predictive features without supervision) — mechanism: SAEs on randomly initialized transformers produce indistinguishable features.**
   The paper cites Heap et al. [10] (2501.17727) showing "SAEs trained on randomly initialized transformers produce features qualitatively similar to those from trained models." If SAE features don't reliably distinguish trained from untrained model structure, H-878's proposal to use a reconstruction-only SAE to recover correctness-predictive features may find features that look interpretable but are geometrically arbitrary. The falsifying experiment: train an SAE on L19 prefill activations from a randomly initialized Qwen-2.5-1.5B, compare the feature specificity landscape to one trained on the real model, and test whether "correctness-predictive" features from the random model achieve comparable AUROC to the trained model's features.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's per-layer specificity score (SP) is a diagnostic for identifying which layers carry domain-relevant computation. For Gemma-3-4B-IT, layer 19 has SP=7.82 (3rd highest after L31=8.80, L20=7.00) — independently confirming that layer 19 is among the most domain-specialized for math. This is cross-architecture corroboration of F-2's L19 finding, albeit on Gemma rather than Qwen.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with single-layer L19 DoM):** The paper's "modification budget conservation" (n_layers × α_opt ≈ 11.2) suggests that multi-layer interventions dilute signal when one layer dominates. This is conceptually parallel to F-9's finding that 60-layer trajectory features don't beat single-layer L19 — both point to information concentration at a small number of critical layers.

- **F-10 (PH-null on residual streams):** The paper's finding that SAE features capture activation-space statistical regularities (not weight-space computation) is consistent with F-10's conclusion that topology summary statistics on residual streams are not distinguishable from a Gaussian null — both suggest that naive activation-space decompositions miss the computational structure that actually drives task performance.

- **H-1 (DoM steering):** The paper's stethoscope-not-scalpel thesis directly addresses H-1's activation-space steering approach. Their finding that activation-space SAE directions lose ~97% of modification energy when used for intervention (rather than diagnosis) is a cautionary signal for DoM steering. However, the analogy is imperfect: DoM steering adds to activations during forward pass, whereas their negative result concerns projecting weight-space task vectors through activation-space features. The two failure modes may have different roots.

- **H-42 (SMITIN-style self-monitoring for DoM steering):** The paper's α response curve (Figure 6) shows that even with correct layer selection, the scaling parameter must be tuned within a specific range (α ∈ [0.70, 1.10]). This parallels H-42's hypothesis that DoM steering requires adaptive (not constant) scaling, and the "modification budget conservation" rule (n_layers × α ≈ 11.2) could inform how to set per-layer steering magnitudes.

## Methodologies extracted

- **SAE-derived Specificity Score (SP)** — Per-layer diagnostic: for each SAE feature j at layer l, compute spec(l,j) = mean_activation_target / (mean_activation_other + ε). SP(l) = max_j spec(l,j). Threshold at SP ≥ 4.0 to identify domain-specialized layers.
  *Replication cost*: Requires pre-trained SAEs for Qwen-2.5-1.5B (not publicly available); if SAEs were available, computation is ~30min GPU for feature activation collection.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — no public Qwen SAE suite exists, and training one is expensive. However, a simpler per-layer DoM AUROC sweep (which we can do with cached data) approximates the same layer-selection question.

- **Modification budget conservation rule** — Empirical observation that n_layers × α_opt ≈ constant across configurations with different layer counts. Used to predict optimal scaling when changing the number of intervention layers.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU — can test with cached NPZ by varying number of PCA components or feature subsets in the cov-spectrum probe.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could test whether a similar conservation holds for our probe features across different feature-count configurations.

- **Raw task vector injection with SAE-guided layer selection** — LoRA fine-tune → compute task vector → use SAE SP to select layers → inject unfiltered Δ_W only into selected layers.
  *Replication cost*: ~4 GPU-hours for LoRA fine-tuning + evaluation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — the method is about editing weights for capability enhancement, not about probing or correctness prediction.

## Approaches & framings

- **Stethoscope vs. scalpel distinction:** Interpretability tools (SAEs, probes) are diagnostic (identify *where* to intervene) but not interventional (should not filter *what* to inject). This reframes our DoM probe: it's a stethoscope identifying correctness-likely problems, and treating it as a scalpel for steering (H-1) may encounter the same geometric misalignment they document.

- **Activation-space / weight-space geometric incompatibility:** SAE decoder vectors span the activation manifold; task vectors live in weight space. Projecting between the two loses ~97% of energy. This geometric framing could apply to the cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0.046 orthogonality in F-3 — if prefill and final-token computations live in different "geometric objects" (analogous to activation vs. weight space), near-zero cosine similarity would be expected without implying the two are informationally unrelated.

- **Domain focus > data quantity for task vectors:** Their v2 (3,865 focused NT samples) drastically outperformed v3 (20,000 mixed-domain samples). This parallels our observation that the DoM direction is task-specific (F-5: breathing is content-dependent) — signal concentration matters more than volume.

## 2605.28649 — Interpretability-Guided Layer Selection over Subspace Projection: SAEs as Stethoscopes, Not Scalpels, for Raw Task Vector Model Editing (Lei, Mao, Ciobanu, Das, 2026)

**Relevance:** Demonstrates that SAE-derived features identify domain-specialized layers for math reasoning (on Gemma-3-4B-IT), with layer 19 ranking among the top-3 most specialized — independent corroboration that L19 carries math-relevant computation. Their negative result (SAE projection of weight-space task vectors retains ≤3.5% of energy) is a cautionary signal for activation-space steering approaches (H-1). The layer-level specificity diagnostic (SP score) offers a principled alternative to the manual layer selection used in ITI (2306.03341).

**Key claim we tested:** SAE-derived layer specificity identifies math-specialized layers; projecting weight modifications through activation-space SAE features loses ~97% of functional signal.

**Our result:** **CITED ONLY** — The paper works on Gemma-3-4B-IT (not Qwen), uses SAEs for model editing (not probing/prediction), and addresses weight-space task vectors (not activation-space correctness prediction). The core negative result (activation/weight-space geometric misalignment) is relevant as a conceptual warning for H-1 (DoM steering) but has not been directly tested in our pipeline. The positive result (layer-level diagnostic) independently corroborates L19's specialization, but on a different model.
