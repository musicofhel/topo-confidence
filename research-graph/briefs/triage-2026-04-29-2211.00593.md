# Triage brief — 2211.00593 — Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "single L19 prefill DoM direction" framing.** Wang et al. show that
   apparently-monolithic model behaviors decompose into 26 distinct attention heads in
   7 functional classes (Name Movers, Backup Name Movers, Negative Name Movers,
   S-Inhibition, Induction, Duplicate Token, Previous Token). If MATH-500 prefill
   correctness shares this structure, "L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731" is the *summed
   residual-stream projection of a distributed circuit*, not a fundamental direction.
   *Mechanism*: residual stream at L19 = sum over earlier OV-circuits; a single
   linear probe captures the sum but the underlying computation is not one-dimensional.
   *Falsification experiment*: run path patching from L19 prefill DoM back to all
   earlier (layer, head) pairs on MATH-500. If ≥3 heads contribute non-redundantly
   (their individual ablation drops AUROC by ≥0.02), F-2's "single direction" framing
   is inadequate. The direction is real as a probe, but the mechanism is a circuit.

2. **Refutes F-3's orthogonality interpretation (cos = 0.046).** The IOI paper's
   Backup Name Mover phenomenon (heads that take over when primary heads are ablated)
   shows that orthogonal residual-stream contributions can come from *the same set of
   heads* operating on different positional slots, not from independent circuits. If
   prefill-DoM and final-DoM share their attention-head support but differ only in
   *which token positions* they read from, F-3's implicit "two independent circuits"
   read of the orthogonality is wrong — they are one circuit applied at different
   positions. *Mechanism*: same QK/OV matrices, different attention destinations.
   *Falsification experiment*: path-patch from prefill-DoM and final-DoM separately
   to (layer, head) pairs; compute Jaccard overlap of the top-k contributing heads.
   If overlap > 0.6, F-3 is mechanistically misframed even if cos stays at 0.046.

3. **Refutes H-13's implicit assumption that prefill vs final-token circuits are
   distinct enough to be independently attributed.** H-13 proposes head-level
   attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits; the IOI paper's *minimality*
   criterion (that an explanation should be irreducible) means we should expect to
   find a small minimal head set for *each* probe. But if the minimal sets overlap
   heavily — or if removing the prefill circuit causes backup heads to recover the
   final-token signal — the dichotomy in H-13 collapses into a single circuit with
   two readouts. *Mechanism*: ablation-induced compensation (the IOI paper's
   "Backup Name Mover" effect at ~30% recovery). *Falsification experiment*: ablate
   the top-3 prefill-DoM heads, re-extract final-token L19 activations, refit final-
   token DoM probe. If AUROC stays within 0.02 of unablated baseline (0.7186),
   prefill and final circuits are not independent.

4. **Refutes F-1's "universal across architectures" reading by suggesting circuit
   specificity instead.** Wang et al.'s circuit is task-specific to IOI in GPT-2
   small; the same model has unrelated circuits for other tasks. F-1's universality
   claim (breathing across architectures and scales) might mask task-specific
   sub-structure: different problems might recruit different sub-circuits, and the
   apparent universality is a population average. *Mechanism*: per-problem circuit
   diversity averaged into a smooth breathing curve. *Falsification experiment*:
   cluster MATH-500 problems by per-token PR trajectory shape (k-means on the
   normalized curves). If ≥3 clusters with qualitatively different shapes
   (monotone, bimodal, late-collapse) and each cluster's per-problem trajectory is
   tighter than the global average, F-1's "single universal pattern" is the average
   of multiple circuit-driven patterns.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-13 (Head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — this paper
  is the canonical methodology template. Path patching is the exact technique H-13
  needs. Anchor: AUROC 0.7731 (prefill) vs 0.7186 (final), cos 0.046 (F-3). The
  IOI paper's faithfulness/completeness/minimality framework gives concrete success
  criteria for declaring a circuit "found".

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor)** — IOI methodology
  could decompose the 0.7731 AUROC into per-head contributions. Anchor: 0.7731 OOF
  5-fold AUROC.

- **F-3 (Prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos=0.046)** — IOI shows
  orthogonality at the residual-stream level can hide shared head support. Anchor:
  cos 0.046.

- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature) and H-21 (D-bucket narrower
  prefill attention)** — path patching from D-bucket-only prefill DoM should reveal
  whether D-bucket recruits different heads than A-bucket, providing a mechanistic
  test of the bucket distinction.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — path patching across
  layers tests whether the cross-layer information is genuinely redundant or
  whether L19 only catches the *summed output* of a multi-layer circuit. Earlier-
  layer (Q-circuit, S-inhibition analogue) signals could exist that L19 does not
  reflect.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Path patching** — replace activations along a specific (layer, head) → (later
  layer, head) edge with corrupted-prompt activations; measure logit-difference
  drop. Identifies *which paths* in the computational graph carry task-relevant
  information.
  *Replication cost*: 4–8h H100 per probe direction (need to instrument forward
  pass with activation cache + per-edge replay). Requires TransformerLens or
  equivalent on Qwen-2.5-1.5B (not bundled by default).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is the tool H-13 needs and the most direct
  refutation/confirmation route for F-2/F-3 mechanism.

- **Direct Logit Attribution (DLA)** — project (layer, head) output through the
  unembedding to attribute logit contribution to the final answer token.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached final-token activations (needs per-head
  output, not just residual stream — likely a re-extraction).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a per-head AUROC analogue without the
  full path-patching machinery.

- **Mean ablation / knockout** — replace a head's output with its mean over a
  reference distribution; measure the drop in task performance.
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 if doing full MATH-500 forward pass per head (×336
  heads in 1.5B = many forwards); 30min CPU if working in residual-stream-only
  approximation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap version (residual-stream removal of
  per-head contributions to L19 prefill) is FE-ready.

- **Faithfulness / Completeness / Minimality metrics** — quantitative criteria for
  circuit explanations: (a) faithfulness = circuit reproduces full-model behavior,
  (b) completeness = no necessary component is missing (test by ablating circuit
  complement), (c) minimality = no component is redundant (test by ablating each
  inside the circuit).
  *Replication cost*: derives from above; no extra cost beyond path patching.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives go/no-go criteria for "we found the
  circuit".

- **Causal scrubbing-style hypothesis encoding** — formalize "head H performs
  function F" as a causal graph; replace activations with samples from any
  distribution that should preserve F per the hypothesis.
  *Replication cost*: medium — requires writing a hypothesis-graph DSL or using
  redwood-research's `causal_scrubbing` library.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — overhead too high for a single-finding cross-
  check; defer until we have multiple hypotheses to formalize.

- **Attention pattern visualization** — inspect QK-attention for token-pair
  patterns matching hypothesized roles (Duplicate Token, Previous Token).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached prefill attention if extracted; need
  re-extraction otherwise.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — quick sanity step for any head we suspect of
  D-bucket-specific behavior (H-21).

## Approaches & framings

- **Distributed circuit framing** — task behavior decomposes into a small (~26)
  set of attention heads with semi-interpretable roles. Forces our F-2/F-3 to
  defend "single direction" against "summed circuit output" as alternatives.

- **Backup mechanisms** — when primary heads are ablated, secondary heads
  partially recover the behavior. This reframes F-3's orthogonality: a model with
  redundant circuits can have orthogonal residual-stream signals from the same
  heads operating on different positional slots, mimicking independent directions.

- **End-to-end reverse engineering as feasibility claim** — Wang et al. argue
  mechanistic understanding scales. If true for our setting, H-13 is not a side
  experiment but a core deliverable that would *replace* F-2/F-3's geometric
  framings with circuit framings.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **IOI dataset** — synthetic templates of the form "When Mary and John went to
  the store, John gave a drink to ___" → "Mary". ~14 templates × name/object
  fillers, on the order of 10³–10⁴ prompts. License: open (released with paper).
  Applicable? **no** for MATH-500 task transfer (wrong domain), **yes** as a
  *paired clean/corrupted prompt protocol* template — IOI uses a corrupted
  variant where names are swapped to define a "logit difference" target. We
  could construct an analogous paired prompt for MATH-500 (e.g., correct vs
  numerically-perturbed problem) to enable path patching.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **TransformerLens** is the de facto library for path patching / activation
  caching on small transformers. Native support for GPT-2; Qwen-2.5-1.5B
  requires a HookedTransformer config or equivalent custom hooking. Code:
  https://github.com/neelnanda-io/TransformerLens.
- **Logit difference metric** — use *answer logit* minus *foil logit* rather
  than absolute correctness as the patching target. For MATH-500 this would be
  log P(correct token) − log P(typical wrong token) at the critical answer
  position; choice of foil matters and should be matched.
- **Corrupted prompt construction** — IOI swaps the indirect-object name. For
  MATH-500 a parallel corruption (perturb a single number; swap "x" for "y" in
  algebra; swap problem topic) needs to be designed; the corruption must
  *change the answer* without changing the problem family.
- **Mean ablation reference distribution** — Wang et al. mean-ablate over the
  *training distribution*, not zeros. Zeros are off-manifold and bias the
  ablation toward overstating head importance. Use mean over MATH-500 problems
  of the same length band (CLAUDE.md emphasizes 1024-tok labels — match this).
- 26 / 144 ≈ 18% of GPT-2 small heads are in the IOI circuit. Qwen-2.5-1.5B has
  28 layers × 12 heads = 336 heads; budget ~50–60 candidate heads as a starting
  search before pruning by faithfulness.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Per-head L19 DoM AUROC sweep** — using already-cached prefill activations
  from `pathway11_h100/`, project each (earlier_layer, head) attention-output
  contribution into the L19 residual stream and refit a per-head DoM probe.
  This is a *cheap* approximation to path patching that does not require
  re-running forward passes; gives a per-head AUROC distribution and identifies
  candidate heads for full path patching. Cost: 2–4h CPU if per-head outputs
  were saved at extraction time. **Caveat**: if Stage 2 only saved residual
  streams, we need to re-extract — adds H100 cost. Check
  `DATA_MANIFEST.md` for whether `attn_head_out_*.npz` was cached.

- **Layer-stratified DoM sweep** — already partially done; fit DoM per layer L0
  through L27 and check whether the *peak* AUROC is sharply at L19 or
  smoothly distributed. A smooth distribution implies multi-layer circuit
  contribution; a sharp peak at L19 supports the single-direction reading. If
  this layer-sweep already exists in `pathway11_h100/` results JSONs, the
  cross-check is just reading it.

## 2211.00593 — Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small (Wang, Variengien, Conmy, Shlegeris, Steinhardt, 2022)

**Relevance:** The canonical mechanistic-interpretability methodology paper. Its
path-patching technique, faithfulness/completeness/minimality criteria, and head-
class taxonomy are the template for H-13 (Head-level attribution of prefill vs
final-token circuits). The IOI paper's central message — that apparently-monolithic

challenges F-2's framing of L19 prefill DoM as a single direction and F-3's
framing of prefill/final orthogonality as two independent circuits. The paper
does not study correctness signals or MATH-style reasoning, so its findings
cannot be directly replicated; what we replicate is the methodology applied to
our F-2/F-3 setting.

**Key claim we tested:** "Apparently-unitary model behaviors are mediated by
sparse, attention-head-level circuits identifiable via path patching, with
quantitative faithfulness/completeness/minimality guarantees."

prefill activations. P11-FE593 / FE594 / FE595 / FE596 (READY/BLOCKED) operationalize
