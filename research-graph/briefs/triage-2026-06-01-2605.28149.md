# Triage brief — 2605.28149 — Sign-Aware Gated Sparse Autoencoders: Modeling Anticorrelated Features with Bi-Jump-ReLU Activations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28149
Triaged: 2026-06-01

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 ("single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness")**: SA-GSAE's bipolar latents could decompose the DoM direction into a signed pair of anticorrelated features (correct-predictive vs incorrect-predictive) sharing one dictionary atom. If a half-width SA-GSAE at L19 achieves AUROC > 0.7731 with fewer active features than a standard SAE (i.e., the "single direction" is actually a superposition of a bipolar pair), then the F-2 narrative of "one direction" is a description-level artifact of unsigned decomposition, not a mechanistic claim.
   - *Mechanism*: Bi-Jump-ReLU allows one latent to fire positively for correct and negatively for incorrect (or vice versa). Standard PCA/DoM cannot distinguish this from a single linear direction.
   - *Falsifiable by*: Train SA-GSAE on cached L19 prefill activations; test whether the top bipolar latent's signed activation achieves AUROC matching DoM, and whether ablating just the negative pole degrades prediction.

2. **Refutes F-3 ("prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal — cos 0.046")**: If SA-GSAE trained at L19 reveals that the prefill DoM and final-token DoM decompose into partially overlapping bipolar latent populations, the near-zero cosine between the raw directions may be an artifact of sign cancellation between shared features. Two bipolar latents pointing in opposite directions at prefill vs. final would produce near-zero raw cosine while sharing mechanism.
   - *Mechanism*: Bipolar features that flip sign between prefill and generation would be invisible to unsigned cosine similarity but visible as shared latents in SA-GSAE.
   - *Falsifiable by*: Train SA-GSAE on both prefill and final-token L19 activations; measure overlap in active bipolar latent populations. If Jaccard > 0.3 for top-K bipolar latents, the orthogonality claim needs revision.

3. **Refutes F-10 ("PH on residual streams is not distinguishable from Gaussian null")**: SA-GSAE's dramatic dead-fraction reduction (100-500× on MLP outputs) demonstrates that residual streams have highly structured sparse representations that standard methods miss. If the correctly-structured sparse basis reveals non-Gaussian structure (e.g., PH computed on SA-GSAE latent activations rather than raw residual-stream coordinates shows non-null barcodes), then F-10's null is an artifact of applying PH in the wrong coordinate system, not evidence of true Gaussianity.
   - *Mechanism*: PH applied to 500×1536 raw residuals sees "Gaussian-like" because the signal lives in a sparse overcomplete basis. SA-GSAE coordinate transform could reveal structure PH would detect.
   - *Falsifiable by*: Compute SA-GSAE latent activations for all 500 MATH-500 problems; run Vietoris-Rips PH on the N×K_active sparse matrix; compare barcodes to Gaussian null in the same dimensionality.

4. **Challenges H-795 methodology assumption**: H-795 proposes standard SAE decomposition of L19 DoM. SA-GSAE predicts that unsigned SAEs will undercount correctness-relevant features by splitting bipolar pairs into separate dead latents. If SA-GSAE finds that the dominant correctness latent is bipolar (positive = correct, negative = incorrect), then unsigned SAEs will report ~2× more features at lower individual AUROC — mischaracterizing the mechanistic picture.
   - *Mechanism*: Standard Gated SAE forces separate latents for positive/negative activations along the correctness axis; SA-GSAE merges them.
   - *Falsifiable by*: Train both standard Gated SAE and SA-GSAE at matched width on L19 prefill; compare number of AUROC>0.65 latents and dead fraction.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731, single L19 prefill direction)**: SA-GSAE's bipolar latents could decompose this single direction into a signed feature pair sharing one dictionary atom. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 alignment suggests DoM might be one pole of a bipolar correctness feature rather than a fundamentally one-sided signal.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)**: The near-orthogonality could be sign-cancellation from bipolar latents that flip polarity between computation stages. SA-GSAE provides the tool to test this.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature)**: D-bucket (incorrect, confident) problems may activate the negative pole of bipolar correctness features more strongly than other error types, producing their distinctive geometric signature without requiring a separate geometric explanation.

- **H-391 (Top-K SAE features match DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: SA-GSAE predicts this at half the width due to bipolar efficiency. The half-width result from the paper (half-width SA-GSAE at H ≥ full-width Gated at 2H) directly implies SA-GSAE could match H-391's target at fewer parameters.

- **H-539 (TopK SAE on L19 recovers sparse correctness feature AUROC > 0.7731)**: SA-GSAE's dramatic dead-fraction reduction suggests it finds features that TopK SAEs miss (dead latents never fire). If correctness information lives in bipolar features, TopK SAEs that enforce non-negativity would need more latents to capture the same signal.

- **H-795 (SAE decomposition reveals multiple distinct correctness-predictive latents)**: SA-GSAE directly improves on this design — bipolar sharing means fewer latents needed, and the signed structure reveals whether "multiple latents" is genuinely multiple mechanisms vs. unsigned decomposition splitting a single bipolar axis.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Bi-Jump-ReLU activation** — Polarity-selective gated activation allowing one latent to encode positive and negative directions along a shared dictionary atom, with learnable thresholds δ⁺, δ⁻ for sign selection.
  *Replication cost*: 2-4h GPU (training SA-GSAE on cached L19 activations); 30min CPU (applying pre-trained SA-GSAE to cached NPZs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to H-391/H-539/H-795 at potentially half the latent budget.

- **Auxiliary frozen-decoder reconstruction loss** — Prevents gate collapse by training the gating network against a frozen copy of the decoder, avoiding mode collapse where gates learn trivial solutions.
  *Replication cost*: trivial implementation cost (few lines), same training budget.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — necessary component of SA-GSAE if we train one.

- **Dead-fraction as diagnostic** — Using the fraction of permanently-inactive latents as a primary evaluation metric for SAE quality, with resampling schedules.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU on any trained SAE.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap sanity check for any SAE experiment (H-391, H-539, H-795).

- **Half-width Pareto comparison** — Evaluating SAEs at half width against baselines at full width to measure parameter efficiency of architectural innovations.
  *Replication cost*: requires training both variants (~2× training budget).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural experimental design for comparing SA-GSAE vs Gated SAE on our data.

- **Calibration symmetry (γ± slopes)** — Measuring whether positive and negative activations of bipolar latents have matched reconstruction fidelity.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU post-training.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cheap diagnostic if we train SA-GSAE.

## Approaches & framings

- **Anticorrelated features as shared dictionary atoms**: Reframes the question "how many features does a layer have?" by noting that semantically opposite concepts (correct/incorrect, high/low) naturally pair. This directly intersects F-2's "single direction" — if correctness is a bipolar feature, the DoM direction is not "one feature" but the shared axis of two anticorrelated features.

- **Sparsity via signed magnitude rather than L1 on activations**: Avoids L1 shrinkage bias that could suppress weak but meaningful activations. Relevant to H-539's TopK approach — TopK also avoids L1 bias but through hard selection rather than signed-magnitude parameterization.

- **Parameter-efficiency threshold for bipolar sharing**: The paper derives that bipolar sharing wins when bipolar fraction p ≳ 8/(d_in + 4). For d_in=1536 (our L19 residuals), threshold is ~0.5%. If correctness features are bipolar at >0.5% frequency, SA-GSAE dominates.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Width 16,384 (half of 32,768) at L₀=64 is the sweet spot for mid-depth hookpoints on ~1-3B models
- Threshold warmup (40k–80k steps) is critical — too-early thresholds cause dead-latent collapse
- Dead-latent resampling every ~4k–8k steps (not continuous)
- Symmetric magnitude tying (r⁺ = r⁻) is a safe default — negligible performance loss, fewer parameters
- The paper finds reproducible "reconstruction collapse" at full-width on residual stream hookpoints of SmolLM3-3B; half-width avoids this — relevant cautionary note if we try full-width on Qwen-2.5-1.5B residuals
- Input dim d_in=2048 for both tested models; our L19 residuals are d_in=1536, well within tested range
