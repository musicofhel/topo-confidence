# Triage brief — 2505.23556 — Understanding Refusal in Language Models with Sparse Autoencoders

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23556
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Wei Jie Yeo, Nirmalendu Prakash, Clement Neo, Roy Ka-Wei Lee, Erik Cambria, Ranjan Satapathy (NTU / SUTD / IHPC A*STAR), May 2025.

Code: https://github.com/wj210/refusal_sae

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor) — single-direction DoM is provably suboptimal vs an SAE-feature subset.** Yeo et al. show that for refusal, a hybrid CosSim+AP procedure restricting to ~K\*=20 SAE features at a single layer matches the full activation-steering direction in jailbreak rate (Fig 2: CosSim+AP ≈ AS upper bound at 0.7–0.85 across Harmbench/Jailbreakbench/Advbench), while a dense projection onto the full V_R\* direction loses interpretability and (Table 4) generalises far worse OOD. If correctness is similarly mediated by a small SAE-feature subset, the L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731 is a coarse linear summary leaving signal on the table. **Mechanism**: a linear DoM averages over many feature directions; SAE features disentangle them, and only a sparse subset is causally tied to the prediction target. **Falsifiable test**: train (or load) an SAE on Qwen-2.5-1.5B post-layer-19 residuals; compute V_R\* = mean(correct) − mean(incorrect) at L19 prefill on MATH-500; restrict the feature pool to top-K₀=10 features per layer by cos(v_i, V_R\*); run AP on those features against an "I"/"Here" output proxy; compare AUROC of the resulting K\*=20 sparse-feature linear classifier against the raw L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 on the same 5-fold OOF split. Prediction (would refute the implicit claim that "DoM is the right summary"): sparse-feature AUROC ≥ DoM AUROC, with the lift growing on OOD problems.

2. **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% on MATH-500) — dense-probe selective prediction will collapse OOD; sparse-feature probe will not.** Table 4 of Yeo: on the WildJailbreak adversarial OOD test, the dense linear probe scores 0.68 average / 0.32 vanilla-vs-adversarial gap on Gemma and 0.51 / 0.93 gap on Llama, while a sparse-feature probe trained only on F_R activations scores 0.86 / 0.03 gap and 0.75 / 0.17 gap. Our F-8 result is a dense-probe number computed in-distribution on MATH-500. **Mechanism**: the dense probe overfits to high-frequency features that shift under distribution change; sparse-feature probes are constrained to the small causal subspace that transfers. **Falsifiable test**: take the cached prefill L19 activations on MATH-500; (a) re-derive the F-8 71.6%-at-coverage-0.5 number, (b) train a sparse-feature probe over top-K\* SAE features in the same activations, (c) evaluate both on an OOD math distribution we have not labelled (AIME-style problems, GSM-Hard, or held-out MATH-500 categories). Prediction: the dense probe degrades by ≥10 selective-prediction-accuracy points OOD; the sparse-feature probe degrades by ≤3.

3. **F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — orthogonality of *dominant linear directions* does not preclude a shared SAE-feature substrate.** Yeo's F_common vs F_specific decomposition (Sect 4.2) establishes that two seemingly distinct behaviours at the linear-direction level can share a causal feature set. The cos=0.046 number was computed between two single vectors; if SAE-decomposed prefill and final-token L19 activations have substantial top-K active-feature overlap, the orthogonality narrative is incomplete and the prefill→final temporal story (which currently treats them as two unrelated mechanisms) needs revising. **Mechanism**: a position-conditioned linear projection averages over a large feature basis; near-orthogonal averages are compatible with high feature-set Jaccard. **Falsifiable test**: SAE-decompose Qwen L19 activations at prefill and at the final answer token across MATH-500 problems; for each problem, compute top-K (e.g. K=20) active SAE-feature sets at each position; measure mean Jaccard overlap. Prediction that would refute F-3-as-disconnect: Jaccard ≥ 0.5 despite cos=0.046, identifying which features are common (the "refusal-axis"-like backbone) vs prefill-specific.

4. **F-4 (asymmetric collapse: correct trajectories collapse harder at final token) — could be partly an "uncertainty/refusal-flavoured" feature artifact rather than pure geometric collapse.** Table 2 of Yeo: F_R refusal-feature activations on Gemma final chat tokens are 0.73 on successful jailbreaks and 0.18 on failed ones — i.e. refusal/uncertainty-flavoured features at the final tokens are *strongly* sensitive to whether the model is "sure" of itself. If incorrect MATH-500 trajectories carry hedging or uncertainty-flavoured SAE features at the final token (analogue: model is internally "refusing to commit"), the participation-ratio asymmetry between correct and incorrect could be driven by activation in a small set of uncertainty SAE features rather than by a geometry-of-the-whole-cloud collapse. **Mechanism**: PR is a covariance-spectrum statistic; a few feature directions activated only on incorrect trajectories can shift PR without a true global geometric change. **Falsifiable test**: identify uncertainty/hedging SAE features in a pretrained Qwen SAE (label-free, e.g. via Neuronpedia auto-explanations); ablate those features from final-token L19 activations and recompute correct-vs-incorrect PR. Prediction that would refute pure-geometry F-4: ablation eliminates the asymmetric-collapse gap.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Yeo reduces a single-vector "refusal direction" (Arditi 2024 lineage) into a small SAE-feature subset that matches the linear direction's effect (Fig 2). Direct precedent for replacing/augmenting our DoM probe with an SAE-feature probe at the same layer.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Yeo's F_common/F_specific decomposition (Sect 4.2, Tab 1: Gemma F_H suppression rate 0.48 vs random 0.05; F_R suppression 0.71 vs random 0.06) is the methodology to test whether prefill and final-token streams share causal features beneath the orthogonal linear summaries.
- **F-8 (selective prediction 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** — Yeo Table 4 shows sparse-feature probes have radically smaller in-distribution-vs-OOD gap (0.03 Gemma, 0.17 Llama) than dense probes (0.32, 0.93). Direct upgrade path to making F-8 OOD-robust.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)** — same single-layer-is-enough conclusion as Yeo's V_R\* in Gemma layer 15 / Llama layer 11. Cross-paper convergence on "the work happens at one layer".
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — Yeo's K\*=20 feature clamping is a per-position SAE-feature steering protocol; offers a mechanistic alternative to raw-DoM steering for H-1.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes replace supervised DoM for refusal)** — Yeo's sparse-feature probe pipeline is, in spirit, a label-free-feature-discovery + small-supervised-readout protocol; should be added as a candidate label-free probe family alongside SEPs.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Yeo's Attribution Patching on SAE features is the natural feature-level analogue; H-13 should consider feature-level AP before head-level circuits.

## Methodologies extracted

- **CosSim feature filtering** — restrict initial feature pool to top-K₀ SAE features whose decoder rows have the highest cosine similarity to V_R\* (a difference-in-means direction); shrinks search space before AP. *Replication cost*: trivial once an SAE is loaded (minutes CPU). *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural prefilter for any SAE-feature search against our DoM directions.
- **Attribution Patching on SAE features (AP-on-SAE)** — linear approximation of the indirect effect of patching feature activations between clean (harmful) and corrupt (harmless) prompts; importance score per feature via two forward passes + one backward. *Replication cost*: ~hours on a single H100 once a pretrained Qwen SAE is loaded. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the current SOTA way to identify causal features in residual streams; directly relevant to F-2/F-3/H-13.
- **Hybrid CosSim+AP (their Sect 3.2 / Alg 1)** — cosine-similarity prefilter, then AP within the prefiltered pool, then top-K\* by mean IE across sequence positions. *Replication cost*: same H100 day as above. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is the headline contribution, drop-in replacement for V_R\* projection.
- **Activation Steering / projection-out (Arditi-style)** — V_R = mean(harmful) − mean(harmless) at each layer, project out from residual stream (Eq 4); upper bound on what feature-set ablation can achieve. *Replication cost*: already implemented in our pipeline (this is just F-2's DoM construction). *We'd plausibly run this*: trivially yes — we already have this.
- **Feature clamping at constant c** — set f_E(z) = c (e.g. c=−3 for suppression, c=+3 for forced refusal); reconstruct activation via decoder. *Replication cost*: minutes once features are identified. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is how H-1 per-position steering would be implemented in feature-space.
- **F_common vs F_specific decomposition** — F_common = ⋂ F\*_j across behaviour categories j; F_specific,j = F\*_j \ F_common. Tests whether shared features across categories carry the core mechanism. *Replication cost*: trivial post-AP. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural way to test F-3's prefill/final-token feature overlap.
- **Suppression rate metric** ΔA(R) = (A(F_R) − A(F_R | do(·))) / A(F_R) — quantifies indirect causal influence of upstream feature set on downstream feature set. *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful to formalise prefill→final-token causal claims.
- **HARMBENCH classifier as jailbreak metric** — fine-tuned classifier (Mazeika 2024) over LLAMA-GUARD or string-matching for jailbreak success. *Replication cost*: minutes (HF model). *We'd plausibly run this*: not directly relevant to MATH-500 (we have ground-truth correctness), but worth knowing about for any future safety-adjacent work.
- **Sparse-feature linear probe** — train a logistic probe directly on F_R feature activations (dimension = |F_R|) rather than dense d_model activations; reported to generalise OOD far better than dense probes. *Replication cost*: minutes once features extracted. *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the highest-value methodology import for F-8 OOD robustness.
- **Adversarial-prompt suppression analysis** — measure ΔA(F_R) per-token along an adversarial suffix to localise which suffix tokens drive jailbreak. *Replication cost*: trivial post-AP. *We'd plausibly run this*: not for MATH-500 directly, but conceptually applicable to decomposing which prefill tokens drive the F-2 signal.

## Approaches & framings

- **"LLMs encode harm and refusal as separate feature sets"** — F_H upstream (harmful concepts), F_R downstream (refusal/compliance), with a causal F_H → F_R relation (Tab 1 suppression rates). Reframes "single direction" results as a 2-stage feature pipeline. Directly intersects F-3 (prefill/final-token orthogonality could be a 2-stage *content* vs *commit* decomposition rather than two unrelated streams).
- **"Common vs specific features"** — common features across behaviour categories drive the core mechanism; specific features are upstream triggers. Maps onto our prefill/final-token architecture: prefill features may be specific (problem identity), final-token features common (commit/uncertainty), and the shared backbone is in F_common. Reframes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM) as "DoM captures F_common, CoE-60 captures F_common + a few F_specific".
- **"AS is the upper bound, AP-on-SAE-features approximates it with interpretable structure"** — establishes that interpretable-feature-set ablation matches dense direction ablation. Counter-frame to "DoM is special": DoM is just a coarse linear summary of a small feature set; the SAE features are the underlying mechanism.
- **"Adversarial jailbreaks suppress F_R rather than bypass F_H"** — shifts the adversarial-robustness framing from "input-level evasion" to "internal feature-suppression". Analogue for our work: incorrect trajectories may *suppress correct-commit features* rather than activate incorrect-content features.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Models: Gemma-2-2B (instruct), Llama-3.1-8B (instruct), both safety-aligned chat models.
- SAEs: GemmaScope (expansion factor 32, Lieberum et al. 2024, arxiv 2408.05147), LlamaScope (expansion factor 8, He et al. 2024, arxiv 2410.20526). Both trained on **base model** activations; transfer to chat models is acknowledged as an open question (Sect 4.4 limitations: "we believe this can be improved by training SAEs on chat-model activations").
- Optimal V_R\* layer: Gemma layer **15**, Llama layer **11** (out of 26 / 32) — both around the 50–60% mark, similar to our L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers).
- Hyperparameters: K₀ = 10 (CosSim prefilter per layer), K\* = 20 (final feature set), c = −3 (Gemma) / c = −1 (Llama) for jailbreak clamping.
- Generation length: 256 tokens, greedy decoding.
- BOS token excluded from feature analysis (anomalously large activations, found non-informative — same caveat in Kissane et al. 2024a).
- Jailbreak-success classifier: HARMBENCH classifier (preferred over LLAMA-GUARD which assigns low scores to incoherent outputs, and over string-matching which fails on the inverse direction).
- AP intervention pair: clean = harmful instruction with target output "I", corrupt = harmless instruction with target output "Here". Indirect-effect metric m = P(y_corrupt) − P(y_clean).
- Code: https://github.com/wj210/refusal_sae

## 2505.23556 — Understanding Refusal in Language Models with Sparse Autoencoders (Yeo et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** Methodologically directly applicable to F-2/F-3/F-8/F-9 and H-1/H-12/H-13. Yeo et al. show that the "single linear direction in residual stream" story for safety refusal (Arditi et al. 2024, arxiv 2406.11717) is a coarse summary of a small SAE-feature subset (K\*≈20) that, when found via Attribution Patching restricted by cosine similarity to the difference-in-means direction V_R\*, matches the full activation-steering upper bound. Crucially, sparse-feature linear probes generalise dramatically better OOD than dense probes from the same layer (their Tab 4: dense gap 0.32–0.93 vs sparse gap 0.03–0.17 on WildJailbreak vanilla vs adversarial). Same single-layer-is-enough conclusion as our F-9. The F_common vs F_specific decomposition is the natural way to test whether F-3's prefill/final orthogonality is a property of the dominant linear summaries only or of the underlying feature substrate.
