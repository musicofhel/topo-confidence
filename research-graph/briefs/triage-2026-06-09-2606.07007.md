## Refutations

**R1 — refutes F-2's parsimony interpretation (single direction *is* the signal).**
F-2 holds that one L19 prefill DoM direction predicts correctness at AUROC
0.7731, and that DoM ≈ PC1 (cos 0.9216) makes "a single direction" the natural
unit of the finding. This paper's **feature-splitting** result says a
semantically rich concept is frequently *not* representable by a single neuron/
direction but is split across several, each carrying partial signal. Mechanism:
if "correctness" is a split concept, the supervised DoM direction is a linear
*superposition* of multiple split SAE features, not an atomic axis — and the
already-observed lifts (2-feat (PC1,PC9)=0.7856; cov-spectrum=0.7928) are exactly
the multi-neuron *approximation* gain the theory predicts over single-neuron
*detection*. Falsifiable: train a Top-K SAE on the cached 500×1536 L19 prefill
activations; if `max single-feature AUROC ≪ 0.7731` while a sparse k-feature set
recovers ≥0.79, F-2's "single direction" framing is a superposition artifact, not
a primitive.

**R2 — refutes H-540 (single SAE feature separates D-bucket) and stresses F-7.**
F-7 claims the D-bucket has a distinctive *collective* geometric signature.
The paper's **feature-absorption** notion (concept C₁'s activation region is a
strict subset of a broader concept C₂'s region) predicts that a narrow concept
like "D-bucket / hard-problem" is absorbed into a more general difficulty or
topic feature, so no single SAE feature cleanly isolates it. Mechanism: absorption
makes the D-bucket extent a sub-region of a coarser feature's extent → a
single-feature probe inherits the coarse feature's false positives. Falsifiable:
train SAE on cached L19 activations, label D-bucket membership, test subset-
inclusion of the D-bucket-active set within each feature's active set; if every
D-bucket-selective feature is strictly contained in a broader feature, H-540's
"one feature suffices" prediction fails.

**R3 — refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).**
F-9 says cross-layer Chain-of-Embedding (CoE-60) trajectory features add nothing
over a single L19 DoM projection. The paper's **hierarchical-concept / concept-
lattice** machinery distinguishes *detection* (a coarse projection flags the
concept) from *approximation* (full reconstruction of the concept manifold). A
trajectory feature spanning layers can sit *higher* in the concept lattice (its
Intent strictly contains the single-layer Intent), meaning the single layer only
*detects* what CoE *approximates*. Mechanism: if correctness is a hierarchical
concept, AUROC parity at the detection level masks an approximation-level gap.
Falsifiable: build the formal-concept lattice over per-layer activation features;
if the CoE concept's Intent strictly contains the L19-DoM concept's Intent
(proper lattice ancestry), "redundant" is wrong — they are detection vs
approximation of the same hierarchical concept.

**R4 — challenges the metric basis of F-2 / F-8 (AUROC conflates detection and
approximation).** Both F-2 (0.7731) and F-8 (selective prediction, 71.6% answered
acc @ 0.5 coverage) report AUROC/coverage curves. The paper argues
*detection*, *separation*, and *approximation* are increasingly strong and
geometrically distinct — and AUROC is a detection/separation metric blind to
approximation error. Mechanism: the selective-prediction ceiling in F-8 may
reflect a detection-level information limit, not a true correctness-manifold
limit; a multi-neuron approximation could break the ceiling. Falsifiable:
decompose the DoM probe into the paper's three notions (AUROC = detection;
linear-margin = separation; reconstruction-MSE of a correctness target =
approximation) and test whether approximation-level features push answered-acc
above 71.6% at fixed 0.5 coverage.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

  multi-neuron capacity bound is a principled language for *why* PC1 alone gets
  0.7731 but (PC1,PC9)=0.7856 and cov-spectrum top-20=0.7928 do better — the

  single-feature claim.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM).** Concept-lattice hierarchy reframes
  "redundant" as "detection vs approximation of one hierarchical concept."
- **F-8 (71.6% answered acc @ 0.5 coverage).** Detection-vs-approximation
  distinction questions whether the ceiling is informational or metric-induced.
- **SAE-feature correctness-probe hypothesis cluster** (per admission note:
  H-217/H-290/H-391/H-539/H-632/H-795 SAE-feature correctness probes; H-540
  single SAE feature separates D-bucket). This paper supplies the geometric
  conditions under which those probes *can* exist (single-neuron representability)
  and the failure modes (splitting/absorption) when they can't.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Concept-as-set / set-alignment formulation** — a concept is the set of data
  points exhibiting it; "learning" = aligning human-defined and model-induced
  point sets.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (relabeling our 500 samples as concept-sets).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it reframes correct/incorrect and D-buckets as
  concept-sets directly usable against cached features.

- **Detection / separation / approximation hierarchy** — three increasingly
  strong notions of "a unit represents a concept," with geometric conditions and
  error bounds for each.
  *Replication cost*: 15–30min CPU to instantiate the three metrics on cached
  DoM/PCA features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly diagnoses whether DoM is
  detection-only (R4).

- **Single- vs multi-neuron representability + capacity constraints** — geometric/
  capacity bounds for when a concept is captured by one neuron vs a multi-neuron
  unit.
  *Replication cost*: analytic; pairs with an SAE train (~30–45min CPU).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — core of R1.

- **Top-K SAE (and ReLU SAE) training** — the paper's empirical instruments.
  *Replication cost*: small dict (e.g. 4–8× over 1536) Top-K SAE on 500×1536 is
  ~30–45min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — prerequisite for R1/R2.

- **Feature-splitting / feature-absorption / feature-family set-theoretic
  diagnostics** — subset-inclusion and union tests over feature active-sets.
  *Replication cost*: minutes once SAE features exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — R1/R2 mechanism checks.

- **Formal concept analysis → concept lattice** — build (samples × binarized
  activations) context, compute (Extent, Intent) formal concepts, order by Intent
  inclusion.
  *Replication cost*: a small FCA lib over 500 samples × ~k binarized features is
  seconds–minutes CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — R3 mechanism (lattice ancestry of CoE vs L19).

## Approaches & framings

- **Concepts as point-sets, learning as set-alignment.** Shifts our correct/
  incorrect question from "is there a direction?" to "is the correct-set a
  representable concept, and at what notion?" — sharpens F-2/F-8.
- **Detection ⊂ separation ⊂ approximation ladder.** A reusable lens: AUROC sits
  low on this ladder; our headline numbers may be measuring the weakest notion.
- **Many-to-many concept↔neuron structure organized by concept lattices.** Says
  neuron-interpretation and concept-learning *need not agree* — a caution for any
  future "this SAE feature = correctness" claim in our program.
- **Splitting / absorption as set relations.** Gives us concrete, falsifiable
  failure modes for single-feature correctness/D-bucket probes rather than vague
  "polysemanticity."

## 2606.07007 — A Geometric View for Understanding Concept Learning and Neuron Interpretation in Sparse Autoencoders (Zhang, Lin, Lee, 2026)

**Relevance:** A unified geometric theory of *when* a concept is representable by
a single neuron vs a multi-neuron unit in sparse autoencoders, formalizing
concepts as point-sets and learning as set-alignment, with three notions

multi-feature concept, and supplies the geometric conditions and failure modes

**Key claim we tested:** A semantically rich concept is often NOT representable
