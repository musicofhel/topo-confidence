# Triage brief — 2502.20914 — Everything, Everywhere, All at Once: Is Mechanistic Interpretability Identifiable?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20914
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (Single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — the "single direction" narrative may be non-identifiable.** The paper demonstrates that for a fixed behavior, multiple incompatible computational abstractions (directions/subspaces) can equally satisfy predictive criteria. Our logistic regression found *one* DoM direction, but there may exist other directions in the 1536-d space achieving comparable AUROC through different mechanisms. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 already shows our supervised direction is not unique — PC1 is a distinct direction achieving comparable performance (AUROC 0.7856 via 2-feat). If many near-optimal directions exist, the claim of a "single direction" overstates what the data licenses. **Falsifiable by:** enumerating all 1D projections achieving AUROC ≥ 0.75 on cached L19 activations; if the set is a narrow cone (angular diameter < 10°), F-2 is robust to identifiability concerns.

2. **F-3 (Prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — orthogonality may be an artifact of selecting one solution from two non-identified families.** If both the prefill DoM and the final-token DoM are each members of larger families of near-equivalent directions (per non-identifiability), then the measured orthogonality between two specific selections doesn't establish that the *families* are structurally orthogonal. The cos = 0.046 between our particular solutions could be consistent with other pairs from the same families having cos >> 0.046. **Falsifiable by:** bootstrapping or perturbing the logistic regression (different regularization, subsampled data, random seeds) to generate the family of near-optimal directions for both prefill and final-token, then measuring all pairwise cosines between families. If the inter-family distribution is tightly centered near 0, F-3 is robust.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) — the "distinctive" signature depends on which computational abstraction you project into.** The identifiability framework (Definition 2 + Theorem on non-uniqueness of mappings) implies that the geometric signature of a cluster may change depending on which subspace mapping is chosen. Our D-bucket signature is defined relative to the DoM direction and covariance structure, but an equally valid alternative mapping might dissolve the D-bucket's distinctiveness or reveal other buckets as equally distinctive. **Falsifiable by:** projecting D-bucket activations into the top-5 alternative near-optimal subspaces (directions achieving AUROC ≥ 0.73); if the D-bucket retains its distinctive cluster separation in all such subspaces, F-7 is identifiability-robust.

4. **H-5 (Prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — causal interpretation of what the direction "means" is exactly the non-identifiable part.** Even granting that the direction predicts correctness (predictive criterion), the paper shows that interpreting *what algorithm* a subspace implements (the "what" in what-then-where) is systematically non-unique. Familiarity vs decomposability are two candidate algorithms for the same behavior; the paper's framework suggests both could be causally aligned with the same subspace simultaneously, making the hypothesis under-constrained as stated. **Falsifiable by:** designing intervention experiments that differentially activate under familiarity vs decomposability interpretations, then measuring IIA for each.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The identifiability framework directly questions whether the DoM direction is the unique direction satisfying the "predicts correctness" criterion, or one of a family. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 already shows at least one near-duplicate exists. The full 1536-d ceiling at 0.7847 being *below* the 2-feat model (0.7856) is actually evidence *for* identifiability — it suggests the information is concentrated, not diffusely spread.

- **F-3 (cos = 0.046):** The paper's finding that "a single algorithm can be causally aligned with different subspaces" directly motivates testing whether the prefill/final orthogonality is robust to subspace choice or an artifact of one particular solution pair.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM):** This redundancy finding is actually *consistent* with non-identifiability — CoE-60 and DoM may be two valid computational abstractions for the same underlying behavior. Their equivalence supports rather than refutes the non-identifiability interpretation.

- **F-10 (PH null):** Not directly relevant. The topology null is about statistical distinguishability from Gaussian, which is orthogonal to the identifiability-of-explanations question.

- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves accuracy):** If steering is the pragmatic goal, identifiability matters less — the paper acknowledges "predictive and/or manipulability standards" may suffice without requiring uniqueness (Section 5.1). A steering experiment that works constitutes pragmatic validation regardless of whether the direction is uniquely identifiable.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Exhaustive circuit enumeration** — enumerate all subgraphs of a computational graph that achieve zero circuit error, counting the multiplicity of valid explanations.
  *Replication cost*: Not applicable to our setting (we use probes, not circuits in the MI sense). We don't have a computational graph to enumerate.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — our signals are linear probes on activations, not circuit-level explanations.

- **Intervention Interchange Accuracy (IIA)** — measure causal alignment between a candidate algorithm and neural activations by performing counterfactual interventions on the algorithm's variables and testing whether mapped neural activations respond identically.
  *Replication cost*: Needs fresh forward passes with interventions (H100 hours for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on 500 problems × multiple interventions).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for full causal framework; yes for a simplified version (see FE below) testing whether our DoM direction satisfies the consistency criterion.

- **Multi-task training as identifiability reducer** — training on multiple tasks simultaneously (n=1→6) significantly reduced the number of valid computational abstractions (p=0.05 up to 4 tasks).
  *Replication cost*: N/A (architectural finding about training dynamics, not a post-hoc analysis method).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — our model is already trained.

- **Subspace multiplicity counting** — for a given explanatory direction, systematically search for alternative directions/subspaces achieving equivalent predictive performance, counting the "cone" of near-optimal solutions.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached 500×1536 activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly applicable to testing whether DoM is identifiable.

- **Sparsity-parameterized circuit search** — enumerate circuits above a sparsity threshold (fraction of excluded components > 0.3) to get lower bounds on valid explanations.
  *Replication cost*: N/A for our probe-based approach.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

## Approaches & framings

- **Identifiability-of-explanations** — borrows from statistical identifiability (Rothenberg 1971) to ask whether MI criteria guarantee unique explanations. Intersects our work by reframing the question from "does direction X predict Y?" to "is direction X the *unique* direction predicting Y under our criteria?" This is precisely the question that cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 already partially answers — near-uniqueness (small cone) rather than perfect uniqueness.

- **Predictive vs manipulability vs understanding goals** — the paper argues explanations serving different epistemic goals (prediction, control, understanding) need different identifiability guarantees. Our F-2/F-8 findings primarily serve prediction and selective-prediction (pragmatic); H-1 steering serves manipulability. The paper validates that non-identifiable explanations can still be useful for prediction/control, which *supports* our applied stance.

- **Incompatible computational abstractions** — two explanations are "incompatible" if they share explanatory goal and simplification strategy but posit different algorithms or embeddings. The DoM vs PC1 directions (cos=0.9216) are technically "compatible" (nearly identical), but DoM vs a hypothetical orthogonal direction achieving AUROC 0.75 would be "incompatible" in this framework.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Test DoM direction identifiability on cached activations:** Run logistic regression with L2 regularization swept over [0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0] on cached L19 prefill activations (500 × 1536). For each regularization strength, extract the weight vector. Compute pairwise cosines between all resulting directions. If all cosines > 0.99, DoM is effectively identifiable; if some cosines < 0.9, there's genuine non-identifiability. Can use: `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` cached data + existing logistic regression pipeline.

- **Bootstrap stability of DoM:** Resample 500 problems with replacement (100 bootstrap iterations), refit logistic regression each time, measure angular spread of resulting directions. Uses same cached data. Script: ~20 lines on top of existing sklearn pipeline.

- **Alternative-direction AUROC curve:** For each of the top-20 PCA directions, compute single-direction AUROC. Plot the "identifiability curve" — how quickly AUROC drops as you move away from PC1/DoM. Data already exists in `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/results.json`.

**Relevance:** Directly challenges whether explanatory directions/subspaces found by MI methods (and by extension, supervised probing directions like our DoM) are unique. The cos(DoM, PC1)=0.9216 and the steep AUROC drop-off from PC1→PC2 provide preliminary evidence for near-identifiability in our setting, but this needs systematic verification.

**Key claim we tested:** "For a fixed behavior to explain, multiple incompatible directions/subspaces can satisfy predictive and causal-alignment criteria equally well" — applied to our correctness-prediction probing.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — The identifiability concern is legitimate and needs quantitative assessment via P11-FE130450 (direction cone diameter), P11-FE130451 (orthogonality bootstrap), and P11-FE130452 (AUROC identifiability curve). Preliminary evidence from cos(DoM, PC1)=0.9216 and the full-dimensional ceiling (0.7847 < 2-feat 0.7856) suggests our setting may be near-identifiable, but this is not yet systematic.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE130450, P11-FE130451, P11-FE130452, H-850, H-851
