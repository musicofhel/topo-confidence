# Triage brief — 2405.15302 — Understanding the Language Model to Solve the Symbolic Multi-Step Reasoning Problem from the Perspective of Buffer Mechanism

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15302
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill / final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — re-framing risk that *invalidates the current interpretation*.**
   The paper's central claim is that the residual stream stores multiple intermediate
   results **simultaneously** in mutually-orthogonal "buffers" defined by distinct
   `W_vo^(l)` projection matrices, with `(W_vo^(l−1),T) W_qk^(l) W_vo^(0),T ≈ I`
   ("same-token matching", §4). They explicitly show pairwise cosine between
   `W_vo^(0), W_vo^(1), W_vo^(2)` and `I` is near zero (Appendix D). Our F-3 currently
   reads cos = 0.046 between prefill-DoM and final-DoM as evidence that the two
   directions encode **uncorrelated** features (different problems are easy/hard for
   each). The buffer-mechanism framing predicts a strictly different reading: the two
   directions are orthogonal **because they are separate buffers carrying different
   reasoning intermediates of the same chain**. Mechanism: each is the read-out of a
   distinct `W_vo` slot. Falsifiable: compute the kernel score
   `KS(Ker^(l)) = Trace(σ(Ker^(l)))/d_m` at L19 of Qwen-1.5B using the trained head
   weights (paper's Appendix G recipe). If KS(L19) is high, the prefill and final-token
   readouts at that layer are reading *complementary buffers of the same active
   computation*, not independent uncorrelated probes. F-3's "orthogonal because
   independent" interpretation collapses; the directions become two mandatory legs
   of a single reasoning circuit.

2. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 — chosen layer is the right one) — layer
   selection challenge.**
   The paper predicts that the layer where multi-step reasoning actually happens is
   the layer where `Ker^(l) ≈ I` and `MS(h^(l))` peaks. Mechanism: only at the layer
   whose query/key/value/output matrices align as buffer→extractor pairs can the
   relevant intermediate be retrieved. Our F-2 fixed L19 by sweep-AUROC; the buffer
   mechanism makes a **structural** prediction independent of correctness labels.
   Falsifiable: compute MS and KS per layer for Qwen-1.5B on the MATH-500 prompt set
   (uses the cached Stage 2 NPZs and head weights; no new generation). If MS/KS
   peaks at a layer ≠ L19, two things follow: (a) F-2's chosen layer is suboptimal
   under the paper's mechanism, and (b) re-extracting DoM at the MS-peak layer should
   beat 0.7731. If MS/KS instead peaks at L19, F-2 gains a *theoretical* anchor it
   currently lacks — but the converse (peak elsewhere) refutes our layer choice.

3. **F-9 (CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) — paper predicts the
   opposite for K=1 / VTS regimes.**
   The paper distinguishes Vertical Thinking Strategy (VTS, no CoT, all reasoning
   inside one forward pass — *exactly* our K=1 greedy regime on MATH-500) from
   Horizontal Thinking Strategy (HTS, CoT, buffer reuse across emitted tokens). Under
   VTS the model **must allocate a fresh `W_vo^(l)` per intermediate result**; under
   HTS it can reuse buffers because a new token absorbs each intermediate. Mechanism:
   in VTS the per-layer features are **non-redundant by construction** (different
   layers occupy different buffer subspaces). F-9 says the opposite empirically:
   CoE-60 trajectory features collapse into the L19 DoM signal, i.e. are redundant.
   If the paper is right, F-9 should specifically *fail* on K=1 MATH-500 (pure VTS)
   and only *hold* on long-CoT generations where buffer reuse dominates. Falsifiable:
   re-run the CoE-60 vs L19-only AUROC comparison **separately** on (a) <50-token
   generations (≈VTS) and (b) >500-token generations (full HTS). The paper predicts
   F-9 only holds in regime (b); we currently aggregate both regimes.

4. (bonus) **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits) —
   paper predicts circuit *reuse*, not circuit *split*.**
   H-13 hypothesises that prefill and final-token correctness signals come from
   distinct attention heads. The paper's buffer mechanism instead predicts the
   *same heads* may be reused at different reasoning steps, with the distinction
   carried by which `W_vo` buffer they read/write into (the head's `W_vo` matrix
   itself is structural, but multi-step reuse happens via the extractor matrices
   `W_qk` aligning with previous-layer `W_vo`). Falsifiable: compute per-head
   `cos(W_vo^(l,h), W_vo^(l',h))` across pairs of layers for Qwen-1.5B. If
   intra-head cross-layer cosines are large, the same head is shared; if small, H-13's
   distinct-heads hypothesis is favored.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — the paper's buffer-orthogonality
  prediction (§4 + App D) provides a *mechanistic explanation* for why the two
  directions are near-orthogonal: they are separate W_vo buffers. Anchor: 0.046
  matches the "near-zero pairwise cosine between W_vo's" that the paper validates
  on its trained 3-layer model.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731, OOF 5-fold)** — the paper's matching-score
  / kernel-score diagnostic (§5) is a *layer-selection criterion* with no labels
  required. We fixed L19 by AUROC sweep; the paper offers a label-free alternative.
- **F-7 (D-bucket has distinctive collective geometric signature)** — the paper
  argues VTS *requires a fresh buffer per step*; D-bucket's pathological behaviour
  (K=1 right, K=8 wrong) could be re-framed as **buffer collision**: an intermediate
  step writes into a buffer already occupied by the answer, so K=1 happens to extract
  the right slot but K=8 sampling perturbs into adjacent slots. Anchor: D-bucket
  size = 67/88 incorrect-K=1 problems where K=8 also fails (RESEARCH_GRAPH.md).
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — paper's RMBA shows that *adding a small
  random matrix* to W_qk and W_vo — not a single fixed direction — improves
  multi-step reasoning. RMBA introduces only 132 trainable parameters globally
  but adds a different fixed random `Z^(l)` per layer/head. Anchor for H-1's
  steering-vector debate: a per-head/per-layer random projection may be a more
  robust steering surface than a single mass-mean direction.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution)** — paper's MS/KS per-head decomposition gives a
  concrete recipe for head-level analysis on real LLMs (App G).
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing)** — the paper's HTS-via-buffer-reuse
  argument explains *why CoT works*: instead of needing N distinct buffers, a CoT
  step emits a token that takes over carrier duty. C_exact verification adds one
  HTS step on top of a VTS answer — the buffer mechanism predicts the verification
  pass succeeds when the verifier's `W_qk` aligns with the answer's `W_vo`.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Matching Score (MS)** — `MS(h^(l)) = E_X[Trace(σ(h^(l)(X)))]/n`. Layer-level
  measure of "same-token matching" — how strongly each token's query attends to
  itself after multi-step buffer extraction. Computable from a single forward pass
  on cached prompts.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached Qwen-1.5B Stage 2 prompts.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — provides a label-free layer-selection
  criterion for F-2 / H-13.

- **Kernel Score (KS)** — `KS(Ker^(l)) = Trace(σ(Ker^(l)))/d_m` where
  `Ker^(l) = W_vo^(l−1) W_qk^(l) W_vo^(0),T`. Diagnoses layers structurally, from
  weights only — *no activations needed*.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU per layer per model (weights only).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — cheapest layer-selection diagnostic on offer.
  Could give F-2 a structural rationale for L19 (or refute it).

- **Multi-head MS / KS extension (App G)** — recipe for computing MS/KS on
  multi-head Transformers (GPT-2, Phi-3, Llama, Qwen). Includes per-head extraction
  and aggregation.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on Qwen-1.5B (28 layers × 12 heads).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct map onto our model.

- **Buffer cosine diagnostic** — pairwise `cos(W_vo^(l), W_vo^(l'))` and
  `cos(W_vo^(l), I)` to test buffer orthogonality.
  *Replication cost*: <10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — gives F-3 a mechanistic falsifier.

- **RMBA (Random Matrix-Based Algorithm)** — replace `W_qk^(l) → W_qk^(l) + α^(l) Z^(l−1)`
  and `W_vo^(l) → W_vo^(l) + β^(l) Z^(l)` with `Z^(l) ~ N(0, 1/d_m)` fixed and
  `{α^(l), β^(l)}` learnable. 132 total trainable parameters for 12-layer GPT-2.
  Improves multi-step reasoning data-efficiency by >65% on PrOntoQA, LogicAsker,
  PararulePlus and lifts Clutrr/RuleTaker/StepGame/LogicInference final accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 to RMBA-finetune Qwen-1.5B + re-evaluate MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — interesting as a parameter-efficient
  intervention to test against H-1 steering and H-19 verification, but not yet
  obviously a *correctness-prediction* method.

- **Same-token matching ablation** — set `Ker^(l) = I` artificially and check
  whether OOD generalisation is preserved (the paper's setting: tokenOOD ∉ training).
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (model surgery + re-evaluation).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — OOD test set construction would need a fresh
  symbolic dataset we don't have.

- **Causal intervention to identify reasoning circuits (App H)** — they cite Feng
  & Steinhardt (2310.17191), Meng et al. (ROME, 2022), Vig et al. (2020). Standard
  patching machinery applied to the symbolic dataset.
  *Replication cost*: covered by P11-FE3 (already in queue).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **already planned** — see P11-FE3.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Buffer" as the noun for residual-stream subspace.** Reframes the residual
  stream as a structured composition of orthogonal subspaces, each addressable by
  a different `W_vo` matrix. Intersects F-3 directly: prefill / final-token
  orthogonality stops being "two unrelated probes" and becomes "two complementary
  buffers in one reasoning circuit".
- **VTS vs HTS as a buffer-allocation distinction.** VTS = allocate fresh buffer
  per step (expensive in parameters); HTS (= CoT) = reuse buffers via emitted
  tokens. Cleanly explains why CoT helps reasoning more than depth does. Intersects
  H-19 (verification-pass = HTS step) and H-22 (Abstract-CoT compresses
  buffer-allocation cost).
- **Same-token matching as the core of multi-step transfer.** Argues that the
  *only* nontrivial requirement on weights is `Ker^(l) ≈ I`. This is a *structural*
  property of trained weights, not of activations — gives F-2 a label-free layer
  test.
- **Induction heads as "1-step reasoning"; multi-step ≠ stacking 1-step.** They
  show RMBA improves *multi-step*, but IMBA (the analogous identity-matrix trick
  from Boix-Adsera 2023) improves only single-step. Cautions us against
  generalising single-step probe results to multi-step regimes.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **3-layer single-head Transformer for the symbolic task** — `d=201`, `d_m=400`,
  `d_q=d_k=d_v=64`, batch 100, AdamW, lr 2e-5 → 1e-4 (warmup 400 epochs) → 1e-5
  (decay 3600 epochs). 200,000 sequences. AMD EPYC 7742 server, 256GB RAM.
- **GPT-2 12-layer for real datasets** — 5 epochs, lr 1e-5, RMBA hyperparameters
  initialised at 0.05.
- **RMBA per-head extension (§6)** — `W_qk^(l,h) → W_qk^(l,h) + α^(l,h) Z^(l−1,h)`,
  `W_vo^(l,h) → W_vo^(l,h) + β^(l,h) Z^(l,h)`. Each head gets its own Z fixed at
  init. Trainable parameter count per layer = 2 × n_heads (so 132 = 12 layers ×
  11 heads × something — paper's exact accounting is in App E; 132 is reported in
  the abstract).
- **Sign condition** — they observe `α^(l) · β^(l) ≥ 0` enhances reasoning;
  `< 0` does not. Useful constraint for reproductions.
- **Buffer cosine validation (App D)** — they explicitly compute pairwise cosines
  between `W_vo^(0), W_vo^(1), W_vo^(2)` and `I` for the 3-layer trained model.
  This is the cleanest empirical anchor for the buffer-orthogonality claim.
- Code: not visibly linked in the body; standard practice is supplementary GitHub
  but unverified here.

## 2405.15302 — Understanding the Language Model to Solve the Symbolic Multi-Step Reasoning Problem from the Perspective of Buffer Mechanism (Wang, Wang, Zhang, Zhou, Jin, Hu, Sun, Li, Zhang, Xu, 2024)

**Relevance:** Proposes a mechanistic *buffer mechanism* in transformers, where
each token's residual stream stores multiple intermediate results in
near-orthogonal subspaces defined by the value-output projection matrices `W_vo^(l)`,
and information is retrieved by `W_qk^(l)` ≈ `W_vo^(l-1)` ("same-token matching").
Distinguishes Vertical Thinking Strategy (VTS, single-pass multi-step) from
Horizontal Thinking Strategy (HTS = CoT, buffer reuse via emitted tokens). Two
probes (Matching Score, Kernel Score) are computable on real LLMs from weights
alone — directly applicable to Qwen-2.5-1.5B and our F-2 / F-3 / F-9 numerical
anchors. Paper provides a structural, label-free alternative to our AUROC sweep
for picking L19, and a mechanistic re-reading of F-3's cos = 0.046 prefill/final
orthogonality.

**Key claim we tested:** Multi-step reasoning in transformers proceeds by
storing distinct intermediate results in distinct W_vo "buffers" that are
addressable via Ker^(l) ≈ I; CoT works by reusing buffers across emitted tokens
rather than allocating new ones.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** The buffer mechanism makes specific structural
predictions about Qwen-2.5-1.5B head weights (KS peaks at the F-2 layer; W_vo's
are pairwise near-orthogonal; prefill_DoM and final_DoM align with different
W_vo subspaces) that we can falsify with cached weights and existing NPZs. If
confirmed, F-3's interpretation flips from "two independent probes" to "two
buffer reads of one circuit" and gives F-2 a label-free anchor. If refuted on
Qwen-1.5B, the buffer framing does not lift past 3-layer toy models.
