# Triage brief — 2604.28119 — Do Sparse Autoencoders Capture Concept Manifolds?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28119
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

**R1: F-2's single-direction DoM probe may be capturing a fragmented tile of a correctness manifold, not the manifold itself (refutes the sufficiency claim in F-2).**

F-2 establishes that a single L19 prefill direction (DoM ≈ PC1) predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731. Bhalla et al. demonstrate that continuous concepts in LLM representations are organized along low-dimensional *manifolds*, not single directions (Sec. 3), and that SAEs — and by extension linear probes — capture these manifolds only in a fragmented "dilution" regime where individual features act as localized detectors covering restricted regions of the geometry (Sec. 5, Fig. 5–6). If "correctness confidence" is a continuous concept with manifold structure (not a binary direction), then the DoM probe is a single tile of that manifold, and the AUROC 0.7731 ceiling may reflect the limited coverage of a localized detector rather than an intrinsic information limit. The falsifiable experiment: fit a group of directions (via their Ising-coupling pipeline or multi-direction probe) on L19 prefill activations for MATH-500 and check whether the group AUROC significantly exceeds 0.7731. If it does, F-2's "single direction" framing is incomplete.

**R2: F-10's PH-at-null result may be expected under manifold-tiling theory — PH on residual streams tests the wrong object (refutes the implication that topology is absent).**

F-10 concludes that PH summary statistics on residual streams are indistinguishable from a Gaussian null, interpreted as "topology adds no signal beyond covariance." Bhalla et al.'s framework (Sec. 4, Defn. 2) shows that under additive superposition of manifolds, the *joint* representation space is a Minkowski sum of embedded manifolds. Standard VR-PH applied to the joint cloud would see the superposed sum, not the individual manifolds — exactly the scenario where PH produces a Gaussian-looking null because the superposition washes out per-manifold topology (App. C, Lemma 1 shows this explicitly for point-based methods). The refutation mechanism: F-10's PH-null may be an artifact of applying PH to the superposed cloud rather than to factor manifolds recovered via Ising-based grouping. The test: apply the Ising unsupervised discovery pipeline to cached L19 activations, recover atom groups, project activations onto each group's subspace, then recompute PH on the projected sub-clouds. If any sub-cloud shows non-trivial topology (e.g., correct vs. incorrect form distinct clusters with different Betti numbers), F-10's null verdict was premature.

**R3: F-3's prefill/final-token orthogonality may reflect tiling of the same manifold from different receptive fields, not structurally different signals (weakens F-3).**

F-3 shows cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, interpreted as evidence that prefill and final-token encode structurally orthogonal information about correctness. Bhalla et al.'s tiling theory (Sec. 4, "From Capture to Tiling") shows that different features can tile *different regions* of the same manifold with nearly orthogonal decoder directions while collectively representing the same underlying geometry (Fig. 8 shows orthogonal receptive fields tiling the same "days" manifold). If correctness is a manifold tiled by different atoms at different sequence positions, the near-zero cosine could reflect positional receptive-field separation on the same manifold rather than genuinely different information streams. The test: check whether the joint {prefill_DoM, final_DoM} subspace spans a single manifold (via restricted R² on the 2D projection over the correctness label) vs. two genuinely independent manifolds (the Ising coupling between the two directions should be near-zero if independent, strongly negative if tiling the same manifold).

**R4: The cov-spectrum probe's lift to AUROC 0.7928 (F-2 extension) may be explained by the probe assembling a diluted manifold tile-by-tile, not by eigenvalue structure per se.**

The 20-eigval cov-spectrum probe (FE881) lifts AUROC from 0.7731 to 0.7928 by using log-eigenvalues of the residual covariance. Bhalla et al.'s dilution analysis (Sec. 5, Fig. 6) shows that manifold geometry is encoded across many partially redundant features — exactly the regime where a covariance-spectrum probe would succeed by implicitly reconstructing the manifold's ambient subspace from its spectral decomposition. The mechanism: the cov-spectrum probe may not be detecting a genuinely new spectral signal but rather reassembling the diluted manifold representation that a single DoM direction fragments. The test: compare cov-spectrum AUROC against a probe trained on Ising-grouped feature activations (which explicitly recover the manifold); if they converge, the spectral signal is manifold-geometric, not eigenvalue-specific. This would not invalidate the probe but would reinterpret F-2's factored decomposition (directional + spectral) as an artifact of diluted tiling.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's central finding — that SAEs in dilution regime fragment manifolds across many localized features — directly questions whether the single DoM direction at L19 is the optimal unit of interpretability. Their restricted-R² metric (Fig. 6) could be applied to our L19 activations to quantify how many directions are needed to "capture" the correctness manifold.

- **F-10 (PH-at-null):** The additive-mixture-of-manifolds formalism (Defn. 2) provides a theoretical framework for why PH on superposed residual streams finds nothing — the topology of individual factor manifolds is destroyed by Minkowski summation. This is the strongest theoretical explanation for F-10's null result we've encountered.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with L19 DoM):** CoE-60's trajectory features being redundant with single-layer DoM at L19 is consistent with Bhalla et al.'s finding that different SAE architectures recover the same manifold geometry despite learning different individual features (App. B.3, Fig. 13). The trajectory and single-layer approaches may be tiling the same manifold.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution):** The admission note flagged this connection. The paper argues that interpretability should target geometric groups of features rather than isolated directions — directly relevant to whether head-level circuit attribution (H-13) would find coherent manifold structure or fragmented tiles.

- **H-26 (non-linear probe crossing topological phase transition):** Bhalla et al.'s tiling/capture/dilution framework provides a concrete mechanism for why a non-linear probe might beat the linear DoM: a non-linear probe can aggregate across tiles of a curved manifold, while a linear probe is restricted to a single tangent direction.

- **H-35 (F-10's PH-null is summary-statistic-bound):** The paper provides strong theoretical support for H-35's premise: PH applied to the full superposed cloud is fundamentally the wrong object to test for manifold structure.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing):** Their manifold framework could explain breathing as movement along a correctness manifold — expansion/collapse corresponds to traversal along the manifold's intrinsic coordinates rather than a change in ambient dimensionality per se.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Ising-coupling community detection on binarized SAE/probe codes** — Fit pairwise Ising model (Eq. 4/16) on binarized feature activations to discover groups of features that jointly tile a manifold. Uses pseudo-likelihood maximization with EBIC regularization, then Louvain community detection on |J|.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (SAE codes needed; could adapt to probe residuals on cached NPZs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could apply to cached L19 activations to test whether correctness-related structure forms a coherent Ising community.

- **Restricted-R² subspace capture metric** — Greedily select decoder directions that maximize variance explained of a target manifold; measure how many directions are needed for near-full reconstruction.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly measures how many L19 directions are needed to capture the correctness signal, testing F-2's single-direction sufficiency.

- **Manifold tiling receptive-field analysis** — For each feature, compute the mean pairwise distance among manifold points where it activates, normalized by the manifold's overall pairwise distance. Distinguishes capture (broad) from shattering (narrow) from dilution (mixed).
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applicable to DoM direction's activation pattern over MATH-500 samples.

- **Additive Mixture of Manifolds (AMM) generative model** — Formalization (Defn. 2) where representations decompose as x = Σ fᵢ(mᵢ), each mᵢ on a low-d manifold. Extends LRH to curved geometries.
  *Replication cost*: theoretical framework, no compute needed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could test whether L19 activations are better modeled as AMM than as linear superposition.

- **Steering along manifold centroids** — Activation patching along PCA-space interpolation between concept centroids at L19, measuring smooth changes in next-token logits for diagnostic tokens (Sec. 3, App. B.1).
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 (requires forward passes).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — superseded by our P10 steering program and the rotation-refutation result.

## Approaches & framings

- **Concepts as geometric objects, not directions:** The paper reframes the fundamental unit of interpretability from isolated linear directions to low-dimensional manifolds. This directly challenges our framing in F-2 where DoM is treated as a direction — in their framework it would be a single tile of a broader correctness manifold.

- **Capture vs. tiling vs. dilution taxonomy:** A three-regime classification of how SAEs/probes represent manifolds. This provides vocabulary for describing our observation that cov-spectrum beats single-direction (dilution regime → the correctness manifold is spread across many spectral components).

- **Population coding / tuning curves from neuroscience:** Each SAE feature acts like a neuron with a receptive field, selective for a restricted region of the manifold. If DoM is a single "neuron" in this analogy, the AUROC ceiling may reflect the limited receptive field of one neuron out of a population code.

- **Ising model for conditional independence structure:** Using statistical physics (pairwise Ising) to infer functional groupings of features. This could be applied to probe coefficients or PCA loadings to discover whether our L19 features form coherent communities.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- All manifold analysis uses Llama-3.1-8B layer 19 residual stream (d=4096) — same layer we use for Qwen-2.5-1.5B (d=1536). This convergence on L19 across models is noteworthy.
- SAE training: Adam lr=1e-4, batch 16384, 500M tokens from The Pile. Expansion factors 8× and 16×. TopK sparsities: k ∈ {64, 128, 256}.
- Ising model fitting: pseudo-likelihood maximization with L-BFGS, EBIC regularization (γ=0.5), Louvain community detection on |J|.
- Code available at https://github.com/goodfire-ai/sae-manifold.
- Their restricted-R² protocol (greedy atom selection by residual variance explained) is directly portable to our PCA-based analysis.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Restricted-R² on cached L19 PCA components:** Take our 500×1536 L19 prefill activation matrix, compute PCA, then measure restricted-R² (Eq. 14) asking how many PCs are needed to reconstruct the correct/incorrect class centroids. If 1 PC suffices (R²≈1), the correctness signal is genuinely 1D (supporting F-2); if many PCs needed, it's manifold-like. Script: `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/` PCA code + cached NPZs.
- **Ising coupling on binarized DoM projections:** Binarize L19 activations by whether each PC's projection exceeds its median. Fit pairwise Ising model. Check whether PCs form communities aligned with correctness. Uses: cached activations from `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/`.
- **Tuning-curve analysis of DoM:** Plot DoM projection value as a function of problem difficulty (MATH level) or correctness label to check for smooth "tuning curve" structure vs. sharp binary separation. Uses: cached DoM projections from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`.
