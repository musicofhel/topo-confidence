# Triage brief — 2301.00234 — A Survey on In-context Learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00234
Triaged: 2026-04-29

This is the Dong et al. ICL survey (Peking U + collaborators, v1 Dec
2022, latest revision Oct 2024). It is a *survey*, not a primary
empirical paper, so refutations come from the theories and findings the
survey curates and from the survey's own demonstration-selection
experiment (Table 2). The survey's value to this project is as a *map of
neighboring claims* whose mechanisms we have not yet tested against our
F-N findings.

## Refutations

1. **F-3 (prefill / final-token DoM orthogonality, cos = 0.046) is
   refuted if the "label words as anchors" hypothesis (Wang et al.
   2023b, summarized §5.2.1) is the right mechanism for the prefill→final
   gap.** The survey reports that demonstration label-word tokens
   "aggregate and distribute key information for the final prediction."
   If correct, prefill DoM and final-token DoM are not two independent
   directions but the *same* induction-head circuit's output measured
   pre-anchor and post-anchor — orthogonality is then an artifact of the
   readout layer norm and aggregation gate, not evidence that two
   different computational quantities are at play. **Falsifiable test**:
   ablate (zero-out) the residual-stream contribution of label-anchor
   tokens at L19 during forward pass on MATH-500 prefill activations; if
   F-3 is real, prefill and final-token DoM remain orthogonal post-
   ablation; if Wang et al. is right, both DoM directions collapse onto
   the same low-rank subspace and cos(prefill,final) jumps from 0.046
   into the 0.5+ range. We have the cached prefill+final activations to
   run this on cached NPZs.

2. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731 as a "correctness" predictor)
   is refuted if Pan et al. 2023b's task-recognition / task-learning
   decomposition (survey §5.2.2 "Other Views") is the operative
   decomposition.** Their decoupling says ICL ability splits into
   *recognizing* the task type vs *executing* the task; recognition is
   what is decidable before reasoning starts, execution is not.  The
   prefill activations strictly precede CoT, so prefill DoM can only
   carry recognition signal — meaning AUROC 0.7731 is the AUROC of
   "model recognizes this as a problem-type it tends to get right," not
   the AUROC of "this specific problem will be solved correctly."
   **Falsifiable test**: take MATH-500 problems with the *same* gold
   accuracy rate but different surface formats (e.g., word-problem vs
   bare-equation rephrasings), extract prefill L19 DoM, and check
   whether AUROC drops on the novel-format set despite matched
   difficulty. If AUROC drops, F-2 is best reframed as a
   task-recognition probe (sharpening H-5) and the F-8 selective-
   prediction story shifts: it works because the model knows whether
   *this kind of problem* tends to be solvable, not whether *this*
   problem will be solved.

3. **F-10 (persistent homology on residual streams matches the Gaussian
   null) is refuted if the Bayesian / kernel-regression view of ICL
   (survey §5.2.2) is correct.** Xie et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2023e,
   and Han et al. 2023a frame ICL as implicit Bayesian inference over a
   shared latent concept θ, with Han showing the limit converges to
   kernel regression. Under this view, the residual stream of correct
   trajectories should organize on a low-dimensional concept manifold
   (linear/planar, not topologically interesting). PH-at-null does *not*
   rule out a planar/linear concept structure — it only rules out
   higher-genus structures (loops, voids). F-10's framing as "no
   structure" therefore over-claims; the right negative result is "no
   *higher-order* topological structure," and the latent-concept axis
   would be invisible to PH but visible to a 2-PC projection of the
   class-conditional means. **Falsifiable test**: project the cached
   1024-tok L19 activations onto the top-2 PCs of the
   correct-vs-incorrect class-conditional difference; if a clean
   1-D concept axis appears with separation comparable to DoM AUROC,
   F-10's "Gaussian null" framing is misleading and should be tightened.

4. **F-1 (universal dimensional breathing) is refuted if Chan et al.
   2022's burstiness condition (survey §5.1.1) is what makes ICL
   emerge.** Chan reports that ICL emerges only when training data has
   *bursty* distributional properties — clustered rather than uniform.
   If breathing is downstream of ICL, then breathing should *not* be
   universal: it should appear in models trained on bursty corpora and
   be absent (or qualitatively different) in models trained on uniform-
   distribution data, contradicting F-1's "universal across
   architectures and scales" claim. **Falsifiable test**: extract per-
   token L19 activations on MATH-500 from a non-instruction-tuned
   model trained on a deliberately non-bursty corpus (e.g., a Pythia
   shuffled-context variant if available) and check whether the
   characteristic PR inflation curve shows up. If breathing disappears,
   F-1's universality claim should be qualified to "transformers
   trained on natural-language burstiness."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731)** ↔ §5.2.1 "Functional
  Modules" + §5.2.2 "ability decoupling." The survey's framing of
  prefill-time computation as task-recognition cleanly aligns with the
  prefill side of F-2 and motivates a sharper TR-vs-TL split for the
  AUROC.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** ↔ Wang et al. 2023b
  "label words as anchors" (cited §5.2.1). The orthogonality might be
  fully explained by the anchor-aggregation mechanism; a per-head
  attribution would test this.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** ↔ §5.1.2 demonstration-query
  similarity (Liu et al. 2022 KATE). D-bucket fragility (K=1 right /
  K=8 wrong, the 36-problem subset) might be a *demonstration-similarity
  failure mode*: the model "recognizes" the problem but the in-context
  retrieval circuit lands on the wrong demonstration cluster.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 ↔ single-layer L19 DoM redundancy)** ↔ Olsson et al.
  2022 induction heads (§5.2.1). If induction-head circuitry is
  layer-localized in 1.5B Qwen, CoE-60's per-layer trajectory features
  collapse to L19 because L19 is the induction-head layer.

- **H-5 (prefill encodes familiarity vs decomposability)** ↔ Pan et al.
  2023b TR/TL split. The survey provides the named decomposition that
  H-5 is groping toward; H-5 should cite it directly and adopt the
  vocabulary.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)**
  ↔ §5.2.1 functional modules + Wang 2023b anchor analysis. The survey
  catalogs the exact tooling H-13 needs.

- **H-20 (cross-model kernel alignment)** ↔ §5.2.2 Han et al. 2023a
  kernel-regression limit. Survey provides the theoretical handle for
  the "kernel" half of H-20.

- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT breathing)** ↔ §3.1 meta-distillation pretraining
  (Li et al. 2024c distill demonstrations into vectors) + §4.1.2 ICV
  (Liu et al. 2024a). Compressed-demonstration regimes are a
  natural sister-experiment to compressed-CoT.

## Methodologies extracted

- **KATE (kNN selection on sentence-embedding similarity)** — pick
  in-context examples by cosine to query embedding.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — selection is for ICL prompt
  construction, but we could borrow the *probe* (kNN over prefill L19
  to a labeled correctness pool) as an alternative to logistic-DoM for
  F-2. Worth a footnote.

- **EPR / UDR (supervised retrieval for demonstrations)** — train a
  dense retriever over (input, demonstration) pairs supervised by
  downstream LM scoring.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day to train; not relevant to MATH-500
  zero-shot regime.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

- **In-Context Vectors / ICV (Liu et al. 2024a, arxiv 2311.06668)** —
  derive a *single vector* from the latent embeddings of demonstration
  examples and use it to adjust LLM latent states at inference.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly comparable to our
  prefill-DoM intervention surface. ICV is essentially a positive-
  control DoM-style direction derived from demonstrations rather than
  correctness labels. Stitching ICV with prefill DoM tests whether the
  two directions are aligned (would be evidence for label-anchor
  mechanism in F-3).

- **Structured Prompting (Hao et al. 2022b)** — encode demonstrations
  with special positional embeddings and rescaled attention.
  *Replication cost*: 2 days code change + 1 day run.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — outside our 1024-tok zero-shot
  regime.

- **Symbol tuning (Wei et al. 2023a)** — replace natural-language
  labels with arbitrary symbols during fine-tuning to force input-
  label-mapping learning.
  *Replication cost*: GPU-week.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

- **Channel scoring (Min et al. 2022a)** — score by
  *P(input | label)* rather than *P(label | input)*. Survey Table 3.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached logits.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for the 7B model — channel scoring as
  an alternative correctness signal for selective prediction at 50%
  coverage (compare against the 71.6% F-8 number). Cheap.

- **Self-consistency / global-local entropy ordering (GlobalE/LocalE,
  Lu et al. 2022)** — ordering demonstrations by entropy of model
  output distribution.
  *Replication cost*: 1 hour CPU on cached logits.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we operate zero-shot; no
  demonstration to order.

- **MDL-based selection (Wu et al. 2023b)** — select demonstration
  subset minimizing code length to compress label.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

- **APE / Auto-Prompt-Engineer + bootstrapped instruction generation
  (Zhou et al. 2023c, Wang et al. 2023f)** — use LLM to generate and
  select instructions.
  *Replication cost*: GPU day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no.

## Approaches & framings

- **Task recognition vs task learning decomposition (Pan et al. 2023b,
  §5.2.2 "Other Views").** Cleanly separates *recognizing the task* from
  *executing the task*. Maps onto our prefill / final-token split: the
  prefill direction sees the question only and so can only carry
  recognition signal; the final-token direction sees the executed CoT.
  This is the right vocabulary for F-2 / F-3 / H-5. We should cite it.

- **Bayesian / latent-concept view of ICL (Xie et al. 2022, Wang et al.
  2023e, Han et al. 2023a, §5.2.2).** Frames ICL as inference over a
  shared latent concept θ, with kernel-regression behavior in the
  many-shot limit. Sharper formal frame for what F-1 / F-7 / F-10 are
  measuring at the residual-stream level — and predicts a low-d
  concept manifold that PH would miss.

- **Implicit gradient descent / dual-form view (Dai et al. 2023a "GPT
  Learns ICL by Gradient Descent," von Oswald et al. 2023, Ahn et al.
  2023, Mahankali et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023c).** Frames Transformer
  attention on demonstrations as implicit fine-tuning. Crosses our
  H-1 (per-position DoM steering as a kind of inference-time
  fine-tune) and H-17 (DoM rotation as RoPE-mechanical vs computed).

- **Algorithmic-learning / algorithm-selection view (Akyürek et al.
  2023, Bai et al. 2023b "Transformers as Statisticians," §5.2.2).**
  Frames the model as dynamically choosing an algorithm (gradient
  descent vs ridge regression vs kernel) per ICL instance. Suggests
  a per-problem family classification — could explain F-7's
  D-bucket as "model picked the wrong algorithm at prefill."

- **Burstiness / data-distributional view (Chan et al. 2022, §5.1.1).**
  ICL emerges only when training data has bursty distribution. Direct
  challenge to F-1's universality claim.

- **Induction-head view (Olsson et al. 2022, §5.2.1).** Specific
  attention heads replicate previous patterns; ICL ability progressively
  develops from these. Maps onto H-13 head-level attribution and the
  L19 specificity in F-2 / F-9.

- **Demonstration-as-anchor view (Wang et al. 2023b, §5.2.1).** Label
  words aggregate and distribute information for final prediction.
  Direct challenge to F-3 orthogonality framing.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Survey Table 2 reports a *fair-comparison* selection-method bake-off
  across GPT-2, GPT-J, Qwen2, Llama3 on SST-2/SST-5/CQA/SNLI/News with
  topk, votek, mdl. Punchline: best method is *model-dependent* (mdl
  best on GPT-2, topk best on Qwen2/Llama3). Implication for our
  pipeline: any DoM-style probe trained on Qwen-1.5B prefill should
  not be expected to transfer cleanly to Llama / GPT-style models.
  Reinforces the F-1 "universal *shape* but maybe not universal
  *content*" qualifier we already flag in H-20.

  distills demonstrations into vectors. Conceptual neighbor of our
  prefill-DoM-as-inference-time-vector framing.

## Proposed HYPOTHESES.md additions

### H-70: Prefill L19 DoM is a task-recognition probe, not a per-problem correctness probe
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2301.00234 + Pan et al. 2023b (cited in survey §5.2.2) + F-2
**Test:** Construct two MATH-500 subsets matched on gold K=1 accuracy: (a) canonical format, (b) rephrased (e.g., equation-only or unusual word-problem framings). Extract prefill L19 activations on both. Compare DoM AUROC. ~1 day H100 to generate variants and re-extract.
**Requires:** H100 (1 day for re-extraction), Qwen-2.5-1.5B, MATH-500 with rephrased variants (new data)
**Would change:** On confirm (AUROC drops sharply on rephrased set despite matched accuracy), F-2's narrative shifts from "prefill knows correctness" to "prefill recognizes task family." F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage) keeps its number but the *mechanism* becomes "abstain on novel-format problems," which has a sharper actionable handle. On reject (AUROC holds), prefill carries genuine per-problem information and TR-only reading is wrong.
**Blocks:** Sharper version of H-5; prerequisite for any cross-task transfer claim about prefill DoM.

### H-71: F-3 prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality is an anchor-aggregation artifact, not two independent circuits
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2301.00234 + Wang et al. 2023b (cited in survey §5.2.1) + F-3
**Test:** On a re-extracted 4-shot MATH-500 prefill set, identify label-anchor token positions; zero-ablate their residual contribution at L19 and recompute prefill DoM, final-token DoM, and cos(prefill, final). ~1 day H100 for the 4-shot extraction, then 2h CPU for ablation + probe.
**Requires:** H100 day (re-extraction), Qwen-2.5-1.5B, 4-shot MATH-500 prompts (new data)
**Would change:** On confirm (cos jumps from 0.046 to >0.5 under anchor ablation), F-3's "two orthogonal circuits" framing collapses to "one induction-head circuit at two timesteps gated by anchor aggregation"; H-13 (head-level attribution) becomes the natural follow-up. On reject (cos stays low under ablation), prefill and final-token DoM survive as genuinely independent computations and F-3 is confirmed.
**Blocks:** H-13 head-attribution interpretation depends on which way this resolves.

### H-72: Residual-stream class-conditional difference at L19 has a clean 1-2 PC concept axis hidden under the F-10 PH null
**Priority:** MEDIUM
**Motivated by:** 2301.00234 + Han et al. 2023a kernel-regression limit (cited survey §5.2.2) + F-10
**Test:** Project cached 1024-tok L19 activations onto top-2 PCs of the correct-vs-incorrect class-conditional mean difference; measure linear / planar separability; compare against prefill DoM AUROC. ~20 min CPU on existing NPZs.
**Requires:** CPU only, cached L19 1024-tok NPZs (already on disk)

**Blocks:** Affects how F-10 is described in any writeup.
