# Triage brief — 2605.19943 — Probabilistic Tiny Recursive Model

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19943
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

**R1. Gradient-guided latent steering adds nothing beyond noise → weakens rationale for DoM steering (H-1).**
The paper explicitly tests Q-guided Langevin sampling (Appendix C), where latent-space gradients of a correctness head steer trajectories toward "good basins." The gradient component contributes zero measurable gain over equivalent noise injection. This directly threatens H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy): if a trained correctness head's gradient is uninformative for steering in a recursive latent space, the same may hold for our DoM direction as a steering vector in the residual stream. The analogy is not exact (TRM's latent space ≠ transformer residual stream; Q head ≠ DoM), but the negative result is structurally similar: a correctness-predictive direction may classify well without being causally useful for intervention.
*Falsifiable test:* Compare DoM-direction steering on Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 against isotropic Gaussian perturbation of matched magnitude at L19. If noise-only matches or beats DoM steering, H-1 is refuted in the same way the paper refutes Langevin guidance.

**R2. Width >> depth for correctness recovery challenges trajectory-feature approaches (F-9, H-6).**
The paper shows that scaling width (K parallel stochastic rollouts) yields +13pp accuracy, while depth scaling (more recursion steps) yields only +3.1pp. This maps onto a challenge to F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM) and H-6 (CoE-60 beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok): if independent stochastic samples dominate sequential deepening, then trajectory-aggregated features (CoE-60 = chain of 60 layers) may be inherently weaker than width-diverse sampling of a single-layer snapshot. The implication is that our trajectory features may be capturing depth-redundant information, and a "best-of-K samples at L19" strategy could outperform CoE-60.
*Falsifiable test:* Take K=10 independent forward passes through Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500 with temperature>0, extract L19 DoM for each, and select the pass with highest DoM confidence. Compare selective accuracy against CoE-60 trajectory features and single-pass L19 DoM.

**R3. Q head (trained correctness classifier) near-perfectly separates correct/incorrect trajectories → our AUROC 0.7731 ceiling may reflect probe weakness, not signal absence (F-2).**
The paper's Q head achieves sigmoid ≈ 1 for correct and ≈ 0 for incorrect trajectories at convergence, with best-Q@K within 1pp of oracle pass@K on PPBench and Sudoku-Extreme. This is far above our DoM AUROC of 0.7731 (F-2) and even the cov-spectrum ceiling of 0.7928. While the architectures differ radically (7M-param TRM with trained Q head vs. linear probe on frozen Qwen-2.5-1.5B residuals), the gap raises the question: does our correctness signal ceiling reflect a genuine information limit in L19 prefill activations, or is a richer (nonlinear, jointly-trained) probe the missing piece? The paper's Q head is jointly trained with the model, so it has access to information our post-hoc linear probe cannot extract.
*Falsifiable test:* Train a small MLP (2-layer, 256-hidden) on L19 prefill activations for correctness prediction. If AUROC jumps significantly above 0.7928, the ceiling is probe-bound, not information-bound, weakening the narrative that L19 contains limited correctness signal.

**R4. "Bad basin" escape via noise suggests D-bucket problems are not permanently stuck — reframes F-7.**
F-7 identifies a distinctive collective geometric signature for D-bucket problems (problems the model gets wrong). The paper's central finding is that many "failure" trajectories are in bad basins that noise can dislodge — 8% of rollouts escape with K=100. If D-bucket membership reflects basin structure rather than an intrinsic geometric property, then F-7's "distinctive signature" may be an artifact of deterministic convergence rather than a fundamental property of problem geometry. Noise perturbation of cached activations could test whether D-bucket's geometric distinctiveness survives.
*Falsifiable test:* Add Gaussian noise (σ sweep) to cached L19 prefill activations for D-bucket problems, re-run the DoM classifier, and check whether D-bucket's geometric separability (F-7) degrades. If it does, F-7's "distinctive signature" is basin-contingent, not intrinsic.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's Q head is a jointly-trained correctness classifier achieving near-perfect separation — a much stronger analogue of our post-hoc DoM probe. The gap (0.77 vs ~1.0) is informative about what joint training buys, though the architecture gap (TRM vs. autoregressive LLM) makes direct comparison speculative.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse):** The paper's three trajectory modes — quick success (fast convergence to good basin), delayed success (escape from bad basin), failure (stuck in bad basin) — map structurally onto F-4's asymmetric collapse. Correct trajectories collapse harder/faster, which parallels "quick success" mode where Q and cell accuracy saturate rapidly.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature):** The "bad basin" concept is a latent-space analogue of D-bucket. Problems stuck in bad basins have low Q values throughout, analogous to D-bucket's distinctive geometric signature. The paper's finding that bad-basin membership is escapable via noise is directly relevant to whether D-bucket is intrinsic or contingent.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% accuracy):** The Q head's role as verifier for trajectory selection is functionally equivalent to F-8's DoM-based abstention. The paper reports best-Q@K within 1pp of pass@K on PPBench — a much tighter verifier than our DoM achieves. This suggests the selective-prediction ceiling may be higher with a richer correctness signal.

- **H-1 (DoM steering):** The Langevin negative result (Appendix C) is the most direct connection — gradient-guided steering via a correctness head adds nothing beyond noise in TRM's latent space.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes familiarity vs. decomposability):** The paper's "quick success" vs. "delayed success" vs. "failure" trichotomy could map onto problem familiarity (quick success = familiar, delayed = decomposable-but-hard, failure = genuinely unfamiliar).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Gaussian noise injection into latent states at each recursion step** — Add N(0, σ²I) to latent state at each computation step; run K parallel rollouts; select best via correctness head.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (apply to cached L19 activations with synthetic "recursion" by re-injecting noise and re-running probe).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can test noise robustness of DoM on cached activations trivially.

- **Q head as trajectory verifier** — Train a binary correctness classifier jointly with the model; use it at inference to select among parallel candidates.
  *Replication cost*: H100 days (would require fine-tuning Qwen with auxiliary correctness head).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — joint training is outside our probe-only paradigm.

- **Width scaling (K parallel rollouts) vs. depth scaling** — Systematic comparison of independent parallel sampling vs. sequential deepening for accuracy gains.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (compare K independent forward passes with temperature sampling vs. single-pass features on cached data).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly testable with temperature-sampled forward passes.

- **PCA visualization of latent trajectory modes** — Project latent states into principal plane per problem, classify trajectories into quick-success / delayed-success / failure modes.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (apply to cached L19 activations across MATH-500 problems).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already have PCA infrastructure from FE291.

- **pass@K / best-Q@K / mode@K metric suite** — Systematic evaluation of oracle upper bound, verifier-selected, and majority-vote selection across K.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (run K forward passes, evaluate each selection strategy).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — natural extension of selective-prediction work.

## Approaches & framings

- **Bad-basin escape as test-time compute scaling.** Reframes inference failures not as model limitations but as optimization getting stuck in suboptimal latent attractors. Intersects F-7 (D-bucket signature): are D-bucket problems in bad basins that noise could dislodge, or genuinely hard?

- **Width vs. depth scaling axes.** Distinguishes two orthogonal ways to spend test-time compute: more sequential reasoning steps (depth) vs. more independent samples (width). Intersects F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with L19 DoM): trajectory depth may be informationally redundant, with width providing the real gains.

- **Verifier headroom as diagnostic.** The gap between pass@K (oracle) and best-Q@K (model's own verifier) quantifies how much better the correctness signal could get. Intersects F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 ceiling): our gap between probe performance and oracle is the "verifier headroom" in our setting.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- TRM uses a 2-layer network fθ with 5-7M parameters, recursively applied n times per latent recursion, T latent recursions per deep recursion
- Q head is a linear projection on a single token: fQ(y) = w^T y[:,0] + b; gradient is constant (input-independent)
- For gradient-dependent steering, they replaced with attention-pooled Q head (~8× faster per Langevin step)
- Noise scale σ is task-dependent: 0.2 for PPBench, 0.3 for Sudoku-Extreme, 1.0 for Maze-Hard, 0.2 for ARC-AGI
- All training/eval on single H100 80GB
- Code not linked in paper (no GitHub URL found)

**Relevance:** Introduces stochastic noise injection into recursive latent-state refinement as a test-time compute scaling strategy, with a jointly-trained correctness head (Q head) as trajectory verifier. **Key claim we tested:** Gaussian noise injection into latent states enables escape from suboptimal solution basins, and a learned correctness head can select among stochastic trajectories nearly as well as an oracle verifier.
