# Triage brief — 2605.19376 — Generative Recursive Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19376
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill direction predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731) — mechanism: deterministic latent trajectory collapse.**
   GRAM demonstrates that deterministic recursive refinement converges to a single attractor trajectory (§1, §4.2 — Table 1 coverage ≤36.1% for deterministic models). If the L19 DoM direction is itself a collapsed attractor of the forward pass's deterministic computation, then DoM's apparent predictive power may reflect mode-collapse geometry rather than a genuine correctness signal. A stochastic-perturbation analysis of L19 hidden states (sampling ε around the deterministic trajectory as in GRAM's Eq. 4–5) could test whether DoM AUROC survives under trajectory diversification or degrades, which would indicate the signal is an artifact of single-path convergence.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Inject Gaussian perturbations (σ = 0.01–0.1) into L19 residual stream during prefill, re-extract DoM across N perturbation samples, and measure whether DoM direction stability and AUROC 0.7731 hold or collapse.

2. **Refutes F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage / 71.6% accuracy) — mechanism: width-based multi-trajectory scaling outperforms single-direction thresholding.**
   GRAM's Latent Process Reward Model (LPRM, §2.3) trains a value head v_ψ(z_t) to predict trajectory-terminal correctness from latent states, then selects the best-of-N trajectory — fundamentally a learned selective-prediction mechanism operating on latent geometry. If an LPRM-style value head trained on L19 hidden states outperforms the DoM-threshold selective predictor (71.6% at 50% coverage), it would show that DoM captures only a low-rank shadow of the full correctness signal extractable from the same layer, and that F-8's selective-prediction approach is suboptimal.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Train a small MLP value head on L19 prefill activations to predict correctness probability; use best-of-K sampling of CoT trajectories ranked by this head; compare selective-prediction curves to DoM threshold.

3. **Refutes F-3 (prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal, cos = 0.046) — mechanism: stochastic trajectory bridging.**
   GRAM shows that stochastic latent trajectories can bridge between initially distant regions of latent space (Fig. 18–19: trajectories traverse from high-loss to low-loss regions via diverse paths). The near-orthogonality of prefill vs. final DoM (cos = 0.046) might not reflect genuinely independent signals but rather the deterministic trajectory's particular path through a loss landscape with multiple basins. Under stochastic perturbation of intermediate computation, the two directions might align more closely (higher cosine), suggesting the orthogonality is a deterministic-path artifact.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Extract DoM at multiple intermediate token positions along CoT generation with and without stochastic perturbation of residual streams; measure whether cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) increases under perturbation.

4. **Refutes H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability) — mechanism: recursive depth as alternative explanation.**
   GRAM demonstrates that recursive depth (number of refinement steps) directly controls reasoning capability — accuracy scales monotonically with depth (Fig. 4). This suggests an alternative explanation for prefill DoM: it may encode the model's estimate of *required computation depth* rather than familiarity or decomposability. Problems where the model needs more recursive refinement would have different geometric signatures not because of familiarity but because of computational difficulty.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Correlate L19 DoM magnitude with problem difficulty measured by number of reasoning steps in ground-truth solutions; if DoM tracks step-count better than familiarity proxies, H-5's framing is refuted.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731):** GRAM's LPRM value head (§2.3, Eq. 16) is trained to predict correctness from latent states — conceptually identical to a probe on hidden states. The LPRM operates on recursive-reasoning latent states z_t rather than LLM residual streams, but the principle is the same: a linear/MLP readout from intermediate representations predicts output quality. GRAM achieves this in a 10M-parameter model; we achieve it in a 1.5B-parameter model. The key difference is GRAM's latent states are explicitly designed for iterative refinement, while our L19 residual states are a byproduct of autoregressive generation.

- **F-4 (correct trajectories collapse harder — asymmetric collapse):** GRAM's stochastic guidance framework (Eq. 4–5) reveals that deterministic models suffer mode collapse — all runs converge to identical trajectories (Fig. 1a). This is the macro-architecture analogue of F-4's observation that correct trajectories collapse geometrically. In GRAM, deterministic collapse prevents multi-solution discovery; in our setting, the asymmetric collapse of correct-vs-incorrect trajectories is what makes DoM predictive.

- **F-10 (PH adds no signal beyond Gaussian null):** GRAM's visualization of latent trajectories uses PCA to project latent states into 2D (§D.6, Fig. 18–19). The fact that PCA suffices for visualizing the meaningful structure in GRAM's latent space is consistent with F-10's finding that topological methods add no signal beyond what covariance-based methods capture — the relevant geometry is captured by the first few principal components.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering):** GRAM's "learnable stochastic guidance" (Eq. 4–5) is a form of per-step steering where μ_θ(u_t) provides a state-dependent direction and σ²_θ(u_t) controls exploration magnitude. This is architecturally parallel to steering via DoM: a fixed direction applied to hidden states. GRAM's finding that guidance direction matters (removing it → 0% accuracy, Table 3b) suggests that if DoM steering works, the direction quality is critical.

- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok):** GRAM's hierarchical instantiation separates high-level (h) and low-level (l) latent states operating at different timescales (§2.1, Eq. 6–9). This multi-scale decomposition parallels the CoE approach of aggregating across layers/positions. GRAM finds that hierarchical structure helps (Table 3a: +DS+HR+SG = best), suggesting CoE's multi-layer aggregation could add signal beyond single-layer DoM.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Stochastic latent guidance (ε_t ~ N(μ_θ(u_t), σ²_θ(u_t)I))** — Gaussian perturbation with learned mean and variance added to deterministic update at each refinement step. Enables multi-trajectory exploration of latent space.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU to implement perturbation on cached activations; no new forward passes needed for a static analysis.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can test perturbation sensitivity of DoM direction on cached L19 activations.

- **Latent Process Reward Model (LPRM)** — Value head v_ψ(z_t) trained via regression (Eq. 16) to predict trajectory correctness from intermediate latent states. Used for best-of-N trajectory selection.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU to train an MLP on cached L19 activations predicting correctness.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly comparable to DoM probe but as a learned value function rather than a fixed direction.

- **Width-based inference-time scaling** — Sampling N parallel stochastic trajectories and selecting via majority vote or LPRM. Orthogonal to depth-based scaling.
  *Replication cost*: N/A for our pipeline (requires generation-time intervention, not post-hoc analysis of cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — would require modifying inference loop, which is H100-bound.

- **PCA + K-D tree latent trajectory visualization** — Projects high-dimensional latent state sequences into 2D via PCA, interpolates with K-D tree to construct continuous loss landscape (§D.6).
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU on cached activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could visualize L19 hidden-state trajectories across CoT tokens for correct vs. incorrect problems.

- **Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) with Q-learning halt head** — Auxiliary head predicting halt/continue Q-values, trained via temporal-difference loss (Appendix A.1, Eq. 15). Allows variable recursion depth per input.
  *Replication cost*: Would need multi-layer features and forward passes; ~2h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires forward-pass intervention architecture we don't have.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning as stochastic latent trajectory.** Reframes extended computation not as sequence extension (CoT) or single-pass inference but as sampling from a distribution over latent refinement paths. Intersects F-2/F-3: if the model's internal computation is better modeled as a trajectory distribution than a point trajectory, then DoM (a single direction) captures only the mode, not the distribution.

- **Width vs. depth as orthogonal scaling axes.** Inference-time compute can scale by running deeper (more steps) or wider (more parallel samples). Our DoM probe is a depth-1 width-1 readout. The framing suggests asking whether multiple DoM readouts at different layers (width across layers) or at different CoT positions (depth across tokens) would improve AUROC beyond the single L19 readout.

- **Recursive state refinement decoupled from parameter count.** A 10M-parameter model achieves 97% on Sudoku-Extreme by iterating shared weights. This decoupling principle intersects H-4 (Sharpness Dimension correspondence): the geometry of iteratively refined hidden states may relate to the effective sharpness dimension of the computation manifold.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- GRAM uses 10M parameters total: embedding dim D=512, 8 attention heads, SwiGLU MLP, K=4–6 inner steps, T=3 outer steps per supervision step, N_sup=16 supervision steps.
- Training on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs. Sudoku: 2h. ARC-AGI: 5 days. N-Queens 8×8: 1h.
- AdamW lr=1e-4, weight decay 1.0, gradient clipping 1.0, EMA decay 0.9999.
- KL balance coefficient 0.8 (prevents posterior collapse). KL coefficient β varies by task (0.04–0.5).
- Code: https://ahn-ml.github.io/gram-website/ (Apache 2.0 likely, builds on HRM/TRM repos).
- Stochastic guidance implemented as learned mean + variance MLP heads on deterministic update u_t; perturbation only applied to high-level state h, not low-level state l.

### H-897: DoM AUROC 0.7731 is a sharp attractor ridge that degrades under isotropic Gaussian perturbation of L19 activations
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2605.19376 + F-2
**Test:** Add Gaussian noise (σ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5}) to cached L19 prefill activations, retrain DoM probe under 5-fold OOF, measure AUROC degradation curve. Sharp drop (>0.05 AUROC at σ=0.05) → confirm; gradual (<0.01 at σ=0.1) → reject.

**Blocks:** nothing
