# Triage brief — 2506.00653 — Linear Representation Transferability Hypothesis: Leveraging Small Models to Steer Large Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00653
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **LRT vs F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor, AUROC 0.7731 on 1.5B)** — LRT predicts that for two models in the same family/tokenizer trained on similar data (Qwen-2.5-1.5B, Qwen-2.5-7B), there exists an affine map A : R^1536 → R^3584 such that the 1.5B prefill DoM maps to (approximately) the 7B native prefill DoM. **Refutation mechanism**: train this affine map on P11 cached hidden states, then evaluate the *mapped* 1.5B DoM as a correctness probe on 7B activations. If mapped-DoM AUROC on 7B is ≪ 7B native DoM AUROC (and ≪ random-baseline-controlled threshold), the prefill correctness direction is *not* a feature in any LRT-style universal subspace — it is model-specific. That would weaken F-2's interpretation as a "real" feature direction and instead reframe it as scale-coupled. Falsifiable by P11-FE70 (~30 min H100 train + cosine eval on cached data).

2. **LRT vs F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Affine maps **do not** preserve orthogonality in general (only orthogonal/scaled-orthogonal maps do). LRT's framework allows arbitrary `A = P_T^⊤ (P_S^⊤)^†`. **Refutation mechanism**: if F-3's near-orthogonality is a *universal-feature-space* property (i.e., the prefill and final-token features are orthogonal in the shared V_U basis), LRT predicts the cosine should be approximately preserved across scales — i.e., 7B native cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0.046. If on 7B the natively-trained cosine is large (>0.3), F-3's "orthogonal directions" claim is not a feature-space invariant but a 1.5B-specific artifact of the projection P_S. Falsifiable by recomputing 7B native cos directly from P11 cached 7B L21 prefill + final activations (no new compute — pure post-hoc measure).

3. **LRT vs F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective geometric signature)** — LRT predicts that any *linearly-encoded* distinguishing signature (e.g., D-bucket vs A-bucket separability via linear probe) is preserved under affine mapping. **Refutation mechanism**: train a D-bucket linear probe on 1.5B L19 activations, map it to 7B via the LRT affine map, and evaluate. If LRT holds, mapped-probe AUROC on 7B D-bucket ≈ probe trained directly on 7B. If the 7B native D-bucket signature is qualitatively different (e.g., nonlinear, or in a different feature subspace), the LRT-mapped probe will fail badly. This would *contradict LRT* but also call F-7 into question on whether the "collective signature" is a stable feature direction at all rather than a model-specific configuration.

4. **LRT vs H-11 (Big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B)** — LRT proposes the *direct* mechanism for H-11 (and even reverses it: small→large is enabled by affine map). **Confirmation/refutation hinges on**: if LRT works in our setting, H-11 has a clean implementation path (train affine, project DoM, distil). If LRT fails on Qwen for correctness directions specifically (while working for Gemma refusal-style directions, as the paper shows), that suggests *behavioral* steering directions are LRT-transferable but *correctness* directions require something stronger — H-11 then needs a different mechanism (cross-model probing, not affine map). Falsifiable by the same P11-FE70 cross-eval.

5. **Side refutation — "universal feature space" vs F-10 (PH at Gaussian null on trained residual streams)** — LRT's universal feature space `W_U ∈ R^{n×D}` predicts a *low-dimensional* shared structure. F-10 says topological structure (PH) on residual streams matches the Gaussian null. These are consistent only if D is large and features are dense/Gaussian-mixed enough to wash out topology. **Refutation mechanism**: if LRT holds and the universal subspace is *geometrically distinctive* (e.g., specific clusters, manifold), then PH on the projected hidden states should differ from Gaussian null — contradicting F-10. Falsifiable by running PH on the LRT-mapped representations (`A·h^S + p`) and comparing to PH on h^T directly.

Direct test: does correctness AUROC transfer the same way? The 0.7731 → ?? mapped-7B AUROC is the headline number this experiment generates.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — LRT's framework is silent on whether affine maps respect intra-model angle relationships; this is a clean test.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — directly probed by mapping a 1.5B D-bucket-vs-A-bucket linear classifier to 7B and re-evaluating.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy)** — LRT replaces the steering vector lookup: instead of training DoM on each scale, train once on 1.5B and project. If LRT works, H-1 generalises to "per-position DoM trained on smallest available model steers all scales".
- **H-8 (prefill direction stable across training checkpoints)** — checkpoint-vs-checkpoint is the easiest LRT case (same arch + scale). LRT predicts the affine map is approximately the identity for matched-scale, matched-arch model checkpoints.
- **H-11 (big-to-small distillation of 7B self-prediction into 1.5B)** — LRT *is* the proposed mechanism. Testing LRT on Qwen-2.5-1.5B↔7B is testing H-11.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution)** — LRT operates at full hidden-state level. H-13 would not be directly addressed; orthogonal direction.

Headline anchors used: AUROC 0.7731 (1.5B prefill), 0.7186 (1.5B final), cos 0.046 (F-3).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Affine hidden-state map (l2l)** — `min_{A,p} E_x ‖A·h^S_l_S(x) + p − h^T_l_T(x)‖²` trained with AdamW, LR 1e-4, batch 32, ctx 512, 4 epochs.
  *Replication cost*: ~200 min on a single H100/Grace Hopper at 10M tokens; smaller pilot ~30 min at 1M tokens (we have these cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — minimal, directly on cached P11 Qwen 1.5B + 7B activations. Headline cross-check.

- **SAE-coefficient → latent map (s2l)** — map source-model SAE codes `c^S_l_S(x)` directly to target latents `h^T_l_T(x)`; theoretically lossless if SAE features are universal.
  *Replication cost*: requires Qwen-2.5 SAEs (Gemma Scope-equivalent). Not currently available; either train from scratch (~1 day H100) or wait for HF release.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — blocked on Qwen SAE availability; superseded by l2l for our purposes.

- **CAA-derived steering vector** — `v_l = mean(h_l(x_pos)) − mean(h_l(x_neg))` (Panickssery 2312.06681). Already canonical in our F-2/F-3 DoM construction.
  *Replication cost*: trivial; we already do this.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's our DoM construction.

- **Logit-difference propensity for steering eval** — `m_LD = Logit(y+) − Logit(y−)` on Tan et al. 2024 MCQ tasks; sweep α, report cross-model correlation.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min H100 if we steal Tan et al.'s 13 tasks. Or skip and use MATH-500 K=1 accuracy directly as our propensity proxy.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — we have a richer correctness signal (MATH-500 + DoM AUROC), so logit-diff on MCQ behavioral tasks is downstream.

- **Frobenius reconstruction error of SAE decoder linear-relatedness** — solve `M̂ = argmin ‖W_T − W_S M‖²`, report Frobenius residual vs random-matrix baseline.
  *Replication cost*: minutes given two SAEs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: only if we obtain Qwen SAEs.

- **Refusal removal via steering** (Arditi 2406.11717 + LRT) — the harmlessness-rating-via-GPT-4.1 protocol. Off-topic for our correctness focus.
  *Replication cost*: GPT-4.1 API + AdvBench/Alpaca samples.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — different evaluation than our K=1 correctness regime.

## Approaches & framings

- **Universal representation space (`W_U`, `V_U`)** — claims hidden states are projections of a shared higher-dim feature space, with model-specific projection matrices `P_S`, `P_T`. Framing intersects F-1 (universality of breathing) and F-10 (Gaussian-null PH): if features live in a shared `V_U`, breathing should be a property of `V_U`'s subspace usage, not model-specific dynamics.

- **Steering as linear-feature intervention** — reframes steering vectors not as model-internal hacks but as additions in a universal feature space. Our prefill DoM (F-2) is implicitly a vector in some such space; LRT predicts its semantic effect should transfer cross-scale.

- **Layers as projections to *different* subspaces of the same universal `V_U`** — Appendix A's many-to-many mapping framing predicts that layers `l_S` and `l_T` of *similar relative depth* should align. This is operationally similar to our P11 stage markers (Lad et al. 2406.19384).

- **Affine ≠ orthogonal** — the paper's framing breaks the implicit assumption (in some of our analyses) that cross-scale comparisons need rotation/Procrustes alignment; arbitrary affine works.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Source: Gemma-2-2B layer 20; Target: Gemma-2-9B layer 20 (both relative-depth ~70%). For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers) vs 7B (28 layers), L19 ↔ L19 (or matched relative depth — both 0.68) is the natural pair.
- AdamW; LR 1e-4 (l2l) / 1e-3 (chat-mix l2l); batch 32; ctx 512; 4 epochs; 9:1 train:val.
- Final val L2 loss ~5.4 (s2l) – 5.95 (l2l). Important: this is *not* small in absolute terms — typical 9B hidden state norm is O(50–100); residual ~5.95 means RMSE ≈ 2.4 ≈ 3% of norm.
- SAE shapes: Gemma-2-2B (16384, 2304); Gemma-2-9B (16384, 3584). Frobenius reconstruction error of decoder map ≈ 114; "average per-entry small". No quantitative effect-size beyond "smaller than random baseline".
- Compute: single Grace Hopper Superchip; 200 min per training run.
- Code: https://github.com/HumainLab/linear-representation-transferability
- Concurrent work — Oozeer et al. 2503.04429 — used nonlinear autoencoders, reported *negative* results for affine; same data for both map training and steering vectors. The LRT paper claims to fix this by separating the two datasets.

## Replicable intermediates

- **L2 affine-map training on cached P11 NPZs** — Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 + Qwen-2.5-7B L21 (or matched depth) prefill activations are cached at `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/*.npz`. ~30 min on H100 to train an affine map at ~1M-token equivalent (we have 500 MATH-500 prompts × ~512 ctx ≈ 256K tokens; sufficient for a sanity-check fit).

- **Mapped-DoM AUROC on 7B correctness labels** — pure scoring on cached 7B activations + cached 7B correctness labels. ~5 min CPU.
- **Cross-cosine: cos(mapped_1.5B_prefill, mapped_1.5B_final) vs F-3's 0.046** — measures whether affine map preserves (or breaks) the orthogonality. Pure post-hoc.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2506.00653 — Linear Representation Transferability Hypothesis: Leveraging Small Models to Steer Large Models (Bello, Das, Zeng, Yin, Leqi — UT Austin, 2025)

**Relevance:** Proposes a Linear Representation Transferability (LRT) Hypothesis: that hidden states across same-family same-tokenizer models are related by a learnable affine map A : R^{d_S} → R^{d_T}, and that steering vectors transfer cross-scale via this map. Empirically demonstrated on Gemma-2-2B↔9B with refusal removal (CAA + Arditi 2406.11717 protocol) and 13 Tan et al. 2024 MCQ propensity tasks (correlation 0.73–0.98). The paper proposes a universal-feature-space framework `W_U ∈ R^{n×D}` with model-specific projection matrices `P_S, P_T`. Direct relevance to F-2 (prefill DoM), F-3 (orthogonality), F-7 (D-bucket signature), and H-11 (big-to-small distillation) — LRT is essentially the *mechanism* H-11 needs. Paper's positive result on Gemma is potentially in tension with concurrent Oozeer et al. 2503.04429 negative result on affine transfers; paper attributes the difference to data-disjointness in map training. Methodological framing aligns with our matched-relative-depth (Lad et al. 2406.19384) layer choice and our DoM-as-direction interpretation (Park et al. 2311.03658).

**Key claim we tested:** A linear affine map from 1.5B L19 to 7B L21 prefill activations preserves the F-2 prefill DoM correctness signal (mapped-DoM AUROC on 7B ≈ 7B native DoM AUROC).

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — P11-FE70 is READY and minimal (30 min H100 + 10 min CPU on already-cached data). Until run, the paper's claim is plausible but unverified for *correctness* directions in our regime; the paper only demonstrated transfer for *behavioral* steering, which is a weaker test (correctness probes have explicit ground-truth labels, behavioral propensities don't).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE70 (HIGH headline), P11-FE71 (orthogonality cross-check), P11-FE72 (D-bucket signature transfer), P11-FE73 (many-to-many extension), P11-FE74 (PH cross-test), H-72, H-73, H-11.

**Status:** TO TEST
