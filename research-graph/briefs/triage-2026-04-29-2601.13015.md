# Triage brief — 2601.13015 — MeltRTL: Multi-Expert LLMs with Inference-time Intervention for RTL Code Generation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13015
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1's premise that fixed-vector steering must fail.** H-1 holds that fixed
   residual-stream steering breaks because the DoM direction rotates during generation
   (P10 v1 result), and that *per-position* banks are required. MeltRTL applies a
   strictly fixed, unit-normalized correction θ\_h^l per head with binary gate σ\_h^l
   and a single scalar α (Eq. 7), and reports +14.67% absolute functional correctness
   gain on VerilogEval at α≈3.0, K=15 heads — a non-monotonic peak. **Mechanism:**
   per-head outputs may be position-stable even when the residual aggregate rotates,
   because the rotation is dominated by a small number of off-target heads that are
   gated off. **Falsifiable test:** replicate MeltRTL's K-head-α-strength sweep on
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B at the per-head level for MATH-500 K=1 generations; if a fixed
   per-head direction with binary gating moves K=1 accuracy ≥ 49.5% (current 48.6%)
   without α-decay over generation length, H-1's per-position assumption is wrong.

2. **Refutes F-2's implicit linearity claim.** F-2 states prefill L19 DoM is a strong
   linear correctness predictor (AUROC 0.7731 OOF on 1.5B). MeltRTL's probe ablation
   directly measures the linear-vs-nonlinear gap on the same kind of input (per-head
   activations as binary correctness predictors): LR (linear) probes have **high
   variance** (best 59.33%, worst 33.67%) while SVC-RBF consistently leads, peaking
   at 60.00% functional correctness in their downstream eval. They explicitly
   conclude "correctness signals are not linearly separable" in the head-level space.
   **Mechanism:** F-2's strong linear AUROC is at the residual-stream level where
   per-head nonlinearities partially cancel; at finer granularity, signal is curved.
   **Falsifiable test:** train SVC with RBF kernel and a 2-layer MLP on the same
   1.5B prefill L19 activations used to compute F-2 (already cached in
   `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`); if either probe family yields AUROC
   ≥ 0.80 (≥ +0.027 over linear DoM) under matched 5-fold OOF protocol, F-2 needs
   to be rewritten as "linear DoM is **a** strong predictor", not the strongest one.

3. **Refutes F-3's claim that prefill and final-token DoM directions are
   geometrically orthogonal at the layer-19 residual level (cos = 0.046).**
   MeltRTL's correctness-critical heads concentrate in late layers (88–94% of
   selected heads in L42–46 of a 48-layer model, ≈ L26–28 in our 28-layer 1.5B by
   relative depth) and the *same* head set is gated identically across all token
   positions during generation — i.e., a single θ\_h direction works at both prefill
   and final-token positions for the same head. **Mechanism:** F-3's cos(prefill,
   final) = 0.046 is computed on the residual stream (sum across heads). When
   summed, the high-overlap "correctness heads" (which MeltRTL selects) are diluted
   by orthogonal off-target heads. At the per-head level, prefill DoM and
   final-token DoM directions for the *correctness-critical* heads should align.
   **Falsifiable test:** for the top-15 head-layer pairs ranked by per-head L19
   prefill correctness AUROC, compute cos(prefill\_head\_DoM, final\_head\_DoM) per
   head and average. If the mean is ≥ 0.5 (vs F-3's 0.046 at residual level), the
   prefill/final orthogonality is a residual-summation artifact, not a per-circuit
   property — F-3's framing needs a head-level addendum.

4. **Refutes F-9's claim that CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with
   single-layer L19 DoM.** MeltRTL shows that probe **ensembles** (LR ⊕ MLP ⊕ SVC
   majority vote across heads in late layers) are necessary for robust correctness
   selection — single linear probes miss signal that a kernel/nonlinear ensemble
   captures. F-9 was established by comparing CoE-60 to a single linear DoM at L19;
   if instead CoE-60 is compared to a probe ensemble over per-head activations, the
   "redundancy" may dissolve because both CoE-60 and the per-head ensemble pick up
   nonlinear/multi-layer structure. **Falsifiable test:** train a probe ensemble
   over per-head activations from layers 18, 19, 20 (3 × 12 heads = 36 features) on
   1.5B MATH-500, compute AUROC. If ensemble AUROC ≥ 0.81 (matching CoE-60), F-9
   stands; if ensemble AUROC ≥ 0.83, F-9 is wrong about why CoE-60 plateaus and the
   correct framing is "single-layer linear is suboptimal, multi-layer-multi-head
   ensembles match trajectory features".

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731)** — MeltRTL's per-head probe ranking is the
  direct head-level decomposition of F-2's whole-residual-stream DoM. Their best
  single-probe correctness selection score (60.00% downstream functional, K=15
  heads, α=3.0) is in the same regime as our 71.6% selective-prediction at
  coverage 0.5 (F-8). Their claim that "most expert heads (73.7–94.7%) localize in
  layers 42–46" maps to our finding that L19 (≈2/3 depth) carries the residual
  signal in 1.5B — both papers identify late-but-not-final layers as the
  correctness locus.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill\_DoM, final\_DoM) = 0.046)** — MeltRTL applies a single fixed
  θ\_h per head **at every token position during generation**, with no
  position-dependent rescaling, and gets a +14.67% gain. Their method is implicitly
  asserting that the correctness direction at the per-head level is position-stable.
  This is a quantitative challenge to F-3 *if interpreted at the head level* —
  although F-3 was specifically about residual aggregates (cos 0.046), it has been
  used in narrative docs to motivate per-position steering universally.

- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering)** — MeltRTL's α=3.0 fixed-θ steering with
  K=15 heads is the cleanest existing empirical evidence that fixed-vector steering
  *can* work, contradicting the P10 v1 finding that pure fixed-vector steering
  fails at the residual level. The reconciliation hypothesis is that head-level
  decomposition isolates position-stable directions that residual aggregation
  obscures.

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — MeltRTL is
  essentially a complete recipe for H-13's "rank attention heads by correctness
  AUROC, intervene on top-K". Their probe-train-rank-intervene pipeline is directly
  reusable for our prefill/final attribution problem: ablate top-K prefill-AUROC
  heads at final-token position and see if final-token signal degrades, and vice
  versa. If heads are disjoint, F-3's orthogonality survives at the head level; if
  overlapping, the prefill/final dichotomy is a residual aggregation artifact.

- **H-19 (C\_exact verification routing, P11-FE19)** — MeltRTL's α-strength sweep
  (peak at 3.0, degrades past 4.0) is qualitatively the same shape we'd expect
  from a verification-then-correction policy at matched compute. Their non-monotonic
  intervention strength curve is a useful design hint: if we use prefill DoM as a
  routing gate for K=8 self-consistency, the gate's output magnitude should plateau
  around an analogous "α=3.0" regime.

- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive geometric signature)** — MeltRTL's per-category head
  heatmaps (Fig 2) show that combinational/sequential/FSM logic types use **distinct
  head subsets**. The analog for MATH-500 would be: do D-bucket problems (K=1 right,
  K=8 wrong — pathological) recruit a distinctive head subset relative to A/B/C
  buckets? If yes, F-7's "collective" geometric signature is actually a sparse
  head-level signature.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-head activation probing (LR + MLP + SVC-RBF ensemble).** Train binary
  classifier f: h\_i → {correct, incorrect} on per-head representation vectors
  (h\_i ∈ R^{d\_head}, where d\_head ≈ 128 for Qwen2.5). LR, MLP (1 hidden,
  ReLU), SVC with RBF kernel; majority vote.
  *Replication cost:* 30min CPU per layer once per-head outputs are extracted;
  per-head extraction itself is ~30min H100 on cached prompts (single forward
  pass, hook at o\_proj input).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — directly tests F-2's linearity and gives
  per-head ranking for H-13.

- **Probe ensemble majority voting.** ŷ = mode{f\_LR(h\_i), f\_MLP(h\_i),
  f\_SVC(h\_i)} (Eq. 4). Combines linear and nonlinear classifiers to reduce
  per-probe variance.
  *Replication cost:* trivial once individual probes are trained.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — easy ablation against F-2's single linear DoM.

- **Fixed unit-normalized head-level steering with binary gate.**
  x^{l+1} = x̃^{l+1} + α · Σ\_h σ\_h^l · θ\_h^l, with σ\_h ∈ {0,1} from probe
  ensemble and θ\_h unit-normalized correction direction (Eq. 7). α swept over
  {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0}, peak at α=3.0.
  *Replication cost:* 1 day H100 — needs generation pipeline with per-head hook
  forward intervention (Hugging Face hooks, hook on attention.o\_proj input
  before sum).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — direct H-1 refutation test.

- **Top-K head ranking by probe score.** Sweep K ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35,
  40, 45, 48} and pick K with highest downstream task accuracy. Their peak is
  K=15 heads of 32×48 = 1536 total.
  *Replication cost:* trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — head-attribution sparsity test for H-13.

- **L2 norm distribution by correctness.** Plot histogram of ||h\_i||\_2 split by
  y=0 vs y=1 to visualize separability per probe family. They observe that
  SVC-RBF gives clearest separation (correct 15–25 vs incorrect 5–15), LR shows
  overlap with shift, MLP shows multi-peak distributions.
  *Replication cost:* trivial — already have prefill activations cached.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — sanity check on our 1.5B residual stream
  (already done in F-2 plots, but per-head split is new).

- **2D discriminative-direction projection.** Project per-head activations onto
  top-2 probe-discriminative directions (essentially LDA dimensions) and plot
  correct/incorrect centroids. They see clean separation for combinational logic,
  overlap for FSMs.
  *Replication cost:* trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this:* yes — diagnostic for D-bucket vs A-bucket head
  geometry (F-7).

- **Multi-expert routing (category-conditioned head subsets).** Train separate
  probes per task category (combinational / sequential / FSM), use category-matched
  heads at inference. Categories must be pre-determined (they classify by hardware
  domain).
  *Replication cost:* moderate — needs MATH-500 problem categorization (e.g., by
  topic: algebra / geometry / number theory).
  *We'd plausibly run this:* maybe — depends on whether per-topic head subsets
  exist on MATH-500. Lower priority than the core head-probing.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Probe-guided component identification" framing.** Reframes correctness from a
  global residual-stream property to a sparse, head-level circuit. Intersects
  H-13 (head attribution) and challenges F-2's residual-stream framing — F-2 may
  be the aggregate echo of a few correctness-critical heads. The 200-sample probe
  dataset is ~250× smaller than the 500 MATH-500 problems we already have; the
  probe pipeline is essentially free at our data scale.

- **"Train-free correctness improvement" framing.** Positions inference-time
  intervention as a complement to fine-tuning, RL, and self-consistency. Reinforces
  P11's verifier framing: routing/gating/intervention are mutually compatible with
  the K=1→K=8 self-consistency lever (H-19/P11-FE19).

- **"Domain-specialized expert" framing.** Different head subsets handle different
  task types (combinational/sequential/FSM in MeltRTL → potentially algebra/geometry/
  number-theory on MATH-500). Could intersect with H-5 (problem familiarity vs
  decomposability) — perhaps "familiarity" maps to specific head subsets and
  "decomposability" to others.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Base model:** QwenCoder2.5-14B (48 layers, 32 heads/layer with GQA → 1536
  total heads). Probe input is per-head output before o\_proj sum, dimension
  d\_head ≈ 128.
- **Hyperparameters:** α ∈ [0.1, 5.0] (peak ~3.0), K ∈ [5, 48] (peak K=15),
  probe regularization 0.01, max iter 1000. Probe ensemble = LR + MLP + SVC-RBF
  majority vote.
- **Hook location for steering:** add α · σ\_h · θ\_h to the residual stream
  *after* head outputs are summed and projected through o\_proj, equivalent to
  a residual-stream additive correction with head-specific direction. Practically
  this can be implemented as a forward hook on the post-attention residual.
- **Computational overhead:** Eq. 8/9 — adds |L\_i| · O(d\_model) per token vs
  base O(s · d\_model²); empirically 11.27s → 14.27s per sample (27% overhead).
- **Late-layer concentration:** for LR and SVC probes, 88–94% of correctness-
  critical heads are in layers 42–46 (last 5 of 48). MLP probes spread more
  evenly (53.6% mid-layer 32–40). This asymmetry between linear and nonlinear
  probes is itself a useful diagnostic.
- **Code:** https://github.com/mashnoor/melt-rtl (referenced in paper as
  "Code available at" — verify before relying on it).

## Replicable intermediates

- **Per-head LR probe sweep on cached 1.5B L19 prefill activations.** We have
  residual stream at L19; computing per-head outputs requires either re-running

  the extraction script in `pathway11_h100/` to dump per-head outputs. Once
  extracted, train per-head LR predicting K=1 correctness; AUROC histogram across
  12 heads (1.5B has 12 heads/layer). Compare top-1 head AUROC against F-2's
  whole-residual 0.7731. *Cost:* ~30min H100 re-extraction + 5min CPU probing.

- **L2-norm split histogram on prefill L19, by K=1 correctness label.** Already
  cached; pure CPU plotting. Replicates Fig 3 of MeltRTL on our data. *Cost:*
  10min CPU.

- **2D discriminative projection of prefill L19 activations onto LDA directions
  for K=1 correct/incorrect.** Already cached; pure CPU sklearn LDA. Replicates
  Fig 4 of MeltRTL. Useful diagnostic for whether D-bucket sits in the
  "incorrect" cluster centroid or off-axis. *Cost:* 10min CPU.

- **Compare probe ensemble (LR+MLP+SVC-RBF) AUROC vs F-2's linear DoM** on the
  same residual-stream (not per-head) prefill L19 activations. *Cost:* 20min
  CPU. If ensemble does not beat 0.7731, F-2's linearity claim is reinforced; if
  it beats by ≥ 0.027, F-2 needs revising.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2601.13015 — MeltRTL: Multi-Expert LLMs with Inference-time Intervention for RTL Code Generation (Mashnoor, Akyash, Kamali, Azar, 2026)

**Relevance:** Closest active analogue to our DoM-based correctness probing
pipeline. MeltRTL trains LR/MLP/SVC-RBF probes on per-head activation vectors
to predict binary correctness, ranks heads by probe AUROC, and at inference
time applies fixed unit-normalized correction vectors θ\_h with binary gating
σ\_h and a single global intensity α. They report +14.67% absolute functional
correctness gain on VerilogEval (45.3%→60.0%) at α=3.0, K=15 heads, with 27%
inference overhead and no fine-tuning. The methodology is directly portable to
our MATH-500 / Qwen-2.5-1.5B pipeline at low cost (~1 day H100). Their finding
that SVC-RBF dominates linear LR for per-head correctness probes is a direct
challenge to F-2's implicit linearity. Their use of fixed (position-independent)
steering with positive results is a direct challenge to H-1's per-position
requirement. Their per-head approach also offers a head-level lens on F-3's
prefill/final orthogonality, possibly exposing it as a residual-aggregation
artifact.

**Key claim we tested:** Inference-time, fixed-θ, per-head additive steering can
substantially improve LLM correctness without fine-tuning, when steering heads
are ranked by linear/nonlinear probe AUROC and gated by binary ensemble
voting.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — proposed P11-FE73/74/75/76 to replicate the
per-head probe + fixed steering pipeline on Qwen-2.5-1.5B + MATH-500. Specific
predictions: (a) SVC-RBF probes match or beat linear DoM AUROC of 0.7731
(refines F-2); (b) cos(prefill\_head\_DoM, final\_head\_DoM) for top-15
correctness heads is ≥ 0.5 (challenges F-3 at head level); (c) fixed-θ
head-level steering moves K=1 accuracy by ≥ +1.0 absolute (refutes H-1's
per-position necessity).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76, H-73, H-74,
related to H-1, H-13, F-2, F-3, F-7.

**Status:** TO TEST
