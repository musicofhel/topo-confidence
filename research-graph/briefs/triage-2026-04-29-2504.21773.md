# Triage brief — 2504.21773 — MAC-Tuning: LLM Multi-Compositional Problem Reasoning with Enhanced Knowledge Boundary Awareness

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21773
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage = 71.6% accuracy)**.
   Mechanism: MAC-Tuning achieves a 48-percentage-point gap between "sure" answers
   (89.2% factual accuracy) and "unsure" answers (41.2%) on ParaRel — larger than
   the gap implied by F-8's coverage-accuracy curve (which is on MATH-500, but with a
   weaker abstention split). If a two-step LoRA SFT with linguistic confidence labels
   ("I am sure"/"I am unsure") matches or beats prefill-DoM-based gating at the
   *same* coverage on MATH-500-K=1, the conclusion that "frozen residual-stream
   geometry is the right substrate for selective prediction" weakens — a *trained*
   confidence head on the final-token side could dominate.
   Falsifiable test: LoRA-finetune Qwen2.5-1.5B with MAC-Tuning's two-step recipe
   on MATH-500 K=1 (using the cached pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute correctness
   labels as confidence ground-truth), evaluate AP and accuracy@coverage=0.5. If
   accuracy@coverage=0.5 ≥ 0.72 (within noise of F-8's 71.6%) and ≥ 80% on the
   "sure" subset, F-8's prefill-DoM headline is matched or beaten by a cheaper SFT
   alternative.

2. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM > final-token DoM, AUROC 0.7731 vs 0.7186) under
   the post-tuning regime**. Mechanism: MAC-Tuning's confidence is emitted *after*
   the answer (Q+A → C), so the relevant residual stream is exactly the
   post-answer token (the same regime where F-2 says final-token signal is weaker
   than prefill). The paper's 89.2/41.2 split implies a strong final-token-region
   correctness signal *after fine-tuning*. If extracting L19 activations at the
   confidence-token position from a MAC-Tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B yields linear AUROC
   ≥ 0.80 on MATH-500 K=1 — exceeding both F-2's 0.7731 (prefill) and 0.7186
   (final-token, base) — then the prefill > final-token ordering is a property of
   the *frozen* model, not of the residual stream itself. Tuning relocates the
   correctness signal forward in the sequence.
   Falsifiable test: extract post-answer-token L19 from MAC-Tuned 1.5B; compute
   linear AUROC against K=1 correctness; compare against the 0.7731 prefill AUROC.

3. **Refutes H-12 (semantic-entropy probes are the label-free alternative to
   DoM-based abstention)**. Mechanism: MAC-Tuning's labels are *self-generated* —
   the "sure"/"unsure" assignment uses the *base model's* correctness on training
   data, not human labels. So the method is effectively label-free: it only needs
   ground-truth answers (which we have for MATH-500). H-12 frames the choice as
   "supervised DoM probe vs label-free SE probe"; MAC-Tuning collapses that
   dichotomy by being a *self-supervised SFT* method that uses verbalized
   confidence as a substitute for the DoM probe entirely. If MAC-Tuning AP on
   MATH-500 ≥ semantic-entropy AP on MATH-500 at matched compute, H-12 should be
   re-scoped: "label-free *probes*" is a narrower question than "label-free
   *abstention*", and the latter is solved by SFT, not probes.

4. **Refutes H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched
   compute)** if MAC-Tuning's "unsure" flag predicts D-bucket membership (K=1
   right, K=8 wrong, F-7) better than prefill-DoM-based routing. Mechanism:
   MAC-Tuning trains the model to express uncertainty in linguistic form;
   verification-routing (H-19) requires a separate verification pass and an
   activation extraction. If a single decoded "unsure" token has equal or higher
   correlation with D-bucket-on-K=8 than the post-answer-newline activation does,
   the verification-pass cost in H-19 is wasted.
   Falsifiable test: on MATH-500 K=1 + K=8 paired data, compute
   AUROC(MAC-Tuned-confidence-token, D-bucket) vs AUROC(PANL-DoM, D-bucket).

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-8 (selective-prediction via prefill DoM, 71.6% on answered at coverage 0.5,
  K=2.5 avg)**. MAC-Tuning's 89.2/41.2 sure/unsure split on ParaRel is the
  closest comparable headline number — both quantify "answers we trust vs answers
  we should refuse". The methods differ on substrate (frozen residual geometry vs
  fine-tuned verbalized output) but are head-to-head comparable on the same
  selective-prediction utility curve.

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 OOF, vs final-token 0.7186)**. The paper's
  central recipe puts the correctness-bearing token at the *end* of (Q,A,C), which
  is exactly where F-2 says the signal is weaker in the *frozen* model. The paper
  implicitly claims that fine-tuning relocates the signal. Direct head-to-head AP
  comparison is possible.

- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes label-free vs supervised DoM for refusal)**.
  MAC-Tuning is a third corner of this trade-off: self-supervised (no human
  labels), supervised by correctness, expressed in linguistic form. Doesn't fit
  "probe vs probe" framing — forces H-12 to be re-scoped or expanded.

- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing — CRITICAL priority, ROI=10)**. MAC-Tuning
  is a no-extra-pass alternative to verification routing: the same model emits
  both answer and confidence in one decode (with separate fine-tunes). If their
  approach matches verification-routing accuracy at lower compute, H-19's ROI
  drops sharply.

- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)**. The
  paper's Section 3.6/3.7 explicitly studies how the model handles
  multi-problem decomposition (n=1→3→5→10→15) and finds that smaller models
  benefit *more* from MAC-Tuning. If the "underlying knowledge acquired during
  MAC-Tuning" is precisely a decomposability signal, this is a SFT-side
  realization of H-5 — and a comparison against frozen prefill-DoM on the same
  multi-problem setting becomes informative.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Two-step LoRA SFT for separate answer/confidence heads** — stage 1 trains
  P(A|Q), stage 2 trains P(C|Q,A) starting from stage-1 weights.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 day H100 per model × dataset (LoRA r=8, alpha=32,
  lr=1e-5, 3 epochs, batch=1, A100-40GB worked for them).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct apples-to-apples baseline against F-2
  and F-8. Most informative single experiment from this paper.

- **Self-supervised confidence labeling via base-model accuracy** — assign
  "I am sure" if base model's training-set output matches ground truth, "I am
  unsure" otherwise. Removes the need for hand-labeled confidence.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (we already have K=1 / K=8 correctness labels on
  MATH-500 from pathway11_h100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our K=1/K=8 buckets
  (A/B/C/D-bucket from F-7). Could even use bucket membership as the label.

- **Multi-problem dataset construction by combining n single problems into one
  prompt** — n=1, 3, 5, 10, 15 sweep.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (concatenate MATH-500 problems).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful as a stress test for the prefill DoM
  signal (does prefill geometry still separate correct/incorrect when n=3
  problems share a single forward pass?). Could become a new data point for F-2
  / F-5.

- **Cross-task transfer evaluation (train on n=3, test on n=1 and n=5)** —
  isolates "did the model memorize the format" from "did it learn underlying
  uncertainty calibration".
  *Replication cost*: 4h CPU on cached eval, given a MAC-Tuned model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we run MAC-Tuning at all — same eval cost.

- **Linguistic-confidence-token AP/ECE evaluation** — read confidence as the
  marginal probability of "sure" vs "unsure" tokens at the final position.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — a vanilla (non-MAC-Tuned) baseline of this is
  a free experiment we could run *today* on our 1.5B / 7B Qwen base models with
  no fine-tuning.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Knowledge boundary" as the construct** — frames confidence as awareness of
  the boundary between parametric knowledge and instruction-set queries. Adjacent
  to but distinct from our "geometric correctness signal" frame. Their frame is
  *trainable* by construction; ours is *measurable* on a frozen model. The
  comparison forces the question: which is the right level of analysis when
  selective-prediction is the goal?

- **Multi-problem regime as a stress test for confidence calibration** — they
  argue that multi-problem inputs are a fundamentally different setting. Our
  pathway 11 work is single-problem; if breathing/F-1 or DoM/F-2 behaves
  differently in multi-problem inputs, that's a falsification of the
  "general-purpose correctness signal" framing.

- **Verbalized vs internal confidence as substitutes** — paper implicitly
  frames verbalized linguistic confidence as a substitute for internal probes
  (Chen et al. 2024, Liang et al. 2024b cited in their related work). If true,
  this dovetails with H-12's framing but with a different baseline.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **GSM (Cobbe et al. 2021)** — 7.5k train / 1k test grade-school math QA,
  HF, Apache 2.0. Applicable: yes — direct math-reasoning analog to MATH-500.
  Confounder: GSM is much easier than MATH-500 so not a 1-for-1 replacement.

- **MMLU (Hendrycks et al. 2021)** — multi-domain QA, HF, MIT. Applicable: yes
  — useful for cross-benchmark transfer of confidence signals (analog to F-5
  cross-benchmark work in P5).

- **ParaRel (Elazar et al. 2021)** — factual relation QA, HF, MIT. Applicable:
  no — purely factual recall, no chain-of-thought, doesn't engage the
  reasoning-traces side of our pipeline.

- **CoQA (Reddy et al. 2019)** — conversational QA, HF, restricted (research
  only). Applicable: no — multi-turn dialogue, off our axis.

- **MTI-Bench (Son et al. 2024, arxiv 2402.11597)** — 5k instances × 25 tasks,
  *the* multi-problem benchmark. Applicable: yes if H-22 / cross-task work
  expands; this would be the canonical multi-problem benchmark.

- **SQA (Iyyer et al. 2017)** — sequential table QA, sequential setting, HF
  available. Applicable: no — table-grounded QA is far from MATH-500 reasoning.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **LoRA fine-tune**: r=8, alpha=32, lr=1e-5, 3 epochs, batch_size=1,
  temperature=0, A100-40GB. HuggingFace PEFT.
- **Two-step training**: parameters Θ₀ = base, Θ₁ = post-stage-1; never train
  both stages jointly (their Merge-AC ablation lost up to 25% AP).
- **Prompt template** for confidence: "Are you sure you accurately answered the
  question based on your internal knowledge? Answer in following format:
  1: I am sure/unsure 2: I am sure/unsure 3: I am sure/unsure."
- **Training scaling caveat**: at n=10, n=15 the per-epoch dataset shrinks
  (5000 train questions / n), so undertraining at large n. Implication for our
  replication: hold n moderate (3 or 5) for first-pass.
- **Code release**: https://github.com/no-touch-fish/Multi-QA-Tuning .
- **Evaluation gotcha**: AP is the area under PR curve over a binary confidence
  ranking — not standard accuracy@coverage. Need to compute both for direct
  comparison with F-8.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Vanilla linguistic-confidence baseline** (no fine-tuning): on cached
  pathway11_h100 K=1 MATH-500 generations from Qwen-2.5-1.5B and 7B, prompt the
  base model "Are you sure (1-shot, post-answer)?", read P("sure")−P("unsure")
  at the response token. Compute AP against K=1 correctness labels. Compare
  against F-2's 0.7731 prefill-DoM AUROC and F-8's 71.6%@coverage=0.5. Cost:
  4h H100 (inference only, no training). This is the *cheapest* refutation
  test on the menu.

- **Multi-problem stress on existing prefill-DoM**: take our MATH-500 K=1
  generations, concatenate problems into n=3 batches (preserving seed), re-run
  forward pass, extract prefill L19 DoM, compute AUROC against per-problem
  correctness. Cost: 1 day H100 (regenerate prefill activations on multi-problem
  prompts). Tests whether F-2 survives multi-problem regime.

- **Confidence-token-position activation extraction** from base model: given a
  fixed prompt "Are you sure?", extract L19 at the post-answer-token from
  base Qwen-2.5-1.5B, compute linear AUROC against K=1 correctness. Compare
  to F-2's 0.7186 final-token-DoM AUROC. Cost: 4h H100.

## Cross-paper signals

- **2505.23224** (He et al., MMBoundary) — already in graph (status:
  pending_triage). Same first-author cluster as MAC-Tuning (Yi R. Fung is senior
  author on both). MMBoundary does step-level confidence calibration in MLLMs;
  MAC-Tuning is the text-only precursor with multi-problem framing. Connection:
  same "calibrate confidence via SFT" school; both target H-12 / F-8.

- **2402.11597** (Son et al., MTI-Bench) — NOT in graph; recommend admission.
  This is the canonical multi-problem benchmark; if we ever stress-test
  prefill-DoM in multi-problem regime, MTI-Bench is the dataset. Also relevant
  to H-22 (cross-task transfer of breathing).

- **2406.10881** (Chen et al., teaching LLMs to express knowledge boundary from
  internal signals) — NOT in graph; recommend admission. This is the closest
  *probe-based* prior to MAC-Tuning. It uses internal signals to express
  knowledge boundary, which is what our F-2 / F-8 work does. Direct prior art
  for the supervised-internal-probe corner of H-12.

- **2406.10786** (Wang et al., zero-shot LLMs handling multiple problems at once)
  — NOT in graph; recommend admission only if multi-problem regime becomes a
  serious test direction. Otherwise off-axis.

- **R-tuning** (Zhang et al. 2024, NAACL) — instructs LLMs to say "I don't know".
  Not on arxiv with a clean ID; cited extensively. Direct precursor to
  MAC-Tuning's "I am sure / I am unsure" SFT recipe. If we replicate
  MAC-Tuning, R-tuning is the single-problem baseline.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2504.21773 — MAC-Tuning: LLM Multi-Compositional Problem Reasoning with Enhanced Knowledge Boundary Awareness (Huang et al., 2025, EMNLP 2025)

**Relevance:** Two-step LoRA SFT method that separates answer prediction from
confidence prediction in a multi-problem setting. Trains base model to emit
"I am sure"/"I am unsure" after answering, with self-supervised labels (base
model's own correctness on training set). Up to 25% AP gain over baselines
across LLaMA3-8B / Qwen2-7B / Llama-3.2-3B / Phi-3.5-mini on CoQA, GSM,
MMLU, ParaRel (independent), MTI-Bench, SQA (sequential). Human eval: 89.2%
factual accuracy on "sure" answers vs 41.2% on "unsure" — a 48-pp gap. This
is the SFT-based-selective-prediction corner of the same problem F-8
addresses with frozen prefill-DoM.

**Key claim we tested:** Two-step SFT separation of answer and confidence
yields a strong, well-calibrated linguistic-confidence signal; superior
to single-step joint training (Merge-AC) by up to 25% AP.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — directly comparable to F-8 on MATH-500 K=1.
Two refutation-paths: vanilla "Are you sure?" baseline (free, no
fine-tuning, P11-FE82) and full MAC-Tuning replication (1-day H100,
P11-FE83). The cheaper test alone could reframe F-2 / F-8.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE82 (vanilla baseline), P11-FE83 (MAC-Tuning
replication), P11-FE84 (multi-problem prefill stress test), H-83, H-84,
H-85, H-86.

**Status:** TO TEST
