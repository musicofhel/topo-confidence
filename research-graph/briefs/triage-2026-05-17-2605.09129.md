# Triage brief — 2605.09129 — Data-driven Circuit Discovery for Interpretability of Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09129
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 prefill DoM predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731).**
   DCD demonstrates that hypothesis-driven analysis (fitting one mechanism to a whole dataset) can conflate multiple distinct mechanisms — circuits differ structurally (Jaccard 0.26–0.66) and functionally (up to 79pp faithfulness drops) across subpopulations of the same task. If MATH-500 correctness is actually driven by 2–4 distinct computational strategies (analogous to Nikankin et al.'s "bag of heuristics" for arithmetic), then our single-direction DoM is an *average over mechanisms*, not *the mechanism*. Clustering MATH-500 by L19 activation similarity and fitting per-cluster probes could yield per-cluster AUROCs significantly exceeding 0.7731, demonstrating that F-2's single direction conflates mechanistically distinct subgroups.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* K-means cluster 500 L19 prefill activations (k=2..8, silhouette-selected), fit per-cluster logistic DoM probes with LOO-CV, test whether max per-cluster AUROC > 0.7731 + 0.02 (i.e., meaningfully above noise).

2. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature).**
   DCD's core finding is that pooled analysis masks sub-mechanism structure. The D-bucket's "collective" geometric distinctiveness could be an artifact of mixing problems solved by completely different circuits. If DCD-style clustering of D-bucket activations reveals that the D-bucket splits into 2+ clusters with non-overlapping geometric profiles (e.g., one cluster has high PC1 projection, another has high PC9, neither has both), then F-7's claim of a single "collective signature" would be refuted — it would be a mixture artifact rather than a coherent population property.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Cluster D-bucket (≈125 problems) L19 activations, compute per-cluster centroid distances and principal-direction cosines. If inter-cluster cosine < 0.5 for any pair, the "collective" framing fails.

3. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).**
   DCD shows that averaging attribution scores across heterogeneous examples can mask distinct mechanisms. If the "redundancy" between CoE-60 and single-layer DoM holds only on average but fails for specific subpopulations (i.e., some clusters are better explained by CoE-60, others by DoM alone), then the redundancy claim is dataset-specific. This would re-open CoE-60 as a complementary signal for specific problem types.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Cluster MATH-500 by L19 activations, compute per-cluster AUROC for (a) DoM-only and (b) CoE-60-only. If any cluster shows CoE-60 AUROC > DoM AUROC + 0.05, F-9's blanket redundancy claim is refuted for that subpopulation.

4. **Challenges H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability).**
   DCD suggests that what appears to be a single interpretable signal (familiarity/decomposability) could actually be multiple distinct computational strategies mixed into one direction by averaging. The DoM direction might encode "familiarity" for one cluster and "decomposability" for another, making the H-5 framing misleadingly unitary.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* Cluster by activation similarity, correlate per-cluster DoM projections with familiarity proxies (frequency in training) vs decomposability proxies (multi-step vs single-step). If different clusters show different dominant correlates, H-5 needs reformulation as a mixture.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** DCD's central thesis — that single-mechanism explanations are dataset-specific artifacts of pooled analysis — directly questions whether our DoM direction is mechanistically unitary or a conflation of multiple internal circuits.

- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature):** The paper's entity-binding positional variants show Jaccard similarity falling from 0.76 (adjacent positions) to 0.41 (distant positions), demonstrating that even within a single "task," position changes produce structurally different circuits. Analogously, the D-bucket's (difficulty-stratified) geometric signature may decompose into positionally or structurally driven sub-mechanisms.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ L19 DoM):** DCD's mixed-dataset finding — that a single pooled circuit achieves 89.8% faithfulness on arithmetic AND 85.1% on entity-binding at 5% circuit size — shows that "redundancy" in pooled evaluation can mask the existence of distinct sub-circuits. Our CoE-60/DoM redundancy finding (F-9) could be the same artifact.

- **F-2 (cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216):** The near-identity of supervised DoM and unsupervised PC1 is consistent with there being a dominant single mechanism, which would resist DCD-style splitting. If DCD clustering fails to improve AUROC, this *strengthens* F-2's claim that a single direction genuinely captures the mechanism (not just its average).

- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels):** Directly testable via DCD — if per-cluster analysis reveals subpopulations where CoE-60 outperforms, H-6 would be confirmed for those subsets.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Per-example edge attribution clustering (DCD Stage 1)** — Compute per-example attribution vectors for all edges, PCA-reduce to ~50–100 dims, cluster with K-means (k selected by silhouette/gap statistic). Groups examples by computational mechanism similarity.
  *Replication cost*: Requires fresh forward passes with gradient computation; ~2–4h H100 for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on 500 examples (full EAP-IG is expensive).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — EAP-IG requires a patching-based attribution pipeline we don't have implemented, and cost is high for our 1.5B model.

- **Activation-space clustering (cheap analogue)** — Cluster examples by their raw hidden-state vectors (or PCA-reduced versions) rather than edge attributions. A coarser proxy for "mechanism similarity" but available from cached data.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU using cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 prefill activations with standard sklearn.

- **Cross-dataset faithfulness evaluation** — Discover a circuit on dataset A, evaluate on dataset B. Measures generalization of mechanistic explanations.
  *Replication cost*: Requires circuit discovery infrastructure (not available in our pipeline).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't do circuit discovery.

- **Best-of-K faithfulness** — For multi-circuit systems, select the most faithful circuit per test example and report the average.
  *Replication cost*: Trivial once multiple probes exist.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we can adapt this: fit K probes (one per cluster), assign test examples to best-matching cluster, report per-example best-probe AUROC.

- **Silhouette-score-based k selection** — Standard method for choosing number of clusters without ground truth labels.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — standard sklearn.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Dataset-specific circuits vs general task circuits"** — The reframe that what was thought to be "the mechanism for X" is actually "a mechanism for this particular dataset instantiation of X." Intersects F-2: our DoM direction might be "the direction for MATH-500-as-a-dataset" rather than "the direction for mathematical reasoning." Cross-benchmark testing (MMLU, GSM8K) becomes even more urgent.

- **"Letting the model determine the scope of explanation"** — Instead of imposing human task boundaries, let computational similarity group examples. Intersects H-5: the familiarity/decomposability distinction should emerge from clustering rather than being imposed a priori.

- **"Bag of heuristics" rather than unified algorithm** — From cited Nikankin et al.: LMs implement arithmetic via multiple heuristics, not one circuit. If MATH-500 correctness prediction similarly relies on multiple heuristics detectable in geometry, our single-direction story needs nuance.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **IOI (Indirect Object Identification)** — Synthetic, templated sentences (2-person, 3-person, letter variants). GPT-2/Qwen/Llama. Not released as formal benchmark. Applicable? no — different task domain, different scale.

- **Entity Binding** — Synthetic (entity, container) pair lists with positional variants. Applicable? no — different task.

- **Arithmetic Addition (2-operand, 3-operand)** — Single-token addition on Llama-3.1-8B. Applicable? weakly — overlaps with MATH-500 arithmetic problems conceptually, but format is completely different (single-op vs multi-step CoT reasoning).

- **Sequence Completion (k-gram)** — Induction-head probing on GPT-2/Qwen/Llama. Applicable? no.

All datasets are synthetic/templated; code released at https://github.com/Ziyu-Yao-NLP-Lab/data-driven-circuit-discovery. License not specified.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- EAP-IG attribution with integrated gradients (default 10 interpolation steps) for edge scoring
- PCA dimensionality reduction to r=50 before clustering (GPT-2: ~10^4 edges; Llama: ~10^6 edges)
- K-means clustering with silhouette score for k selection; also tested agglomerative hierarchical and divisive clustering
- Truncated SVD as alternative to PCA (comparable results)
- Circuit size evaluated at 5% of edges as standard operating point
- Random seed 32 for reproducibility of counterfactual prompt construction
- Code: https://github.com/Ziyu-Yao-NLP-Lab/data-driven-circuit-discovery

## Replicable intermediates

- **Cluster MATH-500 by L19 activation similarity:** Use cached L19 prefill NPZ (500 × 1536), PCA to 50 dims, K-means (k=2..8, silhouette-selected). Check whether clusters separate by correctness label or problem type. Script: `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/` codebase has PCA infrastructure; clustering adds ~20 lines.

- **Per-cluster DoM AUROC:** For each cluster from above, fit a logistic probe on the cluster's subset and compute AUROC. Compare to pooled 0.7731. If any cluster's AUROC exceeds pooled by >0.02, this is a partial replication of DCD's "dataset-specificity" finding in our domain.

- **Best-of-K probe selection:** Fit K cluster-specific probes, assign each test example to its nearest cluster centroid, apply that cluster's probe. Report resulting AUROC vs pooled single-probe AUROC.
