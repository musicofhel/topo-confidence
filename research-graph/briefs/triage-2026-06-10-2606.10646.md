# Triage brief — 2606.10646 — How Does Reasoning Flow? Tracing Attention-Induced Information Flow for Targeted RL in LLMs (FlowTracer)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10646
Triaged: 2026-06-10

Authors: Zhichen Dong, Yang Li, Yuhan Sun, Weixun Wang, Yijia Luo, Zinian Peng, Taiheng Ye, Chao Yang, Wenbo Su, Yu Cheng, Bo Zheng, Junchi Yan (SJTU / Alibaba / Shanghai AI Lab, 2026).

**One-line:** Builds an attention-induced token DAG, answer-conditions it via a Doob-h-like reweighting that enforces local flow conservation, injects unit flow prompt→answer, and uses the resulting per-token *throughput* to identify a sparse "reasoning backbone" of high-flow hub tokens — then reweights the GRPO loss (γ_flow=1.5 on top-40%) for targeted RL credit assignment.

## Refutations

**R1 — F-2 (single L19 prefill DoM, AUROC 0.7731) reads the wrong token.** Our prefill DoM is a difference-of-means on the residual stream at *one fixed position* (last prefill token), implicitly treating that position as the privileged correctness readout. FlowTracer's central empirical claim is that information is *not* uniformly distributed and that proximity-to-answer tokens are systematically *over-credited* while the true causal hubs sit at sparse mid-sequence delimiters/anchors (causal intervention: masking top-20% high-flow tokens flips correctness 14.9% vs 0.5% for low-flow, 4.5% random). **Mechanism:** if correctness-predictive geometry concentrates on high-flow hub tokens, a flow-weighted aggregate of L19 token activations would beat the single-position DoM because uniform/last-token pooling dilutes the decisive-token signal with filler. **Falsifiable:** extract L19 hidden states at FlowTracer-identified high-flow positions on MATH-500, build a flow-weighted DoM, and compare OOF AUROC to 0.7731. A material lift (>~0.79, beating the 0.7928 cov-spectrum ceiling) refutes the "fixed-position readout is sufficient" assumption baked into F-2.

**R2 — F-3 (prefill⊥final, cos 0.046) may be an artifact of ignoring the routing middle.** F-3 reads the residual stream at the two *ends* of the generation (prefill vs final token) and finds them structurally orthogonal. FlowTracer says the task-relevant signal is *routed* prompt→answer through intermediate high-flow hubs that neither endpoint captures. **Mechanism:** the apparent orthogonality could simply mean we are sampling the two terminals of a flow path whose information actually lives in the hub-dominated interior; the directions look unrelated because the carrier was never read. **Falsifiable:** compute L19 DoM at high-flow hub positions; if hub-DoM has non-trivial positive cosine with *both* prefill-DoM and final-DoM (i.e. it bridges them), F-3's "structurally, not positionally" distinct claim is incomplete — the "structure" is the routing backbone, not an intrinsic two-direction split.

**R3 — F-5 (breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics) is in tension with "structural delimiters dominate."** F-5 attributes the geometric signal to problem content rather than autoregressive/positional mechanics. FlowTracer's §5.2 finds that **structural delimiter tokens (punctuation, newlines) recover almost the entire RL gain (38.8% vs 39.4% full), while semantic-content tokens add only a smaller complementary gain (37.6%)** — and that high-flow hubs are predominantly delimiters/symbolic anchors, robust to answer-format changes (top-40% overlap 1.00 across answer forms). **Mechanism:** if the correctness-relevant routing rides on format/delimiter structure rather than semantic content, the "content-dependent" framing of breathing is at least partially a structural/positional phenomenon. **Falsifiable:** partition cached prefill/generation tokens into delimiter vs content; if L19 DoM-projection separability and breathing amplitude concentrate on delimiter positions, F-5 needs qualification.

**R4 — F-7 (D-bucket has a *collective*, not per-problem, geometric signature).** FlowTracer is an explicitly *per-problem* causal localizer — it identifies which individual tokens, when masked, flip *that problem's* answer. **Mechanism:** if the hardest (D-bucket) failures correspond to identifiable per-problem deficits or perturbations of specific high-flow hub tokens, then the failure is localizable per-instance, contradicting F-7's claim that the D-bucket signature is collective and not per-problem. **Falsifiable:** run FlowTracer throughput on D-bucket problems and test whether a per-problem hub-throughput statistic (e.g. backbone concentration / max-hub mass) separates D-bucket from the rest; per-problem separability would dent F-7.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731 prefill DoM; final-token DoM 0.7186).** FlowTracer's "proximity tokens over-credited, mid-sequence hubs under-credited" directly predicts a flow-weighted readout would close the gap and possibly exceed the **0.7928** cov-spectrum ceiling and **0.7847** full-1536 L2 ceiling. Core anchor to beat.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046).** FlowTracer's prompt→answer backbone is the missing middle that F-3's two-endpoint orthogonality ignores.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).** F-9 establishes that the *layer*-trajectory adds nothing beyond single-layer L19. FlowTracer proposes an *orthogonal* trajectory axis — the *token*-flow trajectory. Whether token-flow features are also redundant with L19 DoM (extending F-9) or non-redundant (refuting the generalization "trajectories don't help") is an open, testable fork.
- **Middle-layer corroboration.** FlowTracer finds reasoning flow concentrates in middle attention (layers 15–25 of 36 for Qwen3-4B ≈ depth 0.42–0.69). Our **L19 of 28-layer Qwen-2.5-1.5B ≈ depth 0.68** sits at the top edge of that band — a cross-architecture corroboration of "mid/upper-mid depth carries the reasoning signal" (relevant to F-2's layer choice).
- **F-8 selective-prediction (71.6% acc @ 50% coverage).** A hub-token-aware confidence feature could be stacked into the selective-prediction ensemble.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final circuits)** and the admission note's **H-431/H-434 (token selection), H-62/H-665 (attention-graph TDA):** FlowTracer's attention-DAG + flow scoring is a concrete method for all four.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Attention-induced DAG construction** — time-ordered DAG over tokens; edge weight W_ik = aggregated attention a(x_k,x_i) (mean over mid layers/heads), treated as a non-stochastic linear influence operator (out-degree need not sum to 1).
  *Replication cost*: needs attention maps from forward passes — H100 (we have no cached attention).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's the engine behind the strongest refutation (R1).

- **Doob-h-like answer-conditioning reweighting** — define h(i)=Σ_{k>i} W_ik h(k) (reachability potential to an answer sink, h(sink)=1), set W'_ik = W_ik·h(k)/h(i); Theorem 3.1 proves Σ_k W'_ik = 1 (local flow conservation), auto-suppressing dead-end branches (h(k)≈0).
  *Replication cost*: pure linear-algebra on a given weighted DAG — minutes CPU *if* a graph exists.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the transform is graph-agnostic and can be applied to *any* token/feature similarity DAG we build from cached states.

- **Forward unit-flow throughput** — inject 1/|Q| from a super-source over prompt tokens, propagate f(k)=Σ_{i<k} f(i)W'_ik, define throughput τ(k)=f(k)+Σ_{j>k}φ(k→j). Gives a per-token credit scalar.
  *Replication cost*: minutes CPU given W'.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — yields the per-token weights for R1's flow-weighted DoM.

- **Causal masking intervention** — mask attention of top/bottom/random 20% tokens during regeneration; measure answer-change rate and correctness-reverse rate (Table 1: 45.9/14.9 vs 14.9/0.5 vs 29.5/4.5).
  *Replication cost*: H100 (regeneration with masked attention).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no near-term — needs generation infra; high value but Tier-2.

- **GRPO loss reweighting (γ_flow hard top-k mask)** — multiply surrogate term by γ_flow=1.5 for top-40% high-flow tokens, 1 otherwise. Found hard ranking mask > continuous (raw/sigmoid/log1p/tanh-z) reweighting because flow is heavy-tailed.
  *Replication cost*: full RL run, H100 days.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't do RL post-training (local-only, no GPU budget for RL).

- **Heavy-tail → hard-rank denoising insight (Table 7)** — ranking signal is more reliable than calibrated magnitude; hard top-k beats cumulative-mass thresholding and continuous transforms.
  *Replication cost*: 20–40min CPU as a *feature-selection* analogue on our cached features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — see Tier-1 FE.

- **Layer-range ablation** (early 0–15 / mid 15–25 / late 25–35 / all) for where the flow signal lives.
  *Replication cost*: free against our per-layer cache (no_cot prefill_all_layers is 29×1536).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — Tier-1 cross-check.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning as conserved influence flow over a DAG, not point-wise token scores.** Reframes "which tokens matter" from per-token heuristics (entropy/attention-max/gradient) to a global multi-hop routing problem. Intersects our entire single-direction/single-position program: we read geometry at fixed points; FlowTracer reads it as a path integral prompt→answer. This is the framing most likely to expose a blind spot in F-2/F-3.
- **Answer-conditioning as a Doob h-transform.** Importing the probabilistic Doob h-transform (condition a process on hitting a target set) into LLM interpretability — a clean, reusable lens for "what part of the representation is *destined for* the answer." Could re-cast our DoM as an unconditioned readout that an answer-conditioning step would sharpen.
- **Decoupling "generating logic" (high-flow delimiters) from "maintaining fluency" (low-flow content).** A structural/semantic dissociation that intersects F-5's content-vs-mechanics question head-on.
- **Credit as ordinal ranking, not calibrated weight.** Generalizable claim that heavy-tailed internal signals are best used as ranks — applies to our DoM-component and eigenvalue features.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Attention aggregated over **middle layers L/3–2L/3** (e.g. 15–25 of 36); all-layer average *underperforms* middle-only.
- Single extra batched forward pass between rollout and update → only **2.1–4.5% overhead** (1K: 2.1–2.2%; 8K: 4.0–4.5%).
- **Top-40%** token selection is the sweet spot (Top-20% under-covers, Top-60% dilutes); **γ_flow=1.5** optimal (0.5/1.2/1.8/2.0/3.0 all worse).
- Hard top-k mask > continuous reweighting (raw flow / sigmoid / log1p / tanh+z / MAD) and > cumulative-mass soft thresholding — because flow throughput is heavy-tailed.
- Answer region defined by `\boxed{}`; top-40% set is robust to answer-form changes (overlap 1.00 full-line/explanation, 0.93 MCQ).
- Backbones: Qwen3-4B/8B-Base, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B. GRPO, no KL/entropy reg, lr 1e-6, T=0.99, top-p 1, top-k 100, batch 512.
- No public code link found in the fetched HTML (appendix references `app:implementation`, not resolved).
