# Triage brief — 2505.20322 — Beyond Prompt Engineering: Robust Behavior Control in LLMs via Steering Target Atoms

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20322
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 ("Prefill L19 DoM is *the* correctness predictor — single direction, AUROC 0.7731")**
   - *Mechanism*: STA replaces a single mass-mean direction (CAA-style, which is what our DoM is) with a *sparse multi-dimensional code* — atoms thresholded by both amplitude (Δa ≥ α) and frequency (Δf ≥ β) of activation between positive/negative classes. STA reports beating CAA on safety steering precision; the same selection recipe applied to correctness labels at L19/L20 would, if it lifted AUROC well above 0.7731, reframe F-2 as "low-dim sparse code mediates correctness, not a single direction."
   - *Falsifier*: download Gemma Scope SAE for `gemma-2-9b-it` layer 20, run prefill on MATH-500, fit STA atom selection against correctness labels, compare AUROC to a CAA/DoM baseline trained on the *same* L20 prefill activations. If STA-AUROC > DoM-AUROC by ≥0.03, F-2's single-direction framing is strictly weaker than STA's sparse-code framing. (Same Qwen experiment requires us to train SAEs from scratch — Qwen Scope doesn't exist yet — but the cross-architecture Gemma test is sufficient.)

2. **Refutes F-3 ("Prefill and final-token DoM are orthogonal, cos = 0.046")**
   - *Mechanism*: STA aggregates activations by **mean-pooling over the entire answer** (`ā_pos = mean over answer tokens`). Their steering works downstream without per-position separation. If atom-level steering trained on mean-pooled activations transfers cleanly to *both* prefill and final-token positions, then the orthogonality we observe at the L19 single-direction summary may be an artifact of aggregating over a non-stationary signal — the mean-pool collapses prefill and final-token components into a coherent atom basis, even though the position-specific DoM summaries look orthogonal.
   - *Falsifier*: from cached P11 1024-tok per-token activations, mean-pool over the answer span, fit DoM. Compare the resulting "answer-mean DoM" to (a) prefill-only DoM, (b) final-token DoM, in cos similarity and in AUROC. If answer-mean DoM is ≥0.5 cosine-aligned to *both* prefill and final-token DoMs while still hitting ≥0.75 AUROC, F-3's orthogonality is a per-position summary artifact, not a structural fact.

3. **Refutes H-1 ("Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy")**
   - *Mechanism*: STA's reasoning experiment on **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B / GSM8K** explicitly shows that SAE-atom steering changes *reasoning length* "while maintaining accuracy" — i.e., the controllable axis under steering is length, not correctness. If the only available steering knob in this circuit class is length, then H-1's predicted accuracy lift could materialize as a length change with no accuracy delta — refuting the *causal* mechanism behind H-1.
   - *Falsifier*: run H-1's steering protocol but log generation length per problem alongside accuracy. If steering at +k·DoM produces a >20% change in mean output length but ≤2pp change in MATH-500 accuracy across the bucket, H-1's mechanism is misattributed and the correct framing becomes "DoM controls length, length correlates with correctness." (This is also a length-band confound test orthogonal to H-15.)

4. **Refutes F-9 ("CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM")**
   - *Mechanism*: F-9 says CoE's 60-feature trajectory summary collapses to single-layer DoM at no AUROC cost. STA's premise is that *multiple* sparse atoms — not one direction — carry the steerable signal. If STA-style atom selection at L19 yields a 5–20-atom code with AUROC materially above DoM (per refutation 1), then the "CoE-60 → 1-D DoM" collapse is a coincidence of CoE's specific 60 features, not evidence that the underlying signal is one-dimensional. F-9 then has to be re-stated as "CoE-60 happens to project onto DoM-aligned subspace, but a sparse SAE basis recovers extra signal CoE-60 misses."
   - *Falsifier*: same as refutation 1, plus also run STA-atom probe vs CoE-60 probe head-to-head on MATH-500 P11 cache. If STA-atom AUROC > CoE-60 AUROC, F-9 needs revision.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 / AUROC 0.7731**: STA's amplitude+frequency atom-selection is a structured generalization of mass-mean (CAA / DoM). Their L20 (Llama-3.1-8B) and L24 (Gemma-2-9B-pt) layer choices bracket our L19 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B) — same "late-mid residual stream" target. If STA atoms beat single-direction CAA on safety, the reasoning-correctness analog is a direct sanity check on F-2's headline number.
- **F-3 / cos 0.046**: STA aggregates *across* the answer span. Our orthogonality is computed *between* prefill and final-token positions. The aggregation choice could explain (or break) the orthogonality observation.
- **F-7 / D-bucket has collective signature**: STA's frequency criterion (Δf — atoms that consistently fire in one class) is conceptually adjacent to "find features shared across a problem subset." A frequency-only filter on cached prefill might recover D-bucket atoms.
- **H-1 / per-position DoM steering**: STA *is* H-1's natural competitor — sparse atom steering vs single-direction DoM steering on a sister model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is in P11-FE5). The reasoning-length-not-accuracy result is a refutation hook (above).
- **H-7 / density-based outlier detection finds D-bucket**: STA's atom selection is a structured alternative to density-based outlier detection. If STA atoms cluster D-bucket together, H-7 has a cheaper path.
- **H-12 / semantic-entropy probes (label-free)**: STA still requires labels (positive/negative samples). Confirms that fully label-free correctness detection remains open.

## Methodologies extracted

- **STA atom selection (amplitude × frequency thresholding on SAE features)** — Per SAE feature j, compute Δa_j (mean activation diff between pos/neg samples) and Δf_j (frequency-of-activation diff). Keep only atoms where both thresholds are met: a_target_j = Δa_j if (Δa_j ≥ α and Δf_j ≥ β) else 0.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached prefill activations *if* we have an SAE; new SAE training is multi-day H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in alternative to DoM at L19, the most direct test of F-2's "single direction" claim.
- **Mean-pool-over-answer aggregation** — All atom amplitudes are computed as means over answer-token activations, not per-position.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on existing P11 per-token NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — orthogonality test for F-3 (refutation 2).
- **SAE-atom steering on residual stream** — Add scaled atom vectors back into residual at the same layer used for selection.
  *Replication cost*: needs SAE; otherwise H100 day per model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: only if H-1's DoM steering is null/weak — STA is the obvious fallback.
- **Reasoning-length steering via SAE atoms (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B / GSM8K)** — Same atom-selection recipe applied to long-vs-short CoT examples; steering vector then controls output length while preserving accuracy.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day + need SAE for Qwen-7B (none public).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — needs SAE we don't have, but the *finding* (length is the steerable axis) feeds H-1 / H-15 confound logic.
- **Pretrained Gemma Scope / Llama Scope SAE consumption** — Pull pre-trained SAEs from open releases rather than training fresh.
  *Replication cost*: download + adapter wiring, ~half day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we ever extend the project to Gemma-2 / Llama-3.1 — gives us an out-of-family generalization test for free.

## Approaches & framings

- **Sparse atomic decomposition vs single direction** — The paper's central reframing: "the steerable signal is a small set of disentangled atoms, not a vector." This directly intersects F-2 / F-9 (single-direction framing) and H-1 (single-vector steering).
- **Joint amplitude+frequency thresholding** — Frequency adds an "is this atom *consistently* class-specific" filter on top of mean-difference. Cheap improvement on top of any DoM-style selection.
- **Mean-pool-over-answer** as the aggregation choice — implicit claim that within-answer position is exchangeable. Tension with our F-3 orthogonality framing.
- **Length as the steerable axis in reasoning models** — They show STA controls *length*, not correctness, in DeepSeek-R1-Distill. Reframes H-1's "steering moves accuracy" as possibly "steering moves length, accuracy follows length sometimes."

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Layer choices: **L20 for Llama-3.1-8B, L24 for Gemma-2-9B-pt, L20 for Gemma-2-9B-it** — all in the late-mid residual stream, bracketing our L19 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B). Confirms the L19/L20/L24 band as the canonical "where the action is" zone.
- SAE source: **Gemma Scope** (https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope) for Gemma family, **Llama Scope** for Llama-3.1. No Qwen SAE released.
- Reasoning model: **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B** — same architecture family as Qwen-2.5 base; this is the same model pinned by P11-FE5.
- Aggregation: **mean over answer tokens**, not over the full prompt+answer sequence.
- Selection thresholds α, β are model-and-task-tuned (paper does not commit to defaults).
- Baselines: **Prompt_hand**, **Prompt_auto**, **CAA** (Contrastive Activation Addition), **SAE_AXBENCH**.
- Headline numerical anchor: STA on Gemma-2-9B-it averages 97.56% across SafeEdit + RealToxicPrompts; on Llama-3.1-8B drops to 72.23% — the technique is *much* less reliable on Llama than on Gemma, suggesting SAE-quality dominates STA-quality.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Mean-pool-over-answer DoM, cached NPZs only** — From P11 per-token L19 activations, compute the mean-pooled DoM, measure cos to prefill DoM and to final-token DoM, and AUROC. Tests refutation 2 today, no GPU needed.
- **Frequency-criterion-only filtering on cached prefill** — Apply Δf-style frequency thresholding on raw L19 prefill activations (treat each activation dim as a feature, no SAE). If this lifts AUROC above 0.7731, F-2's "single direction" framing weakens.
- **Gemma Scope download + Gemma-2-9B-it MATH-500 prefill** — Half-day to wire up STA selection on a real SAE; gives a clean cross-architecture sanity check on F-2 (does sparse-atom probe beat DoM probe on a model where we *have* a real SAE?).
- **Length-confound check on existing H-1 steering plan** — Even before running H-1, log generation length deltas; if H-1 ever runs, this is the refutation test for it.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.20322 — Beyond Prompt Engineering: Robust Behavior Control in LLMs via Steering Target Atoms (Wang et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** STA is the natural sparse-multi-atom generalization of our L19 DoM. They show on Gemma-2-9B-it / Llama-3.1-8B at L20–L24 (same residual-stream depth band as our L19) that selecting multiple SAE features by amplitude *and* frequency thresholds beats single-direction CAA on safety steering, and that the same recipe controls reasoning *length* on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/GSM8K. Three of our load-bearing claims (F-2's "single direction," F-3's prefill/final orthogonality, H-1's "steering moves accuracy") all have direct refutation paths through their methodology.

**Key claim we tested:** A sparse multi-atom code (amplitude × frequency thresholded) is the right unit of intervention at the late-mid residual stream — not a single mass-mean direction.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — three cheap CPU-only refutation experiments (P11-FE73, P11-FE74) can be run today against cached P11 activations. STA's strongest threat to F-2 is the multi-atom probe vs single-direction DoM head-to-head; the strongest threat to H-1 is their reasoning-length-not-accuracy result on the same Qwen family. We have not yet run any of these.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P11-FE76; H-73, H-74, H-75.

**Status:** TO TEST
