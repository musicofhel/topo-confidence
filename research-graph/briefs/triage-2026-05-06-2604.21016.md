# Triage brief — 2604.21016 — SGD at the Edge of Stability: The Stochastic Sharpness Gap

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21016
Triaged: 2026-05-06

## Refutations

**R1 — F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) may be a training-recipe artifact, not an architecture-intrinsic signal.**
The paper proves that the equilibrium sharpness — and critically, the *top Hessian eigenvector* u that defines the dominant curvature direction — depends on batch size through the stochastic sharpness gap ΔS = ηβσ²\_u/(4α). If our L19 DoM direction (cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216) is geometrically related to this top eigenvector, then different training batch sizes would shift the equilibrium and potentially rotate the dominant direction. The AUROC of 0.7731 might not transfer across models trained with different batch sizes, even on the same architecture. **Falsifiable:** Train two copies of Qwen-2.5-1.5B on the same data with batch sizes differing by 10×, extract L19 prefill DoM from each, and compare AUROC and cos(DoM₁, DoM₂). If the directions diverge significantly (cosine < 0.5), F-2's single-direction claim is batch-size-confounded.

**R2 — H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence) requires the *stochastic* equilibrium, not the deterministic one.**
H-4 proposes a formal correspondence between breathing and the Sharpness Dimension / EoS framework. But this paper shows that SGD-trained models (like Qwen-2.5-1.5B) operate at a stochastic equilibrium S\_eq = 2/η − ΔS, strictly *below* the deterministic 2/η threshold. The oscillatory dynamics along the top eigenvector are qualitatively different: noise-inflated second moments (E[x̂²\_t] = δ²) with a shifted mean for ŷ\_t. Any formal mapping between breathing amplitude and sharpness must account for the stochastic gap, not just the deterministic EoS theory from Tuci et al. (2604.19740). **Falsifiable:** Measure the top Hessian eigenvalue at the Qwen-2.5-1.5B converged checkpoint. If S(θ) ≈ 2/η (no gap), the stochastic correction is negligible and H-4 can proceed with the deterministic framework. If S(θ) ≪ 2/η, the breathing phenomenon cannot be a direct manifestation of deterministic EoS oscillations.

**R3 — F-4 (asymmetric collapse) could be an artifact of operating in the stochastic EoS suppression zone.**
F-4 observes that correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at the final token. The paper's theory predicts that at the stochastic equilibrium, the oscillatory dynamics (x̂\_t) along the top eigenvector have *suppressed oscillation amplitude* compared to the deterministic case (same E[x̂²\_t] = δ² but broken periodic structure — Section 5.4, Figure 5). If the asymmetric collapse reflects layer-wise oscillatory dynamics analogous to the training-time EoS oscillations, the noise-suppressed equilibrium predicts that the collapse should be *less asymmetric* in models trained with smaller batches (stronger suppression). **Falsifiable:** Compare breathing/collapse amplitude in Qwen-2.5-1.5B (likely SGD-trained with moderate batch) vs. a model trained with full-batch GD. If collapse asymmetry does not vary with training batch size, the mechanism is independent of EoS dynamics and this refutation fails.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS):** This is the primary connection. The paper provides the *stochastic* extension of the self-stabilization framework that Tuci et al.'s Sharpness Dimension (2604.19740) builds on. The closed-form gap ΔS = ηβσ²\_u/(4α) is exactly the kind of "formal correspondence" H-4 asks for — but for training dynamics, not inference-time activations. Any attempt to link breathing amplitude to sharpness must incorporate this stochastic correction. The relevant headline number is cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 — if PC1 corresponds to the top Hessian eigenvector u, this aligns our DoM with the paper's central quantity.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal):** The paper's progressive sharpening → EoS → oscillation cycle is a *training-time* analog of the layer-wise "breathing" we observe at inference. The universality claim of F-1 would be strengthened if the EoS mechanism (which the paper shows is universal across FC-Tanh, FC-ReLU, CNN, ResNet) leaves a geometric fingerprint in inference-time representations.

- **F-10 (PH null on residual streams):** The paper works entirely in spectral/eigenvalue terms — top Hessian eigenvalue, eigenvector projections, gradient noise variance. No topological quantities appear. This is consistent with F-10's finding that topology adds no signal beyond covariance: the *operative* geometric quantities at the edge of stability are spectral (eigenvalue, eigenvector), not topological.

- **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts problem difficulty):** The paper shows that the *amplitude* of oscillations at EoS (E[x̂²\_t] = δ² = 2α/β) is determined by the progressive sharpening rate α and stabilization strength β. If breathing amplitude in inference is related, then per-problem "α" and "β" analogs would predict difficulty. The cov-spectrum probe (AUROC 0.7928, top-20 log-eigvals) is essentially a spectral summary of the local curvature — the same kind of quantity the paper identifies as load-bearing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Top Hessian eigenvalue tracking (progressive sharpening / EoS measurement)** — compute λ\_max(∇²L(θ)) periodically during training, identify the progressive sharpening phase and EoS plateau.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day (requires backward passes through the full model for each checkpoint).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't have Qwen training checkpoints and retraining is out of scope.

- **Projected gradient noise variance σ²\_u = u⊤Σ\_b u** — compute the covariance of mini-batch gradients, project onto the top Hessian eigenvector, measure how this scales with batch size.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day (Hessian eigenvector + gradient covariance computation).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — same reason as above; we're an inference-time project.

- **Batch Sharpness (Andreyev & Beneventano 2025)** — expected directional curvature of the mini-batch Hessian along the mini-batch gradient direction. The quantity that actually saturates at 2/η for SGD.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — training-time metric.

- **Stochastic coupling analysis** — decompose SGD trajectory into constrained reference + oscillatory deviation, prove the deviation tracks predicted dynamics.
  *Replication cost*: conceptual framework, no direct computational cost. The mathematical machinery (PGD reference trajectory, cubic Taylor expansion, coupling theorem) is applicable to any optimization setting.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — theoretical tool for analyzing training dynamics, not applicable to inference-time activation analysis.

- **Mean-field stationarity test** — verify E[(1+ηŷ\_t)²x̂²\_t] ≈ (1+ηȳ)²E[x̂²\_t] (Assumption 7). Tests whether the cross-correlation between the two oscillatory coordinates is small.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (if we had the relevant time series).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires training-time trajectory data we don't have.

## Approaches & framings

- **Self-stabilization as implicit regularization:** The paper frames SGD's batch-size-dependent flatness as a *self-stabilization* mechanism rather than noise-as-regularizer in the usual sense. The restoring force is cubic (third-order loss structure), not quadratic. This is a distinct lens from the standard "noise helps generalization" framing — the mechanism is geometric (nonlinear coupling between curvature and gradient), not statistical (averaging over noise).

  Intersection with F-N/H-N: If inference-time "breathing" has a self-stabilization analog (expansion creates a restoring force toward lower intrinsic dimensionality), this framing would predict that breathing is not merely fluctuation but a *regulated* oscillation with a deterministic equilibrium shape.

- **Projected noise variance as the operative noise statistic:** Not the total gradient noise, but only the component projected onto the top eigenvector (σ²\_u) determines the sharpness gap. This "relevant projection" idea is analogous to our finding that only a *single direction* (DoM ≈ PC1) carries the correctness signal — most of the 1536 dimensions are irrelevant.

  Intersection with F-N/H-N: Strengthens the conceptual case for F-2's single-direction dominance. The paper shows that even in the loss landscape, only one projected quantity (σ²\_u along the top eigenvector) matters for the equilibrium.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Architectures tested: FC-Tanh (2 hidden layers), FC-ReLU, CNN, ResNet, all on CIFAR-10.
- Learning rate η = 0.01 (stability threshold 2/η = 200), varied η ∈ {0.005, 0.008, 0.01, 0.015, 0.02}.
- Batch sizes: {50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 2500} plus full-batch GD.
- 3–5 random seeds per configuration. Equilibrium sharpness = average of λ\_max over final 20 measurements.
- Cross-entropy loss did NOT produce EoS dynamics on CNN+CIFAR-10 (sharpness collapsed to ~15, far below 2/η). MSE loss required for clean EoS behavior. This is a significant caveat for extending results to LLM training (which uses CE loss).
- No code link provided in the paper.

**Relevance:** Extends the Edge of Stability self-stabilization framework from full-batch GD to mini-batch SGD. Derives a closed-form sharpness gap ΔS = ηβσ²\_u/(4α) showing that gradient noise projected onto the top Hessian eigenvector suppresses the equilibrium sharpness below 2/η. Relevant to H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence) because it provides the stochastic correction that any formal breathing-EoS mapping must incorporate. Also conceptually relevant to F-2 (DoM direction) via the role of the top Hessian eigenvector as the geometrically load-bearing direction.

**Key claim we tested:** That the equilibrium loss-landscape sharpness depends on batch size through the projected gradient noise variance, and that the top Hessian eigenvector u is the operative geometric quantity.
