# Triage brief — 2604.23985 — Representational Curvature Modulates Behavioral Uncertainty in Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23985
Triaged: 2026-05-09

**Authors:** Jack King, Evelina Fedorenko, Eghbal A. Hosseini (2026)
**Models:** GPT-2 XL (1.5B, 48 layers), Pythia-2.8B (32 layers)
**Datasets:** LAMBADA (long-context), Universal Dependencies (short-context)
**Core claim:** Contextual curvature — the angle between consecutive displacement vectors in the residual-stream trajectory — correlates with next-token entropy (peak r≈0.15 at middle layers), and this relationship is causally selective: only trajectory-aligned perturbations modulate entropy.

## Refutations

**R1 — Against F-2 (single L19 direction sufficiency at AUROC 0.7731).**
The paper's perturbation hierarchy (§3.3) demonstrates that activation-subspace perturbations — which include PCA/DoM-type directions computed from activations at a single position — "failed to produce a reliable relationship between ΔC and ΔH." Only trajectory-subspace perturbations (PCA of the sample's own inter-position difference vectors) reliably modulated entropy. If this extends from entropy to correctness prediction, it implies that our single-position DoM projection captures only a subset of the uncertainty signal, and the full signal lives in the inter-position trajectory geometry. A curvature-based predictor computed over positions could exceed AUROC 0.7731, refuting the effective sufficiency of single-direction projection.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute per-position curvature at L19 from `twothirds_positions` (7 generation positions × 1536d), fit curvature-based AUROC, compare to 0.7731.

**R2 — Against F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer DoM).**
F-9 declares trajectory features redundant with L19 DoM. However, CoE-60 operates on the *layer* axis (trajectories across depth), while this paper's curvature operates on the *position* axis (trajectories across tokens within a layer). The paper shows position-wise curvature is functionally distinct from single-position activation features. If position-wise curvature at L19 adds predictive power beyond DoM, it refutes F-9 by demonstrating a non-redundant trajectory feature — one that CoE-60 never tested because it aggregated the wrong axis.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute position-wise curvature from `twothirds_positions`, combine with L19 DoM in a 2-feature logistic regression. If combined AUROC > 0.7731 by ≥0.01 and curvature coefficient is significant, F-9's blanket redundancy claim is weakened.

**R3 — Against H-1 (per-position DoM steering).**
H-1 proposes steering along the DoM direction at each position. The paper's perturbation results (Figure 4C) show that steering must be *trajectory-aligned* (aligned with the sample's own inter-position displacement vectors) to affect entropy. The DoM direction is an activation-subspace direction (mean-difference of correct vs. incorrect activations), which falls in the "activation-subspace" category that *failed* to modulate entropy. If the same selectivity holds for correctness, DoM-direction steering would be geometrically misaligned and ineffective — entropy/confidence wouldn't budge, directly undermining H-1's mechanism.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Decompose DoM into trajectory-aligned and trajectory-orthogonal components using per-sample difference vectors; steer along each separately. If trajectory-orthogonal DoM has no effect while trajectory-aligned component does, H-1 needs reformulation.

**R4 — Against F-4 (asymmetric collapse interpretation).**
F-4 interprets the observation that correct trajectories "collapse harder" at the final token as a dimensional phenomenon. This paper offers a competing mechanistic account: what we call "collapse" may be *trajectory straightening* — lower curvature in middle-to-late layers corresponds to higher model confidence. Correct answers would have straighter trajectories (lower curvature) in later layers, which manifests as apparent dimensional collapse when measured by participation ratio. If curvature better separates correct/incorrect than PR at the final token, the collapse interpretation is superseded by the straightening interpretation.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute inter-layer curvature from `final_all_layers`, correlate with correctness, compare separability to final-token DoM AUROC 0.7186.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

**F-1 (dimensional breathing, universal across architectures):** The paper's finding that trajectory curvature decreases from early to middle layers during training parallels the breathing pattern — both describe a layer-dependent geometric transformation of the residual stream. The paper's "temporal straightening hypothesis" could be a mechanistic explanation for why breathing happens: the model learns to straighten trajectories for prediction, and breathing (expansion→contraction) is the dimensional signature of that straightening. Cross-model replication on GPT-2 XL and Pythia-2.8B partially supports F-1's universality claim.

**F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's curvature-entropy correlation peaks at middle layers (layer 23 in GPT-2 XL with 48 layers ≈ 48%, layer ~11 in Pythia-2.8B with 32 layers ≈ 34%). Our L19 in Qwen-2.5-1.5B with 28 layers = 68% depth. The layer-of-peak-curvature-predictiveness is model-specific; our per-layer sweep shows peak at L21 (AUROC 0.7718) with L19 within 0.0013 of peak. The curvature result suggests the predictive signal at these layers has a trajectory-geometric interpretation.

**F-10 (PH null — topology adds no signal beyond covariance):** The paper's curvature is a first-order geometric measure (angles between consecutive displacement vectors), not a topological one. Its success where PH failed (F-10) reinforces that the useful geometry in residual streams is *local* and *first-order* (curvature, direction) rather than *global* and *topological* (homology). This is consistent with F-10 and strengthens the case for focusing on directional/spectral features.

**H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS):** The temporal straightening hypothesis connects to H-4's question about the formal relationship between breathing and other geometric characterizations. Curvature could serve as the bridge — if sharpness dimension tracks similar layer-dependent curvature profiles, the formal correspondence H-4 seeks may run through trajectory straightening.

**F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046):** The paper's curvature is position-dependent. The near-orthogonality of prefill and final DoM could be explained if the trajectory *bends* sharply between prefill-end and final-token — curvature would be high at that transition, and the optimal projection direction would rotate accordingly. This is a testable geometric interpretation of cos=0.046.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Contextual curvature (arccos of consecutive displacement vectors, window-averaged)** — Compute angle between adjacent first-order difference vectors in the residual stream: $c_k = \arccos(v_{k+1} \cdot v_k / \|v_{k+1}\| \|v_k\|)$, averaged over backward window of 3. Applied across positions within a layer or (by analogy) across layers at a fixed position.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU on cached NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly computable on `prefill_all_layers` (29×1536) and `twothirds_positions` (7×1536).

- **Trajectory-subspace perturbation hierarchy** — Five-level perturbation specificity test: full-space → random-subspace → activation-subspace → trajectory-subspace → planar-subspace. Each level restricts perturbation directions; only trajectory-aligned and planar perturbations modulate entropy. Tests causal selectivity of geometric features.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h H100 (requires forward passes with interventions).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not immediately — requires per-position forward-pass interventions we haven't built, but the hierarchy design is informative for interpreting F-2/F-9.

- **Curvature regularization during training** — Auxiliary loss $\mathcal{L}_{curv} = \lambda \frac{1}{n}\sum c_k$ applied at selected middle layers, λ linearly annealed from 0 to 1e-2. Straightens trajectories and modestly reduces entropy without hurting validation loss.
  *Replication cost*: Multiple GPU-days (full pretraining).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't pretrain models.

- **Cross-validated curvature→entropy regression (10-fold OLS, Fisher z-pooling)** — Standard evaluation framework: predict entropy from curvature via 10-fold CV OLS, pool Pearson r via Fisher z-transform, report t-based 95% CI.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to curvature→correctness prediction evaluation.

- **Importance-reweighted perturbation analysis** — When comparing perturbation families with different ΔC distributions, reweight to match marginal |ΔC| distribution. Prevents confounding perturbation magnitude with directionality.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (post-processing).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, if we ever run perturbation experiments.

## Approaches & framings

- **Temporal straightening hypothesis:** LLMs progressively straighten representational trajectories to facilitate prediction via linear extrapolation. Curvature is inversely related to predictability. Intersects F-1 (breathing may be the dimensional signature of straightening) and F-4 (collapse may be straightening).

- **Perturbation selectivity as causal test:** Not all directions in activation space are created equal — interventions must respect the trajectory geometry to modulate behavior. Intersects H-1 (steering needs trajectory alignment, not just activation-space alignment).

- **Curvature as a first-order geometric feature distinct from topology:** Explicitly positions curvature (local, differential) against higher-order geometric invariants. Reinforces F-10's finding that topology (global, algebraic) doesn't add signal.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Curvature window of 3 tokens is sufficient — larger windows add no predictive power (Figure A5).
- Perturbation magnitude scaled to local trajectory: |δ| = 0.2|v_k|, making effects proportional across tokens and layers.
- Subspace dimensionality m=2 for trajectory-subspace perturbations is sufficient (robust at m∈{2,5,10}).
- Fisher z-transform pooling for cross-fold correlation estimates avoids bias from averaging raw r values.
- Curvature regularization applied at layers 7 and 8 of 12-layer GPT-2 Small (~58–67% depth), consistent with the middle-layer predictive peak.
