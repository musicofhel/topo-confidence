# Triage brief — 2605.05115 — Manifold Steering Reveals the Shared Geometry of Neural Network Representation and Behavior

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05115
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) by demonstrating that linear steering is geometrically inappropriate.** The paper shows that linear (diff-in-means) steering—exactly the protocol H-1 proposes—cuts through off-manifold regions of activation space, producing "unnatural" intermediate distributions with teleportation artifacts and incoherent outputs. Manifold steering outperforms linear steering by 2.8× on the cumulative Bhattacharyya energy metric across all tasks. If the same manifold-vs-linear gap holds for MATH-500 prefill DoM steering, then H-1's planned linear DoM steering may be doomed to produce fluency collapse rather than accuracy gain—not because the direction is wrong, but because the geometry is. **Falsifiable experiment:** run H-1's linear DoM steering on MATH-500 and simultaneously fit a manifold to L19 activations and steer along it; compare accuracy and fluency metrics of both.

2. **Challenges F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731 as a linear-probe ceiling) by implying the linear probing assumption misses manifold curvature.** The paper demonstrates that the correct geometry of activation space is curved, not flat—distances along fitted activation manifolds correlate with behavior-space distances at r=0.99, while linear (Euclidean) distances correlate much worse (r=0.35–0.70 depending on task). If L19 prefill activations for MATH-500 live on a curved manifold, then a linear probe (logistic regression on DoM projection) is fitting a hyperplane through a curved space. The 0.7731 AUROC may be a linearization artifact, with a manifold-aware probe (e.g., geodesic distance to correct-class centroid on a fitted manifold) potentially lifting or lowering the AUROC. **Falsifiable experiment:** fit a cubic spline or thin-plate spline manifold to the 500 L19 prefill centroids (correct vs. incorrect) in PCA-64 space; compute geodesic distances; compare AUROC of geodesic-distance classifier vs. linear DoM.

3. **Challenges F-10 (PH null on residual streams) by suggesting PH is the wrong geometric tool—manifold fitting via splines detects curvature that PH summary statistics miss.** The paper successfully recovers rich curved geometry (circles, helices, sheets) in activation spaces using cubic splines and thin-plate splines, without any topological homology. F-10 concluded that PH statistics on residual streams are indistinguishable from Gaussian null, but the paper implies the problem isn't absence of geometry—it's that PH summary statistics are insensitive to the specific type of curved manifold structure present. Spline-fitted manifold curvature could be a fundamentally different geometric signal. **Falsifiable experiment:** fit spline manifolds to L19 prefill activations (correct/incorrect subsets separately); measure curvature and compare against Gaussian-null controls; if curvature is significantly non-Gaussian, F-10's verdict is incomplete rather than wrong.

4. **Challenges F-3 (prefill/final-token DoM orthogonality) by predicting that the two directions may simply be tangent vectors to different locations on the *same* activation manifold.** The paper shows that a single manifold M_h in activation space can have varying tangent directions depending on location (e.g., the weekday circle has tangent vectors pointing in different directions at each day). cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 could be consistent with two points on a curved manifold whose tangent spaces are approximately orthogonal—not two independent computational circuits as currently interpreted. **Falsifiable experiment:** fit manifold to L19 activations across token positions; check whether prefill and final-token centroids lie on the same manifold with orthogonal tangent planes, or on genuinely separate manifolds.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's manifold-fitting methodology provides a direct alternative to linear probing. If the L19 activation space has significant curvature, the linear DoM AUROC of 0.7731 may be an underestimate (or overestimate) of the true manifold-aware separability. The 2-feat (PC1, PC9) AUROC of 0.7856 and the cov-spectrum AUROC of 0.7928 already suggest the linear probe doesn't capture everything—manifold geodesic distances could provide a complementary geometric handle.

- **F-10 (PH null):** The paper's success at finding curved geometry via spline fitting directly contrasts with F-10's failure to find non-Gaussian structure via PH. This is consistent with H-35 (F-10's PH-null result is summary-statistic-bound, not topology-bound)—the paper provides a concrete alternative geometric framework that could resolve the apparent contradiction.

- **H-1 (DoM steering):** The paper's core finding is that linear steering (the protocol H-1 proposes) is fundamentally inferior to manifold-aware steering. This directly informs how H-1 should be designed: fitting a manifold first, then steering along geodesics.

- **H-26 (non-linear probe above linear DoM AUROC):** The paper motivates H-26 from a different angle—if activation geometry is curved, a non-linear probe is not just more expressive but geometrically correct.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse at final token):** The asymmetric collapse finding maps onto the paper's energy-based view: correct trajectories may follow low-energy geodesics on the activation manifold (collapse = convergence to manifold attractor), while incorrect trajectories scatter into high-energy off-manifold regions.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with L19 DoM):** If both CoE-60 trajectory features and single-layer DoM are linear projections of the same underlying manifold geometry, their redundancy is explained: they're both linearizing the same curved structure.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Activation manifold fitting via cubic splines** — Reduce activations to 64-dim PCA subspace, compute concept centroids, fit periodic/natural cubic splines through centroids in intrinsic coordinates. Produces a smooth parameterization of the manifold.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (we have cached NPZs, just need PCA + spline fit).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 prefill activations (500 × 1536).

- **Thin-plate spline (TPS) manifold fitting** — Multi-dimensional generalization of cubic splines for fitting 2D+ manifold surfaces to activation centroids.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — could fit 2D manifold surface to L19 activations if we have two intrinsic coordinates (e.g., correctness + difficulty).

- **Hellinger-space behavior manifold fitting** — Map output distributions to √p (Hellinger coordinates), fit splines on the unit sphere using log-map/exp-map to stay on the simplex.
  *Replication cost*: 1h H100 (needs output distributions from forward passes, which we don't have cached).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no for now — requires fresh forward passes to extract output distributions.

- **Geodesic distance computation on fitted manifolds** — Discretize manifold path into 150 sub-intervals, accumulate ambient Euclidean distance along path. Provides an alternative distance metric to Euclidean.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (once manifold is fitted).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — trivial once spline is fitted.

- **Manifold steering (geometry-aware intervention)** — Interpolate in intrinsic (manifold) coordinates rather than ambient (Euclidean) space; map back through spline parameterization for actual activation replacement.
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 (requires intervention forward passes).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if H-1 is being run — should be the primary comparison.

- **Pullback optimization (behavior → activations)** — Fix target behavioral trajectory on M_y, optimize L-BFGS for activation path whose interventions reproduce that trajectory. Parameterized as natural cubic spline through 10 control vectors in 32 PCA dimensions.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires output distribution access and is computationally expensive.

- **Cumulative Bhattacharyya energy metric** — Measures "naturalness" of an intervention trajectory by summing Bhattacharyya distances from each waypoint's output distribution to nearest point on behavior manifold.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (given output distributions).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no without cached output distributions.

- **Scaled isometry test** — Pearson correlation between pairwise geodesic distance matrices on M_h and M_y. Tests whether two manifolds encode the same underlying conceptual structure.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU (given both manifolds fitted).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: partial — can test whether activation manifold distances correlate with correctness labels even without behavior manifold.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Finding the right geometry, not the right direction"** — Reframes steering from a direction-finding problem (LRH) to a geometry-finding problem. Directly challenges the DoM framing (F-2, H-1) by arguing that the direction is only the linearization of the correct geodesic. Intersects F-2 by suggesting the 0.7731 AUROC is a first-order (linear) approximation of a richer geometric signal.

- **Energy-based view of naturalness** — Output distributions on unintervened passes trace low-energy regions; steering is natural to the extent it stays in those regions. Provides a principled way to evaluate whether our DoM steering (H-1) produces natural vs. degraded outputs—measure energy along the steering trajectory.

- **Activation-behavior isometry** — The claim that M_h and M_y are scaled isometries of a shared conceptual geometry. If true for math reasoning (not just weekdays/letters), this would mean the L19 activation geometry directly reflects the structure of correct-vs-incorrect answer distributions—potentially explaining why a single direction (DoM) works so well.

- **Intrinsic coordinates as units of causal analysis** — Proposes manifold intrinsic coordinates as the right primitive for mechanistic interpretability, not individual neurons or directions. Relevant to H-13 (head-level attribution) and H-66 (path-patching faithfulness)—the causal unit may be manifold coordinates, not head outputs.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- PCA to 64 dimensions before manifold fitting; intervene only in top-64 PCA subspace, preserve orthogonal complement.
- Pullback optimization restricted to top-32 PCA dimensions (half the manifold subspace).
- 50 waypoints for steering paths; 20 waypoints for pullback.
- L-BFGS with strong-Wolfe line search for pullback optimization; 50 outer steps, 5 inner iterations.
- Hellinger space = √p mapping; behavior manifold fitted via log-map/exp-map on unit sphere.
- Unsupervised intrinsic coordinate for cyclic tasks: θ = atan2(PC2, PC1).
- Model: Llama 3.1 8B, layer 28, bfloat16. All interventions at last-token residual stream.
- Code: https://github.com/goodfire-ai/causalab/tree/manifold_steering
