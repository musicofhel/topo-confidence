# Triage brief — 2605.12466 — Solve the Loop: Attractor Models for Language and Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12466
Triaged: 2026-05-17

## Refutations

**R1: Against F-4 (asymmetric collapse) — equilibrium internalization predicts the OPPOSITE asymmetry.**

The paper's central empirical finding is "equilibrium internalization": after training, the backbone proposal already lies near the fixed point, so well-learned patterns require little refinement. Applied to our setup: if correct MATH-500 answers correspond to patterns the model has better internalized, their L19 representations should already be near the "attractor" (i.e., near the final-token representation), implying *less* collapse from prefill to final token for correct answers, not more. F-4 observes the opposite — correct trajectories show MORE dimensional collapse at the final token. The refutation fires if: measuring the per-sample residual norm ‖final_state - T(final_state)‖ (using one-layer approximation) shows correct samples are closer to equilibrium at L19 (backbone end) than incorrect, which would mean F-4's "harder collapse" is a measurement artifact of a convergence phenomenon rather than a correctness-discriminative geometric structure.

*Falsifiable by:* Compute inter-layer residual norms ‖h_{L20} - h_{L19}‖ / ‖h_{L19}‖ for each sample from cached `prefill_all_layers`. If correct samples have lower residual norms (closer to fixed point) but still show higher collapse in the final token, F-4 survives with a nuance. If residual-norm predicts correctness at AUROC ≥ 0.77, then F-4's "collapse" may be reframed as "proximity to attractor."

**R2: Against F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM) — attractor dynamics predict trajectory features carry unique convergence information.**

Under the attractor model framework, what matters is the *path to the fixed point*, not just the representation at one layer. CoE-60 features explicitly capture the multi-layer trajectory (per-layer cosines, norms, etc.). If standard transformers implicitly solve for fixed points via iterated layer application, then CoE features encoding the convergence trajectory should carry information about *how many effective iterations* were needed — which is diagnostic of problem difficulty and correctness. F-9's finding that CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer DoM would mean the convergence path has no additional information beyond the single-point snapshot, directly contradicting the attractor model's emphasis on convergence dynamics as the mechanism.

*Falsifiable by:* Extract "convergence rate" features from cached all-layer activations (e.g., ratio of ‖h_{L+1} - h_L‖ at L19 vs L10), and test whether these add predictive power beyond the L19 DoM direction. If they do, F-9 needs revision.

**R3: Against F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality, cos=0.046) — tied embedding space forces same attractor for both positions.**

The attractor model operates entirely in a tied embedding space — all iterates (backbone proposal AND equilibrium) live in the same output representation space. If Qwen-2.5-1.5B implicitly implements attractor-like dynamics, then both prefill-end and final-token states should be converging toward the *same* fixed-point attractor in embedding space, which predicts the dominant correctness-discriminative direction should be *shared* between positions (high cosine). The observed near-zero cosine (0.046) between prefill DoM and final-token DoM would require that prefill and final token are using *different* attractors — which breaks the weight-sharing premise that makes the attractor model framework applicable to standard transformers.

*Falsifiable by:* For each sample, compute the "refinement vector" (final_all_layers[L+1] - final_all_layers[L]) averaged over late layers for both prefill and final positions. If these refinement vectors are aligned (cos > 0.3) but the DoMs are not, it suggests the attractor target differs by position, weakening the attractor interpretation of F-3.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing):** The paper's attractor dynamics provide a mechanistic explanation for breathing — expansion during backbone proposal (layers 0–18) corresponds to exploring the representation space, while collapse (layers 19+) corresponds to convergence toward the fixed point. The paper's convergence criterion ‖A(ŷ)‖/‖ŷ‖ < ε directly maps to the breathing collapse magnitude.

- **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM, AUROC 0.7731):** Under the attractor model, L19 (at 2/3 depth of 28 layers) is where the "backbone" ends and "attractor refinement" begins conceptually. The DoM direction at L19 may encode how far the backbone proposal is from equilibrium — a "distance to attractor" signal. Papers reports backbone proposal is near equilibrium for well-learned patterns; our 0.7731 AUROC might measure exactly this.

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse):** Direct tension — see R1 above. The paper predicts less movement for well-internalized (correct) patterns, but F-4 shows more collapse for correct.

- **F-10 (PH null on residual streams):** Attractor dynamics predict that trained representations cluster near fixed points in a low-dimensional manifold. If the attractor basin is nearly linear (spectral radius of Jacobian < 1 uniformly), representations would look Gaussian to PH — consistent with F-10's null.

- **H-4 (breathing ↔ sharpness dimension):** The spectral radius of the Jacobian J_ŷ at the fixed point controls convergence speed in the attractor model. This spectral radius is exactly an "effective sharpness" measure, potentially providing the formal correspondence H-4 seeks.

- **H-6 (CoE-60 vs single-layer at 1024-tok):** R2 above directly addresses this — the attractor framework predicts CoE should outperform single-layer if convergence dynamics matter.

  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU (need to estimate Jacobian from finite differences on cached data).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — primarily useful if we build a probe that differentiates through equilibrium.

  *Replication cost*: N/A (requires training run, not applicable to cached inference data).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we work with frozen inference caches.

## Approaches & framings

- **Recurrence-as-fixed-point:** Reframes depth in standard transformers as iterative convergence toward a fixed point. Intersects F-1 (breathing) by providing a dynamical-systems explanation for why representations expand then collapse — they're converging to an attractor.

- **Backbone/attractor decomposition of depth:** Splits a transformer into "proposal" (early layers, generative) and "refinement" (late layers, contractive). Intersects F-2 by explaining why L19 (2/3 depth) is special — it's approximately the boundary between proposal and refinement phases.

- **Self-distillation through equilibrium targets:** The backbone learns to match the attractor's output without explicit distillation loss. Provides a theoretical lens for H-5 (problem familiarity): well-internalized problems have shorter "distance to equilibrium" at the backbone output.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Anderson acceleration window size and convergence tolerance ε are key hyperparameters (paper uses defaults from DEQ literature).
- One-step IFT approximation (u ≈ v) loses only 0.14 PPL vs full solve but saves 4.8× memory — establishes that full Jacobian computation is rarely needed.
- Additive injection (ŷ_{t+1} = T(ŷ_t + ŷ_0)) outperforms concatenation for preventing fixed-point collapse.
- Code at https://github.com/jacobfa/Attractor (nanochat training recipe, PyTorch).
- Backbone is standard causal transformer; attractor module is 1-2 smaller transformer blocks with weight tying.
