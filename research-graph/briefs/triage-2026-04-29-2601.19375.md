# Triage brief — 2601.19375 — Selective Steering: Norm-Preserving Control Through Discriminative Layer Selection

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19375
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Quy-Anh Dang, Chris Ngo (VNU University of Science / Knovel Engineering Lab). Submitted 2026-01-27.
Code: https://github.com/knoveleng/steering

## Refutations

1. **F-2 ("Prefill L19 DoM is *the* canonical correctness predictor") is at risk of being a coarse pick from a discriminative-layer continuum.**
   - Mechanism: SS introduces the criterion `μ̃pos(k) · μ̃neg(k) < 0` (opposite-signed scalar projections) and shows that for harmful/harmless on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct it picks a *contiguous middle-to-late band* of layers, not a singleton. We selected L19 by AUROC ranking on a single contrast (correct vs incorrect MATH-500 prefill); we never tested whether L19 is the *only* layer that satisfies the opposite-sign criterion or merely the AUROC argmax inside a wider band.
   - Falsifiable test: compute `μ̃correct(k) · μ̃incorrect(k)` for all 28 layers of Qwen2.5-1.5B from cached P11 prefill NPZs. If the discriminative set L_disc is e.g. {L13…L24}, then F-2's "L19 is special" framing collapses to "L19 is the AUROC peak inside L_disc." If L_disc = {L19} (or {L18, L19, L20}) then F-2 survives as written.

2. **F-3 ("prefill DoM ⊥ final-token DoM, cos = 0.046") is in tension with SS's max-inter-layer-cosine global-direction assumption.**
   - Mechanism: SS's feature-direction selection (Eq 8) chooses the layer-`k` candidate `d^(k) = μ_pos − μ_neg` whose *average cosine similarity to all other layers' candidates is maximised*. For harmful/harmless this works (they ship a single global direction). If our F-3 finding (near-orthogonality between prefill and final-token DoM directions on the same correctness contrast) were a generic geometric phenomenon, SS's global-direction assumption should fail symmetrically — but they observe layer-wise consistency. The contradiction implies F-3 is task- or position-conditional (prefill-position vs final-token-position) rather than a universal residual-stream property.
   - Falsifiable test: replicate Eq 8 on our cached Qwen2.5-1.5B correctness contrast across all 28 layers and prefill-vs-final-token DoMs separately. If the inter-layer cosine matrix shows a dense block within prefill-position layers (and within final-token-position layers) but near-zero coupling across the two position groups, then F-3's orthogonality is a *position* effect, not a generic DoM-rotation phenomenon, and our framing for H-1 needs to acknowledge that.

3. **H-1 ("per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy") has a plausible *implementation-induced* refutation that we have not protected against.**
   - Mechanism: SS proves (Proposition 1) that the standard Angular-Steering implementation `h - proj_P(h) + ‖proj_P(h)‖ · [b1 b2] R_θ [1 0]^T` *violates norm preservation* and causes generation collapse on Qwen2.5-1.5B specifically (their Table 1: SAS gets 13.46% HarmBench ASR + perplexity blow-up; SS norm-preserving variant gets 74.04%). Our H-1 plan presumed naive activation-addition at L19. If we run that on Qwen2.5-1.5B and observe MATH-500 accuracy drop to ~chance with garbled output, we will *misread* this as "DoM steering doesn't move correctness" when the true cause is the same norm violation SS documents.
   - Falsifiable test: implement both the SS norm-preserving rotation `R^P_θ = I − (b1 b1^T + b2 b2^T) + [b1 b2] R_θ [b1 b2]^T` and the naive Angular-Steering implementation on Qwen2.5-1.5B at L19. If only the norm-preserving variant produces controllable MATH-500 deltas (no perplexity blow-up), we have evidence H-1 is true *and* that our prior H-1 plan would have falsely refuted it.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, OOF 5-fold). SS gives a *principled* layer-selection criterion (`μ̃pos·μ̃neg < 0`) that we never applied. Their Figure 2b on Qwen2.5-7B shows the criterion is satisfied by a band of middle-late layers — exactly where L19 sits in Qwen2.5-1.5B's 28-layer stack (~68% depth). Cross-check: does L19 sit inside the discriminative band on the correct/incorrect contrast?
- **F-3** (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046). SS's Eq 8 picks one global direction via max inter-layer cosine — a procedure that *only works* if cross-layer DoMs are aligned. F-3's near-zero cosine across positions (prefill vs final) is consistent with their finding only if interpreted as a position effect, not a layer effect.
- **H-1** (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy). SS provides exactly the implementation we need: norm-preserving rotation (Prop 2) + discriminative-layer subset. Adopting this recipe before running H-1 protects us from collapse-induced false negatives.
- **H-13** (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits). SS's per-layer μ̃pos·μ̃neg analysis is layer-resolved, not head-resolved, so it doesn't directly attack H-13 — but the *same* criterion can be specialised to per-head outputs (project μ̃pos − μ̃neg onto per-head subspaces and look for sign disagreement).

Numerical anchors used: AUROC 0.7731 (F-2), cos 0.046 (F-3), and the Qwen2.5-1.5B 28-layer stack (Pathway 11 H100 cache, the model SS specifically reports as the "small-model collapse" case at SAS 13.46% ASR vs SS 74.04% ASR).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Norm-preserving rotation matrix** — `R^P_θ = I − (b1 b1^T + b2 b2^T) + [b1 b2] R_θ [b1 b2]^T`, where `R_θ` is the standard 2D rotation matrix and `{b1, b2}` is an orthonormal basis spanning the steering plane. Proposition 2 establishes ‖R^P_θ h‖ = ‖h‖ exactly. Drop-in replacement for the Vu/Nguyen Angular-Steering implementation, which they prove violates norm preservation.
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU (numpy / torch one-liner once `b1`, `b2` are constructed).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's a strict generalisation of activation addition (covers θ = 0 / 90 / 180 cases) and the H-1 implementation we should adopt.

- **Discriminative-layer selection criterion** — `L_disc = { k : μ̃pos(k) · μ̃neg(k) < 0 }`, where `μ̃class(k) = μ_class(k) · d̂_feat` is the scalar projection of the class mean onto the global feature direction. Steering is applied only at `k ∈ L_disc`.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached prefill NPZs (Qwen2.5-1.5B has 28 layers × 2 means × 1 dot product).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly answers "is L19 unique or one of many?"

- **Global feature direction via max-inter-layer cosine consistency** (Eq 8) — `d̂_feat = argmax_{d^(k)} (1/L) Σ_j cos(d^(k), d^(j))`. Picks the layer-k DoM most consistently aligned with all other layers' DoMs.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached prefill NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives an alternative to "AUROC argmax" for picking the canonical layer; useful as a cross-check.

- **Steering-plane construction via DoM + orthogonalised first PC** — `b1 = d̂_feat`, `b2 = orthogonalise(PC1({d^(k)}))`. The 2D steering plane is anchored by the global feature direction plus the dominant residual variance of per-layer candidates.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed if we adopt the norm-preserving rotation pipeline.

- **Coherence metrics suite** — perplexity (threshold 2.0), 4-gram repetition diversity, Unicode-script language consistency, gzip compression ratio. Used to detect generation collapse during steering.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU per generation pass.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for any future H-1 sweep — gives us collapse detection independent of MATH-500 accuracy.

- **tinyBenchmarks capability-retention sweep** — zero-shot accuracy on five 100-sample benchmarks (tinyAI2_arc, tinyGSM8K, tinyMMLU, tinyTruthfulQA, tinyWinogrande) at the steering setting that maximises ASR.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min H100 per (model × angle).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if we ever publish a steering result — guards against "we moved MATH-500 by trashing capabilities."

## Approaches & framings

- **Layer heterogeneity as a first-class steering object.** Treats per-layer steering eligibility as the *primary* knob, not an afterthought. Intersects F-2's L19-uniqueness story by reframing it as "L19 is one element of L_disc, picked by AUROC" rather than "L19 is geometrically privileged."
- **Continuous control via rotation angle θ ∈ [0°, 360°)** rather than scalar coefficient α. Generalises ActAdd (small θ) and DirAbl (θ = 90°) into one parameter family. Intersects H-1: per-position steering becomes a *2D* sweep (layer set × angle) rather than a 1D sweep (coefficient).
- **Distribution-shift framing of failure modes.** SS attributes generation collapse on small models to *norm violation* causing post-norm-layer distribution shift, not to feature-extraction error. Suggests the right diagnostic for any failed H-1 run is "did norms move?" rather than "is the DoM wrong?"

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Hardware: single A40 (48 GB). Greedy decoding, temperature = 0. So replication does NOT need H100.
- Models tested: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2B/9B-it. Direct overlap with our Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B targets.

- PPL collapse threshold = 2.0× baseline (red-star markers in Figure 3). Concrete operational definition we can adopt for H-1 diagnostics.
- Code: github.com/knoveleng/steering. CC BY 4.0, public.
- They claim O(L_disc · d_model) per-token cost vs O(L · d_model) for uniform-layer steering. For Qwen2.5-1.5B with d_model = 1536 and L = 28, if L_disc = 8, that's a 3.5× speedup at inference — meaningful for cost-aware H-1 sweeps.
- Norm-preserving rotation derivation (Appendix B.2) decomposes `R^P_θ` into orthogonal projection onto the complement space `Q` and rotation within plane `P`. Re-derivable in <30 min if we want a self-contained proof in our docs.

## 2601.19375 — Selective Steering: Norm-Preserving Control Through Discriminative Layer Selection (Dang & Ngo, 2026)

**Relevance:** Directly bears on F-2 (single-layer DoM), F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality), and H-1 (per-position DoM steering). Two methodological contributions matter: (1) a closed-form norm-preserving rotation `R^P_θ = I − (b1 b1^T + b2 b2^T) + [b1 b2] R_θ [b1 b2]^T` that fixes a documented norm-violation bug in Vu & Nguyen 2025's Angular Steering; (2) a discriminative-layer criterion `μ̃pos·μ̃neg < 0` that picks a *band* of steerable layers rather than a singleton. They demonstrate on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct — exactly our model — that naive Angular Steering causes generation collapse (HarmBench ASR 13.46%) while their norm-preserving variant achieves 74.04%. Their Figure 2 documents non-uniform per-layer norm growth and progressive opposite-sign discriminability emergence in middle-late layers on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, also our model.

**Key claim we tested:** Discriminative layers (where class means project with opposite signs onto the global feature direction) are middle-to-late, and confined to this band; uniform-layer steering causes collapse on small models.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — three FutureExperiments queued (P11-FE73, FE74, FE75) and three hypotheses (H-73, H-74, H-75) translate the methodology to our correctness contrast. The H100 test (FE74) replaces our naive activation-addition H-1 plan with their norm-preserving rotation primitive. We have not yet run any of the three.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73 (L_disc cross-check), P11-FE74 (norm-preserving rotation at L19, supersedes naive H-1 plan), P11-FE75 (prefill/final inter-layer cosine matrix), H-1 (in-flight, blocked by FE74), H-73, H-74, H-75.
