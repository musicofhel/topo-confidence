# Triage brief — 2401.13558 — Task structure and nonlinearity jointly determine learned representational geometry

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13558
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 frames as a "correctness predictor") may be re-explained as input-geometry preservation.**
   - *Mechanism*: Paper shows ReLU-family activations (modern transformer FFNs use SiLU/GELU/SwiGLU — ReLU-family in symmetry terms) produce hidden representations that **preserve input geometry**, not label geometry, with neurons specializing for input-space regions. Under this lens, the prefill L19 representation should encode problem identity / surface form / topic / difficulty (input geometry), and the AUROC=0.7731 against correctness is driven by the **input↔correctness correlation** of the dataset rather than an abstract "correctness direction." The paper's Section 5 explicitly shows that as input-output alignment _c_ drops, target alignment in the hidden layer also drops sharply for ReLU networks — i.e., the network never builds a clean label axis.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Build a problem-feature regressor on MATH-500 (token length, formula count, topic, has-fraction, has-system-of-equations). Residualize prefill L19 DoM scores against these features. Predict AUROC drops near chance after residualization → DoM is detecting input geometry, not correctness. If AUROC survives, F-2's framing is intact. (Cached: `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` already has per-problem DoM scores.)

2. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 framed as "orthogonal circuits") may be the generic ReLU-network behavior, not evidence of asymmetry.**
   - *Mechanism*: The paper documents that ReLU networks build **many parallel coding directions** for distinct latent variables (high parallelism score). In such representations, any two probes for semantically different binary attributes will be approximately orthogonal — that is the expected behavior, not a special finding. F-3 currently treats 0.046 as low-and-meaningful, but the paper predicts that cos(probe_a, probe_b) ≈ 0 is generic across **arbitrary** label pairs in ReLU-family transformers.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Compute pairwise cos for DoM directions trained on N orthogonal labels at L19 (correctness, problem-topic-one-hot, length-quartile, has-equation, presence-of-CoT-pivot-token). If all pairwise cos values cluster near 0.05, F-3's cos=0.046 is null-distribution and the "asymmetric circuit" interpretation collapses. If cos(prefill, final) is uniquely low while same-label probes from different layers stay high, F-3 stands.

3. **F-4 (asymmetric collapse — correct trajectories collapse harder at final token) may be a Tanh-like vestige that should not be present in a fully ReLU-like network.**
   - *Mechanism*: The paper roots neural collapse (Papyan et al. 2020) in symmetric, two-sided-saturating nonlinearities (Tanh, or ReLU+positive-saturation in their Fig. 6a). Pure ReLU/GELU/SiLU networks resist neural collapse and instead spread neurons across input regions. If F-4 is universal across modern LLMs, it suggests there's a Tanh-like saturation operating somewhere in the residual stream (RMSNorm? attention softmax saturation?) that drives a label-aligned axis. If we cannot identify that mechanism, F-4 is a bug, not a feature.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Run the cached final-token PR comparison (correct vs incorrect, P11) **separately for the post-RMSNorm and pre-RMSNorm streams**. Predict: the asymmetric-collapse signal lives mostly post-norm (where saturation enters) and is weaker on the raw residual. If both streams show identical asymmetry, the F-4 mechanism is independent of saturation and the paper's framing fails. If post-norm dominates, F-4 needs a saturation-attribution paragraph.

4. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) may be input-cluster-driven, not correctness-related.**
   - *Mechanism*: The paper's analysis of ReLU networks (Section 4.1) shows neurons specialize for **input-space subregions**. A "D-bucket" — problems where K=1 right but K=8 wrong — could be a coherent input-space cluster (problems with a particular surface pattern that the model gets right by chance once and wrong systematically when sampled). The "collective geometric signature" is then just the cluster centroid in input space, with no causal relationship to correctness machinery.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Train a topic/style classifier (TF-IDF or sentence-embedding) to discriminate D-bucket from non-D-bucket on MATH-500 problem **statements alone** (no model activations). If F1 ≈ the F1 of the L19-DoM-based D-bucket detector, the geometric signature is text-style, not residual-stream-specific. If activation-based detection beats text-based by a large margin, F-7's "collective" claim survives.

5. **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS) competes with the paper's task-structure × nonlinearity mechanism.**
   - *Mechanism*: H-4 attributes breathing to optimizer-edge sharpness dynamics. The paper offers a different story: representational dimensionality during training is driven by the symmetry of the activation function and the input/output kernel alignment of the task — purely from gradient flow, no EoS needed. These are competing explanations, not complementary.
   - *Falsifiable test*: Differential intervention. Patch a Tanh-like saturation onto Qwen-2.5-1.5B's MLP up-projection (custom hook clamping pre-activation magnitude). Predict: under task-structure×nonlinearity theory, breathing amplitude should change qualitatively (Tanh-like → more target-aligned → smaller amplitude, less PR variation). Under EoS theory, breathing should be unchanged (it's a sharpness/loss-landscape property, not an activation property). A clean differential separates the two hypotheses.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1** (dimensional breathing universal across architectures): the paper's Section 7 shows multi-layer networks evolve target/input alignment differently per layer, and Section 8 confirms similar effects in CNNs (CIFAR-10, STL-10) — suggests breathing-style dimensionality dynamics could be a generic feature of representation-learning networks. But the paper's mechanism (input/target alignment evolution) competes with the "breathing as cognitive workspace" framing in F-1's motivation.

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731): see Refutation #1 — paper predicts the prefill direction is encoding input geometry, with AUROC against correctness inheriting input↔correctness correlation. This is a serious reframe-or-refute candidate.

- **F-3** (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046): see Refutation #2 — paper predicts orthogonality is generic in ReLU-family nets.

- **F-4** (asymmetric collapse): see Refutation #3 — paper roots collapse in Tanh-like saturation; modern transformers should resist it. The fact that we observe it suggests RMSNorm/softmax saturation is mediating, which would be a novel mechanism finding.

- **F-7** (D-bucket collective signature): see Refutation #4 — paper's input-region-specialization story offers a non-correctness explanation for D-bucket coherence.

- **F-10** (PH at Gaussian null on trained residuals): consistent with the paper. Their finding is that representational structure shows up in **kernel alignment / parallelism / CCGP** (linear, dimensional metrics) — not in homological structure. PH-at-Gaussian is unsurprising; the right tools are kernel-space, not topology-space. **This actually supports F-10's pivot away from PH** rather than refutes it.

- **H-4** (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension/EoS): see Refutation #5 — paper provides a competing theoretical mechanism.

- **H-9** (breathing amplitude predicts difficulty): paper's Section 5.1 shows that as input-output alignment _c_ varies, hidden-layer geometry varies smoothly — could ground "difficulty" as "input-output alignment of the problem." Supportive but reframes the mechanism.

- **H-12** (semantic-entropy probes / label-free DoM): paper's PS and CCGP are label-free disentanglement metrics that could substitute for supervised DoM probes. Useful tool transfer.

- **H-20** (cross-model kernel alignment fluctuates during inference): paper's CKA-based analysis is a direct precedent for measuring kernel alignment between paired models. Strong methodological connection.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA / target alignment / input alignment)** — `C(K_Z, K_Y) = Tr(K_Z K_Y) / sqrt(Tr(K_Z K_Z) Tr(K_Y K_Y))`. Quantifies how much hidden representations track labels vs inputs.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached prefill/final-token NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — CKA between residual stream at each layer and (a) one-hot correctness, (b) problem-topic-one-hot, (c) input-token-mean-pool gives a direct quantitative test of "is the prefill direction label-aligned or input-aligned?"

- **Parallelism Score (PS)** — for binary latent variables y_i, condition on the other k−1 factors, take the mean-difference vector for y_i = ±1, average pairwise cosine similarity across conditioning sets. Measures disentanglement.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU; needs MATH-500 problems annotated with ≥2 binary labels (correctness + topic, e.g.).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — PS for correctness conditioned on topic would tell us whether "correctness direction" is invariant across problem types or topic-specific.

- **Cross-Condition Generalization Performance (CCGP)** — train a linear decoder on a restricted slice of the input-label assignments, test on held-out slices. Measures axis abstraction.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached L19 activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — CCGP for prefill DoM trained on topic A → tested on topic B is a direct test of whether the DoM is an abstract correctness axis or a topic-specific signal. **High-priority** because it directly addresses Refutation #1.

- **Activation function perturbation (Section 9)** — replace ReLU with min(max(x,0), 1) (two-sided saturation) or shifted ReLU (linear at origin). Isolates which property of the nonlinearity matters.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 for a fine-tune-like experiment, or could be done as a forward-pass-only intervention via custom hook for diagnostic purposes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — high cost, but offers the cleanest differential test for Refutation #5 / H-4. Worth a small-scale prototype on a 7B intervention.

- **Decoding accuracy on trained vs. untrained dichotomies** — compare SVM accuracy for the trained binary label vs. random alternative label assignments to the same input clusters. Measures input-information preservation.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached L19 features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — substitute "trained dichotomy" = correctness, "untrained dichotomies" = random binary partitions of MATH-500 problems. If untrained-dichotomy accuracy at L19 is high, the network is preserving rich input information (ReLU regime). Direct evidence for Refutation #1.

- **Gradient flow projection onto inter-class / intra-class axes** — visualize learning dynamics in 2D. Specific to single-hidden-layer toy nets, not directly portable to transformers.
  *Replication cost*: not applicable.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — the analysis assumes frozen output weights and discrete neuron groups; transformer attention/MLP coupling makes the axes ill-defined.

## Approaches & framings

- **Input geometry vs target geometry as competing pressures.** Hidden representations live on a continuum: ReLU-family preserves input geometry, Tanh-family collapses to target. Modern LLMs are ReLU-family — strong prediction is that residual-stream geometry encodes input structure with correctness as a downstream readout, not as a primary axis. This reframes F-2 and F-3.

- **Activation function symmetry as a representational hyperparameter.** Symmetric saturating nonlinearities (Tanh, two-sided ReLU) drive label collapse; asymmetric ones (ReLU/SiLU/GELU) drive input preservation. Adds a missing variable to the breathing-mechanism conversation in H-4.

- **Disentanglement vs binding tradeoff** (Section 10). Disentangled (Tanh-like) reps are compact and generalize; entangled (ReLU-like) reps support memory and binding. Reframes the "compute-vs-knowledge" tension in H-19.

- **Neural collapse as activation-function-dependent.** Reframes F-4 from "universal end-state of training" to "Tanh-symmetry-induced behavior." Forces us to identify which Tanh-like component (RMSNorm? softmax? attention?) is mediating the asymmetric collapse we observe in transformers.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Synthetic 4-cluster Gaussian mixtures** — toy task, P=4 inputs, 2-cluster binary labels. Not applicable.
- **Parameterized P-cluster k-binary-target tasks** (Section 6) — synthetic, with controlled input-output kernel alignment _c_. Not applicable directly, but the *methodology* of generating tasks at controlled _c_ could inspire a synthetic curriculum if we ever want to test breathing under known input-output structure.
- **CIFAR-10, STL-10** — small-image classification, used in Section 8's CNN experiments. Standard, HF-available. Not applicable to MATH-500/CoT regime.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Single-hidden-layer networks with **frozen output weights at ±1** (or discretized) — simplifies analysis; not portable.
- Cross-entropy loss with sigmoid output.
- Section 9 perturbed activations: `f(x) = min(max(x, 0), 1)` for two-sided saturation; `f(x) = max(x + b, 0)` for shifted ReLU. **These are easy to implement as forward-pass hooks** — could serve as a low-cost diagnostic of saturation effects in transformer FFNs.
- Training noise σ_train vs σ_test distinction (Section 5.2) — useful methodological pattern: keep test conditions fixed while varying training noise to isolate dynamics from evaluation.
- No public code linked in the paper text; would need to reimplement (small, ~few hundred lines).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2401.13558 — Task structure and nonlinearity jointly determine learned representational geometry (Alleman, Lindsey & Fusi, ICLR 2024)

**Relevance:** A foundational ICLR 2024 paper from the Fusi lab (Columbia Neuroscience) that derives, in shallow ReLU vs. Tanh networks, a clean theoretical story for when hidden representations align with target labels vs. preserve input geometry. The headline mechanism — that ReLU-family nonlinearities cause input-geometry preservation while Tanh-family cause target-label collapse — applies in principle to any ReLU-family deep net, including modern transformers (SiLU/GELU/SwiGLU are all ReLU-family in symmetry terms). This makes the paper a direct competitor to several of our framings: F-2 (DoM as correctness predictor — paper says it should be input-geometry); F-3 (orthogonal prefill/final — paper says orthogonality is generic in ReLU-family); F-4 (asymmetric collapse — paper roots collapse in symmetric saturation, which RMSNorm/softmax may be supplying); H-4 (breathing as EoS — paper offers task-structure×nonlinearity as a competing mechanism).

**Key claim we tested:** ReLU-family networks build hidden representations that preserve input geometry rather than collapse to label geometry; correctness-axis-like findings should therefore be interpreted as input-feature axes correlated with correctness.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Four diagnostic experiments (P11-FE70 CKA; P11-FE71 CCGP-across-topics; P11-FE72 untrained-dichotomy decoding; P11-FE73 pairwise-probe-cosine null) are runnable on cached Pathway 11 NPZs at CPU cost. Outcomes will determine whether F-2/F-3/F-4 framings need a substantive reframe or stand as currently written.
