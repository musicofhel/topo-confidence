# Triage brief — 2604.19740 — Generalization at the Edge of Stability

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19740
Triaged: 2026-05-16

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-10 (PH topology null) — mechanism: SD bound implies PH should be non-trivially structured, not Gaussian-null, if the model operates at EoS during inference.** The paper proves that generalization is governed by the Sharpness Dimension of a fractal attractor with dimension strictly less than ambient. If inference-time residual streams inherit attractor geometry from training (the underlying premise of H-4), then the *topology* of inference activations should reflect this low-dimensional fractal structure — persistent homology should detect non-Gaussian structure in principal directions aligned with expanding Lyapunov directions. F-10 finds PH indistinguishable from Gaussian null, which would refute the hypothesis that inference activations inherit training attractor geometry. **Falsifiable test:** Compute PH on L19 activations restricted to the top-j* expanding directions identified by the Hessian spectrum (rather than all 1536 dims), where j* is the Sharpness Dimension. If PH remains null even in this subspace, the fractal attractor structure doesn't propagate to inference.

2. **Refutes H-4 (breathing ↔ SD correspondence) — mechanism: SD is defined over training-time parameter-space dynamics, not activation-space inference-time dynamics. The two live in different spaces with no proven formal link.** The paper's Sharpness Dimension measures expansion/contraction of the *parameter-space* attractor under SGD iterations. Our "breathing" (PR rise-then-collapse) occurs in *activation space* during a single forward pass. The paper provides no theorem or even conjecture linking parameter-space SD to inference-time activation geometry. If SD is strictly a training-time parameter-space phenomenon (as the paper's formalism requires), then H-4's conjecture that training-time EoS and inference-time breathing "correspond formally" is a category error — the two quantities live in fundamentally different spaces (R^d_params vs R^d_hidden). **Falsifiable test:** Compute the SD of the model at convergence. Independently measure PR breathing amplitude at L19. If cross-model rank-correlation between SD and breathing amplitude is ≤ 0.2 across Qwen-2.5-{0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B}, the formal correspondence fails.

3. **Refutes F-2 (single L19 direction predicts correctness at 0.7731) — mechanism: if generalization depends on the *full* Hessian spectrum (not trace or top eigenvalue), then a single direction (≈ top Lyapunov direction) cannot capture generalization-relevant structure.** The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 4.5) shows that the generalization gap depends on the *complete ordered spectrum* of Lyapunov exponents and their partial sums — not just the top eigenvalue/direction. This directly implies that a single direction (our DoM/PC1) captures at most the top Lyapunov direction's contribution and misses the remaining j*-1 expanding directions. The cov-spectrum probe (0.7928 AUROC) partially addresses this by using top-20 log-eigenvalues, but if SD theory applies to inference-time activations, then the *ordering and partial sums* of all eigenvalues should matter — not just a linear combination of the top 20. **Falsifiable test:** Compute SD-analog from the inference-time Jacobian of L19 activations w.r.t. input tokens. Compare AUROC of "inference SD" predictor to DoM (0.7731). If inference-SD AUROC > 0.82, the single-direction probe is provably suboptimal in a way the SD framework predicts.

4. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM) — mechanism: if SD shows that *trajectory-based* topological measures (PH-Dim-Fwd, Eα) capture generalization when single-point measures fail (their Fig 2-3), then multi-layer trajectory features (CoE-60) should carry non-redundant signal vs. single-layer DoM.** The paper shows (Fig 2, Fig 3) that backward PH-Dim (trajectory-based) outperforms point-based measures in granulated Kendall correlation. If the same logic applies at inference time, then CoE-60's trajectory features (which capture the sequential evolution of representations across layers — analogous to training-trajectory features) should contain non-redundant information that a single-layer snapshot (L19 DoM) misses. F-9 claims they're redundant. **Falsifiable test:** Compute "activation trajectory PH-Dim" across layers 1-28 of Qwen-2.5-1.5B for each MATH-500 problem. Compare AUROC of this trajectory-based measure vs L19 DoM alone. If trajectory PH-Dim > 0.80 and is non-redundant with DoM, F-9 is weakened.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10 (PH at Gaussian null):** Directly predicted by this paper's framework. The paper's empirical finding (Fig 2) that "both Euclidean and data-dependent PH-Dims fail to capture generalization behaviour" in the EoS regime aligns with our F-10 finding that PH adds no signal beyond covariance on inference activations. The paper provides a theoretical explanation: at EoS, the attractor's complexity is captured by the *ordered Lyapunov spectrum*, not by PH of point clouds. Our AUROC 0.7731 (DoM) and 0.7928 (cov-spectrum top-20 eigvals) are both spectral measures — consistent with SD theory.

- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ SD):** The paper is the direct motivator of H-4. Now having the full paper, the key formal object is the "transition index" j* — the largest index where partial sums of ordered sharpness values remain non-negative. If our dimensional breathing (PR rise at prefill, collapse at final token) reflects the transition from j*-large to j*-small regimes during generation, then cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0.046 might correspond to a rotation of expanding directions as the model transitions between generation phases.

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's SD depends on log|1 - ηα| for all Hessian eigenvalues α. Our DoM direction approximates the top eigenvalue direction. The cov-spectrum probe (0.7928) uses the top-20 eigvals — closer to SD's full-spectrum approach. The gap between DoM (0.7731) and cov-spectrum (0.7928) may reflect exactly the information in directions 2-20 that SD theory says matters.

- **F-1 (Dimensional breathing universal):** The paper's framework shows that at EoS, the effective dimensionality of the parameter-space attractor is < d. Our "dimensional breathing" at inference time (participation ratio oscillating) is a distinct phenomenon — but if the model's Hessian-spectrum structure is reflected in its activation-space geometry (a non-trivial bridge), then the effective dimensionality of activations might similarly be bounded by an inference-time analog of SD.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Sharpness Dimension (SD) computation** — Compute ordered Lyapunov exponents λ_1 ≥ ... ≥ λ_d from the Hessian spectrum via log|1 - ηα_i|, then find j* = argmax{j : Σ_{i=1}^j λ_i ≥ 0}. SD = j* + (Σ_{i=1}^{j*} λ_i) / |λ_{j*+1}|.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU for cached eigenvalues; H100 hours for fresh Hessian computation on Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the SD formula itself is trivial to compute; the bottleneck is the Hessian spectrum.

- **Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature (SLQ) for spectral density estimation** — Run multiple Lanczos decompositions with Rademacher probes on independent minibatch Hessians. Average the resulting Ritz-value quadratures to estimate full Hessian spectral density without materializing the full Hessian.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for Qwen-2.5-1.5B (124M params took their full pipeline; 1.5B would be ~10x).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — exceeds local compute budget (2060 Super). Would need to adapt to activation-space Jacobian instead.

- **Inference-time analog: activation Jacobian SD** — Apply the SD computation not to the parameter-space Hessian but to the layer-to-layer Jacobian of activations (d(h_{l+1})/d(h_l)). This gives an "inference SD" measuring how many directions expand vs contract during the forward pass.
  *Replication cost*: 2h H100 (need Jacobian-vector products through each layer for 500 samples).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests H-4 bridge hypothesis.

- **Kendall τ and Granulated Kendall Coefficient (GKC)** — Correlation measures that capture not just rank correlation but hyperparameter-conditioned correlation. GKC conditions on one hyperparameter varying while others are fixed.
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU (pure statistics on cached features).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — can apply to our DoM, cov-spectrum, and PH features as alternative to AUROC for measuring generalization-gap correlation.

- **SD-PS (pseudo-spectrum estimator)** — Convert smoothed spectral density to equal-mass quantiles, apply log|1 - ηα| transform, compute SD on the quantile pseudo-eigenvalues. Avoids discretization artifacts of histograms.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU if spectral density is already estimated.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — applicable to our covariance eigenvalue spectrum directly.

## Approaches & framings

- **Attractor-centric generalization:** Rather than analyzing single converged points, analyze the geometric properties of the *set* the optimizer explores asymptotically. This reframes generalization as a property of the attractor's fractal dimension rather than any individual solution's flatness. Intersects F-10 and H-4 — our finding that PH is null on activations may reflect that the attractor geometry is invisible at the activation level (only visible in parameter space).

- **Full spectrum vs. top eigenvalue:** The paper proves that generalization depends on the *complete* Hessian spectrum and partial determinants, not just trace or top eigenvalue. This challenges any single-direction predictor (including our DoM) and motivates full-spectrum approaches (our cov-spectrum probe is partial validation).

- **Training dynamics as random dynamical systems:** Formalizing SGD as an RDS with pullback attractors. If extended to inference-time token generation, each token position could be viewed as a "step" in an RDS, with the generation trajectory converging to an attractor — our "breathing" pattern might be the transient approach to this attractor.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- SLQ uses m=100 Lanczos iterations per run, R_SLQ=50 independent runs (minibatch + probe resampled between runs, fixed within each run).
- SD formula: j* = argmax{j : Σ_{i=1}^j λ_i ≥ 0}, then SD = j* + (Σ_{i=1}^{j*} λ_i) / |λ_{j*+1}|. This is the Kaplan-Yorke dimension applied to the ordered log-singular-values of I - η∇²R̂_S(w).
- Kernel bandwidth for SD-KDE: Silverman's rule of thumb on the Ritz values.
- Code: https://circle-group.github.io/research/GATES (announced but may not be live yet).
- GPT-2 experiments: 5×5 grid over LR and batch size for SGD/SGD-momentum; 4×3×3 grid over LR, batch size, and weight decay for AdamW.
