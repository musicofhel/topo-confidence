# Triage brief — 2205.14334 — Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14334
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 ("Prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor, *better than final-token*", AUROC 0.7731 vs 0.7186) if Indirect-logit-style final-token probe transfers to Qwen-2.5-1.5B.** The paper's Indirect logit — finetuned logprob of "True" *appended after the model's answer* (a final-position scalar, supervised on cross-entropy against groundtruth) — achieves the *best* MAD = 7.1 on the harder Multiply-divide eval, beating finetuned verbalized probability (MAD 19.0). That is a final-token signal outperforming a finetuned-from-zero-supervision verbalized signal on the task with the bigger arithmetic distribution shift. **Mechanism**: a True/False discrimination head trained at the final position can read off correctness from the unembedding direction of "True"; under F-3's near-orthogonality (cos 0.046) this direction is geometrically distinct from prefill DoM but may carry comparable information. **Falsifiable test**: append "True/False:" after each Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500 K=1 answer, finetune (or zero-shot prompt) the True-token logprob against groundtruth, compute final-token AUROC; if ≥ 0.7731 the prefill-beats-final ordering of F-2 is a probe-design artifact, not a fact about geometry.

2. **Refutes F-7 ("D-bucket has a distinctive collective (not per-problem) geometric signature") if MATH-500 correct/incorrect activations are linearly separable in 2D as in their Fig. 7.** The paper shows that a *trained 2D linear projection* of GPT-3 question-answer embeddings already cleanly separates correct from incorrect on Multiply-divide after only ~5 epochs of supervised projection training. If the same is true for cached Stage 2 Qwen-1.5B L19 activations on MATH-500, then B-bucket (K=1 wrong → K=8 right) and D-bucket (K=1 right → K=8 wrong) are likely points sitting near the linear margin — a continuous-margin interpretation, not a "distinctive collective signature" living off-cluster. **Mechanism**: linear-probe margin distance becomes a sufficient predictor of bucket assignment; F-7's collective-vs-per-problem split would be epiphenomenal of margin density, not an extra geometric structure. **Falsifiable test**: project cached L19 prefill activations into 2D supervised by K=1 correctness, plot B-/D-bucket points; if they cluster near the boundary, F-7 needs to be rephrased as "low-margin" rather than "distinctive collective."

3. **Refutes H-5 ("Prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability") in favor of an "in-distribution accuracy memorization" reading if their heuristic-baseline contrast doesn't replicate.** The paper's logistic-regression baseline on hand-crafted difficulty features (n-digits, operator, number format) achieves MSE 29.7 / MAD 31.2 on Multi-answer — clearly worse than verbalized 22.0/16.4 — establishing that surface heuristics cannot fully explain calibration generalization (their Sec. 3.4 + Table 2). H-5 implicitly relies on the same kind of contrast: prefill DoM should beat a difficulty-only heuristic baseline. **Mechanism**: if a logreg over Qwen-1.5B-MATH-500 surface features (problem-statement length, integer magnitudes, operator counts, novel-symbol ratio) reaches AUROC ≥ 0.75, it would refute the "epistemic decomposability" framing — prefill DoM may just be encoding "is this a familiar arithmetic class" via in-distribution memorized accuracy. **Falsifiable test**: train logreg on hand-crafted MATH-500 features against K=1 correctness; compare AUROC to prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731.

4. **Refutes H-12 ("Semantic-entropy probes (label-free) replace supervised DoM for refusal", LOW priority) by offering verbalized probability as a *different* label-free alternative.** The paper's stochastic 50-shot calibration (Sec. 3.3, Fig. 6) approaches the calibration of the 10k-example finetune *without any supervised tuning of the model's own weights* — using only in-context examples. **Mechanism**: instruction-following + few-shot demonstrations can elicit a calibrated verbalized probability without DoM-style supervised probe training. **Falsifiable test**: prompt Qwen-2.5-1.5B with 50 random (problem, answer, accuracy-based-confidence) examples drawn from a held-out MATH-500 split, ask it to output "Confidence: X%" after each test answer, compute AUROC + selective-prediction risk-coverage curve at coverage 0.5; if it matches F-8's 71.6% accuracy at K=2.5 average compute, supervised DoM (E3 in F-8) loses its key advantage and H-12's "SEP-only" framing is too narrow.

5. **Refutes the *implicit* reading of F-3 ("Prefill and final-token DoM directions are orthogonal, cos 0.046") that orthogonality means distinct circuits.** The paper's Indirect logit (final-position True-token probe) and verbalized probability (which is decoded from the position right after the answer) carry comparable information per their Table 1, despite living in geometrically distinct surfaces (one is a single-token logit, the other is a sequence of digit tokens). This is direct precedent for "two probes hit the same latent uncertainty signal via different surface decoders." **Mechanism**: orthogonality of *probe directions* is consistent with high *information overlap* if both directions are linearly decoded from a shared latent. **Falsifiable test**: per-MATH-500-question, compute Pearson correlation between (a) prefill L19 DoM score and (b) final-token Indirect-logit-style True-token logprob; if r > 0.7 despite cos 0.046, the two probes share latent uncertainty content and F-3's "two-circuit" implicature should be downgraded.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731, final-token AUROC 0.7186)** — paper's "Linear probe on GPT-3 embedding" (Multi-answer MSE 31.2 / MAD 30.1, Multiply-divide MSE 14.0 / MAD 14.2) is the same construct: a supervised linear probe on hidden representations predicting correctness. Their probe loses to verbalized finetune but generalizes — direct comparator and validation of the F-2 approach.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — paper's Indirect logit places a correctness probe at the *final position* (after the model's answer), which by construction sits in a different surface from a prefill-position probe. Their Indirect-logit-beats-verbalized result on Multiply-divide gives data on relative power of final-position probes.
- **F-8 (selective-prediction acc 71.6% at coverage 0.5)** — same problem class. Verbalized probability and Indirect logit are alternative selective-prediction signals; Table 1's MSE/MAD numbers are direct comparators in calibration-quality terms.
- **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null)** — the paper's evidence that a *linear* 2D projection of GPT-3 embeddings cleanly separates correct from incorrect (Fig. 7) reinforces F-10's null result on topological structure: the correctness signal lives in linearly accessible directions, not in higher-order topology.
- **H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability)** — paper's heuristic-features-baseline contrast (Sec. 3.4) is the exact methodology H-5 needs to make its claim falsifiable.
- **H-12 (SEPs replace supervised DoM for refusal, LOW)** — paper's verbalized probability is a *different* label-free alternative; H-12 should be broadened to "label-free uncertainty signals" rather than SEPs-specifically.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — Indirect logit is a clean final-token probe whose head-attribution would directly settle whether final-token DoM and final-token True-logit share circuits (auxiliary to H-13's prefill-vs-final question).

## Methodologies extracted

- **Verbalized probability finetuning** — supervised SFT on (question, model's-greedy-answer, label) where label is `floor(100 * p_hat_T)` and `p_hat_T` is empirical accuracy on sub-task `T`. Decoded greedily.
  *Replication cost*: ~10k labeled MATH-500-style examples + 1–2 H100 hours of LoRA SFT on Qwen-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a label-free-at-inference correctness signal directly comparable to F-2/F-8.

- **Indirect logit probe** — append "True/False:" after the model's answer, finetune cross-entropy against groundtruth correctness, use logprob("True") as confidence at the final position.
  *Replication cost*: ~10k labels + same SFT setup; or use cached activations + a 1-token head trained CPU-only.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests the F-2 prefill > final ordering and is the cheapest refutation lever in this brief.

- **Stochastic k-shot calibration** — for k ∈ {1..50}, sample k (q, a, conf) tuples per test question, build context, decode test answer using "Expected Value decoding" (top-5 token weighted sum).
  *Replication cost*: ~1h GPU on Qwen-1.5B; no training.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — pre-finetune sanity check for whether MATH-500 calibration emerges with just in-context examples, addresses H-12 cheaply.

- **Linear probe on pretrained embeddings (paper's Fig. 7 + Table 2 row 3)** — trained 2D projection of (question, answer) embeddings supervised on correctness, measure linear separability and probe MSE/MAD.
  *Replication cost*: minutes on cached Stage 2 NPZs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly comparable to F-2 DoM and to F-7's collective-signature claim.

- **Hand-crafted heuristic baseline** — logistic regression on (n-digits-of-each-operand, operator-type, number-format), MSE/MAD vs verbalized.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU, plus feature engineering for MATH-500 problem types.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — operationalizes H-5's "epistemic vs surface heuristic" contrast.

- **MAD calibration metric (equal-sample-size binning)** — divide model probabilities into K bins of equal sample count, compute mean |acc(bin) − conf(bin)|. Distinct from MSE (Brier), which mixes calibration with sharpness.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — formalizes the calibration leg of F-8 alongside accuracy-at-coverage.

- **Constant baseline** — set a constant confidence p* = argmin MSE on training set; evaluate on eval set as a "lower bound any calibrated probe must beat."
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sanity check for our future selective-prediction reports.

## Approaches & framings

- **Verbalized vs logit-based vs internal-probe trichotomy** — paper organizes correctness signals into three families (Fig. 2). Maps cleanly onto our project: F-2's DoM ≈ "internal probe" (finetuned linearly on hidden states), Answer logit ≈ greedy log-probability (we don't currently use), Indirect logit ≈ final-token True/False probe. Suggests we should explicitly carry all three baselines in F-8 reports.

- **Honesty as alignment frame for uncertainty signals** — paper frames calibrated verbalized probability as a building block of "honesty" (model communicates its internal state truthfully). Connects F-8 selective prediction to a broader goal: a refuse-and-spend system that surfaces uncertainty in tokens humans can read.

- **Latent pre-existing uncertainty features** — paper claims (Sec. 3.4 + Fig. 7 + 50-shot result) that GPT-3 *already* encodes correctness-relevant features pre-finetune; finetuning just "locates" them. This is the strongest published precedent for F-2's interpretation that the prefill DoM direction reflects a pre-existing geometric feature rather than something induced by probe training. Suggests H-8 (DoM stable across checkpoints) is downstream of a stronger claim: DoM stable across pretrained models that share the same training distribution.

- **Distribution shift in *label distribution*** — Add-subtract median acc 21% vs Multi-answer median 65%; calibration must shift in confidence levels, not just rank-order. Distinct from "content shift." Useful framing for thinking about MATH-500 difficulty stratification (the L1–L5 split): F-8 numbers are reported pooled across difficulty; should report per-level too, mirroring this paper's analysis.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **CalibratedMath** — 21 arithmetic sub-tasks (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, floor division, modulo, remainder, percentages, fraction reduction, rounding, arithmetic sequences, 3-step ops, "less-than/greater-than/prime/square/two-sum/multiple" multi-answer prompts), each with multiple difficulty levels. Programmatically generated, integers as answers. Open-source on GitHub. Applicable? **Partial** — synthetic arithmetic, not directly comparable to MATH-500 chain-of-thought reasoning. Useful as a controlled stress test for the *generalization* property of selective prediction (label-distribution shift), but not a substitute for MATH-500 numbers.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Model**: GPT-3 175B "davinci" via OpenAI API supervised finetuning. Smaller models (GPT-J 6B, GPT-3 7B) too weak on the arithmetic — guessing 0% would be reasonable calibration.
- **Training set size**: 100 questions per sub-task × ~100 sub-tasks ≈ 10k examples.
- **Verbalized-words categories**: 5 buckets ("lowest"/"low"/"medium"/"high"/"highest") mapped to interval midpoints. **Counterintuitive finding**: meaningless category names (e.g., made-up tokens) worked *slightly better* than meaningful ones — possibly because meaningful words drag in pretrained associations.
- **Decoding**: greedy for finetuned verbalized; "Expected Value decoding" (sum of top-5 token logprobs weighted by their probabilities) for k-shot — gives a continuous probability instead of a discrete token.
- **Early stopping**: available for verbalized (stop when train MSE plateaus); *not* available for Indirect logit (cross-entropy on True/False is monotone in training set, so they had to hand-tune epoch count). This is an asymmetry to watch for in any replication.
- **Code**: paper directs to a GitHub release of CalibratedMath (verify URL during replication).
- **Limitations the authors flag**: (i) only one model size tested, (ii) content shift is small (all arithmetic), (iii) no RL — supervised SFT only; they suggest RL with proper-scoring-rule rewards as a future direction.

## 2205.14334 — Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words (Lin, Hilton, Evans, 2022)

**Relevance:** Establishes that GPT-3-175B can be finetuned (or 50-shot prompted) to express calibrated verbalized confidence about its own arithmetic answers, *without* using model logits, and that this calibration generalizes under distribution shift to held-out task families. Their secondary contribution — a finetuned final-token "True/False:" probe ("Indirect logit") — wins MAD on the OOD Multiply-divide eval (MAD 7.1) over verbalized probability (MAD 19.0), establishing that *final-token* logit-based probes can be highly competitive correctness signals. Their tertiary contribution — Fig. 7 + Table 2 row 3 linear probe on pretrained embeddings — is the published precedent closest to F-2's prefill-DoM construction. Each of these three points gives us a refutation lever or a methodological floor we currently lack.

**Key claim we tested:** "Internal correctness signals are present in pretrained transformers and can be elicited via several geometrically-distinct probes (verbalized digits, final-token True-token logit, supervised linear probe on embeddings) — these probes carry overlapping but not identical information about the same underlying epistemic uncertainty."

**Our result:** **TO TEST** (HIGH priority via H-73, H-74, H-75; MEDIUM via H-76). The paper does not test on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500, so a clean replication is needed. Three open contests: (i) does an Indirect-logit-equivalent final-token True-probe match prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731 on MATH-500 (refutes F-2 ordering if yes), (ii) does 50-shot verbalized confidence match F-8's 71.6%-at-coverage-0.5 selective-prediction (broadens H-12 if yes), (iii) does a logreg-on-difficulty-features baseline match prefill DoM AUROC (refutes H-5's epistemic framing if yes).
