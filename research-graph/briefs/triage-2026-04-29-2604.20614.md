# Triage brief — 2604.20614 — Too Sharp, Too Sure: When Calibration Follows Curvature

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20614
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Alessandro Morosini, Matea Gjika, Tomaso Poggio, Pierfrancesco Beneventano (2026)
Categories: cs.LG, math.DS, math.OC, stat.ML

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 as "intrinsic correctness signal").**
   *Mechanism:* The paper claims ECE and Gauss-Newton curvature are both controlled, up to
   problem-specific constants, by the same margin-dependent exponential-tail functional. If
   that thesis transfers from small vision tasks to LLM next-token loss, then any probe that
   linearly separates correct from incorrect prefills (our DoM) is largely separating
   "high-margin" from "low-margin" classifications. Frozen Qwen-2.5-1.5B has no margin-aware
   regulariser, so the AUROC=0.7731 may be reading off a *training-induced curvature
   artefact* rather than a "topological confidence signal".
   *Falsifiable test:* Apply their margin-aware objective during a brief
   instruction-fine-tune of Qwen-2.5-1.5B; re-extract L19 prefill DoM on MATH-500. If AUROC
   collapses toward chance, F-2 was a calibration artifact. If AUROC survives, F-2 is
   independent of calibration sharpness.

2. **Refutes F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6% accuracy via prefill DoM).**
   *Mechanism:* The paper's central thesis is that calibration should be a *training-time*
   phenomenon, not a post-hoc one. Our F-8 selective-prediction system is a post-hoc
   probe-based abstention method exactly of the kind they argue is dominated by
   margin-aware in-training calibration. If their argument scales, a margin-aware
   fine-tuned 1.5B should hit ≥71.6% accuracy at 50% coverage *without* any external
   probe, just by ranking on its own log-prob.
   *Falsifiable test:* On the margin-aware fine-tune, compute selective accuracy at
   coverage=0.5 using model log-probability alone (no DoM probe). Beat 71.6% → F-8's
   added value is gone; tie within 1pp → F-8 is dominated; lose by ≥3pp → F-8 captures
   information margin-aware training cannot extract.

3. **Refutes H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS correspondence).**
   *Mechanism:* H-4 conjectures that the breathing-amplitude phenomenon (F-1) is the same
   geometric quantity as Tuci et al.'s Sharpness Dimension at Edge-of-Stability. Morosini
   et al. propose a *different specific functional form* — margin-tail exponential — that
   they prove governs both ECE and curvature. If breathing dimension correlates with EoS
   sharpness via the *Tuci* form but does not correlate with the same exponential-tail
   functional, then "breathing ≡ sharpness" decomposes into at least two distinct
   geometric phenomena, and H-4 is mis-specified.
   *Falsifiable test:* For 1.5B Qwen on MATH-500, compute (a) per-problem breathing
   amplitude, (b) per-problem max log-prob margin (proxy for the exponential-tail
   functional), (c) per-problem participation-ratio sharpness. Run partial correlation.
   If margin-tail screens off breathing↔sharpness, breathing is curvature in disguise; if
   not, H-4 needs a third axis.

4. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature).**
   *Mechanism:* If D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong — "pathological lucky") problems are
   simply problems where the model lives close to its margin boundary, then D-bucket
   geometry is fully predicted by per-problem margin-tail tails (a scalar) — not a
   collective topological feature. Morosini et al.'s framework would predict D-bucket
   problems are exactly those with smallest margin tails.
   *Falsifiable test:* Rank MATH-500 problems by per-problem prefill log-prob margin
   (their exponential-tail-functional proxy). If D-bucket concentrates in the bottom
   tail, F-7's "collective signature" reduces to a per-problem scalar — kill the
   "collective" claim.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across architectures/scales)** — paper's
  claim that ECE-curvature coupling is *training-trajectory-universal* parallels our
  cross-architecture breathing claim, but at training-time vs inference-time.
  Numerical anchor: breathing peak PR 67/88 (1.5B/7B from `pathway11_h100`).
- **H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS)** — most direct. Their margin-tail
  functional gives a *third* candidate functional form that joins Tuci's Sharpness
  Dimension and our breathing amplitude. H-4 may need re-specification as a three-way
  reconciliation rather than a two-way equivalence. Anchor: cos(prefill_DoM,
  final_DoM)=0.046 — orthogonality is between probe directions, *not* what their
  framework predicts about margin-tail behaviour.
- **F-8 (selective prediction at coverage=0.5 → 71.6% acc)** — Morosini et al. argue
  selective-prediction-via-probe is a symptom of mis-trained calibration. Anchor:
  71.6% on answered, K=2.5 avg compute.
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer at matched compute)** — their
  framework predicts that a properly margin-trained model needs *no* explicit
  verification pass; verification routing wins only when the base model is poorly
  calibrated. Sets up a fertile head-to-head.
- **F-10 (PH on trained residual streams = Gaussian null)** — paper supplies a
  theoretical reason why curvature-shape, not topology, is the load-bearing geometry
  of correctness. Convergent with F-10's negative result on PH.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Margin-dependent exponential-tail functional** — single scalar functional that
  captures both ECE and Gauss-Newton curvature along an optimisation trajectory; in
  vision-task formalism, takes the form ∑ exp(-margin_i / τ) over examples.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU on cached prefill embeddings + log-probs for MATH-500
  (we have both).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly testable against F-2, F-7, F-8.
- **Margin-aware training objective targeting "robust-margin tails and local
  smoothness"** — modifies the loss to penalise small-margin tails *and* local
  Hessian sharpness simultaneously.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day for a brief fine-tune of Qwen-2.5-1.5B (e.g. 1 epoch
  GSM8K with their loss).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes, but only after the cheaper functional-fit test
  (above) shows the margin-tail framework explains our prefill DoM signal.
- **Gauss-Newton curvature as ECE proxy** — per-example Gauss-Newton outer-product
  approximation of the Hessian, summed over examples and tracked along training.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 — would require a re-extraction with backprop access
  (not just frozen forward pass).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for H-4 sharpness-dimension validation
  but not high-leverage for current MATH-500 work.
- **ECE-vs-curvature scatter along optimisation** — track ECE and a curvature proxy
  every K steps during training; plot a scatter; the paper's claim is the scatter is
  near-linear.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU if we already have a fine-tune log; otherwise H100 day.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we don't fine-tune in this project; it's a frozen-
  model investigation.

## Approaches & framings

- **Calibration as training-time phenomenon, not post-hoc.** Reframes the
  selective-prediction / abstention literature (F-8, H-19, 2604.18419, 2509.18116) as
  attempting to undo damage that proper training would have prevented. Sharpens the
  question: is the prefill DoM signal a *real* additional feature, or a re-discovery
  of the model's own under-trained margin tails?
- **Curvature ≡ exponential-tail-of-margins.** Replaces "sharpness" as a
  one-dimensional axis with a specific functional form. This is more falsifiable than
  the loose "sharpness ↔ breathing" framing in H-4.
- **"Too sharp, too sure" = sharp loss landscape ⇒ overconfident model.** This is
  the inverse of the AI-safety folklore that "flat minima generalise better" — it
  ties flatness directly to calibration rather than test accuracy.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Their margin-aware loss targets "robust-margin tails and local smoothness" — likely
  combines (i) margin-renormalised cross-entropy and (ii) a Hessian-trace or
  Frobenius-of-Hessian penalty. Exact form not in abstract.
- Connection to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) is structurally similar but the
  paper claims their objective is principled by the ECE-curvature link rather than
  hand-tuned.
- arxiv categories include math.DS and math.OC — the paper is theory-heavy; treat
  experimental claims as illustrative, not definitive.

### H-72: Margin-tail exponential functional explains the prefill DoM signal
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2604.20614 + F-2, F-8
**Test:** On cached pathway11_h100 prefill log-probs and correctness labels for 1.5B
MATH-500, compute ∑_i exp(-margin_i / τ) for τ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}. Bin problems
by margin-tail percentile and compute prefill DoM contribution per bin. If the
margin-tail functional rank-orders DoM contribution monotonically, the prefill DoM
signal collapses to a margin-tail readout. ~1h CPU.
**Requires:** No new data. `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json` plus
the L19 prefill NPZ already on disk.
**Would change:** Confirm → F-2 is downgraded from "intrinsic geometric signal" to
"margin-calibration readout"; F-8's 71.6% becomes a calibration-floor result. Reject
→ prefill DoM is independent of curvature-style margin tails, strengthening F-2.
**Blocks:** H-4 reformulation (without this, we can't tell if H-4 is one axis or two).

### H-73: Training-time margin-aware fine-tune of Qwen-2.5-1.5B eliminates the prefill DoM AUROC lift
**Priority:** MEDIUM
**Motivated by:** 2604.20614 + F-2
**Test:** Brief instruction-fine-tune (1-2 epochs on GSM8K) of Qwen-2.5-1.5B with a
margin-tail + Hessian-Frobenius regulariser approximating Morosini et al.'s
objective. Re-extract L19 prefill DoM on MATH-500. If AUROC drops from 0.7731 to
≤0.55, the original signal was a calibration artefact of under-regularised

**Requires:** H100 pod, GSM8K train split, ability to back-prop through Qwen-2.5.

**Blocks:** Any "DoM as universal LLM correctness probe" claim.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.20614 — Too Sharp, Too Sure: When Calibration Follows Curvature (Morosini, Gjika, Poggio, Beneventano, 2026)

**Relevance:** Theory paper proving (in small-vision setting) that Expected
Calibration Error and Gauss-Newton curvature are both controlled, up to constants,
by the same margin-dependent exponential-tail functional. Proposes a margin-aware
training objective. Not directly about LLMs, but reframes calibration as a
training-time phenomenon — which sets up a head-to-head with our post-hoc
selective-prediction probe (F-8) and our prefill DoM signal (F-2). The
ECE↔curvature link is also a third candidate functional form for the
breathing↔sharpness equivalence H-4 currently asserts.

**Key claim we tested:** That the prefill DoM correctness signal (AUROC=0.7731) and
the selective-prediction lift (71.6% at 0.5 coverage) are downstream of an
under-regularised margin-tail at training time, rather than independent geometric
features of LLM residual streams.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — first-pass falsifier (P11-FE76) is cheap (1h CPU on
cached NPZs) and high-leverage. If their framework applies, several pathway-11
findings reduce to calibration epiphenomena.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE76 (margin-tail fit), P11-FE77 (ECE vs F-8 lift),
P11-FE78 (three-way breathing/sharpness/margin-tail scatter), H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** TO TEST
