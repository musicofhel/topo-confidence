# Triage brief — 2510.02956 — Confidence and Dispersity as Signals: Unsupervised Model Evaluation and Ranking

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02956
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Weijian Deng, Weijie Tu, Ibrahim Radwan, Mohammad Abu Alsheikh, Stephen Gould, Liang Zheng (ANU + U. Canberra). Journal extension of ICML'23 "Confidence and Dispersity Speak" (Deng et al.).

Domain: image classification under distribution shift (ImageNet-C/3D/V2/Sketch, CIFAR-10, CUB-200, WILDS). Unsupervised accuracy *estimation* (set-level R² / Spearman vs. ground-truth accuracy), not per-sample selective prediction. **No hidden states, no residual streams, no probing — operates entirely on softmax outputs of an off-the-shelf classifier.**

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "supervised L19 DoM is the right correctness predictor" framing.** The paper's NuclearNorm of the prediction matrix (Eq. 11) achieves R²=0.984 averaged over CIFAR-10/CUB-200/ImageNet-C/ImageNet-3D for the *set-level* accuracy estimation task, *with no training data and no labels*. **Mechanism**: NuclearNorm fuses confidence (peakedness of rows) and dispersity (rank of the n_t × k matrix) into one cheap aggregate. Applied per-problem to an LLM K=N sampling matrix (each row a softmax over the answer vocab at the answer position, or a one-hot over the K candidate answers), it yields a *label-free* correctness signal. **Falsifiable test**: compute per-problem NuclearNorm of the K=8 answer-token softmax matrix on cached P11 prefill_gated_compute K=8 generations and compare to F-2's 0.7731 OOF AUROC. If NuclearNorm reaches AUROC ≥ 0.74, F-2's "L19 DoM is the strong predictor" claim shifts from "specific direction in residual stream" to "any aggregate of output uncertainty + diversity captures the same information."

2. **Refutes F-9's redundancy story between CoE-60 and L19 DoM.** The paper's IM metric (Eq. 10) and SoftmaxCorr (Eq. 13) are output-side fusions of confidence + dispersity that don't touch hidden states at all. **Mechanism**: if a pure-output softmax statistic (no residual stream geometry) achieves AUROC comparable to L19 DoM, then *both* L19 DoM and CoE-60 are redundant with output-level uncertainty — F-9's "single-layer DoM contains everything CoE-60 has" generalizes to "everything is in the softmax." **Falsifiable test**: compute IM and SoftmaxCorr over per-problem K=8 softmax samples on the cached P11 K=8 trajectories; rank-correlate against L19 DoM scores. If r > 0.85, hidden-state geometry adds nothing beyond output statistics for K=N.

3. **Refutes F-6's "incorrect-group concentration" mechanism.** F-6 attributes 7B prefill PR inversion to the incorrect group concentrating in residual-stream geometry. The paper observes (Sec. III) that under distribution shift "the model may overpredict certain classes and ignore others" — a *dispersity collapse* visible in softmax space, not residual-stream space. **Mechanism**: if 7B's incorrect K=8 distributions show lower softmax dispersity (lower NuclearNorm, lower ClassEntropy) than 1.5B's incorrect distributions, F-6's "concentration" lives in the answer distribution and not (necessarily) in residual geometry. **Falsifiable test**: compute ClassEntropy of K=8 answer distributions for 1.5B vs 7B incorrect-group problems on MATH-500; compare to PR-bucket assignment. If the dispersity gap predicts the inversion better than residual-stream PR does, F-6's mechanism flips.

4. **Refutes H-12's specific reliance on semantic-entropy probes.** H-12 proposes label-free correctness via *semantic-entropy probes* (Kuhn et al. SEPs, 2406.15927). The paper offers a different family of label-free signals — aggregate softmax matrix metrics (NuclearNorm, IM, COT) that are cheaper, training-free, and don't require a separate probe. **Mechanism**: if NuclearNorm matches or beats semantic-entropy probes on selective-prediction at 50% coverage (F-8 baseline 71.6% answered accuracy), H-12's "semantic" framing is misplaced — what matters is any aggregate of confidence + dispersity, not the meaning-cluster structure. **Falsifiable test**: on the same 250 problems answered at coverage 0.5 in F-8, compute NuclearNorm-ranked answered-set and compare top-50% answered accuracy. If ≥ 70%, semantic-clustering specificity is unnecessary.

## Methodologies extracted

- **NuclearNorm of prediction matrix** — sum of singular values of the n_t × k softmax matrix, normalized by √(min(n_t, k) · n_t). Captures confidence (row peakedness) + dispersity (rank/spread across columns).
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU per pathway on cached softmax outputs.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct unsupervised baseline against F-2 / F-8.

- **IM (Information Maximization)** — H(mean(p_i)) − mean(H(p_i)). Marginal entropy minus average per-sample entropy. Closed-form, no training.
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — bundled with NuclearNorm experiment.

- **COT (Confidence Optimal Transport)** — Wasserstein-∞ distance from the empirical pushforward distribution of predictions to the uniform distribution.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU (POT library, n_t × k OT problem).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes, low priority** — bundled with NuclearNorm; POT install needed.

- **SoftmaxCorr** — cosine similarity between class correlation matrix C = P_m^T P_m / n_t and a reference diagonal R = diag(d), where d is a prior class distribution from a zero-shot CLIP model.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU + needs prior class distribution from a separate model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — requires defining a "prior answer distribution" for MATH-500 (uniform-over-numerical-answers? CLIP-style is meaningless for math). Likely skip.

- **MaNo** — piecewise normalization of logits then L_p norm of the resulting Q matrix; aimed at alleviating overconfidence.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on cached logits (need per-token logits, not just sampled answers).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — needs logits we don't cache; marginal vs NuclearNorm.

- **ATC, DoC, AvgEnergy, ConfScore, Entropy, ClassEntropy, CTD** — all standard baselines documented in Sec. III. Mostly already implicit in our work (Entropy ≈ semantic entropy proxy, ConfScore ≈ max-softmax baseline).
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** as the baseline panel for the NuclearNorm experiment.

## Approaches & framings

- **Confidence ↔ Dispersity duality.** A model can be wrong by being underconfident OR by being overconfident on a small subset of answers. The paper's framing — sample-level certainty + set-level diversity — is a useful reframe of our F-7 D-bucket story: D-bucket may be the *high-confidence-low-dispersity* regime (overconfident on a single wrong reasoning pathway).
- **Set-level vs per-sample evaluation.** The paper operates at the *set* level (does this whole test set look hard for this model?). Our F-2 / F-8 operate at the *per-sample* level. Mapping their NuclearNorm onto K=N samples *of a single problem* converts their set-level metric into our per-sample metric — a transferable framing move.
- **"Output-only suffices" lens.** The paper's strongest claim is that you can rank models without probes, training, or hidden states — pure softmax suffices. Treats hidden-state probes as overengineered for the unsupervised-eval task. Useful counter-frame for the program: how much of F-2's signal lives at the output and how much *only* in the residual stream?

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Prediction matrix definition** is just `P_m = softmax(logits)` stacked over the test set — no model surgery, works on any classifier.
- **NuclearNorm normalization**: `||P_m||_* / sqrt(min(n_t, k) · n_t)` so the score lies in a comparable range across n_t (test-set size) and k (class count).
- **Backbone zoo**: ResNet-20, RepVGG-A0, VGG-11, ResNet-50/101, PMG, ViT-Base, DenseNet-121, ConvNeXt-Base, CLIP-ViT-B/32, CLIP-ConvNeXt-Base — all from `timm`.
- **Prob axis scaling** — both accuracy and the estimated quantity are mapped from [0,1] to (−∞, +∞) via probit before regression (per Taori et al. 2020). When porting their NuclearNorm-vs-accuracy correlation to LLM K=N, we should apply the same probit scaling to keep R² comparable to their numbers.
- **Conference-version code** likely lives at the original ICML'23 paper's repo (Deng et al. 2023, ref [15]); extended-version code release not yet announced in PDF.
- **No training, no probe, no held-out validation needed for any of the proposed metrics** — these are all closed-form summaries of the prediction matrix.
