# Triage brief — 2502.11096 — Mixture of Tunable Experts: Behavior Modification of DeepSeek-R1 at Inference Time

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11096
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 / F-9 (single-direction primacy of L19 prefill DoM).** MoTE
   demonstrates that a sparse, *basis-aligned* set — top 10 of 14,848
   routed experts (0.07%) — mediates 52% of the refusal-vs-non-refusal
   behavior on a held-out sensitive-prompt dataset, with no MT-Bench
   degradation. If correctness in dense Qwen-2.5 is similarly sparse and
   basis-aligned at L19, then a logistic regression on top-K
   *MLP-neuron* differential activations (fTRI-on-neurons) should beat
   the continuous L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731. Mechanism: 0.7731 is the
   projection of a sparse-but-locally-orthogonal mechanism onto a single
   continuous axis, lossily averaging over basis-aligned channels.
   Falsifiable: build the differential-activation map across {correct,
   incorrect} on cached `pathway11_h100` prefill activations, score
   AUROC of `sum(top-K neurons)` for K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 50}; if AUROC > 0.78
   for any K ≤ 50, F-2 must be reframed.

2. **F-3 (prefill-DoM and final-token DoM are orthogonal at cos =
   0.046).** MoTE's distinctive experts are identified from
   *prompt-aggregated* activations but the tuning intervention persists
   across all 58 layers and *all* completion tokens — i.e., the same
   sparse expert set is load-bearing during prefill *and* during
   generation. If the analogue holds in dense models, then the *neurons*
   contributing to prefill-DoM and final-token-DoM should heavily
   overlap (Jaccard ≥ 0.5), even though the continuous direction
   projections appear orthogonal. Mechanism: orthogonality of two unit
   vectors in R^1536 is generic when the underlying sparse mechanism
   has slightly different basis-aligned weights at the two timesteps;
   continuous cos = 0.046 is consistent with shared sparse-neuron
   support. Falsifiable: top-K differential MLP-neuron lists at prefill
   vs. final-token; Jaccard > 0.5 refutes the strong "different
   subspaces" reading of F-3.

3. **F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature).**
   MoTE Figure 4 shows that *every* behavioral class (REFUSED, ALIGNED,
   REASONED) yields a distinctive activation cluster — not only the
   "outlier" class. If we apply class-averaged differential analysis to
   our four buckets {A, B, C, D} on cached prefill activations, the
   number of "distinctive" MLP neurons (e.g. > 5σ over a permutation
   null) should be comparable across all four buckets, not concentrated
   on D. Mechanism: F-7's distinctiveness is a comparative claim
   ("D vs others"); with a class-averaged differencing protocol, every
   class is "distinctive against the rest" by construction unless one is
   genuinely structureless. Falsifiable: per-bucket distinctive-neuron
   counts on `pathway11_h100/{prefill,1024tok}/` activations; if D's
   count is not statistically higher than A/B/C, F-7's privileging of
   D-bucket as uniquely structured is artefactual.

4. **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** MoTE
   achieves a 52% behavior flip with 10 routing edits and zero
   MT-Bench degradation, while H-1's continuous-direction framing
   requires a per-position bank of unit vectors. If the dense-model
   analogue (zero out top-10 differentially-active L19 MLP neurons at
   inference) yields a larger MATH-500 ΔACC at smaller intervention
   budget than per-position DoM steering, then H-1's continuous
   formulation is operationally suboptimal. Mechanism: discrete
   sparse interventions are non-rotating (no per-position
   recomputation), so RoPE-induced direction rotation (H-17)
   automatically does not apply. Falsifiable: head-to-head ablation
   on Qwen-1.5B, MATH-500 K=1, matched compute budget.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC = 0.7731, OOF 5-fold).** MoTE's
  class-averaged differential activation method is a sparse, discrete,
  basis-aligned analogue of DoM. The connection is methodological:
  fTRI = "DoM applied to expert-routing one-hots instead of residual
  streams". Predicts F-2's AUROC is a lower bound on what's recoverable
  from L19 with appropriate basis selection.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill, final) = 0.046).** MoTE's expert-suppression
  carries across both prefill and generation tokens with a single set;
  raises the question whether 0.046 is a basis-projection artefact of a
  shared sparse mechanism rather than evidence of two computationally
  distinct subspaces.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature).** MoTE's t-SNE clusters on
  per-class activation patterns predict that *all* four A/B/C/D
  buckets, not just D, will show class-averaged distinctive structure
  on prefill activations.
- **F-8 (selective prediction at 71.6% on answered, K=2.5 avg).** If
  fTRI-on-neurons beats DoM at correctness prediction, the 71.6%
  selective number at coverage 0.5 is a lower bound; cheaper sparse
  basis-aligned thresholding could replace the linear probe.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM).** A sparse
  basis-aligned predictor would be redundant with L19 DoM in *the
  same direction* but not in the same coordinates — could resolve
  whether F-9's redundancy is mechanism-level or projection-level.
- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null).** PH on continuous residual streams is
  rotation-invariant; sparse basis-aligned mechanisms are not.
  Consistent: localization in basis-aligned coordinates is invisible
  to PH, which is a *consistency* prediction of MoTE's findings.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering).** MoTE-style sparse neuron
  ablation is a rival mechanism. If competitive, H-1's continuous
  bank-of-directions framing is operationally inferior.
- **H-12 (semantic-entropy probes for refusal).** SEPs are
  label-free residual-stream analogues; an SEP's coefficient mass on
  fTRI-flagged neurons would validate H-12 as an unsupervised
  proxy for fTRI. Worth testing.
- **H-13 (head-level circuit attribution).** fTRI's expert-level
  attribution is the MoE analogue of head-level attribution in dense
  attention; both ask "what discrete computational unit carries this
  behavior?".
- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical).** If
  rotation is RoPE-mechanical and basis-aligned neurons are
  rotation-invariant, sparse neuron interventions would not need
  per-position rebasing — directly supports H-17's prediction
  structure.

## Methodologies extracted

- **fTRI (functional Token Resonance Imaging) — class-averaged
  differential activation.** For each behavior class, average
  prompt-aggregated expert-activation maps; subtract the average over
  all other classes; identify top-K experts by resonance. Equivalent to
  a one-vs-rest mean-difference filter on a binarized activation
  signal.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h CPU on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill`
  MLP-intermediate activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is the cheapest direct test of
  Refutation 1.

- **Routing override (suppress / stimulate).** At inference, force
  selected experts' router weights to 0 (suppress) or to max-of-row
  (stimulate) and renormalize the remaining top-k. Dense analogue is
  zeroing or boosting individual MLP-neuron post-activations at L19.
  *Replication cost*: 1d H100 to re-run MATH-500 K=1 on Qwen-1.5B
  with one-line forward-pass hook.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is the test for Refutation 4 /
  H-1.

- **t-SNE on activation maps as cluster-discovery probe.** Pure
  visualization; we already have UMAP+PCA on prefill in
  `pathway11_h100`. Low novelty.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (1h CPU).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we already have equivalent.

- **Parameterized prompt template (Cartesian product) for behavior
  elicitation.** "What happened {time}{place}?" → 756 prompts; cheap
  way to construct controlled behavior datasets without LLM-generated
  ambiguity.
  *Replication cost*: 1h to author template + run inference.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: not for current findings, but worth
  remembering for any future refusal / abstention experiment under
  H-12.

- **Random-expert ablation control.** Always include random-K
  ablation as the null distribution.
  *Replication cost*: same as the targeted ablation.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — must be paired with FE74's targeted
  neuron ablation.

- **MT-Bench impact assay.** 80 multi-turn questions, 7 categories.
  Cheap regression check that an intervention has not broken general
  capability.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — must accompany any steering / DoM
  intervention to prevent hidden capability damage (cf. F-6 inversion
  artefact lessons).

## Approaches & framings

- **"Behavior is partially encoded in a small number of basis-aligned
  units"** — sparse-localization framing as an alternative to
  continuous-subspace framing. Directly intersects F-2/F-3/F-9 (which
  are continuous-subspace claims) and H-1/H-12 (which are
  continuous-direction interventions).
- **System 1 / System 2 framing for behavior classes (REFUSED /
  ALIGNED / REASONED).** Maps onto our A/B/C/D buckets with the
  observation that A-bucket may be System 1 (no CoT inflation needed)
  and D-bucket may be System 2 with broken collective signature. Worth
  noting for F-7 narrative but not load-bearing.
- **"Specialization emerges naturally from large expert count without
  training-time pressure"** — relevant to F-1's universality claim
  (sparse basis-aligned structure may be a generic property of
  high-dimensional models, not architecture-specific).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- DeepSeek-R1: 14,848 routed experts across 58 MoE layers, 256
  experts/layer, top-k=8, plus 1 shared expert/layer.
- Suppression: set targeted expert's gate weight to 0 in top-k softmax
  output, *renormalize remaining top-k*. Crucially — they do *not*
  re-route to alternates; the gate vector is normalized post-zero. The
  dense analogue is zeroing post-GELU MLP neurons at L19, not
  zeroing-and-replacing.
- Stimulation: insert target expert into top-k irrespective of its
  gate; evict the lowest-weighted current top-k member; set target's
  weight to row-max; renormalize. The "evict lowest" detail matters —
  if naively appended, the top-(k+1) softmax dilutes everyone.
- Inference stack: 8× H100 NVL, modified vLLM. Authors note vLLM
  modifications are intended to be released.
- Top-10 distinctive experts for refusal (their Appendix A): layers
  span 14–53 (i.e., not concentrated near input or output). For our
  L19 hypothesis, this suggests checking *all* layers, not only L19.
- Stimulation produces smaller effects than suppression (10% vs 40%);
  asymmetry note — "refusal is easier to break than to enforce". This
  is the opposite asymmetry from F-4 ("correct trajectories collapse
  harder than incorrect"). Worth a paragraph in HYPOTHESES.
- MT-Bench scores actually *improved* with refusal experts suppressed
  (8.97 vs 8.72 average). Sample size n=80; not significant, but a
  hint that refusal circuitry imposes a measurable cost.

### H-73: Correctness in Qwen-2.5-1.5B is sparse and basis-aligned in L19 MLP neurons
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2502.11096 + F-2
**Test:** Compute one-vs-rest mean-difference activation per MLP neuron
at L19 on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill` activations for Qwen-2.5-1.5B,
labeled by correct/incorrect. Score AUROC of `sum(top-K neurons)` for
K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 50, 200} against the L19 DoM AUROC of 0.7731.
Repeat on Qwen-2.5-7B prefill cache. ~4h CPU.
**Requires:** Cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/` MLP-intermediate
activations (already exist on disk per DATA_MANIFEST). CPU-only.
**Would change:** If AUROC > 0.78 for any K ≤ 50, F-2's "single direction"
framing is reframed as a continuous projection of a sparse mechanism;

genuinely the right continuous direction and MoTE-style sparse
localization does not transfer from MoE-routing to dense MLPs.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2502.11096 — Mixture of Tunable Experts: Behavior Modification of DeepSeek-R1 at Inference Time (Dahlke, Klagges, Zecha, Merkel, Rohr, Klemm — TNG Technology Consulting, 2025)

**Relevance:** Methodologically adjacent. The paper's fTRI (functional
Token Resonance Imaging) — class-averaged differential activation
analysis to identify a sparse subset of computational units associated
with a behavior — is the discrete-routing analogue of our DoM (F-2).
fTRI's empirical success (52% refusal flip with 10 of 14,848 experts,
no MT-Bench loss) raises the open question whether correctness in our
dense Qwen-2.5 models is similarly sparse-and-basis-aligned at L19, in
which case F-2's continuous DoM is a lossy projection. The paper is on
a different model family (DeepSeek-R1 MoE, 671B) and a different
behavior (refusal, reasoning language), so transfer is hypothetical, not
direct. Three FEs (P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75) and three Hs (H-73,
H-74, H-75) are proposed to translate fTRI from MoE-routing to dense
MLP-neuron level on cached pathway11 activations.

**Key claim we tested:** Behavior in large MoE models is partially
encoded in a small number of basis-aligned computational units (experts)
identifiable via class-averaged differential activation. Translation to
our setting: correctness in dense models is partially encoded in a
small number of basis-aligned MLP neurons at L19, identifiable via the
same class-differencing method.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Architecture mismatch (MoE vs dense) means
the headline claim does not transfer literally; the methodological
translation is testable on existing cached activations and is the
subject of P11-FE73 and H-73.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE73, P11-FE74, P11-FE75, P10-FE56, H-73,
H-74, H-75.

**Status:** TO TEST.
