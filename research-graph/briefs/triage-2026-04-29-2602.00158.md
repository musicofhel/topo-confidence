# Triage brief — 2602.00158 — RAPTOR: Ridge-Adaptive Logistic Probes

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00158
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the implicit claim that F-2's 0.7731 prefill-L19 AUROC is a ceiling for the *direction*, not just for *DoM-the-estimator*.**
   *Mechanism*: F-2 uses Difference-of-Means (DoM) — mean(correct) − mean(incorrect), normalized — to extract the prefill correctness direction. RAPTOR's headline empirical result is that a validation-tuned ℓ2-regularized logistic probe ("ridge-logistic") matches or exceeds GCS and xRFM on best-layer accuracy across a 7×6 LLM/concept grid (mean best 0.874 vs. 0.854 GCS, 0.871 xRFM). DoM is a special case of unregularized linear probing (no λ), so a properly-tuned ridge probe on the same Pathway 11 prefill activations should *strictly dominate* DoM on AUROC unless our 500-sample regime is large relative to p=1536 (it is not — δ=n/p≈0.33, exactly where Section 5's CGMT analysis predicts λ tuning matters most).
   *Falsifiable*: Refit RAPTOR (sklearn `LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', C=1/λ)` with λ swept over the validation grid) on the cached Pathway 11 1.5B prefill L19 activations against the K=1 correctness labels. If 5-fold OOF AUROC > 0.7731 (with non-trivial gap, e.g. >0.02), F-2's headline number is a DoM-estimator artifact and should be revised upward.

2. **Refutes F-3's "cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, directions are orthogonal" as a property of the data rather than of DoM-instability.**
   *Mechanism*: Tan et al. (2407.12404, already in graph) and this paper's Table 2 both show that DoM-style estimators have low directional stability under data ablation — RAPTOR achieves 0.87–0.98 mean absolute-cosine under 20% data ablation, GCS achieves 0.93–0.99, while xRFM is 0.73–0.86. We have not measured the analogous stability for our DoM directions. If DoM under 20% ablation gives mean cosine ≈ 0.6 on the 500-problem sample, then `cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046` is consistent with both directions being noise-dominated within their own stability bounds, and the orthogonality claim collapses.
   *Falsifiable*: Run K=20 × 20%-drop ablations on the cached prefill / final activations, compute the within-method mean absolute-cosine for DoM. If DoM stability < cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) cushion (i.e., DoM's own ablation noise is comparable to or larger than 0.046), F-3's orthogonality claim is unsafe and should be re-stated as "non-aligned within DoM's noise floor".

3. **Refutes H-1's premise that the *DoM* direction is the right vector for per-position steering of MATH-500 accuracy.**
   *Mechanism*: H-1 plans to bank prefill DoM and steer per-position. RAPTOR + GCAV (per-sample α calibration to drive probe probability to a target p₀) is a strictly more principled steering protocol — and Section 4.5 reports 1.000 probe-target success rate on all four datasets at moderate median |α|=3.6–12.4. If a DoM+fixed-α steer fails to move MATH-500 K=1 accuracy and a RAPTOR+GCAV steer (with λ tuned on the same activations) does, the operational claim of F-2 — that DoM identifies the causally relevant direction — is wrong; what matters is the stability-tuned direction.
   *Falsifiable*: Compare two steering arms on Qwen2.5-1.5B MATH-500: (i) DoM at L19 prefill, fixed α scan; (ii) RAPTOR concept vector at L19 prefill with GCAV per-sample α. If (ii) shifts K=1 accuracy and (i) does not, the H-1 spec must be rewritten around RAPTOR.

4. **Refutes the implicit "supervised-DoM is the right benchmark" framing in F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM).**
   *Mechanism*: F-9's redundancy claim was made against single-layer L19 DoM. The paper's empirical table shows ridge-logistic gives +1.96 best-layer points over GCS (which is closer-to-DoM) on average, with biggest gains on harder concepts (HateXplain +3.51, Sarcasm +2.12). MATH-500 correctness is plausibly a "hard concept" relative to the paper's grid. If RAPTOR-L19 beats DoM-L19 by >2 points and CoE-60's 0.811 then sits between them, the "redundant" verdict for CoE-60 needs to be re-stated as "redundant with the *suboptimal* DoM estimator, not necessarily with a tuned single-layer probe."
   *Falsifiable*: Compute RAPTOR AUROC at L19 prefill on cached Pathway 11 activations. If RAPTOR-L19 > CoE-60 (0.811), CoE-60 is dominated by a tuned single-layer probe and F-9 stands. If RAPTOR-L19 ∈ (0.7731, 0.811), CoE-60 retains an edge over the best single-layer probe and F-9 is overstated.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Ridge-logistic probe with validation-tuned λ (RAPTOR)** — standardize per-train, fit ℓ2-regularized logistic regression, sweep λ on validation split, refit on train+val with λ⋆, fold parameters back to embedding space, normalize weights → concept vector.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU for one (model, layer, label) tuple on cached n=500, p≈1536. ~2 h CPU for full layer sweep on Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly replaces F-2's DoM as the headline correctness probe; sklearn one-liner on cached NPZs.

- **GCAV per-sample steering calibration** — given target probe probability p₀, set α = max(0, (logit p₀ − probe_score) / ‖ω‖₂) per-sample, per-layer; intervene only when baseline already misses target.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once probe weights are in hand; ~1 h CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — strictly better than fixed-α for H-1; we should retire fixed-α from any future H-1 spec.

- **Directional-stability metric via 20%-ablation × K=20 + mean absolute pairwise cosine** — accounts for axis (not oriented vector) under sign ambiguity. Directly testable on our cached activations.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU per (layer, method) on n=500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — closes a long-standing gap (we've never bounded DoM's own noise floor; H-15 (length-band PR) does this for collapse, this would do it for direction).

- **Aspect-ratio δ = n/p as the relevant scaling** — ties probe accuracy to a single dimensionless number, letting us subsample n and predict accuracy via the CGMT fixed-point in Eq. 17–19.
  *Replication cost*: 1 h CPU for δ-sweep at fixed model/layer/concept.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — useful sanity-check that the n=500 regime is not pathologically over- or under-determined for our p=1536; deferred until RAPTOR baseline lands.

- **Best-layer + average-over-layers reporting convention** — every metric reported as `best (avg)`. Explicitly avoids cherry-picking and exposes layer-selection sensitivity.
  *Replication cost*: free — purely a reporting choice.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — should adopt for any future per-layer probe report (currently we cite L19 only).

- **CGMT-based theoretical characterization (Theorem 5.1)** — closed-form prediction of test accuracy from (δ, λ, κ) under Gaussian teacher–student. Gives a smooth-curve sanity check for empirical sweeps.
  *Replication cost*: 1 h CPU for fixed-point solver.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** for headline; **maybe** as a sanity diagnostic if RAPTOR shows non-monotonic behavior in λ.

## Approaches & framings

- **Tripartite probe-quality metric: accuracy + directional stability + computational cost.** Replaces the project's implicit "AUROC alone" metric. Forces us to acknowledge that F-2's DoM is fast and accurate-ish, but its stability is unmeasured.
- **Probe-then-steer pipeline as the operational unit.** Reframes F-2 from "the DoM is a correctness predictor" to "the DoM is one half of a probe-then-steer protocol whose other half (steering) we have not implemented" — clarifying that the AUROC is a *means*, not the end.
- **Proportional regime n/p → δ as the right asymptotic.** We have been implicitly using fixed-p asymptotics ("Qwen-1.5B has p=1536, n=500 is enough"), which the paper says is wrong in this regime. Reframes our small-n caveat.
- **λ as a signal/orthogonal decomposition knob (Eq. 22–23).** Concept-vector stability ≈ ᾱ²/(ᾱ² + σ̄²); ridge tuning *literally* trades signal alignment against orthogonal noise. This gives a mechanistic explanation for why DoM (no regularization) is stable when the signal is strong (well-separated classes) and unstable otherwise — and predicts harder concepts (MATH-500 correctness?) need more regularization.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Standardize using train-only μ and s, then fold back** (Eq. 2–4 + post-fit transform). Avoids val/test leakage. The fold-back step (paper's eq. for ω̂ⱼ = ŵⱼ/sⱼ⁽ℓ⁾, b̂_orig = b̂ − ⟨ω̂, μ⟩) means concept vectors injectable into native (un-standardized) layer representations — important for steering.
- **λ grid is method-internal**: paper does not state grid points but sklearn `np.logspace(-4, 4, 9)` is standard.
- **Reliability gate for steering**: skip layers whose probe test accuracy τ < threshold (e.g. 0.7 for CounterFact, 0.8 for STSA-positive). For MATH-500 at L19 prefill (τ=0.7731), we'd just barely clear a 0.7 gate.
- **Steering tail risk**: max |α| up to 249 in some away-direction settings; we should cap or report tail percentiles, not just medians.
- **Code release**: paper says "We will release the code and experiment scripts upon publication." No GitHub link in the text we read. Treat as not-yet-public.
- **Hardware**: paper benchmarks on Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B, Gemma-7B, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Llama-3.3-70B; per-layer training time on log scale 10⁰–10² seconds → trivially CPU-replicable.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2602.00158 — RAPTOR: Ridge-Adaptive Logistic Probes (Gao, Zhu, Zeng, Zhao, Wang, Ruan, Ding — Northwestern, 2026)

**Relevance:** RAPTOR is a direct candidate replacement for the DoM estimator that produces F-2's headline 0.7731 prefill-L19 AUROC. The paper's empirical contribution is that ridge-tuned ℓ2-logistic regression beats both GCS (closer to DoM) and the nonlinear xRFM on a 7×6 LLM/concept grid (mean best 0.874 vs 0.854 / 0.871) while running faster. Its theoretical contribution — a CGMT-based proportional-regime characterization of ridge-logistic in (n, p, δ, λ, κ) — gives us a closed-form prediction for how AUROC should depend on aspect ratio δ = n/p, which is a useful sanity check for our (n=500, p=1536, δ≈0.33) regime. The paper also introduces GCAV per-sample steering calibration (Eq. 7–8), a strictly better steering protocol than fixed-α scans.

**Key claim we tested:** Validation-tuned ridge-logistic probes match or exceed strong baselines on accuracy while improving directional stability and reducing computational cost; concept vectors derived this way are reusable for additive steering.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** The empirical comparison is on instruction-tuned LLMs and concept-binary datasets (sentiment, factuality, hate speech), not on MATH-500 correctness. We have not run RAPTOR on Pathway-11 prefill activations yet. H-72/H-73/H-74 specify the head-to-heads needed.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE72 (RAPTOR vs DoM AUROC head-to-head), P11-FE73 (DoM stability audit), P11-FE74 (RAPTOR+GCAV steering on MATH-500), P11-FE75 (δ-sweep validation), H-72, H-73, H-74.

**Status:** TO TEST
