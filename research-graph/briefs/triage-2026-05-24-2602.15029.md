# Triage brief — 2602.15029 — Symmetry in language statistics shapes the geometry of model representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15029
Triaged: 2026-05-24

## Refutations

**R1 — F-2 (L19 DoM as statistical echo, not learned computation).**
The paper proves that the leading eigenvectors of a co-occurrence-derived PMI matrix are Fourier modes dictated entirely by data statistics (Assumption 3.1 + spectral decomposition). If the L19 prefill DoM direction (AUROC 0.7731) is simply the leading Fourier mode of co-occurrence between MATH problem tokens and correctness-correlated tokens, then the 0.7731 ceiling is a *statistical inevitability* — any model trained on the same corpus would produce a similar direction without needing a specific "correctness computation" at L19. This would reframe F-2 from "the model learns a correctness direction" to "the model inherits a correctness-correlated co-occurrence direction." Falsifiable by: computing whether the DoM direction aligns with the top eigenvector of the MATH-problem token co-occurrence kernel (from the training corpus), and checking whether a shallow model (Word2Vec-scale) trained on the same data produces a direction with comparable AUROC when applied to cached L19 activations.

**R2 — F-1 (breathing universality is data-universal, not architecture-universal).**
The paper demonstrates that Word2Vec, EmbeddingGemma (308M), and Gemma 2 2B all produce the *same* representational geometry for a given concept set — because the geometry is driven by co-occurrence statistics, not model architecture. F-1 claims dimensional breathing is "universal across transformer architectures and scales," implying a model-intrinsic property. Under this paper's framework, universality would be trivially predicted by shared training data distributions. The architectural claim would be vacuous. Falsifiable by: training a model on a corpus where the co-occurrence kernel for math-relevant tokens is artificially flattened (translation symmetry broken), and checking whether breathing disappears despite identical architecture.

**R3 — F-3 (prefill/final DoM orthogonality as Fourier mode separation).**
The paper shows that different PCA components of an embedding space correspond to different Fourier harmonics (k₁, k₂, ...) of the co-occurrence kernel. If prefill and final-token contexts activate different harmonic modes (e.g., prefill activates the problem-structure mode while final-token activates the answer-format mode), their DoM directions would be orthogonal because they sit in orthogonal Fourier subspaces — not because of two independent computational circuits. The cos=0.046 would be *predicted* by the Fourier decomposition. Falsifiable by: projecting prefill and final-token DoMs onto the Fourier basis of the L19 activation covariance and checking whether they localize to distinct harmonics with negligible overlap.

**R4 — F-10 (PH null explained by Fourier manifold structure).**
The paper predicts that concept representations form low-dimensional manifolds (circles for periodic, rippled 1D for open-boundary) embedded in high-dimensional space. If MATH-500 L19 activations lie on a smooth low-dimensional Fourier manifold plus high-dimensional noise, VR-PH applied to the full 1536-d point cloud would see the noise (Gaussian null) while missing the manifold structure. This would reframe F-10's PH-null as a *dimensionality mismatch* — the meaningful topology is in a low-d Fourier subspace, not the ambient space. Falsifiable by: computing PH on the top-r PCA projection of L19 activations (where r matches the number of significant Fourier harmonics) and checking whether the topology becomes non-trivial.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's central result — that linear probes on embeddings recover Fourier modes of co-occurrence — directly predicts why a *linear* probe at L19 works so well for correctness. The cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 is consistent with PC1 being the leading Fourier mode of a co-occurrence kernel, meaning the supervised DoM probe is rediscovering what data statistics encode. The 0.7731 AUROC ceiling may reflect the spectral gap of the kernel.

- **F-1 (breathing universality):** The paper's robustness result (geometry persists even when direct concept co-occurrences are ablated) parallels the robustness we see in breathing across architectures and scales. Under their "collective effects" model (Section 4.1), many helper words sharing a latent variable create eigenvalues that scale with vocabulary size — explaining why breathing would persist across model sizes (larger vocabulary → more collective support).

- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null):** The Fourier manifold prediction offers a specific geometric hypothesis for *why* PH is null: the signal lives in a low-rank Fourier subspace, and PH on the full ambient space sees only the high-dimensional noise envelope. The error bound ε² ~ r^{-1/D} (Proposition 3.3) quantifies how many PCs are needed to recover the manifold.

- **H-4 (breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS):** The Fourier decomposition provides a formal language for what "sharpness dimension" might mean: the number of significant Fourier harmonics of the co-occurrence kernel. If the sharpness dimension at L19 equals the rank at which PCA captures the Fourier manifold, the two frameworks would converge.

- **H-67 (DoM as task-recognition probe, not per-problem correctness probe):** The paper would predict that the DoM direction recognizes *which region of co-occurrence space* a problem occupies — i.e., task type, not per-problem difficulty. This supports the task-recognition interpretation.

- **H-69 (class-conditional difference at L19 has a clean 1–2 PC concept axis hidden under the F-10 PH null):** The Fourier framework directly predicts this: the class-conditional difference would appear in the first 1–2 Fourier modes, while the PH null reflects the noise in the remaining 1534 dimensions.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PMI-matrix spectral decomposition** — Factorize a normalized co-occurrence matrix M* into its eigenspectrum; compare leading eigenvectors to Fourier modes. Applied to vocabulary subsets.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (compute pairwise similarity matrix on 500 cached activation vectors, eigendecompose, compare to DFT basis).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to the 500×1536 L19 activation matrix as a proxy for co-occurrence structure.

- **Fourier mode fitting for periodic/open-boundary concepts** — Fit sinusoidal components to PCA projections of embeddings; analytically predict amplitudes and wavenumbers from exponential or periodic kernels.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU (PCA on cached L19 activations, fit sinusoids to PC projections ordered by problem difficulty or type).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — tests whether MATH-500 problems form a Fourier-structured manifold in L19 space.

- **Collective-effects robustness test** — Ablate direct concept-concept co-occurrences in M*, retrain, check whether geometry persists via helper words with shared latent variables.
  *Replication cost*: 2h CPU (requires constructing and ablating a proxy co-occurrence matrix from L19 activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires training-corpus access we don't have for Qwen.

- **Linear coordinate decoding with rank-controlled ridge regression** — Train ridge probes at varying PCA ranks r; measure test error scaling ε²(r) and compare to ε² ~ r^{-1/D} theoretical bound.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (ridge regression sweeps on cached activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we already do PCA probing (FE291); adding the rank-controlled error curve is trivial.

- **Gram matrix reconstruction from Fourier modes** — Predict the inner-product structure of concept embeddings from analytically derived Fourier eigenvalues; compare predicted vs. empirical Gram matrices.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — compute Gram matrix of L19 DoM-projected activations for correct vs. incorrect problems, check Fourier structure.

## Approaches & framings

- **Statistical symmetry → representational geometry.** The paper's core claim is that representational geometry is *derivable from* data statistics, not learned de novo by the model. For our program, this reframes the question from "what does the model compute at L19?" to "what co-occurrence structure does the training data have that L19 reflects?" This intersects F-2 (why linear probes work) and F-1 (why universality holds).

- **Collective latent variables as robustness mechanism.** The paper explains robustness of geometry through many words sharing a latent variable (e.g., seasonality), creating eigenvalue gaps that resist perturbation (Weyl-Davis-Kahan bound). This provides a formal language for why the DoM direction might be robust across prompt perturbations (H-46) and fine-tuning (H-10): the latent "correctness" variable is supported by many tokens.

- **Fourier subspace as the "right" PCA subspace.** The error bound ε² ~ r^{-1/D} gives a principled way to choose PCA rank for probing — rather than the ad-hoc choices we currently use (PC1, PC9 for FE291; top-20 for FE881). If D is knowable, the optimal r is determined by the Fourier spectrum.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Fourier mode check on L19 PCA.** Take the 500×1536 L19 prefill activation matrix (cached NPZ), compute PCA, project onto top-k PCs, order the 500 samples by a difficulty proxy (e.g., problem index, or correct/incorrect label), and check whether PC projections show sinusoidal structure. Uses `pathway11_h100/pca_covariance/` cached data. ~10min.

- **Eigenspectrum shape check.** Compute the eigenspectrum of the 500×500 Gram matrix (X X^T) of L19 prefill activations and compare its decay to the paper's Fourier eigenvalue predictions for an exponential kernel. If the spectrum matches, the Fourier-from-co-occurrence framework applies. Uses same cached NPZ. ~5min.

- **Fourier basis vs. PCA basis alignment.** Compute the DFT basis for the 500 samples (ordered by some semantic axis) and measure alignment (subspace overlap) with the PCA basis. High alignment would confirm the Fourier-from-co-occurrence theory applies to our activations. ~10min.
