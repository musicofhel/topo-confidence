# Triage brief — 2505.04881 — ConCISE: Confidence-guided Compression in Step-by-step Efficient Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04881
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 — "Prefill L19 DoM is the strong correctness predictor (AUROC 0.7731)"**
   ConCISE's confidence detector is *output-distribution only*: append the
   probing prompt `"So, I'm"` to the partial reasoning chain, sum
   `P("confident") + P("sure") + P("pretty")·[P("confident"|…pretty) + P("sure"|…pretty)]`.
   No layer probing, no logistic regression, no labels. It is reportedly
   strong enough to drive a hard early-stopping decision (te=0.5) without
   collapsing accuracy — i.e., it carries enough correctness signal to gate
   compute at ~50% length compression with <2pt accuracy loss on MATH-500
   under SimPO across four models including DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B.
   *Mechanism by which it refutes F-2:* if the same `"So, I'm"` probe on the
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct cache reaches AUROC ≥ 0.7731 for K=1-correctness on
   MATH-500, then the L19-prefill-DoM "this is the right layer / right
   subspace" claim is overstated — the next-token distribution at FAS already
   carries the same predictive content with no residual-stream extraction.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* P11-FE181 below — re-run forward pass at FAS for
   each of the 500 cached MATH-500 problems on Qwen-2.5-1.5B, append
   "So, I'm", read out the four token probabilities, compute AUROC against
   K=1-correctness labels.

2. **F-8 — "Selective-prediction via prefill DoM at 50% coverage gives 71.6% @ K=2.5"**
   ConCISE-Decoding (training-free variant) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B
   reports 79.0% acc on MATH-500 at 52% compression *with no abstention* —
   the mechanism is "stop reasoning early when ĉᵢ > te, accept the verified
   answer". This is functionally equivalent to a confidence-gated answering
   policy with ~100% coverage. F-8 trades coverage for accuracy on a
   *fixed-compute base policy*; ConCISE trades reasoning length for
   accuracy on a *fixed-coverage base policy*.
   *Mechanism:* if the operating-curve area swept by varying te in
   ConCISE-Decoding dominates the operating-curve area swept by varying
   coverage in F-8 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B / MATH-500, then the prefill-DoM
   gate is a worse use of the same compute budget than the cheaper
   output-distribution gate.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* sweep te ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8} on
   our cached 1.5B and trace acc-vs-tokens; overlay F-8's
   acc-vs-tokens (coverage→answered=1.0 contracts to a single point at
   K=2.5 avg). Refutes F-8 if ConCISE-Decoding curve dominates.

3. **F-3 — "cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) ≈ 0.046 — two orthogonal circuits"**
   Their Fig 4b shows the answer-axis (sum of `"So, I'm {confident|sure|…}"`
   probabilities) is *low* at FAS (~0.4–0.5) and rises sharply with each
   subsequent reflection step to ~0.95. So there is a single,
   monotonically-rising answer-confidence axis that *spans* the FAS-to-stop
   window — this implies the two-circuit framing of F-3 may just be a
   timestep effect: prefill-DoM and final-token-DoM look orthogonal because
   they index different points on the same evolving axis, not because they
   represent two semantically separate circuits ("can I solve" vs "did I
   solve").
   *Falsifiable experiment:* extract a "So-Im DoM" by linearly probing the
   logits of the four confidence tokens (or unembedding rows) back into
   residual space at L19 for FAS, then measure
   `cos(prefill_DoM, So-Im_DoM_at_FAS)`. If cos > 0.5 the "two circuits"
   reading collapses; if still < 0.2, F-3 holds.

4. **F-4 — "Correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect at the final token"**
   ConCISE's data shows the model's *self-assessed* confidence at FAS is
   markedly lower than at FAS+5-reflections (Fig 4b: ~0.45 → ~0.95 over 5
   reflections). F-4 measures asymmetric collapse at "final token" — but
   if "final token" in our pipeline = FAS (which it likely is, since we
   stop generation at the answer), then we are measuring collapse at a
   point the model itself does *not* yet treat as confident. The
   asymmetric-collapse signal may live at FAS+k where the model's
   internal confidence has stabilized.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* on K=1 generations that include post-answer
   reflections (some do, in our cache), compute participation ratio at
   FAS vs FAS+last-reflection token for correct vs incorrect groups
   separately. If the asymmetric-collapse gap *grows* between FAS and
   FAS+last-reflection, F-4 is undermeasured; if the gap shrinks or
   inverts, F-4's interpretation flips.

5. **F-9 — "CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM"**
   F-9 reduces a 60-dim layer-trajectory feature to a 1-dim single-layer
   direction with Δ=+0.004 favor. ConCISE pushes the reduction one further
   step: a *single output token's logit row*, no layer choice, no probe
   training. If the `"So, I'm"`-probe AUROC on our cached MATH-500 is
   within 0.02 of L19 DoM's 0.7731, F-9's "any single dimension at the
   right layer beats trajectory features" generalizes to "any sufficiently
   answer-aware output projection beats both trajectory features *and* the
   right-layer DoM". The mechanism behind F-9's reduction (signal is
   already 1-dimensional) becomes evidence the dimension lives in the
   *unembedding*, not the residual stream.
   *Falsifiable experiment:* same `"So, I'm"`-probe AUROC measurement;
   additionally compute `cos(So-Im_unembedding_dir, L19_DoM)` after
   pulling the unembedding rows for the four tokens through the inverse
   of the layer-norm + final unembedding. cos > 0.6 confirms F-9
   generalization; cos < 0.2 means the two probes capture different signal
   despite similar AUROC.

## Methodologies extracted

- **`"So, I'm"` confidence probing prompt** — append fixed prompt to a
  partial reasoning chain, sum `P(confident) + P(sure) + P(pretty)·(P(confident|pretty) + P(sure|pretty))`
  as ĉᵢ ∈ [0,1]. Single forward pass, no fine-tuning, no labels.
  *Replication cost*: ~10 min on H100 for 500 MATH-500 problems on 1.5B
  (one forward per FAS+probing-prompt token, batchable).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — strongest cheap competitor to
  L19 DoM as a correctness-prediction probe; this is the highest-ROI
  thing in the paper for us.

- **Confidence Injection (rule-based reflection detection + phrase insertion)** —
  detect reflection-step start by keyword match in
  {wait, alternatively, check, reconsider, reflect, rethink,
  reconsidering, reviewing, reassess, pause, second thought, reevaluate,
  verify, think again}; if matched, retroactively prepend a sampled
  confidence phrase from a 20-phrase pool ("Therefore", "The reasoning
  holds", "All steps are valid", "Let's proceed", …) and regenerate.
  *Replication cost*: ~1 hour to wire into our generation harness; per-problem
  generation is online and not cacheable.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no for steering experiments** (we don't fine-tune;
  this only matters for training-data construction); **maybe yes as a
  generation-time intervention** if H-1 (per-position DoM steering) needs a
  cheap text-level baseline.

- **LLM-as-Judge for reflection grouping** — Qwen-Max prompted to mark FAS
  index and group post-FAS reflection steps; falls over above ~5000 tokens.
  *Replication cost*: API costs + human override above 3000 tok.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — not relevant to our pipeline.

- **Early-stopping verification gate** — after ĉᵢ > te, prompt model with
  "Final Answer:", check against ground truth; only stop if both ĉᵢ > te
  AND answer matches gt. Two-of-three verification: probe + commit + check.
  *Replication cost*: needs ground truth (so MATH-500 only); ~1 hour to wire.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** as a verification-gate baseline against
  H-19 (C_exact verification routing — it is essentially the same idea
  with output-distribution probe instead of activation probe).

- **SimPO preference-pair fine-tuning** — preference pairs are
  (CONCISE-generated chain, baseline-generated chain) with reference-free
  reward. Cuts response length ~50% with <2pt MATH-500 acc loss.
  *Replication cost*: 1.5B SFT tractable on single H100; SimPO needs 4× memory.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — fine-tuning is out of scope for
  topo-confidence's mechanistic-probe focus.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Confidence Deficit" + "Termination Delay" decomposition** — splits
  reflection-redundancy into pre-FAS over-checking (ci < ti despite
  correct) and post-FAS over-confirming (heightened ti makes ci hard to
  exceed). Plugs cleanly into our F-2/F-3/F-4 framing: F-2 (prefill DoM)
  is a "Confidence Deficit predictor" (pre-FAS); the asymmetric collapse
  in F-4 happens precisely in the "Termination Delay" window post-FAS.
  Reframes our two findings as observing the same axis at two
  timepoints, strengthening Refutation #3 above.
- **Reflection-as-confidence-gating model** — `next step is a reflection iff
  ci < ti(context)`. This is structurally a thresholded probe — exactly
  the kind of object we routinely build with logistic regression on L19.
  Suggests reflection-onset itself is a label-free supervision signal for
  training a correctness probe.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 20-phrase confidence pool (Table 3 in the paper) — `Therefore`,
  `The reasoning holds`, `Previous steps are correct`, `All steps are valid`,
  `With this established`, `That sounds reasonable`, `Let's go ahead`,
  `Alright, let's carry on`, `Let's proceed`, `Let's progress`, `So, putting
  it all together`, `The logic stands firm`, `The reasoning process is valid`,
  `Good, let's keep going`, `Everything seems reasonable so far`, `This part
  checks out`, `I think that's solid. So`, `The reasoning holds, let's keep
  going`, `Everything checks out, let's move on`, `All steps are solid, let's
  move forward`. Sample uniformly at runtime to avoid overfitting.
- Reflection keyword set for rule-based detection (Appendix A.3.1) — same
  list as in Methodologies.
- Probing prompt "So, I'm" + four confidence-indicative tokens
  {`confident`, `sure`, `pretty confident`, `pretty sure`} with the sum
  formula in Appendix A.2 (decompose `pretty` as an intermediate token to
  avoid double-counting).
- Early-stopping threshold te = 0.5; sensitivity is flat across {0.5, 0.6, 0.7}
  but degrades sharply at 0.4.
- Models used: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-{1.5B, 7B}, Skywork-OR1-7B-Preview,
  Qwen3-8B (note: these are *R1-distilled / reasoning-tuned* variants,
  *not* the base Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct used in P11 — the probe transfer
  to base models is itself a testable claim).
- Decoding for evaluation: temperature=0.6, top_p=0.95, max_len=16k
  (32k for AIME24).
- Fine-tuning: LlamaFactory; SFT and SimPO both used.

## Replicable intermediates

- **`"So, I'm"`-probe correctness AUROC on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B MATH-500.**
  Forward-pass per problem: load cached prompt through `pathway11_h100`'s
  Qwen-2.5-1.5B harness, run greedy generation (already in cache), append
  the probing prompt at FAS (= post-answer-newline token, equivalent to
  PANL used in F-7 / FE18), read out next-token logits, sum the four token
  probabilities, log against the `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/results.json`
  correctness labels. Compute AUROC. Cost: ~30 min H100, no new training.
  Direct head-to-head with F-2's 0.7731.
- **te-sweep risk-compute curve.** Once the probe is logged, sweep te ∈
  {0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8}; for each, mark "would-stop-here"
  positions, count tokens-up-to-stop, compute accuracy on the K=1 final
  answers reached at stop. Overlay against F-8's risk-coverage curve from
  the same JSON. Cost: ~5 min CPU on the logged data.
- **`cos(So-Im_dir, L19_DoM)`.** Pull the unembedding rows for `confident`
  and `sure`, project through `(W_unembed^+ ∘ LayerNorm^-1)` to recover an
  L19 residual-space direction, compute cosine with the existing
  `prefill_DoM`. Cost: ~10 min CPU using the existing extractor.
- **None for the SimPO fine-tuning headline (50% compression at maintained
  acc)** — that result needs fresh training runs we won't do.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2505.04881 — ConCISE: Confidence-guided Compression in Step-by-step Efficient Reasoning (Qiao, Deng, Zeng, et al., 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct competitor to F-2 / F-8: ConCISE introduces the
cheapest possible correctness probe — a fixed `"So, I'm"` probing prompt
appended to the partial reasoning chain, with c_hat computed as a sum
of four next-token probabilities. No fine-tuning, no labels, no internal
layer extraction. They use it to gate early-stopping during reasoning
(ConCISE-Decoding) and to construct compression-fine-tuning data
(ConCISE-SFT, ConCISE-SimPO) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B,
Skywork-OR1-7B, Qwen3-8B. Headline: ~50% length compression with <2pt
accuracy loss across MATH-500 / GSM8K / AIME24 / GPQA-diamond. The
mechanism is structurally a thresholded probe at the answer-token,
which is exactly what F-8 does with prefill DoM — making this the
most direct head-to-head test we have for whether the L19 DoM signal
is in the unembedding or in the residual stream.

**Key claim we tested:** A label-free output-distribution probe at
the answer-token can act as a correctness predictor strong enough to
gate compute decisions with minimal accuracy loss.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** (FE181 / H-181 will compute the AUROC
head-to-head against F-2's 0.7731). Until the AUROC is logged on
Qwen-2.5-1.5B, the relevance for our pipeline is hypothetical —
their evidence is on R1-distilled models which may have different
output-distribution calibration than base Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE181, P11-FE182, P11-FE183, H-181,
H-182, H-183. Cross-link with H-19 (C_exact verification routing,
Kumaran 2604.22271) — same verify-then-stop pattern, different probe.

**Status:** TO TEST
