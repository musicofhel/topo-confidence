# Triage brief — 2410.11042 — Persistent Topological Features in Large Language Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11042
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-10 (PH = Gaussian null on trained streams) if zigzag descriptors
   beat a rank-matched Gaussian null on layer pruning.** The paper's headline
   downstream task is layer pruning via plateau of ¯Z_1 (inter-layer persistence
   for short-lived features), claimed comparable to Gromov 2403.17887 angular
   distance and Men 2403.03853 Block-Influence. F-10 only ruled out PH on
   *correctness prediction* (AUROC 0.690 trained, 0.693 Gaussian null on same
   features at L19). The paper does *no* Gaussian-null sanity check (only a
   token-shuffling test in App. F that preserves unigram statistics). Mechanism:
   if the prunable plateau is just the locus of low intrinsic-dim, the same
   plateau will appear in any rank-matched isotropic-Gaussian point cloud — so
   their "comparable to SOTA" claim would be a function of intrinsic-dim
   trajectory, not topology, and F-10's "PH null on trained streams" extends to
   pruning. Falsifiable: replace each layer's residual stream with rank-matched
   Gaussian samples (same per-layer empirical covariance), re-run their kNN
   filtration → ¯Z_1 → 10%-of-max prune set; if the prune set is similar and
   benchmarks ≈ same, F-10 is *strengthened* (PH null generalizes). If their
   trained-LLM prune set markedly outperforms the Gaussian-matched prune set,
   F-10's "PH = null" claim is *narrower than stated* — null-on-correctness but
   not null-on-pruning.
2. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM is a strong correctness predictor) if their
   four-phase map places L19 of Qwen-2.5-1.5B in the prunable third-phase
   plateau.** They divide 32-layer Llama/Mistral/Pythia into four phases via
   ¯Z_1 dynamics; their pruning criterion marks third-phase plateau layers as
   removable. For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 layers), L19 sits at ~68% depth — exactly
   the region where their algorithm prunes third-phase layers in Llama 2/3 and
   Mistral. F-2 says L19 carries correctness signal at AUROC 0.7731 (OOF
   5-fold). If their pruning algorithm marks L19 as prunable on Qwen-1.5B and
   the model still performs well after removing it, F-2's claim that L19 is the
   *correctness-bearing* layer is undermined: the AUROC may reflect a static
   linear readout of an upstream layer's content that any layer in the plateau
   carries equivalently. Falsifiable: run their pipeline on Qwen-2.5-1.5B at
   1024-tok; check (a) whether L19 ∈ prune set, (b) MATH-500 accuracy after
   removing L19.
3. **Refutes F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across scales) if zigzag
   phase boundaries differ qualitatively between Qwen-1.5B and Qwen-7B at
   matched 1024-tok generation.** They claim qualitative universality across
   four 32-layer ~7B models; F-1 claims dimensional-breathing universality
   across architectures *and* scales (1.5B vs 7B). Both papers stop short of
   testing scale × architecture interactions. Mechanism: zigzag phase maps are
   sensitive to absolute layer count and intrinsic-dim trajectory shape; if
   1.5B's third phase is squashed or absent, the topological universality is
   actually a 7B-class artifact. Falsifiable: extract zigzag descriptors on
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B and 7B from cached pathway11_h100 NPZs; if peak layer (in
   normalized depth) and phase widths agree to ≤2 layers, F-1 strengthened;
   otherwise F-1 narrowed.
4. **Refutes F-9 (CoE-60 trajectory features redundant with single-layer L19
   DoM) if zigzag descriptors built across all 28 layers carry residual signal
   beyond L19 DoM in correctness prediction.** F-9 claims multi-layer
   trajectory features (CoE-60) collapse to single-layer linear DoM at AUROC
   0.7731. The paper builds layer-by-layer topological features that are *not*
   linear functions of any single layer — they encode kNN-graph evolution.
   Mechanism: if their B_1 / ¯Z_1 curves added to L19-DoM gain ≥ 0.02 AUROC on
   MATH-500 1024-tok, F-9's "redundant" framing fails for at least one
   trajectory parameterization, and the project's "single-layer L19 DoM is
   sufficient" stance needs amendment. Falsifiable: fit logistic regression on
   {L19 DoM} vs {L19 DoM ⊕ B_1(ℓ) and ¯Z_1(ℓ) for ℓ=1..28}, OOF 5-fold on
   pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute label set.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10 (PH null at AUROC 0.690 vs 0.693 Gaussian)** — paper provides exactly
  the missing positive-result counterpart. Their entire pipeline runs PH on LLM
  activations *without* a Gaussian-null sanity check; F-10 is the missing
  control they don't run.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — paper probes the same
  last-token-of-prompt location as our prefill probe ("we choose to focus on
  the last token representation of each sequence at each layer"). They never
  test correctness prediction; we did.
- **F-1 (breathing universal across architectures and scales)** — paper claims
  qualitative universality across 4 models (Llama2/3, Mistral, Pythia 6.9B),
  all 32 layers. Their inter-layer-persistence peak in middle layers is a
  topological echo of the intrinsic-dim peak Valeriani 2023 (their ref [13])
  and our F-1 breathing. Independent measurement → corroboration.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM)** — paper's B_1 and ¯Z_1 are alternative
  multi-layer trajectory featurizations to CoE-60. If F-9 generalizes, they
  too should be redundant with L19 DoM on the correctness task.
- **F-7 (D-bucket distinctive collective signature)** — paper's *collective*
  framing (births/persistence are point-cloud-level, not per-prompt) is
  conceptually the same lens as F-7's collective D-bucket signature.
- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** —
  zigzag features could supply a layer-level-only attribution; would need
  attention-head-conditioned filtration to address H-13 directly.
- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT shows compressed or qualitatively different breathing)**
  — their phase characterization is a candidate measurement to apply to
  Abstract-CoT runs once available.

## Methodologies extracted

- **kNN-graph filtration with k=4 over residual streams** — instead of
  Vietoris-Rips at fixed radius, build kNN graph at each layer and expand to
  m-dimensional simplices.
  *Replication cost*: 20–60 min CPU on cached pathway11_h100 NPZs
  (residual_stream_layers/L*.npz) for 500 prompts × 28 layers.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — drop-in alternative to the
  Vietoris-Rips-on-coordinates filtration that produced F-10's null result; a
  kNN-stable filtration was specifically untested in EXP-026.
- **Zigzag persistence across layer sequence (intersection layers as time
  steps)** — turns layers into time index, computes barcode for features
  appearing/disappearing across layer transitions. Uses Dionysus2 + FastZigZag
  ([59] Dey & Hou).
  *Replication cost*: ~2h CPU per model with their 10K-point default; ~15 min
  with 500 prompts.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly replicates their headline pipeline
  for our F-10 / F-9 refutation tests on Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
- **Effective Persistence Image (cPI_p)** — collapses intersection-layer
  contribution into model layer indices via the 4-cell sum
  cPI_p(b/2, d/2) = Σ PI_p(b−i, d−j) for i,j ∈ {0,1}.
  *Replication cost*: CPU minutes once barcodes computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — needed to compare phase boundaries to F-1
  breathing curves.
- **Births' relative frequency B_p(ℓ) with weight |ℓ−ℓ_i|^α** — power-weighted
  summary; α<0 emphasizes short-lived, α>0 long-lived features.
  *Replication cost*: trivial post-barcode.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes.
- **Inter-layer persistence ¯Z_p(ℓ)** — power-weighted average of
  persistence across layer pairs, normalized by Betti number.
  *Replication cost*: trivial post-barcode.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — this is their pruning criterion.
- **Pruning criterion: layers within 10% of max ¯Z_1 are prunable** —
  data-driven, unsupervised, dataset = Pile.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU per model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — provides a direct test of refutation R2 on
  Qwen-1.5B.
- **Sliding-window pruning sweep (5-block window, stride 2)** — empirical map
  from prune location to performance, used to validate phase identification.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100 per model on a 5-shot benchmark
  (lm-eval-harness MMLU/HellaSwag/Winogrande).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful but expensive; lower priority than
  the unsupervised pruning criterion check.
- **Token-shuffling control (App. F)** — keeps unigram frequencies, kills
  semantic structure. They observe drop in long-lived ¯Z_1 in second half but
  short-lived B_1 unchanged.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — Gaussian null is the more informative
  control given F-10.

## Approaches & framings

- **Zigzag-as-time-evolution**: treat layers as discrete time and compute PH on
  the layer sequence rather than within a single layer. This is a substantive
  reframing — F-10's null was on per-layer Vietoris-Rips PH; zigzag is a
  *different* TDA primitive. Worth re-running F-10 under their primitive
  before extending the "PH = null" claim.
- **Four phases (rapid → stable → transition → final)**: a topological version
  of the phase decomposition that F-1 finds via PR (and Cheng 2023 / Valeriani
  2023 find via intrinsic dim). Cross-check: do their phase boundaries (in
  normalized depth) line up with the breathing inflection points in F-1?
- **Pruning as showcase, not endpoint**: they explicitly position their
  descriptors as expressive and use pruning as one downstream task — not a
  unique application. Suggests their B_p / ¯Z_p curves could be used as features
  for any model-level prediction, including our correctness task.
- **Performance correlates *inversely* with peak ¯Z_1**: across their 4 models,
  Pythia (worst) has highest peak; Mistral and Llama 3 (best) lowest. If true
  in general, this is a model-level correctness predictor — but n=4 with high
  collinearity (parameter count, training tokens). Predictive of nothing
  per-prompt.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Zigzag library stack: **Dionysus2** + **FastZigZag** ([59] Dey & Hou,
  https://github.com/taohou01/fzz). Their repo:
  https://github.com/RitAreaSciencePark/ZigZagLLMs (note: the prior admission
  note's repo URL "RitaSciencePark/topo_llm" is wrong; correct repo is
  RitAreaSciencePark/ZigZagLLMs).
- Default hyperparameters: kNN=4, max simplex dimension m=4 (so p ∈ {0,1,2,3}),
  500-prompt subsets, 16 subsets for mean ± std.
- Last-token-of-prompt extracted from each *normalization layer* + final norm
  applied to output layer. (Same probe location as our prefill probe — the
  geometry is computed at the same residual-stream-after-layer-norm point we
  use.)
- Computational cost they report: 10K points × 4096 dim × 32 layers, kNN=10,
  m=10 → ~2h on AMD EPYC 7H12. Our case (500 × 1536 × 28, kNN=4, m=4) should
  be <30 min CPU per model.
- "1-dim holes are the only homology dimension worth tracking" empirically:
  H_0/H_2/H_3 give too few features for kNN graphs at small k.
- They use **Pile as proxy for the prune criterion** even when evaluating on
  MMLU/HellaSwag/Winogrande — i.e., the pruning is *unsupervised at the
  benchmark*. This is a generalization-relevant property to preserve in any
  replication.

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2410.11042 — Persistent Topological Features in Large Language Models (Gardinazzi, Viswanathan, Panerai, Ansuini, Cazzaniga, Biagetti, 2024 / v3 Jun 2025)

**Relevance:** Applies zigzag persistence (a TDA primitive distinct from the
Vietoris-Rips PH that F-10 ruled out) to LLM residual streams, treating layers
as time. Builds two descriptors — births' relative frequency B_p and
inter-layer persistence bar Z_p — and uses them to identify a four-phase
processing trajectory and to define an unsupervised layer-pruning criterion
(layers within 10% of max bar Z_1 on Pile). Experiments on Llama 2/3 7B,
Mistral 7B, Pythia 6.9B with last-token-of-prompt activations (same probe
location as our prefill probe). Pruning is competitive with Gromov 2403.17887
angular distance and Men 2403.03853 Block-Influence. **No Gaussian null
control** — only a token-shuffling test that preserves unigram statistics.

**Key claim we tested:** Zigzag-PH descriptors are expressive enough to (a)
identify processing phases that correlate inversely with model performance
(n=4) and (b) drive layer pruning competitively with similarity-based methods.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** Their results are consistent with F-1's
breathing-phase narrative qualitatively, but the missing Gaussian-null control
means we cannot tell whether their pruning signal is topological structure or
a function of intrinsic-dim trajectory. F-10 ruled out PH-on-correctness as a
Gaussian-null-distinguishable signal at a single layer; zigzag is a different
primitive and remains untested. Two READY experiments (P11-FE92, P11-FE93)
will close the gap with ~2h CPU + 30min H100.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE92 (zigzag PH-null), P11-FE93 (L19-prunability),
P11-FE94 (zigzag descriptors vs L19 DoM in correctness prediction); H-95, H-96.

**Status:** TO TEST
