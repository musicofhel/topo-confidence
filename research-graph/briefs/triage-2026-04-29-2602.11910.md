# Triage brief — 2602.11910 — TADA! Tuning Audio Diffusion Models through Activation Steering

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11910
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Łukasz Staniszewski, Katarzyna Zaleska, Mateusz Modrzejewski, Kamil Deja (Warsaw University of Technology / IDEAS Research Institute), Feb 2026.

Domain: text-to-music audio diffusion (AudioLDM2, Stable Audio Open, Ace-Step) — **not LLMs**. The prior admission note flagged it for layer-localization + activation-steering methodology overlap; this brief takes that seriously while keeping in mind that the substrate is cross-attention in a diffusion U-Net / DiT, not autoregressive residual streams.

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2's "single layer L19" framing — concepts may live in a *small shared subset*, not one layer.**
   Mechanism: TADA's activation-patching results show concept control concentrates in **2–4** cross-attention layers per architecture (AudioLDM2: {44,45,50,51} of 64; Ace-Step: {6,7} of 24; Stable Audio Open: {11,12} of 24), and the same small subset controls *all* tested concepts (vocals, tempo, mood, genres, instruments). If correctness is "just another concept," our F-2 claim that L19 alone carries the prefill signal in Qwen-2.5-1.5B is undercomplete: a 2-3 layer ensemble (e.g. {L18, L19, L20}) might be the true functional bottleneck and could exceed AUROC 0.7731.
   Falsifiable experiment: refit the 5-fold OOF prefill DoM probe over concatenated layers L17–L21 (and broader sweeps). If the best k≥2 ensemble beats single-layer L19 by ≥0.02 AUROC on the same fold splits, F-2 needs to be reframed as "small subset of mid-layers" rather than "L19 specifically." The cached P11 H100 prefill activations have all 28 layers — this is a CPU-only re-fit on existing data.

2. **Refutes the P10 v1 "fixed-vector steering universally fails" verdict that motivates H-1's per-position bank.**
   Mechanism: TADA shows fixed-vector CAA succeeds in audio diffusion, where the steering vector is computed from temporal-frame-averaged activations and applied at the cross-attention output of *one* layer at *one* point in the residual update, with a **ReNorm** step that rescales the post-intervention vector to match the pre-intervention activation norm. The dimensions our P10 v1 differs on are: (a) we steered across many AR positions (DoM rotates), (b) we may not have re-normalized. If failure mode (a) is the only true cause, then a TADA-style **prefill-only, ReNormed CAA at L19** should still shift correctness — closer in spirit to the diffusion setting where steering is a single-shot intervention, not a per-token loop. If it does shift accuracy, H-1's "per-position DoM bank" infrastructure is overkill: a single ReNormed prefill CAA may suffice.
   Falsifiable experiment: implement TADA-style CAA (mean-difference vector from correct/incorrect prefill activations at L19, ReNormed) applied **only at prefill** (not during generation), sweep α ∈ {-100, ..., 100}, measure MATH-500 K=1 accuracy. If accuracy moves monotonically with α, the H-1 "rotation" frame is wrong about prefill.

3. **Refutes F-9's "CoE is redundant with single-layer DoM" framing if SAEs decompose L19 into multiple semantic features.**
   Mechanism: TADA shows that **TopK SAEs (m=4, k=64) trained on the dominant functional layer recover sparse, concept-specific features** — and their TF-IDF scoring (Eq. 6: μ_j(P_c) / (μ_j(P_c̃) + ε), log-modulated) reliably picks per-concept features that enable *more precise* steering than dense CAA. If our L19 prefill direction is actually a superposition of (correctness, difficulty, problem-type, length) features, then a TopK SAE on cached L19 activations should:
     - Recover a single sparse "correctness" feature with AUROC ≥ 0.7731 (and possibly higher than DoM, undercutting the "single direction" framing).
     - Recover *additional* features (difficulty, length) that, combined, beat L19 DoM and may *also* beat CoE-60 — turning F-9's "CoE = DoM" equivalence into "CoE is a coarse proxy for a sparse set of L19 features that SAE can isolate cleaner."
   Falsifiable experiment: train a TopK SAE (m=4, k=64) on the cached 1.5B L19 prefill activations from `pathway11_h100/`, score features with TADA's Eq. 6 (correct vs incorrect), pick the top feature, compute correctness AUROC. If best-feature AUROC exceeds 0.7731 by ≥0.02, the F-2/F-9 "single direction" frame is wrong.

4. **Refutes F-7's "D-bucket has only a collective signature, not per-problem" claim.**
   Mechanism: F-7 says we couldn't find a per-problem geometric distinguisher of D-bucket (K=1 right, K=8 wrong). TADA's TF-IDF feature score is exactly the tool that would surface a sparse per-problem feature if one exists — it's specifically designed to find features that fire on a small subset and are silent elsewhere. If a TopK SAE trained on prefill L19 has a feature that scores high under TF-IDF for the 36 D-bucket problems vs the 207 A-bucket problems (using their Eq. 6 with P_c = D-bucket prompts, P_c̃ = A-bucket prompts), F-7 falls.
   Falsifiable experiment: Reuse the SAE from refutation 3, re-score features with D vs A bucket as the contrast set, threshold the top feature on out-of-fold problems. If hit-rate on D-bucket exceeds 50% with FPR ≤ 20%, F-7 is wrong about "no per-problem signature."

## Methodologies extracted

- **Activation patching with K/V substitution at cross-attention** — Cache cross-attention keys K_l = cW_K and values V_l = cW_V on a "target" run with prompt P_c (concept-bearing); on a "source" run with counterfactual prompt P_c̃, substitute K/V at layer l from the cached run; measure how much the patched generation matches concept c via audio-text similarity. Direct port to LLMs: substitute K/V at layer L during prefill on a "wrong" problem with K/V cached from a "right" problem, see if the model's correctness improves.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100 for full 28-layer × 500-problem sweep on 1.5B (much smaller than full retraining).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it's a clean alternative to DoM probing for finding the functional bottleneck and gives causal evidence (not just correlational AUROC).

- **Contrastive Activation Addition with ReNorm** — v_c = mean(h_c) − mean(h_c̃), normalized; apply as h'_l = ReNorm(h_l + α·v_c, h_l) where ReNorm rescales h'_l to match ||h_l||_2. The ReNorm step is the part our P10 v1 likely lacked.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached prefill activations + 4–6h H100 for the actual generation sweep across α.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — the cheapest test of refutation 2.

- **TopK Sparse Autoencoder (Gao et al. 2025; Bussmann et al. 2024 architecture)** — encoder W_enc ∈ R^{md×d}, decoder W_dec, bias b_pre; f = TopK(W_enc(h − b_pre)); reconstruct ĥ = W_dec f + b_pre. Hyperparameter search over m ∈ {2,4,8,16}, k ∈ {16,32,64}; selection criterion: lowest reconstruction error + lowest fraction of dead/high-frequency features.
  *Replication cost*: 4–8h CPU to train on ~50k cached L19 prefill activations (small relative to typical SAE training).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — central to refutations 3 and 4 and to H-12 upgrade.

- **TF-IDF feature importance scoring (their Eq. 6)** — score(j, c) = μ_j(P_c) · log(1 + 1/(μ_j(P_c̃) + ε)), where μ_j is mean activation of SAE feature j on prompt set P. Picks features that fire selectively on the concept set and are silent elsewhere.
  *Replication cost*: trivial (CPU minutes on a trained SAE).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — cleaner than our current top-AUROC feature selection.

- **Counterfactual prompt-pair generation via GPT-4** — Take captions containing concept c, replace concept-associated terms with counterparts via GPT-4 prompt while preserving everything else. Yields paired stimuli that differ only in c.
  *Replication cost*: a few API dollars; needs a "correctness" counterfactual definition for math (harder than swapping "violin" → "trumpet").
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for clean activation-patching contrasts but the math-correctness counterfactual is non-trivial. A first pass: pair MATH-500 problems with their "right next-step" vs "wrong next-step" continuations.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Functional layer specialization" as a general phenomenon across U-Net and Transformer diffusion architectures** — TADA argues this is *not* model-specific. Our F-1 says dimensional breathing is universal across transformer architectures (Qwen, Phi-3, Llama). TADA's universality result extends *across architecture families* (U-Net + DiT). If functional-layer specialization is genuinely architecture-agnostic, it's a co-finding with F-1 (both are generalization-across-arch claims about residual-stream geometry / function localization).

- **Causal patching > correlational probing as the primary tool** — TADA's whole pipeline is patching-first, then steering. Our F-2/F-3/F-7/F-9 are correlational (probe AUROC, OOF discrimination). Reframing: we should add a causal-patching layer-importance map for correctness on Qwen-1.5B as a cross-check on F-2. If patching at L19 alone changes correctness more than patching elsewhere, F-2's correlational story is corroborated; if patching reveals a different layer set (e.g. L23 patches change accuracy more than L19), F-2 is at risk.

- **"Steering localized > steering global" empirical regularity** — TADA explicitly compares (a) all-layer steering vs (b) functional-layer-only steering vs (c) non-functional-layer steering, and finds (b) wins on Δalignment and preservation. For us, this is the cleanest argument *against* multi-layer ensembles like CoE-60 winning over single-layer probes — IF TADA's regularity transfers.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **AudioLDM2** (U-Net, 64 cross-attentions): functional layers at {44, 45, 50, 51}, slight contribution from {46–49}.
- **Ace-Step** (transformer, 24 cross-attentions): functional layers at {6, 7} — middle of the stack.
- **Stable Audio Open** (transformer, 24 cross-attentions): functional layers at {11, 12} — also middle. *Note that L19 in 28-layer Qwen is also middle-to-late, similar relative depth.*
- **CAA strength sweep**: α ∈ {−100, −90, …, 90, 100} (large magnitudes — they explicitly need ReNorm to keep the activation norm bounded).
- **SAE config (Ace-Step layer 7)**: expansion m = 4, k = 64. Selection criterion: lowest reconstruction error and lowest fraction of dead + high-frequency features (their App. B.1–B.3).
- **Audio sample count**: 8 seeds × 256 prompts = 2048 generations per concept for activation-patching impact estimation.
- **Evaluation metrics for steering**: Preservation (LPAPS, FAD vs unsteered baseline), ΔAlignment (CLAP/MuQ similarity at α_max − α_min), Smoothness (std of consecutive Δalignment), Audio Quality (Audiobox CE/CU/PC/PQ).
- **Code**: https://github.com/luk-st/steer-audio (referenced in paper).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2602.11910 — TADA! Tuning Audio Diffusion Models through Activation Steering (Staniszewski, Zaleska, Modrzejewski, Deja, 2026)

**Relevance:** Domain is text-to-music audio diffusion (AudioLDM2, Stable Audio Open, Ace-Step), not LLMs. Methodologically directly relevant: activation patching, Contrastive Activation Addition with ReNorm, TopK Sparse Autoencoders with TF-IDF feature scoring, all applied to cross-attention layer outputs. Three findings transfer cleanly as testable hypotheses: (1) functional concepts concentrate in a small *shared* subset of mid-layers (2–4 of 24–64) — refutes F-2's single-layer claim if it generalizes, (2) ReNormed fixed-vector CAA succeeds — undermines our P10 v1 fixed-vector kill if applied prefill-only, (3) TopK SAE + TF-IDF scoring beats dense steering vectors — direct upgrade for H-12.

**Key claim we tested:** "Semantic concepts in deep generative models concentrate in a small, shared subset of attention layers, and steering localized to those layers is more effective than global steering."
