# Triage brief — 2511.15210 — Unveiling Intrinsic Dimension of Texts: from Academic Abstract to Creative Story

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15210
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-10 ("PH on trained residual streams is at the Gaussian null") is contradicted in the cross-genre regime.** Pedashenko et al. compute PHDim on residual-stream embeddings of Gemma-2-2B, **Qwen-2.5-1.5B**, and RoBERTa-base across 22 corpora and find PHDim cleanly stratifies texts into low (~7.5–8: arxiv, pubmed, sci_gen), medium (~8–9: cnn, wikipedia), and high (~9–10.5: wp, eli5, cmv) bands with cross-estimator pairwise r > 0.67 and PHDim ↔ MLE r > 0.86 on the same embeddings (their §5.1). If PHDim were at the Gaussian null on trained residual streams (F-10/EXP-026/EXP-018), there would be no genre signal — the agreement of three independent estimators across three model families directly refutes "PH = null" as a global statement. *Mechanism*: F-10 was measured on a homogeneous-genre prompt corpus (MATH-500 problems), where between-prompt PHDim variance is small and gets washed out by within-corpus noise; the Gaussian null may hold *within* a single text genre but fails when the prompt distribution spans genres. *Falsifiable test*: assemble a 500-text corpus mixing 250 MATH-500 problems with 250 wp/eli5 stories from the COLING dataset, extract Qwen-2.5-1.5B residual states at the same layer used for F-10, run our existing PH script (`pathway8_layer_pcb/persistence.py`). If PHDim discriminates genre with cross-validated AUROC > 0.8, F-10 must be re-scoped to "PH = null *within* prompt genre," not "PH = null on trained residuals."

2. **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) may be a length/lexical-diversity signal in disguise.** The paper's central empirical claim is that PHDim and (cross-)entropy are *uncorrelated* after controlling for length (r ≈ -0.005 for normalized cross-entropy vs PHDim; r = 0.301 raw, dropping to noise once length is regressed out, their Fig 1). This means: any scalar derived purely from hidden-state geometry (which is what L19 prefill DoM is, modulo a label-fitted projection) should be approximately uncorrelated with raw model uncertainty *after length control*. Yet the L19 DoM AUROC is 0.7731 against a label that is itself confounded with problem length and surface lexical features (longer multi-step MATH-500 problems tend to fail more often). *Mechanism*: prefill L19 DoM may be picking up "is this prompt long/lexically-diverse/templated" rather than "is this prompt one the model can solve." *Falsifiable test*: regress prefill L19 DoM scalar on per-prompt PHDim + token length + TAACO function-word ratio + lemma-TTR (the four strongest TAACO predictors of PHDim in the paper's Fig 4) using the existing 500-problem MATH cache. If residual AUROC < 0.65 (vs. 0.7731 raw), F-2 needs to be reframed as a hybrid lexical + correctness signal, not a pure correctness predictor.

3. **F-7 ("D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature") is plausibly a prompt-genre artifact, not a reasoning-fragility signature.** The paper shows ~3 ID units of variation arise purely from genre/style (~7.5 for arxiv vs ~10.5 for wp), with strong positive correlation with TAACO lexical-diversity metrics (lemma-MATTR, trigram-lemma-TTR, all r > 0.4 in Fig 4) and negative correlation with cross-sentence overlap. D-bucket problems may simply be the long-prompt or lexically-diverse subset of MATH-500. *Mechanism*: collective geometry of D-bucket residuals reflects prompt surface form (length, n-gram diversity, sentence overlap), not a hidden "fragile reasoning pathway." *Falsifiable test*: compute TAACO features on the 36 D-bucket vs 207 A-bucket prompt strings; if D-bucket prompts have systematically higher lemma-MATTR or longer length, F-7's "distinctive geometric signature" can be reproduced from prompt-string features alone (CPU only, ~30 min on cached `pathway11_h100/d_bucket_problems.json`). Reject F-7's mechanism if surface-feature classifier reaches AUROC > 0.85 separating D from A buckets.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (Dimensional breathing universal across architectures)**: The paper finds PHDim concentrates in a narrow 5–15 band across Gemma-2-2B / Qwen-2.5-1.5B / RoBERTa-base / OPT / LLaMA / Mixtral / GPT / T5 — a cross-architecture invariant. This is *consistent* with F-1's universality claim about breathing, but reframes the invariant as a property of *text*, not of *reasoning dynamics*: the 5–15 band emerges on **static text embeddings**, no autoregressive generation required. Compare to our peak PR ≈ 67/88 at 1024-tok mid-generation: this is well above the static text band, suggesting the inflation portion of F-1 breathing is genuinely AR-mechanical, but the prefill/final-token endpoints (PR ~ 10–20) sit inside Pedashenko's static band. AUROC anchor: 0.7731 (F-2 prefill DoM).
- **F-5 (Breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics)**: Strongly *corroborated*. Pedashenko's central finding is that ID is content-driven (genre × style), with TAACO lexical-diversity features being the strongest correlates. Our F-5 sat on small-N evidence; this paper provides external 172,750-text validation that text-content drives geometry of LM hidden states.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM)**: The paper's finding that effective *global linear embedding dimension* peaks at k ≈ 60 (peak PHDim–EV-k correlation, their §5.2) is a striking numerical match to our CoE-60 dimensionality. Suggests CoE-60 is approximating the global linear embedding of the residual manifold; redundancy with L19 DoM (F-9) follows naturally if L19 lies inside that ~60-dim manifold. Anchor: cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 → orthogonality consistent with manifold curvature.
- **H-9 (Breathing amplitude predicts difficulty)**: The paper offers a confounder. ID is genre/lexical-diversity-driven, not reasoning-difficulty-driven; if breathing amplitude correlates with difficulty in our data, it could be mediated by problem-statement lexical complexity rather than reasoning depth.
- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves accuracy)**: Methodology directly importable. Pedashenko's steering equation `x' = x + λ A_i d_i` (where `A_i` is the feature's max activation on a held-out set, `λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 15}`) is a calibrated, reference-scale-normalized steering protocol that we should adopt for the H-1 per-position DoM steering test instead of ad-hoc unit-norm interventions.
- **H-16 (Marchenko-Pastur bias correction preserves breathing magnitude)**: Tangential connection — both papers warn about ID-estimator bias in finite samples; Pedashenko explicitly notes PHDim is computed on random subsamples and exhibits run-to-run variance. Reinforces H-16's importance.
- **H-22 (Abstract-CoT compressed breathing)**: Pedashenko's finding that ID is content-not-AR makes a strong prior prediction: Abstract-CoT (which retains computation but compresses verbalization) should preserve PR amplitude if breathing is content-driven, and reduce it if breathing is AR-mechanical.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PHDim (Persistent Homology Dimension) estimator** — Persistent-homology-based ID via Schweinhart's upper-box-dimension framework (Tulchinskii et al. 2023b). Combines local + global geometry; comparatively stable under domain shift / noise.
  *Replication cost*: 10 min CPU on cached prefill activations (~500 prompts × 1.5k dim).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — re-cast EXP-026 with PHDim computed *across* MATH-500 problems with paired non-math text to test refutation #1.

- **TwoNN, MLE, TLE estimators** — Triangulating ID via complementary nearest-neighbor / extreme-value estimators. Cross-estimator pairwise r > 0.45; PHDim ↔ MLE r > 0.86.
  *Replication cost*: 5 min CPU each (sklearn / scikit-dimension).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — sanity check on our prefill PR via ID-estimator triangulation; PR ≈ EV-rank, but MLE/TwoNN measure local manifold dimension, useful for comparing to participation ratio.

- **SAE feature steering with reference-scale normalization** — `x' = x + λ A_i d_i` where `A_i` is feature `i`'s max activation on a held-out set and `λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 15}`. Report largest λ that preserves coherence.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day if we install Gemma-Scope-equivalent SAEs for Qwen; ~2 h CPU if we use existing direction vectors with the same reference-scale rescaling.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — calibrated steering scale is a missing piece in H-1; we currently have no principled λ choice.

- **TAACO linguistic feature extraction** (Crossley et al. 2019) — Lexical diversity, cohesion, sentence-to-sentence overlap. Used to externally validate ID-vs-content relationship.
  *Replication cost*: 20 min CPU on 500 MATH-500 prompts (pip install taaco).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — required to test refutation #2 (regress F-2 DoM on TAACO features) and refutation #3 (TAACO classifier for D vs A bucket).

- **Effective-rank EV-k correlation peak** — Compute correlation between PHDim and explained-variance-at-rank-k across many corpora; the peak k estimates the global linear embedding dimension. They report k ≈ 60.
  *Replication cost*: 10 min CPU on cached residuals.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of F-9's CoE-60 ≈ global-linear-dim hypothesis.

- **Extremal-activation analysis for SAE features** — Identify features by contrasting texts at activation quantiles, then assess domain specialization via average activation by corpus.
  *Replication cost*: 4 h CPU once SAEs available.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — useful for H-1 only if we get a Qwen-specific SAE; not core path.

- **Weisfeiler-Lehman kernel for syntactic-diversity** — Average pairwise WL-kernel distance between dependency-parse trees as a syntactic complexity scalar. Found to be uninformative for well-formed text (Pedashenko's §5.2).
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — paper itself says it's uninformative for well-formed prompts; MATH-500 prompts are well-formed.

## Approaches & framings

- **ID as a *complementary* axis to entropy.** Pedashenko frames ID and entropy as orthogonal complexity axes (geometric vs predictive); after length control they decouple (r ≈ -0.005 normalized). This is a sharper version of our F-2 vs F-1 separation: prefill DoM is a geometric signal, model uncertainty is a predictive signal — they should not be substitutes. Reinforces our framing of F-2 / F-8 as orthogonal to standard logprob-based selective prediction.

- **"Narrow operating band, far below ambient dimension."** The paper makes the point that all well-formed text concentrates in PHDim 5–15 despite ambient dim O(10²–10³). This is the same compression intuition as our PR ≪ d_model framing for breathing, generalized beyond AR generation. Useful for the eventual breathing write-up.

- **Genre/style as the dominant axis of geometric variance.** Treating genre as a primary covariate (rather than reasoning state) is a framing shift we should adopt: when computing per-problem geometric scalars, *always* report alongside surface-feature controls (length, lemma-TTR, function-word ratio).

- **ID as bounded-by-compression, not predicted-by-compression.** Wedge-shaped support `10·CR ≲ ID ≲ 25·CR`. Compression provides bounds, not pointwise prediction. Useful framing if we ever try to predict PR from prompt-level statistics.

- **COLING / GenAI Content Detection Task 1** (Wang et al. 2025) — 261,758 → 172,750 texts after filter (≥ 150 Gemma tokens, stable PHDim across estimators). 22 source domains: arxiv, pubmed, sci_gen, wiki_csai, wikipedia, cnn, peerread, eli5, wp, imdb, cmv, etc. License: research; HF accessible. **Applicable**: yes — direct source for the genre-mixed corpus needed in refutation #1, and for stress-testing our prefill DoM signal on out-of-distribution text.

- **OneStopEnglish** (Vajjala & Lučić, 2018) — 189 human-authored texts × 3 proficiency levels = 567 total. License: research, HF accessible. **Applicable**: yes — proficiency analog of difficulty; useful for H-9 if reframed as "PR depends on prompt complexity" via a ground-truthed difficulty axis on non-math text.

- **RAID** (Dugan et al. 2024) — Robust AI-text detection benchmark. 1000 prompts × 2.0–0.2 temperature sweep; 20 prompts × 5 domains for steering. **Applicable**: maybe — temperature sweep is useful for D-bucket sampling-stability tests (H-21).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- **Embedder layer choice** — paper analyzes "all layers" but feature steering is anchored at Gemma layers 16, 24, 25 (mid-to-late residual). Suggests our L19 mid-layer choice is consistent with the empirically-useful regime.
- **Token-length floor** — texts < 150 Gemma tokens are filtered out due to PHDim instability. Important: our 1024-tok labels safely clear this floor; our 256-tok labels do *not* on average for math problems with short solutions. Another reason to deprecate 256-tok numbers (already in the graveyard but reinforces it).
- **Subsample variance** — PHDim is computed on random subsamples; report variance across runs (paper notes this in Limitations).
- **SAE source** — Gemma-Scope canonical residual SAE, width 16k. No Qwen analog publicly released yet (as of paper); H-1 per-position SAE steering is blocked on either a Qwen SAE release or training one in-house.
- **Steering λ ladder** — `λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 15}`, report largest coherence-preserving λ. Adopt this calibration for H-1.
- **Ambient-vs-effective dimension ratio** — global linear embedding ~60 vs ambient hundreds-to-thousands → ~17–34× compression. Caution against interpreting any per-token PR > 60 as the model "using more dimensions"; it's likely sampling-noise inflation.
- **Cross-encoder agreement** — Gemma and Qwen agree closely (high pairwise r) for a fixed estimator; RoBERTa diverges more. Suggests our Qwen↔Gemma comparisons (e.g., 1.5B↔7B) should generalize, but RoBERTa-baseline encoder comparisons need an extra control.

## 2511.15210 — Unveiling Intrinsic Dimension of Texts: from Academic Abstract to Creative Story (Pedashenko, Kushnareva, Khassan Nibal, Tulchinskii, Kuznetsov, Zharchinskii, Maximov, Piontkovskaya, 2025)

**Relevance:** Direct methodological and empirical neighbor. Uses **Qwen-2.5-1.5B** (our headline model), Gemma-2-2B, and RoBERTa-base as embedders for a battery of intrinsic-dimension estimators (PHDim, TwoNN, MLE, TLE) on 172,750 texts across 22 corpora. Establishes (i) a narrow operating band of PHDim ≈ 5–15 across architectures and genres, (ii) cross-estimator pairwise r > 0.45 on the same embeddings (PHDim ↔ MLE r > 0.86), (iii) ID and (cross-)entropy are *uncorrelated* after length control (r ≈ -0.005 normalized vs 0.301 raw), and (iv) ID is dominated by genre/style and TAACO lexical-diversity features. Provides external 172,750-text validation of F-5 (content-dependence of geometry) but raises sharp confound concerns for F-2 and F-7, and refutes any universal reading of F-10. The SAE feature-steering protocol (`x' = x + λ A_i d_i` with calibrated `λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 15}`) is directly applicable to H-1.
