# Triage brief — 2302.00294 — The geometry of hidden representations of large transformer models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00294
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **F-2 ("Prefill L19 DoM is the strong correctness predictor") would be refuted if the ID minimum for Qwen-2.5-1.5B sits at a different layer.** Valeriani–Doimo show that the *most semantically rich* representation is the one at the relative ID minimum after the first peak — an unsupervised layer-selection rule that is dataset/architecture-specific (relative depth ≈ 0.4 in iGPT, "plateau" in pLMs, ≈ 0.3 in Llama-v2-70B SST). For Qwen-2.5-1.5B (28 transformer blocks) L19 is at relative depth 19/28 ≈ 0.68, which by their typology sits *in the final-ascent phase*, not at an ID minimum. **Falsifiable test:** run TwoNN on cached per-layer prefill activations for Qwen-2.5-1.5B on MATH-500. If the ID minimum is at L≠19 (e.g. L8–L12) and a logistic probe at that layer's residual stream beats AUROC 0.7731 OOF, F-2's "L19 is privileged" framing is dataset-contingent rather than structural — the same probe at a different layer (chosen by an unsupervised geometric rule the paper provides) would outperform our headline number.

2. **F-1 (universality of dimensional breathing across architectures and scales) is in tension with the paper's own evidence that AR-LM ID profiles diverge qualitatively across model families.** The paper documents that GPT-2-XL on SST shows *no second peak*, whereas Llama-v2-70B on SST shows *three peaks and two minima* — i.e. depth-axis ID profiles are not architecturally universal among autoregressive LMs, even on the same task. Our F-1 claims breathing is universal across Qwen 1.5B / 7B / family-internal scales, but we have not stress-tested across families (GPT-2, Pythia, Llama, etc.) and have not formally compared the *shape* (number of peaks, qualitative phase structure) — only amplitude. **Falsifiable test:** run the per-token PR analysis on Pythia-1.4B and Llama-3-8B at matched MATH-500 prompts. If peak count or phase shape qualitatively differs from Qwen at the same parameter count, F-1's "universal" wording is too strong and must be downgraded to "Qwen-family-internal."

3. **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046, near-orthogonality interpreted as separate circuits) admits an autoencoder explanation that does not require independent circuits.** Valeriani–Doimo argue that large transformers are "sophisticated autoencoders" — the encoder compresses to a semantic minimum, then the decoder *re-expands* to reconstruct the input/output distribution. Under that picture, the prefill (post-encode) and final-token (post-decode) states are necessarily in different subspaces because the decoder re-introduces dimensions that the encoder discarded. F-3's "orthogonal → distinct mechanisms" inference is therefore consistent with a single autoencoder pipeline, not two circuits. **Falsifiable test:** measure DoM at the ID-minimum layer (call it L\*) for Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Predict (a) cos(DoM\_L\*, prefill\_DoM\_L19) ≈ 1 if prefill\_L19 sits in the post-encode subspace, and (b) cos(DoM\_L\*, final\_DoM\_L19) ≈ 0 because final\_L19 sits in the post-decode subspace. If both predictions hold, the orthogonality is a *layer geometry* artifact — not a circuit-level fact — and F-3 needs reframing.

4. **F-9 ("CoE-60 redundant with single-layer L19 DoM") is unstable if Qwen has more than one ID minimum.** The paper shows Llama-v2-70B on SST has *two* ID minima, with semantic content highest at the first. CoE features that integrate across all layers will capture *both* minima; a single-layer probe at L19 will capture at most one. F-9's redundancy claim assumes the optimal predictor is captured by one layer. **Falsifiable test:** if Qwen-2.5-1.5B's per-layer ID curve on MATH-500 shows ≥2 minima, refit a 2-layer concatenated probe at those minima and compare to CoE-60. If 2-layer-concat ≈ CoE-60 > single-L19 by ≥3 AUROC points, F-9 must be rephrased: "CoE-60 redundant with multi-minimum layer probe," not single-layer.

5. **H-5 ("prefill encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability") is at risk of being a re-discovery of the paper's "ID minimum encodes the dominant abstract feature."** The paper finds that the plateau / first-minimum representation encodes a *single dominant abstract attribute* per training objective: remote homology (proteins), class label (iGPT/ImageNet), sentiment (Llama/SST). H-5 posits a *richer* dual-axis structure (familiarity × decomposability). **Falsifiable test:** if we probe MATH-500 prefill at the ID-minimum layer for two label structures — (a) "model has seen this problem-type during training" and (b) "problem decomposes into sub-steps" — and find that one axis dominates while the other gives chance AUROC, H-5's dual-axis hypothesis collapses to "prefill encodes one MATH-relevant abstract feature, in line with this paper."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universal):** The paper's "ID profile across depth" is the *depth-axis analogue* of our temporal breathing. Their iGPT-L peak ID ≈ 32 at relative depth ~0.2 then min ≈ 22 at ~0.4 → second peak; our 1.5B per-token PR shows analogous expand-contract dynamics across token positions. Anchor: cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 is consistent with a depth-axis-style autoencoder reading.
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's unsupervised layer-selection rule (argmin ID) competes directly with our supervised choice of L19. If their rule picks L≠19 with comparable or better AUROC, F-2's privileged-layer claim is challenged.
- **F-3 (orthogonality, cos 0.046):** Reframes our orthogonality finding as encoder/decoder geometry rather than independent circuits. We currently have no neutral test distinguishing the two interpretations.
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective geometric signature):** The paper's *neighborhood overlap with ground-truth labels* (χ^{l,gt}_k) is a directly applicable probe for D-bucket vs A-bucket separability without training a classifier — a bucket-level Voronoi check.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 ≈ single-layer L19):** Their multi-minimum result for Llama-v2-70B suggests F-9 may be Qwen-1.5B-specific (single-minimum regime); test whether 7B has multiple minima.
- **F-10 (PH = Gaussian null on trained streams):** The paper measures ID with TwoNN, not PH. ID is finite and meaningful in their data, while PH (in our data) is null. This is a useful methodological-agnosticism check: ID-based geometry is informative where PH is not — strengthens F-10's "use the right tool" framing.
- **H-5:** see refutation 5.
- **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts difficulty):** Their χ^{l,gt} curves are *task-difficulty-agnostic* in shape (same across iGPT-S/M/L); the *amplitude* scales with model size (peak χ = 0.15 → 0.27 → 0.35). Maps onto H-9's "amplitude = difficulty" idea but with the prediction inverted (amplitude scales with *model capacity*, not problem difficulty).
- **H-16 (Marchenko–Pastur correction):** Same project's TwoNN tool family (DADApy / Glielmo et al. 2022); compatible with the MP bias-correction work in 2509.26560 already in our graph.

## Methodologies extracted

- **TwoNN intrinsic-dimension estimator (Facco et al. 2017, DADApy)** — ID inferred from the ratio µ_i = r_{i,2}/r_{i,1} of first-to-second NN distances; under locally-constant density µ_i is Pareto with shape = ID, fit by MLE or linear regression on the CDF.
  *Replication cost*: ~10 min CPU per layer for our Qwen prefill cache size (≤ 500 problems × 1536 dims).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests refutations 1, 4, and is the ID half of H-16.

- **Neighborhood overlap χ^{l,m}_k** — average fraction of common k-NN between layer l and layer m representations of the same data points; A^l_ij = 1 iff x^l_j is in top-k NN of x^l_i. Symmetric matrix on layer pairs.
  *Replication cost*: O(N² × L) per pair → ~1h CPU on cached activations for 500 problems × 28 layers, k=10.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct test of F-3 reinterpretation; cheaper alternative to training per-layer probes.

- **Neighborhood overlap with ground truth χ^{l,gt}_k** — same metric but A^{gt}_ij = 1 iff y_i = y_j. Unsupervised semantic-content probe (no probe to fit).
  *Replication cost*: ~5 min on cached layer activations once χ_k computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a probe-free analogue of our DoM AUROC, directly testable against F-2.

- **Average-pool over sequence dimension before distance computation** — collapses Rl×d sequences to a single Rd vector. Choice motivated by variable-length proteins; for Qwen MATH-500 we have variable token counts post-prefill.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — this is *different* from our last-token / single-position activation extraction; could change F-2's AUROC by ~5 points either direction.

- **ID-minimum unsupervised layer selection** — argmin_L ID(L) over the post-first-peak layers gives the "best for downstream task" layer without labels.
  *Replication cost*: trivial after TwoNN curve.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — produces an unsupervised competitor to our supervised L19 choice.

- **Layer-wise ID profile during training (checkpoint trajectories)** — repeat ID(L) at training-step checkpoints; first the peak emerges, then the plateau compresses, then the final-layer behavior diverges (re-expand for pLM, second peak for iGPT).
  *Replication cost*: needs Qwen pretraining checkpoints, which we don't have.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — checkpoints not available for Qwen-2.5; possible on Pythia.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Transformers as sophisticated autoencoders"** — the dominant framing of the paper. Encoder phase: data expansion to high-ID, then compression to low-ID semantic minimum. Decoder phase: re-expansion to reconstruct inputs/outputs. This is a *depth-axis* version of an idea our project applies on the *token-axis* (breathing). Direct intersection with F-1, F-3 reinterpretation.
- **"Optimal layer = relative ID minimum, identifiable without supervision"** — recasts the layer-selection problem from "train a probe at each layer and pick the best" to a geometric, unsupervised rule. Threatens F-2 (which uses supervised AUROC ranking to pick L19).
- **"Phase structure of the ID curve"** — ID profile decomposes into discrete *phases* (peak, plateau, ascent for pLMs; +second peak for iGPT; +three peaks for Llama-v2). This is a useful schema for our temporal breathing curves: do they decompose into the same kind of discrete phases?

## Implementation details worth capturing

- ID estimator: **DADApy** TwoNN, MLE or CDF-linear-regression fit to µ_i ratios. Pip-installable.
- Neighborhood overlap: k = 10 for proteins (288-class problem), k = 30 for images (300-class problem). Robustness across k shown in their Fig. S5.
- They extract activations **after the first normalization layer** of each block (pre-attn LayerNorm output), then **average-pool along sequence dim**.
- Verification that ID profile is **invariant to whether activations are extracted post-attention or post-MLP** (Fig. S4) — useful for our extraction-point sensitivity.
- ID profile is **not invariant** to the average-pool vs full-feature space choice (Fig. S3): pooling reduces ID magnitude but preserves shape on CIFAR10.
- Code: github.com/diegodoimo/geometry_representations (already in our graph as `repo_url`).
- Compute used: 2× V100 GPUs, 256GB RAM — implies their entire pipeline runs on consumer-tier hardware modulo activation extraction.
