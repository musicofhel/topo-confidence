# Triage brief — 2605.29971 — Causal Interventions on Continuous Variables: A Case Study on Verb Bias in Steering Vectors for In-Context Learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29971
Triaged: 2026-05-31

## Refutations

1. **Refutes the implicit assumption in H-1 that additive steering-vector injection is sufficient to move correctness.** Zhou et al. show that even when a continuous variable (verb bias) is localized in a PCA-derived direction and successfully edited, the *error signal* encoded in that same steering vector is "present but not naturally deployed" by additive injection. If the L19 DoM direction (F-2, AUROC 0.7731) contains an analogous decomposition — a bias component that steers additive preference and an error component that drives learning — then additive DoM steering (H-1) would shift the model's "confidence posture" without engaging the implicit-learning update that actually changes accuracy. Mechanism: run their counterfactual editing framework on DoM-extracted steering vectors and measure whether the correctness-predictive component vs. the error-residual component are independently causal. Falsifiable: if DoM steering at L19 produces monotonic accuracy gains proportional to edit strength α, the decomposition does not apply and the refutation fails.

2. **Challenges F-2's single-direction sufficiency claim via their multi-component editing result.** The paper finds that restricting edits to the top-5 PCA components (out of 50 retained) is sufficient for causal manipulation, but the *direction* that carries the continuous variable is not a single PC — it is a regression-weighted combination across components. If the same holds for our DoM direction, then cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216 may overstate PC1's causal role: the remaining 8% of variance in the DoM direction (spread across PC2–PC9+) could carry non-redundant causal signal. Mechanism: apply their beta-regression localization to our cached L19 prefill activations with correctness as the continuous target, compare the resulting β direction to DoM, and test whether counterfactual edits along DoM vs. β produce different downstream effects. Falsifiable: if the regression direction β and DoM are cosine-similar > 0.99, the multi-component concern is moot.

3. **Refutes the assumed layer-specificity of F-2 (L19 peak) by showing layer-dependent emergence patterns differ across variables.** Zhou et al. find verb bias decodability rises from r < 0.2 at layers 0–7 to r ≈ 0.8 at layer 10+ (stable thereafter), while the error signal peaks later at r ≈ 0.91 in layers 17–23, and causal steering effects only appear from layer 15+. This suggests multiple variables with different layer profiles can coexist in the same residual stream. Our F-2 pins DoM's peak to L19 for correctness prediction, but if correctness decomposes into a "confidence bias" component (peaking earlier) and an "error/difficulty" component (peaking later, as for F-5 content-dependence), then L19 may be capturing their superposition rather than a clean signal. Mechanism: replicate their leave-one-out regression diagnostic across all 28 Qwen layers, fitting continuous correctness labels, and check whether the decodability curve has a single peak or two distinct inflection points. Falsifiable: if the decodability curve shows a single monotonic rise plateauing at L19 with no secondary peak, the two-component layer story doesn't apply.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's core method — PCA + regression to localize a continuous variable as a direction in activation space — is directly analogous to how DoM is defined (mean-difference direction of correct vs. incorrect prefill activations). Their finding that 5 PCA components suffice for causal editing maps onto the 2-feat (PC1, PC9) AUROC of 0.7856 from FE291, suggesting the correctness signal may also be low-rank but not rank-1.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046):** The paper's layer-dependent emergence pattern (verb bias decodable by L10, error signal peaks L17–23, causal effects from L15+) provides a mechanistic analogy for why prefill and final-token DoM might be orthogonal — they could reflect different components of a decomposed signal at different processing stages.

- **H-1 (Per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy):** This is the most direct connection. The paper explicitly demonstrates that additive steering works for the bias component but *fails* to deploy the error component. If H-1's steering intervention hits the same wall — shifting confidence posture without engaging error-driven correction — it would explain a null result on accuracy.

- **F-5 (Breathing is content-dependent, not AR-mechanics):** The verb bias variable is an instance of content-dependent continuous variation in steering vectors. Their successful localization validates that content-graded features (not just binary correct/incorrect) can be isolated as directions.

- **H-5 (Prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs decomposability):** The paper's decomposition of steering vectors into bias + error components parallels the hypothesized decomposition of prefill signal into familiarity (bias-like) and decomposability (error-like) components.

## Methodologies extracted

- **PCA + beta-regression direction localization** — Reduce activations to k PCA components, normalize, fit beta regression (for bounded [0,1] targets) or OLS (for unbounded), yielding a direction β in PCA space. Counterfactual edits then move vectors along β with strength α.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU on cached NPZs (PCA already computed for FE291; beta regression is trivial).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to the 500×1536 L19 prefill activation matrix with correctness as the continuous target. Compare resulting β to DoM.

- **Counterfactual editing with minimal L₂-norm update** — Given a target counterfactual value y*, compute the minimum-norm perturbation Δz along the localized direction β to shift the predicted value from η to η*. Includes edit strength parameter α ∈ [0,1].
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU for the edit computation; but testing downstream effects requires forward passes (1–2h H100).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for the edit computation on cached activations; the downstream pass requires GPU budget.

- **Leave-one-verb-out (leave-one-out) diagnostic** — Hold out each category, fit regression on remaining, evaluate on held-out. Tests whether the localized direction generalizes or overfits to specific exemplars.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU — trivially adaptable to leave-one-problem-out on MATH-500.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful for validating DoM generalizability across MATH problem types.

- **Error signal decomposition** — Construct an error variable e = bias - structure (analogous to: e = predicted_correctness - actual_correctness), localize its direction separately, test whether it is causally deployed vs. merely present.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU for the decomposition; causal testing needs forward passes.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes for the decomposition on cached data; causal test needs GPU.

## Approaches & framings

- **Transient activation vs. implicit learning decomposition of ICL.** The paper frames in-context learning as decomposable into a fast activation-steering component (captured by task vectors) and a slower error-driven update component (present in representations but not deployed by additive injection). This intersects F-9 (CoE-60 redundancy with single-layer DoM) — the CoE trajectory might capture the error-driven temporal component that single-layer DoM misses, explaining why they're redundant within-layer but theoretically separable.

- **Continuous-variable causal intervention as alternative to binary probing.** Most prior work on probing (including our DoM) treats the target as binary (correct/incorrect). This paper treats it as continuous and uses regression rather than classification. For us, this suggests treating correctness as a continuous variable (e.g., partial credit, solution quality, or DoM projection magnitude itself as a graded signal) rather than a binary label could reveal finer structure — particularly relevant to F-7 (D-bucket collective signature) where the correct/incorrect boundary may be graded.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- PCA retains 50 components; edits restricted to top 5 by regression coefficient magnitude. This is a useful hyperparameter anchor for our own PCA-based analyses (FE291 used full PCA; their 5-component subset is much sparser).
- Beta regression used for bounded [0,1] variables (verb bias); OLS for unbounded. Our correctness is binary but DoM projection is unbounded — OLS is appropriate.
- Normalization step (zero mean, unit variance per PC) before regression is critical for interpretability of β coefficients.
- Models: Llama-2-13B primary (40 layers, 5120-d), Llama-3-1B/8B for robustness. ~80 H200 GPU-hours total.
- Steering vectors averaged over 10 sequences per condition — analogous to our per-problem averaging.

## 2605.29971 — Causal Interventions on Continuous Variables: A Case Study on Verb Bias in Steering Vectors for In-Context Learning (Zhou, McCoy, Frank, 2026)

**Relevance:** Introduces a principled framework for localizing continuous variables as directions in activation space via PCA + regression, and performing graded counterfactual edits along those directions. Though the domain is psycholinguistic (verb bias in structural priming), the method is directly applicable to our L19 DoM direction and correctness prediction. The key finding — that error signals are representationally present but not causally deployed by additive steering — directly bears on H-1 (DoM steering) and the bias/error decomposition of F-2.

**Key claim we tested:** Continuous variables can be localized as regression-derived directions in PCA space of hidden states, and counterfactual edits along those directions produce graded behavioral shifts — but additive steering vectors fail to deploy error-driven update signals even though they encode them.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — Four Tier-1 CPU experiments (P11-FE130500 through FE130503) directly test whether their PCA+regression localization, leave-one-out stability diagnostic, error decomposition, and multi-component sparsity findings apply to our L19 prefill DoM on MATH-500. One Tier-2 GPU experiment (P11-FE130504) tests the causal deployment question.

**Related experiments:** P11-FE130500, P11-FE130501, P11-FE130502, P11-FE130503, P11-FE130504, H-930, H-931
