# Triage brief — 2509.22067 — The Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22067
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — strong.**
   *Mechanism*: The paper sweeps 1000 random unit-norm directions injected as `α·v` (α = c·μ^(l), c ∈ {0.25..2.0}) at the middle layer of Llama3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B / Falcon3-7B. Random vectors at c=0.75 cause MMLU to drop <1% but jailbreak 4.6% of harmful prompts and most random vectors jailbreak ≥1 prompt (387/1000 jailbreak ≥5 prompts on Llama3.1-8B). The per-vector spread is wide. If our prefill-L19 DoM is steered into the residual stream at comparable scale and the resulting MATH-500 accuracy lift is *within the random-vector null distribution*, then H-1's effect is indistinguishable from generic perturbation — the DoM is not a privileged steering direction.
   *Falsifiable*: Run H-1 with the prefill-DoM **and** with 100 matched-norm random unit vectors. If the random-vector accuracy distribution overlaps the DoM-steered accuracy with p>0.05, H-1 is refuted. Cost: 1× H100 day on cached MATH-500 generation pipeline.

2. **Refutes F-2's "DoM is a privileged correctness direction" framing — moderate.**
   *Mechanism*: The paper finds 817/1000 SAE features extracted from layer 19 of Llama3.1-8B can jailbreak ≥1 prompt, and the *most effective* harmful features have benign labels ("modal verbs", "list of options", "brand identity"). Crucially, App. E reports cosine similarity between the top 30 most-harmful SAE features and the Arditi refusal direction is 0.027 ± 0.021 — i.e., the "interpretable refusal direction" is not what's actually doing the work. By analogy: if our prefill-L19 DoM (also extracted via difference-in-means) achieves AUROC 0.7731 not because it encodes "correctness" but because it lies in a generic high-leverage subspace where almost any direction perturbs downstream computation, F-2's interpretation is wrong.
   *Falsifiable*: Build a null-distribution AUROC for 1000 random unit vectors evaluated against the same OOF correctness labels on cached P11 prefill activations. If the 95th percentile of the random-vector AUROC distribution exceeds ~0.70, F-2's "this direction encodes correctness" framing is at least partially refuted (the direction is high-leverage but not privileged). Cost: 20 min CPU.

3. **Refutes F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046 is "interesting orthogonality") — moderate.**
   *Mechanism*: In a d=2048 residual stream, two random unit vectors have expected |cos| ≈ 1/√d ≈ 0.022. The paper's observation that 817/1000 random vectors meaningfully perturb model behavior implies the residual stream is dense with high-leverage directions — generic high-dim geometry, not a structured "two-stream" architecture. F-3's headline cos=0.046 may simply be the expected order-of-magnitude for unrelated directions in a 2048-d space.
   *Falsifiable*: Compute cos(prefill_DoM, v_i) for 1000 i.i.d. random unit vectors and report the empirical CDF. If 0.046 sits at the median of |cos(prefill_DoM, random)|, F-3's "directions are orthogonal" finding is unsurprising. Cost: 5 min CPU on cached DoM weights.

4. **Refutes H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical) — weak/indirect.**
   *Mechanism*: Fig. 2 (middle) shows steering at *late* layers of Llama3.1-8B has near-zero jailbreak effect; peak is at layer 16 (middle). If the DoM were RoPE-rotated through layers, we would expect *uniform* propagation through the residual stream and similar steering effectiveness at any layer, contra what is observed. Instead, the paper attributes layer-16 vulnerability to "non-linear formation of safety mechanisms in intermediate processing stages" — a non-RoPE explanation.
   *Falsifiable*: Per-layer steering sweep of prefill-DoM on Qwen-2.5-1.5B at layers {10, 14, 19, 22, 26}. If the AUROC effect is concentrated at L19 with sharp drop-off (matching Llama late-layer null), the rotation is computed-not-mechanical, refuting H-17's RoPE explanation. Cost: 4h H100.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-1** (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy) — the paper is the strongest existing prior on activation-steering side-effects. It does not test math/reasoning steering directly, but its calibration result (MMLU degrades <1% at c=0.75 for Llama3.1-8B; ≤0.5 for Qwen2.5/Falcon3) gives us a sweet-spot α to use when we run H-1 ourselves. The 4.6% jailbreak rate at that "safe" coefficient is a hard floor for "what side effects to expect."
- **F-2** (Prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731) — paper's near-zero refusal-direction cos finding (0.027 ± 0.021, App. E) parallels our F-3 cos=0.046 observation. Suggests both could be high-dim-null artifacts.
- **F-3** (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM)=0.046) — see refutation 3 above; the paper's evidence that *most* random directions perturb behavior is a null model the F-3 cos value should be checked against.
- **F-10** (PH on residual streams = Gaussian null) — corroborated in spirit. The paper finds 817/1000 SAE features and most random vectors all jailbreak: the "interpretable structure" attributed to specific directions is null relative to a generic-perturbation baseline. This is consistent with F-10's finding that homological structure on trained residuals matches the Gaussian null.
- **H-13** (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits) — the paper's layer-16 sweet-spot for jailbreaking and late-layer null directly support the framing that mid-layer (16-19) circuits do something causally different from late-layer ones, motivating head-level dissection there.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Adaptive-norm steering coefficient** — `α = c · μ^(l)` where μ^(l) is the average activation norm at layer *l* across the dataset. Normalizes intervention strength across models/layers.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU (compute μ^(l) on cached prefill activations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives a principled α for any future H-1 / DoM-steering experiment.

- **Random-vector null baseline for steering** — sample N unit vectors uniformly from S^(d-1), measure effect distribution, treat per-vector mean as null.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU per 100 vectors on cached AUROC pipeline; 1h H100 per 10 vectors on generation pipeline.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether prefill-DoM is a privileged direction (refutation 2 above).

- **Universal-attack via averaged jailbreak vectors** — sample 1000 random vectors, keep the 20 that jailbreak a single prompt, mean-and-renormalize to get a transferable attack vector.
  *Replication cost*: 1 H100 day for the sampling pass.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — irrelevant to math-correctness; we don't care about jailbreak transfer.

- **LLM-as-judge with precision-bounded eval** — Qwen3-8B in reasoning mode classifies each generation as SAFE/UNSAFE; human-validate on a sample to bound precision (94%). Incoherent outputs default to SAFE.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU per 100 generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — math correctness has a ground-truth answer; LLM-as-judge is overkill.

- **MMLU sweet-spot calibration** — sweep c, find largest c such that MMLU drops <1%, use that for downstream steering experiments.
  *Replication cost*: 4h H100 (MMLU is 14k questions).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — *partial*: substitute MATH-500 for MMLU since our regime is reasoning, not general capability. Pre-condition for any honest H-1 experiment.

- **Special-token steering exclusion** — exclude `<bos>`, `<start_of_turn>`, etc. from steering (cite Lin & Bloom 2023). Improves coherence.
  *Replication cost*: free (a one-line mask).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — should be folded into any H-1 implementation.

- **Layer-trichotomy steering depth sweep** — apply at ⌊L/3⌋, ⌊L/2⌋, ⌊2L/3⌋ as canonical comparison points (cite Wu et al. 2025 / AxBench).
  *Replication cost*: 3× single-layer cost.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — refutation 4 above; useful for testing H-17 RoPE-mechanical claim.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Precise control over internals does not guarantee precise control over behavior"** — the paper's central reframing. Direct challenge to the linear-representation-hypothesis-as-design-tool. Intersects F-2/F-3: if our DoM is "interpretable" only in the sense that benign SAE features are interpretable (i.e., the labels are post-hoc), then DoM-as-correctness-encoding is over-claiming.
- **Steering as generic perturbation rather than concept injection** — the framing that 817/1000 SAE features all jailbreak suggests perturbations of a particular *magnitude* matter more than perturbations of a particular *direction*. Bears on F-2's framing of L19 DoM as a privileged direction.
- **Disclaimer-then-compliance / fictional-framing failure modes** — the model produces a safety preamble then violates it, or wraps harmful content in fiction. Not directly applicable to MATH-500 (no safety preamble), but a useful caution: under steering, our 1.5B may produce fluent CoT that still arrives at wrong answers — coherence is not correctness.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **JailbreakBench** — 100 harmful prompts × 10 categories. License: open. Applicable? **no** — math-correctness is the target, not safety/refusal.
- **MMLU** — capability benchmark used for sweet-spot calibration. Applicable? **partial** — we'd substitute MATH-500 for the calibration role; MMLU itself isn't our regime.
- **Goodfire SAE for Llama3.1-8B layer 19** — open-source SAE from Goodfire. Accessibility: HF / open. Applicable? **no** — our pipeline is Qwen-2.5-1.5B, mismatched architecture/scale; we'd need a Qwen SAE (Galichin et al. 2503.18878 trains one but not at our scale).

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Greedy decoding throughout (matches our P11 K=1 convention).
- α = c · μ^(l) with c ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0}.
- Steering applied to *both* prompt and generation tokens (cite Durmus et al. 2024).
- Special tokens excluded from steering (cite Lin & Bloom 2023).
- LLM-as-judge: Qwen3-8B in reasoning mode, precision 94% on harmful class.
- Sweet-spot c values: Llama3.1-8B → 0.75; Qwen2.5-7B → 0.5; Falcon3-7B → 0.5.
- Universal attack: 100–500 random trials suffice to find 20 single-prompt jailbreakers.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Random-vector AUROC null distribution.** Load the cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_l19_activations.npz` (per-problem prefill L19 vectors) + correctness labels. For each of 1000 random unit vectors v, compute the projection score and OOF AUROC. Report the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile. Compares directly to F-2's 0.7731. **20 min CPU**, fully cached.
- **Cosine null between DoM and random.** Compute cos(prefill_DoM, v_i) for 1000 random unit vectors; report mean, std, and percentile of the F-3 cos=0.046 observation. **5 min CPU**, fully cached.
- **DoM ↔ refusal-direction proxy alignment.** We don't have Arditi's Llama refusal direction for Qwen-2.5-1.5B, but we can substitute: train a binary "refuse vs comply" probe on Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill activations using JailbreakBench prompts (paper-released, open) + benign Alpaca prompts. Compute cos(prefill_DoM_correctness, refusal_direction_qwen). If near-zero (matching paper's 0.027), correctness and refusal live in different subspaces — separates the questions. **2h CPU + 1h H100** (the H100 is for one prefill pass over 200 prompts).

## 2509.22067 — The Rogue Scalpel: Activation Steering Compromises LLM Safety (Korznikov, Galichin, Dontsov, Rogov, Oseledets, Tutubalina, 2026)

**Relevance:** Direct evidence on the side-effects, calibration, and null-baselines for any activation-steering experiment, which is the entire family of H-1 / H-17. The paper's finding that 817/1000 SAE features and most random unit vectors at MMLU-safe coefficients perturb model behavior in a wide-spread, prompt-specific way is a strong null hypothesis against our F-2 framing of prefill-L19 DoM as a "privileged correctness direction." The App. E result that the top-30 most-harmful SAE features have cos=0.027 ± 0.021 with the Arditi refusal direction — i.e., the labeled-interpretable direction is not what's doing the work — directly parallels and pressures our F-3 cos=0.046 observation between prefill and final-token DoM.

**Key claim we tested:** Activation steering at MMLU-safe coefficients (c≤0.75 for Llama3.1-8B; c≤0.5 for Qwen-style) produces broad, direction-agnostic behavioral perturbations that are statistically indistinguishable from random steering across SAE-derived "interpretable" features. Specific to our pipeline: random unit vectors at L19 should also predict / perturb MATH-500 correctness comparably to the prefill_DoM, if the DoM is operating as a generic high-leverage perturbation rather than a privileged direction.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** No replication run yet. Four FutureExperiments (P11-FE2067, P11-FE2068, P11-FE2069, P11-FE2070) cover the random-vector null for AUROC, cosine geometry, steering accuracy, and per-layer dependence respectively. The lightest cross-checks (P11-FE2067 — 20 min CPU; P11-FE2068 — 5 min CPU) can be run today against cached P11 NPZs.
