# Triage brief — 2606.07770 — Contrast encodes inductive bias: separating slow noise from dynamics in predictive representation learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07770
Triaged: 2026-06-10

> **Paper in one line.** Contrastive predictive objectives (JEPA / SimCLR-style / DySIB) that draw negatives *across* trajectories preferentially encode *slowly-varying noise* — features approximately constant within each trajectory — instead of the true latent dynamics. The remedy: sample negatives *within* a single trajectory, which kills the slow-noise shortcut because the noise can no longer separate frames of the same trajectory. Demonstrated on a synthetic moving-dot dataset (SimCLR-JEPA) and rigid-body pendulum movies (DySIB). Not an LLM paper — relevance is a methodological/diagnostic lens, not a head-to-head claim.

## Refutations

The paper's thesis is a **confound lens**: any representation learned by *across-trajectory discrimination* is at risk of encoding trajectory-constant nuisance instead of the dynamics of interest. Our core correctness direction (DoM) is computed as a difference-of-means between the correct and incorrect **problem groups** — i.e. an across-problem (across-trajectory) contrast. That is exactly the regime the paper warns about. Three falsifiable refutations follow.

1. **F-2 (L19 prefill DoM, AUROC 0.7731) may be a slow-noise shortcut, not a correctness mechanism.**
   - *Mechanism:* DoM = mean(correct prefill activations) − mean(incorrect prefill activations). If a problem-level nuisance variable (prompt token-count, problem topic, surface lexical features) is (a) approximately constant within a single problem's token/layer trajectory and (b) correlated with correctness across problems, then a difference-of-means across problems will preferentially align with that *slow* nuisance axis rather than with a per-step reasoning signal — precisely the failure the paper proves for across-trajectory contrastive objectives. AUROC 0.7731 would then be a confound readout, not evidence of an internal correctness computation.
   - *Falsifiable:* Residualize the L19 prefill activations (or the scalar DoM projection) against problem token-count and prompt-length, then recompute the OOF correctness AUROC. If it collapses materially toward chance, F-2's "correctness direction" claim is partly a slow-noise artifact. (Tier-1 FE below.)

2. **F-3 (prefill ⟂ final DoM, cos 0.046) is re-explained as slow-noise vs dynamics separation, not two independent computations.**
   - *Mechanism:* The paper's whole framing is that slow-noise features and true-dynamics features live in *different* subspaces and are recovered by different sampling regimes. The prefill DoM (computed before any generation, maximally "trajectory-constant") is the natural slow-noise candidate; the final-token DoM (after the full reasoning trajectory) is the natural dynamics candidate. Their orthogonality (cos 0.046) is then *expected* under the slow-noise/dynamics dichotomy and does NOT license F-3's stronger reading that the model runs two structurally distinct correctness "computations."
   - *Falsifiable:* Train linear probes from prefill DoM and from final DoM onto problem-constant nuisance variables (length, topic id). If prefill DoM decodes nuisance markedly better than final DoM does, the orthogonality is a slow/fast-timescale split, refuting the "two computations" interpretation.

3. **F-5 / F-1 (breathing is "content-dependent, not AR-mechanics") does not exclude breathing being slow trajectory-specific noise.**
   - *Mechanism:* "Content-dependent" only establishes that amplitude varies across problems. The paper shows that across-trajectory variation is exactly what slow noise looks like — constant within a trajectory, varying across. So "content-dependent not AR-mechanics" is consistent with breathing being a slow nuisance feature (e.g. a token-count or topic correlate) rather than a reasoning-dynamics signal. F-1's "universal" claim is unaffected, but F-5's implied "this reflects reasoning content" reading is at risk.
   - *Falsifiable:* Decompose breathing-amplitude variance into within-problem (across-layer/across-token) vs across-problem components. If across-problem ≫ within-problem and the across-problem component is predictable from length/topic, breathing is slow-noise-like, weakening F-5.

(Secondary, weaker — listed for completeness, not counted toward the ≥3: **F-7** D-bucket "collective geometric signature" could be a slow-noise cluster of problems sharing a nuisance rather than a reasoning-failure cluster; testable with the same within-trajectory contrast.)

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (AUROC 0.7731)** — the paper directly targets the *interpretation* of any across-group difference-of-means direction. The headline 0.7731 and the cos(DoM, PC1)=0.9216 alignment (DoM ≈ PC1, the top covariance axis) make the slow-noise concern sharper: PC1 of a residual cloud is often dominated by global magnitude / length effects, which are textbook slow-nuisance.
- **F-3 (cos 0.046)** — reinterpreted above as a timescale split rather than independent computations.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM)** — CoE-60 is a *trajectory* feature (chain-of-embedding across layers), so it is the natural substrate for the paper's within-trajectory vs across-trajectory experiment; its redundancy with the static DoM is itself evidence consistent with "the trajectory adds nothing beyond the slow component."
- **H-5 (prefill signal = familiarity vs decomposability)** — the paper offers an alternative null: prefill signal = slow nuisance (length/topic), which H-5's test must rule out.
- **H-15 (length-band PR control)** and **H-9 (breathing amplitude predicts difficulty)** — both are length/slow-variable controls already queued; this paper is a strong theoretical motivator and should be cited under them.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Within-trajectory negative sampling (contrastive predictive objective)** — when building the contrastive loss, draw negatives from *other timesteps of the same trajectory* rather than from other trajectories; removes the slow-noise shortcut because trajectory-constant features cannot discriminate within-trajectory frames.
  *Replication cost*: needs per-token (or per-layer) L19 trajectories — fresh extraction for the per-token version (~1 H100 day); the per-layer (CoE) version is runnable on cached CoE features (~30min CPU).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — it is the cleanest existing test of whether DoM is a slow-noise shortcut.

- **Linear-probe decode of noise vs dynamics from frozen representation** — train a linear decoder on the frozen rep to predict (a) the nuisance/noise variable and (b) the future/dynamic state; high noise-decodability flags the shortcut.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU on cached L19 activations (regress against length/topic).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — direct F-2 confound check, near-zero cost.

- **Within- vs across-trajectory variance decomposition** — partition a feature's variance into within-trajectory and across-trajectory components to diagnose slowness.
  *Replication cost*: 20–30min CPU on cached CoE / per-layer activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — diagnoses breathing (F-1/F-5) slow-noise risk.

- **DySIB (Dynamical Sufficient Information Bottleneck) objective** — minimize MI between rep and irrelevant variables while keeping sufficient info about dynamics; the paper shows it inherits the same across-trajectory failure.
  *Replication cost*: H100 day(s), new training loop.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — heavyweight, and the cheaper within-trajectory probe answers our question.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Contrast encodes inductive bias" — the negative-sampling distribution determines *which* features and at *what timescale* a representation captures.** Intersects our work: our DoM is built by an implicit across-problem contrast, so its inductive bias is toward slow, problem-constant structure. This reframes F-2/F-3/F-5 interpretation questions as "what timescale does this direction live on?"
- **Slow-noise / fast-dynamics dichotomy (links to Slow Feature Analysis, Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002).** Gives us a principled axis to split the prefill (slow) vs final-token (dynamics) DoM directions and a vocabulary for F-3.
- **Failure is a property of the objective, not the architecture.** Suggests that any across-group probe on our residuals — supervised DoM, CAST PC1, cov-spectrum — shares the same blind spot; a portfolio-level caution rather than a single-finding one.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Negative-sampling switch is a *one-line* change in the contrastive loss (sample index restricted to same-trajectory frames) — cheap to prototype if we have per-token trajectories.
- They report noise-reconstruction accuracy vs forward-prediction accuracy as paired metrics to show the shortcut was/wasn't taken — adopt this paired-readout when we run our probe.
- Key diagnostic signature of the failure: downstream performance *degrades with noise strength and does not improve* as #trajectories or trajectory length grows. Analog for us: if DoM AUROC does not improve with more problems or longer CoT, that is a slow-noise fingerprint worth checking against existing scaling runs.
