# Triage brief — 2605.08605 — Lattice Deduction Transformers

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08605
Triaged: 2026-05-22

## Refutations

**R1 — Against F-8 (selective prediction via prefill DoM): architectural soundness obsoletes post-hoc probing.**
The LDT achieves *empirical soundness* on Sudoku-Extreme — it returns a correct answer or abstains, never producing a wrong answer — via an explicit CLS sigmoid trained to detect unsatisfiable lattice states (⊥). This is a structurally-guaranteed abstention mechanism, not a post-hoc probe. F-8's selective-prediction stack (71.6% answered accuracy at 50% coverage) is a weaker form of the same goal: deciding when to trust the model's output. If architectural soundness mechanisms (CLS sigmoid + lattice projection) can guarantee 100% precision on abstention with no coverage penalty on solvable instances, our post-hoc DoM probing approach is strictly dominated on any task where such architecture is applicable. The refutation fires if someone builds a sound-by-design inference wrapper for standard LLMs (e.g. projecting residual streams through a verifier lattice at each decoding step) that outperforms DoM-based selective prediction on MATH-500.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Train a CLS-style conflict head on L19 prefill activations (binary: correct/incorrect) with the LDT's asymmetric BCE loss (w+=8, w-=1) and compare precision-at-abstention against the DoM logistic regression probe at matched coverage.

**R2 — Against F-2 (L19 DoM predicts correctness at AUROC 0.7731): DoM may only detect problem familiarity, not reasoning capacity.**
The paper demonstrates that frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, DeepSeek V4-Pro) score *exactly 0%* on Sudoku-Extreme, Snowflake Sudoku, and Maze-Hard — tasks requiring genuine deductive reasoning beyond surface pattern matching. If DoM works by detecting "the model has seen problems like this before" (the H-5 familiarity hypothesis), then on problems where the model has zero competence, the DoM signal would be trivially at chance — it couldn't distinguish "will be correct" from "will be incorrect" because the model is *always* incorrect. This constrains F-2's scope: the AUROC 0.7731 may reflect a distributional familiarity signal on MATH-500 (a benchmark the training corpus likely contains variants of) rather than a genuine reasoning-capacity detector. The refutation fires if DoM AUROC drops to ~0.50 on a benchmark where the model's baseline accuracy is near-zero (analogous to the 0% regime the paper highlights).
*Falsifiable experiment:* Extract L19 prefill activations on a constraint-satisfaction benchmark (e.g. hard Sudoku instances converted to natural language) where Qwen-2.5-1.5B accuracy is <5%, and measure DoM AUROC. If AUROC ≈ 0.50, F-2's signal is familiarity-dependent, not reasoning-intrinsic.

**R3 — Against F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal) and H-837 (attractor dynamics at ~2/3 depth): lattice-constrained recurrence produces structured hidden-state trajectories without emergent breathing.**
The LDT's hidden states follow a *designed* trajectory — each recurrent iteration's latent state is projected through a lattice, enforcing monotone descent toward more informative states (⊤ → solution or ⊥). This is a deterministic precision ordering, not emergent dimensional breathing. If we measured effective dimensionality across LDT's 16 recurrent iterations, we would see dimensionality changes driven entirely by the lattice constraint (progressive candidate elimination = progressive dimension reduction), not by any intrinsic property of transformer computation. This means: (a) if H-837's attractor dynamics are observed in LDT, they're trivially explained by the lattice projection, not by depth-dependent transformer geometry; (b) if breathing-like patterns arise in standard LLMs only because they lack such structural constraints (i.e. breathing = noise from unconstrained computation), then F-1's "universality" claim is actually documenting an architectural deficiency, not a meaningful signal.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Measure participation ratio across LDT's 16 recurrent iterations on Sudoku-Extreme. If PR tracks candidate-set cardinality monotonically, the "breathing" is lattice-driven, not emergent — weakening the analogy to standard-transformer breathing.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The LDT trains a binary sigmoid on a CLS token to detect ⊥ (unsatisfiable state). This is functionally analogous to our DoM probe — both are linear classifiers on hidden states that predict a binary outcome (correct/incorrect vs. satisfiable/unsatisfiable). The key difference: LDT's CLS sigmoid is trained *during model training* as part of the architecture, while our DoM is extracted post-hoc from a frozen model. The LDT's approach suggests that if correctness signals were trained into the model explicitly (rather than emerging as a side effect), they could be far more reliable.

- **F-8 (selective prediction at 50% coverage, 71.6% answered accuracy):** The LDT's empirical soundness — returning correct or abstaining, never wrong — is the ideal limit of selective prediction with zero coverage penalty on solvable instances. Our selective-prediction stack (F-8) with K=2.5 avg at 50% coverage is a soft, imperfect version of the same goal. The LDT shows this goal is achievable in principle with the right architectural inductive bias.

- **H-410 (recurrence preserves DoM signal):** The LDT is a recurrent transformer with 16 internal iterations. Its latent state evolves across iterations, but is explicitly projected through a lattice between passes. If DoM-like signals exist in the LDT, they would be lattice-artifacts. H-410 predicts that recurrence in latent-CoT models (Coconut/Recurrent-Depth) preserves DoM signal; the LDT offers a controlled case where recurrent hidden states are constrained — any DoM signal here would have a known structural explanation.

- **H-837 (attractor dynamics at ~2/3 depth):** The LDT has explicit fixed-point dynamics — deduction descends from ⊤ toward a solution, and the solve loop terminates when no further candidates can be eliminated (fixed point reached). This is a designed attractor, not an emergent one. If standard transformers show similar attractor dynamics (H-837), the LDT provides a formal framework (Galois connections, lattice descent) for interpreting them.

- **F-10 (PH is null beyond covariance):** The LDT's lattice structure is a discrete, algebraic structure — not a topological one. The paper's success without any topological/PH machinery is consistent with F-10's finding that topology adds no signal beyond covariance.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Lattice-tensor encoding** — Multi-hot sigmoid representation where each binary dimension tracks whether a candidate is "still alive," with deduction = pushing sigmoids toward 0. 
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU to implement a discrete projection of L19 activations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — our activations are continuous 1536-d vectors, not discrete candidate sets. The encoding assumes a known finite vocabulary per position, which doesn't map to residual streams.

- **Asymmetric BCE loss (w+=8, w-=1)** — Penalizes false eliminations 8× more than false retentions, biasing the model toward conservatism (keep candidates alive unless confident they're dead).
  *Replication cost*: 5min CPU — just refit logistic regression with class_weight on cached features.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to our DoM correctness probe. In selective prediction (F-8), we care more about not incorrectly rejecting correct answers (false negatives) than about failing to flag incorrect answers (false positives). Asymmetric weighting could improve the precision-coverage frontier.

- **CLS-token conflict detection** — A dedicated CLS token sigmoid trained to detect ⊥ (inconsistency/unsatisfiable state), separate from the per-position candidate logits.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU to check if BOS/CLS token position in our L19 extractions carries correctness signal.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — we currently use mean-pooled prefill for DoM. Checking the first-token (BOS) position alone would test whether a single-position "conflict head" carries signal.

- **On-policy training with evolving lattice states** — Training on states the model itself produces (not fixed dataset), keeping train-time distribution matched to inference-time search states.
  *Replication cost*: N/A — requires model training.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we work with frozen extractors.

- **Parallel solve with pool replacement** — Maintaining a pool of partially-deduced states, sampling from the pool, evolving them, and replacing solved/conflicted instances.
  *Replication cost*: N/A — architectural/training technique.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — not applicable to probing.

## Approaches & framings

- **Reasoning as lattice descent.** The paper frames reasoning as monotone descent in a lattice — from ⊤ (no information) toward ⊥ (inconsistency) or a solution, where each step is a sound deduction that only removes candidates. This intersects H-837 (attractor dynamics): if standard transformer depth can be viewed as implicit lattice descent, then the L19 ≈ 2/3 depth "phase transition" might correspond to a lattice meet operation where the model's candidate set narrows sharply.

- **Soundness vs. completeness tradeoff.** The paper explicitly separates soundness (never wrong) from completeness (always finds a solution), showing you can achieve the former without the latter by allowing abstention. This maps directly to the selective-prediction framing (F-8): our DoM probe trades coverage for accuracy, which is exactly the soundness/completeness tradeoff. The paper formalizes this via abstract interpretation; we could formalize F-8 similarly.

- **Train/test compute tradeoff in sound inference.** More training shortens inference search by orders of magnitude (Figure 3a). This has a loose analogy to H-5 (prefill signal encodes problem familiarity vs. decomposability): models with more training on a domain may produce stronger prefill DoM signals because the "familiarity" channel is better calibrated.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **Sudoku-Extreme** — 1,000 puzzles requiring search/backtracking. Sources: Tdoku generator, RRN corpus, CNN dataset. Not released as a single dataset but constructed from public sources. Applicable? no — grid constraint satisfaction, not math reasoning over natural language.

- **Snowflake Sudoku** — Hexagonal variant, ~30K puzzles generated via CVC5 SMT solver. Not publicly released. Applicable? no — same constraint domain.

- **Maze-Hard** — 30×30 mazes with shortest path ≥110, multiple valid shortest paths. Applicable? no — path-finding, not math reasoning.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- 800K parameters: d=128, 4 layers, 4 attention heads, 16 internal loops, FFN multiplier 4.0, dropout 0.1.
- AdamW: lr=3×10⁻³, weight decay 0.1, β=(0.9, 0.95), gradient clip 1.0, cosine schedule with 10% warmup.
- Asymmetric BCE: w+=4.0, w-=0.5 (equivalently w+/w-=8), λ_ce=0.2, λ_cls=0.1.
- θ_elim ≈ 0.1 (elimination threshold), θ_CLS tuned per-domain (0.6 Sudoku, 0.53 maze).
- Inference augmentation: random digit permutation + dihedral D4 symmetry per step, inverted after deduction.
- Parallel inference: M=8 slots, K=64 chains per slot (512 total batch width), R=1000 rounds budget.
- Eval dropout p_drop=0.05 for small grids, 0 for large mazes.
- 1.8M variant for mazes: d=192, 2D RoPE added to attention.
- Training times: 4min (1K steps) to 15min (4K steps) for Sudoku; 9.7h for Maze-Hard. All on single GPU.
