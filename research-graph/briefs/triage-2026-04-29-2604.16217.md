# Triage brief — 2604.16217 — Beyond Surface Statistics: Robust Conformal Prediction for LLMs via Internal Representations

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16217
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Yanli Wang, Peng Kuang, Xiaoyu Han, Kaidi Xu, Haohan Wang (Imperial / Zhejiang / UIUC / CityU HK), Apr 2026.

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-9 ("CoE-60 is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM").**
   *Mechanism*: Layerwise CP's central empirical claim is that an LM-head-lens
   per-layer entropy-reduction score (LI), summed across **all** transformer
   layers, strictly improves conformal efficiency (APSS) by 16.9% relative on
   MMLU-Pro cross-domain — i.e., aggregating ≫ any single layer in their
   ablation framing. They explicitly motivate this with the Kim et al. (2025a)
   "usable information is non-monotonic across depth" result. If their
   sum-over-all-layers LI nonconformity score yields a strictly better
   correctness ranking than the same-recipe LI restricted to L19 alone on our
   Qwen-2.5-1.5B cached prefill activations, the F-9 redundancy claim is
   wrong: the multi-layer signal carries information beyond L19.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: re-implement LI score (eq 6–8 of the paper) on
   `pathway11_h100/.../residuals_*.npz`, evaluate (a) LI restricted to ℓ=19,
   (b) LI summed over all 28 layers, both used as ranking score for the same
   500 MATH-500 problems. Compare AUROC/APSS at matched calibration. CoE-60 ≅
   L19 is robust only if (a) ≅ (b).

2. **Refutes F-2 ("L19 is the prefill peak").**
   *Mechanism*: F-2 was located by sweeping linear DoM probes layer-by-layer.
   The paper proposes a **probe-free** per-layer correctness signal:
   I_ℓ(x→y) = H_ℓ(y|∅) − H_ℓ(y|x), the entropy reduction from conditioning on
   the question, computed by projecting layer-ℓ hidden states through the
   pretrained LM head (logit-lens). If argmax_ℓ I_ℓ on MATH-500 prefill is at
   a different layer than 19 (e.g., 17 or 24), then "L19 is special" is a
   probe-method artifact — the more probe-agnostic LM-head-lens disagrees,
   and F-2's specific layer claim should be reframed as "an L18–L24 band"
   rather than a single-layer peak.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: compute I_ℓ for ℓ ∈ {0..27} on Qwen-2.5-1.5B
   MATH-500 cached prefill, sweep AUROC vs correctness, compare with the
   linear-DoM AUROC sweep already in `pathway11_h100/...`.

3. **Refutes H-12 ("semantic-entropy probes (label-free) replace supervised
   DoM").**
   *Mechanism*: H-12 picks semantic-entropy as the canonical label-free
   substitute for supervised L19 DoM. Layerwise CP is also label-free
   (forward pass + LM head, no probe training) AND, on TriviaQA + CoQA at
   matched conformal pipeline, beats semantic-entropy by 16% relative APSS
   (0.215→0.192 EMR, 2.30→1.98 APSS on Qwen-2.5-7B TriviaQA per Table 3). If
   LI score also dominates semantic entropy on MATH-500 OOF with our cached
   activations, H-12's specific choice (semantic-entropy) is wrong: the
   right label-free baseline is layer-aggregated logit-lens entropy
   reduction, not output-distribution semantic entropy.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: implement both semantic-entropy (Kuhn et al.
   2023 recipe) and LI score on the same MATH-500 K=8 generations from
   `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`, compare AUROC vs correctness
   labels. If LI > semantic-entropy by ≥3 points AUROC, swap H-12's
   nominee.

4. **Tension with F-7 ("D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric
   signature, not per-problem signal").**
   *Mechanism*: F-7 implies D-bucket items don't have an exploitable
   per-instance hidden-state correctness signal — they're collective
   anomalies. Layerwise CP claims that **per-instance** layer-wise info
   reliably ranks admissible vs inadmissible answers under cross-domain
   shift, with the strongest gains when calibration ≠ deployment. If their
   LI score, computed per-problem, separates D-bucket (high-confidence
   wrong) from A-bucket (high-confidence right) on MATH-500, then F-7's
   "no per-problem geometric signature" overstates the case — D-bucket is
   reachable per-instance, but only via multi-layer entropy reduction, not
   single-layer DoM cosine.
   *Falsifiable experiment*: compute per-problem LI score on the 36
   D-bucket and 207 A-bucket problems from F-7. If D-bucket has
   significantly different mean LI than A-bucket (Welch's t-test p<0.01),
   F-7's framing must be tightened.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2** (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731): Layerwise CP supplies a
  probe-free, label-free comparator. Their LI score is a continuous-valued
  per-layer signal directly comparable to L19 DoM on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B
  prefill activations. If LI-L19 ≅ DoM-L19 in correctness ranking, F-2 is
  corroborated method-independently; if LI peaks elsewhere, F-2's layer
  claim is challenged (see refutation 2).

- **F-9** (CoE-60 redundant with L19 DoM): direct contradiction. The paper
  rests on Kim et al. (2025a)'s claim that usable information is
  non-monotonic across depth, motivating multi-layer aggregation. Our F-9
  finding that aggregating layers via PCA-60 doesn't beat single-layer L19
  is the opposite empirical claim. The cleanest reconciliation is that the
  CoE-60 feature family compresses too aggressively (PCA on raw activations)
  while LI (LM-head-lens entropy reduction) preserves correctness-relevant
  variance better — a methodology refinement of F-9 rather than a wholesale
  reversal.

- **F-8** (selective prediction at 50% coverage = 71.6%): Layerwise CP is a
  conformal-prediction-set method (returns a set of admissible answers
  rather than a binary abstain decision). Their EMR/APSS framework is the
  conformal analog of our coverage/accuracy curve. Bridging F-8 to a
  conformal frame requires only scoring with LI instead of DoM cosine and
  reporting prediction-set size at calibrated risk α — would let us put
  F-8 on conformal validity guarantees, which we currently lack.

- **H-12** (semantic-entropy probes label-free replace supervised DoM): see
  refutation 3. Direct competing label-free recipe.

- **F-10** (PH on trained residuals = Gaussian null): orthogonal but
  thematically adjacent. Layerwise CP makes the geometric signal
  information-theoretic (entropy reduction across depth) rather than
  topological. Worth registering as a competing geometric framing.

## Methodologies extracted

- **LM-head logit-lens per-layer entropy** — project residual stream at
  layer ℓ through the pretrained LM head to get a next-token distribution
  p_ℓ(y_t | y_<t, x), compute average negative log-likelihood of a sampled
  response y as H_ℓ(y|x). Same trick as logit-lens / tuned-lens but used as
  a calibration signal rather than for interpretability.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU on cached residuals (need per-layer
  hidden states and the model's `lm_head` matrix; both available for
  Qwen-2.5-1.5B).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly comparable to L19 DoM
  AUROC on cached MATH-500 prefill data, and lets us put F-2 on a
  probe-free footing.

- **Per-layer V-usable-information score (I_ℓ)** — null-conditioned vs
  question-conditioned entropy difference, I_ℓ(x→y) = H_ℓ(y|∅) − H_ℓ(y|x).
  Empirical instantiation of Xu et al. 2020 / Ethayarajh et al. 2022's
  V-usable info, restricted to a single layer's "view" of the LM head.
  *Replication cost*: 30 min CPU; needs an extra null-prompt forward pass
  per (model, sample) pair. The authors are vague about what ∅ means
  exactly — "absence of question context" — likely the few-shot template
  with the question slot blanked. Worth pinning down before replication.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — pairs well with our existing prefill
  vs final-token contrast (F-2 / F-3).

- **LI score** — sum I_ℓ over a chosen layer subset, default = all layers.
  *Replication cost*: trivial once I_ℓ is computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — single-layer vs all-layer LI is the
  clean test of F-9.

- **Within-pool min-max normalization** (eq 9) — normalize LI scores within
  each candidate-pool {y_m^(i)} per question to remove cross-question
  scale variation.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — if we ever extend our K=8/K=16
  sampling pipeline to a conformal frame, this is the right preprocessing.

- **Answer-frequency aggregation** (eq 12) — average normalized LI scores
  over all candidate responses parsing to the same answer unit; equivalent
  to the within-question voting score from Jiang et al. 2025.
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **maybe** — only matters once we have
  multi-sample MATH-500 generations with semantic equivalence classes,
  which we don't currently use.

- **Convex combination with answer-frequency** (eq 13) F^LW = w_LI F_LI +
  w_f F_freq with w_LI = w_f = 0.5. Internal score regularized by
  cross-sample consensus (self-consistency).
  *Replication cost*: trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct comparator to our
  selective-prediction at K=2.5 (F-8); we currently use only DoM cosine.

- **Split conformal prediction (SCP) wrapper** with EMR / APSS / SSM
  metrics — empirical miscoverage rate, average prediction set size,
  size-stratified miscoverage. Standard CP plumbing from Angelopoulos &
  Bates 2022.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day CPU to build a clean SCP harness over our
  existing K=8 generations.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — would let us state F-8 with
  finite-sample validity guarantees.

- **Sentence-similarity correctness protocol** for open-domain QA
  (Su et al. 2024 / Wang et al. 2025b) — we don't currently need this
  because MATH-500 has exact-match.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — not relevant to MATH-500.

## Approaches & framings

- **"The bottleneck is the uncertainty *signal*, not how CP is adapted to
  shift."** This reframes the conformal-prediction literature: instead of
  fancier reweighting tricks (Tibshirani 2020, Barber 2023), use a
  better-quality scalar score. Maps cleanly onto our F-2 framing where
  the *signal* (prefill L19 DoM) carries the action and the wrapper
  (logistic regression OOF) is interchangeable.

- **"Internal layer-wise information evolves non-monotonically across
  depth"** (Kim et al. 2025a, cited approvingly). Direct theoretical
  competitor to F-9's single-layer-suffices framing — see refutation 1.

- **Information-theoretic interpretation of conformal prediction** (their
  Appendix E, claimed): "prediction-set size at controlled risk reflects
  residual uncertainty." Bridges CP efficiency (APSS) with V-usable
  information. Useful framing for putting F-8 on conformal footing.

- **Empirical-not-validity contribution.** They explicitly say: "we do not
  claim that internal representations remove the need for conformal
  assumptions or restore formal validity under domain shift. Our claim is
  narrower and empirical." Healthy scientific posture; matches the
  refutations-first style we want to import into our own writeups.

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **MMLU-Pro** (Wang et al. 2024a) — 10-option closed-ended QA, 14 subject
  domains, M=50 candidate samples per question. HF gated (license: MIT
  per HF). **Applicable: no** — closed-ended, not free-form math; our
  setup is single-best-answer numeric.

- **MedMCQA** (Pal et al. 2022) — medical 4-option MCQA, M=20 samples per
  question. HF, MIT-equivalent. **Applicable: no** — same closed-ended
  format mismatch.

- **TriviaQA** (Joshi et al. 2017) — open-domain QA, 4000-example subset,
  M=10 samples per prompt. HF, Apache 2.0. **Applicable: no** —
  factoid-recall benchmark, very different signal from multi-step math.

- **CoQA** (Reddy et al. 2019) — conversational QA, 4000-example subset,
  M=10. HF, mixed licenses. **Applicable: no** — same as TriviaQA.

The paper does **not** test on MATH, GSM8K, or any reasoning benchmark.
This is a non-trivial gap: their claim is about closed-ended factuality
QA, while our regime is multi-step reasoning where breathing (F-1) and
asymmetric collapse (F-4) operate. Their cross-domain story is among
**MMLU-Pro subjects**, not across task types. Replicating their gain on
MATH-500 cross-difficulty (e.g., calibrate on Algebra, deploy on
Counting & Probability) is novel.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Calibration/test split ratio: 0.5, repeated 100 random trials.
- Risk budgets evaluated: β ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3} (i.e., target miscoverage).
- Generation: `do_sample=True, num_beams=1, top_p=0.9, temperature=1.0`,
  max length 1 token for MCQA, 36 tokens for open-domain.
- Model suite: Qwen-2.5-3B/7B/14B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct,
  Vicuna-13B-v1.5. White-box (need hidden states + LM head). No
  fine-tuning.
- Prompt strategy: 3-shot for MMLU-Pro/MedMCQA, few-shot for
  TriviaQA/CoQA.
- Strongest backbone: Qwen-2.5-14B (EMR 0.110, APSS 1.68, SSM 0.178).
- Standard deviations of EMR/APSS are smaller for Layerwise CP than for
  SConU-Pro across all five models — a stability gain on top of the
  efficiency gain. Worth noting if we replicate.
- Default layer subset L_s = L (all transformer layers). Authors do not
  ablate single-layer vs all-layer LI, which is exactly the gap our
  refutation 1 fills.
- Gotcha: their I_ℓ(x→y) requires a "null context" forward pass
  p_ℓ(y_t|y_<t, ∅), and the paper is loose about what ∅ is
  (presumably few-shot template with question slot blanked, but not
  pinned down in the main text — Appendix B may help).
- No code/repository link in the v1 PDF (preprint, under review).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.16217 — Beyond Surface Statistics: Robust Conformal Prediction for LLMs via Internal Representations (Wang, Kuang, Han, Xu, Wang; 2026)

**Relevance:** Methodologically the most direct competitor to our F-2 /
F-9 framing we've seen. Proposes "Layer-Wise Information (LI) scores"
— per-layer LM-head-lens entropy reduction summed across all
transformer layers — as a label-free, probe-free conformal nonconformity
score, and claims it strictly improves conformal efficiency (APSS) by
~17% relative under cross-domain shift on MMLU-Pro / MedMCQA, and ~16%
on TriviaQA / CoQA vs semantic-entropy. The non-monotonic-across-depth
theoretical motivation (Kim et al. 2025a) is in direct tension with our
