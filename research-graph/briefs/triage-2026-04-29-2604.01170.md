# Triage brief — 2604.01170 — Online Reasoning Calibration: Test-Time Training Enables Generalizable Conformal LLM Reasoning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01170
Triaged: 2026-04-29

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-2 ("prefill L19 DoM is a *static* correctness predictor at AUROC 0.7731").** ORCA's central
   empirical claim is that *static* probes leave 18–25% relative efficiency on the table because the optimal
   correctness direction drifts across reasoning steps. Their TTT no-QK probe — which is structurally
   identical to a fixed linear probe except that its weights `W_t` are gradient-updated per step against a
   Brier loss — beats their static baseline by 24.9% relative on Qwen2.5-32B in-distribution and by
   2.6–2.7× on MATH-500 OOD. F-2 explicitly frames the prefill L19 DoM as a *single, fixed* direction. If
   ORCA's per-step update is the operative mechanism rather than the layer choice, F-2's "single direction
   suffices" framing fails. **Falsifiable test:** on cached `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` Stage-2
   activations, fit a per-position linear DoM (one direction per token-bucket) at L19 and measure 5-fold
   OOF AUROC. If it exceeds 0.7731 by >2 points, F-2's static framing is overturned.

2. **Refutes F-3 ("cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 — they are orthogonal, structurally distinct").**
   ORCA's inner loop performs continuous gradient descent on `W_t` along the trajectory; the probe
   direction *rotates* monotonically. If the empirical 47.5% savings come primarily from this drift, then
   F-3's "orthogonality" may be a snapshot of a continuous rotation rather than evidence that two
   structurally distinct circuits exist. The mechanism: a single direction, smoothly rotated by token-level
   updates, can produce arbitrarily large angular gaps between the prefill snapshot and the
   end-of-CoT snapshot — including 0.046. **Falsifiable test:** unroll a no-QK TTT update with η=0.01
   starting from our prefill L19 DoM on a held-out MATH-500 batch. If `cos(W_0, W_T)` ≈ 0.05 with no extra
   structure, F-3's "orthogonal circuits" interpretation collapses into "rotating-probe artifact."

3. **Refutes F-8 ("selective prediction via prefill DoM works at 50% coverage → 71.6% accuracy on K=2.5
   avg").** ORCA reports MATH-500 savings of 67.0% (TTT QK, supervised) and 63.7% (TTT QK, consistent
   no-label) at error rate ≤2.3% and δ=0.1. Their "savings" is a per-step early-stopping metric that maps
   roughly to compute-coverage tradeoff. If their numbers replicate at the 1024-tok regime with a probe
   targeting the same correctness label, then our LTT-naive 50%-coverage / K=2.5-avg threshold is
   *strictly dominated* by an LTT-calibrated rule on the same hidden states. F-8's headline becomes a
   weaker baseline rather than a finding. **Falsifiable test:** apply LTT (binomial p-value + fixed-sequence
   testing, the recipe in their §3.4) to our cached Qwen-1.5B prefill DoM scores on MATH-500. If error
   rate stays ≤0.10 with selective accuracy substantially above 71.6% at the same average compute,
   F-8 is superseded.

4. **Complicates F-9 ("CoE-60 trajectory features are redundant with single-layer L19 DoM, ΔAUROC ≤
   0.001").** ORCA's gain comes from *online* trajectory information — gradients accumulated across
   reasoning steps. F-9 was tested with a *closed-form* trajectory aggregation (CoE-60). The two are not
   the same: CoE compresses the trajectory into a fixed-length feature, while TTT lets the trajectory
   *drive an adaptation*. ORCA's gains suggest trajectory information *does* contain non-redundant
   signal when consumed in the right way. **Falsifiable test:** on the same cached residuals, run a no-QK
   TTT inner loop at L19 (η=0.01, Brier vs supervised correctness) for the full reasoning trajectory and
   measure final-step AUROC vs. the static L19 prefill DoM. If TTT lifts AUROC ≥2 points, F-9 must be
   restated as "*static-aggregation* trajectory features are redundant" — not "trajectory information is
   redundant."

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM, AUROC 0.7731 OOF).** ORCA uses mean-pooled *last-layer* hidden states as φ_t —
  a different layer from our L19, with d_φ=5,120 on Qwen2.5-32B. Their static-baseline probe is
  conceptually our DoM probe. Their TTT no-QK probe is "DoM with online gradient updates."
- **F-3 (cos = 0.046).** Their TTT trajectory `W_0 → W_T` is exactly the rotation that F-3 measures as a
  static endpoint comparison; see Refutation 2.
- **F-8 (71.6% at coverage 0.5).** ORCA's supervised TTT-QK saves 67.0% on MATH-500 at error ≤2.1%,
  comparable in spirit but with a different selection rule (LTT-calibrated per-step stopping vs. our
  threshold on prefill score). See Refutation 3.
- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM).** Indirectly threatened by ORCA's trajectory-driven
  gains; see Refutation 4.
- **H-1 (per-position DoM steering moves MATH-500 accuracy).** ORCA validates the per-position
  framing for *probing* (not steering); supports H-1's premise that the DoM direction varies along the
  trajectory.
- **H-11 (1.5B → 7B distillation of self-prediction).** ORCA's cross-model results (Qwen2.5-32B → QwQ
  → Llama-3.3-70B, all gain ~20–34% relative) suggest the TTT recipe is model-agnostic, weakening
  the "self-prediction is model-specific" presumption that H-11 implicitly tests.
- **H-12 (SE probes as label-free DoM replacements).** ORCA's "Consistent" label mode (C_t = I{ans(y_t)
  = ans(y_T)}) achieves 40.7% in-domain savings without ground truth — a strong, methodologically
  similar alternative to SEPs that *outperforms* them in the few comparisons we have. Pushes H-12
  toward "consistency-vs-final-answer probes" rather than "semantic-entropy probes."
- **H-19 (C_exact verification routing beats C_infer scaling at matched compute).** ORCA's static-probe
  baseline is the C_infer analog; their TTT probe is a step-by-step C_infer adaptation. They do *not*
  evaluate verification-routing. H-19 is unchanged but ORCA gives a stronger C_infer benchmark to beat.

## Methodologies extracted

- **No-QK TTT probe** — single linear probe `f(φ_t; W_t) = σ(W·φ_t + b)`, fast weights updated per step
  via online gradient descent on Brier loss against C_t.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on cached Stage-2 NPZs. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — direct test
  of F-9 and Refutation 4.
- **QK TTT probe** — adds learned `θ_K, θ_Q ∈ R^{d_h × d_φ}` projections (d_h=128) so the update
  direction (K) and scoring direction (Q) attend to different aspects of the hidden state.
  *Replication cost*: 1h CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — natural ablation; tests whether
  "direction-of-update ≠ direction-of-scoring" is an effective inductive bias for our regime.
- **Bilevel TTT outer-loop training** — truncated BPTT through the inner-loop unroll, Adam outer
  lr=1e-3, gradient clipping 1.0, inner η=0.01.
  *Replication cost*: 4h CPU on our 5K-equivalent training corpus. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — the
  paper's headline result depends on this; we cannot test refutations 1–3 properly without it.
- **LTT calibration of stopping rules** — sweep threshold grid Λ, compute binomial p-value
  `p_j = P(Binom(n, δ) ≤ n·R̂_n(λ_j))`, apply fixed-sequence testing for FWER, pick most aggressive
  rejected threshold.
  *Replication cost*: 20min CPU. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — gives us finite-sample risk control on
  F-8's selective-prediction claim, currently absent.
- **Consistent-mode label C_t = I{ans(y_t) = ans(y_T)}** — self-consistency-style label that bypasses
  ground truth.
  *Replication cost*: needs intermediate-answer extraction at each step (we currently only checkpoint
  final answers); ~2h CPU + intermediate-answer parser. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — closest
  label-free probe we've seen to date.
- **Score smoothing (rolling window of 10 steps).** *Replication cost*: trivial. *We'd plausibly run this*:
  **yes** — cheap baseline noise-reduction we should test.
- **Mean-pooled last-layer hidden state as φ_t.** *Replication cost*: zero — already cached at L19; switch
  to L_last is a one-line change. *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — sanity check whether layer choice
  matters.

## Approaches & framings

- **Calibration-as-online-objective.** Rather than treating calibration as a post-hoc fit on a held-out set,
  ORCA frames it as an objective the model adapts toward at every reasoning step. Intersects F-2 / F-8
  by suggesting our "single direction, fit once, deployed forever" framing is the *limiting* case of a richer
  online procedure.
- **Bilevel separation of "learn to calibrate" vs. "learn to learn to calibrate."** Conceptually decouples
  per-instance adaptation (inner) from cross-distribution generalization (outer). Maps onto our F-2/F-7
  tension: F-2 is the per-instance signal, F-7 is the collective-bucket signal — ORCA gives a principled
  way to fit both.
- **Conformalize the *deployed procedure*, not the *raw scorer*.** Their LTT calibration controls risk over
  the full pipeline including the online updates and the stopping rule, with finite-sample guarantees.
  Our F-8 currently has *no* finite-sample risk guarantee — this is a methodological upgrade we could
  adopt verbatim.
- **Distribution shift at two levels: sample-level (within-CoT step variation) and dataset-level
  (deployment vs. training prompt distribution).** Useful framing for our 256-tok / 1024-tok confound:
  the truncation-confound was effectively an undeclared dataset-level distribution shift.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Outer Adam lr = 1e-3, gradient clipping 1.0, inner η = 0.01.
- Score trajectories smoothed with rolling window of 10 steps.
- Epoch selection: 20 for no-QK, 10 for QK variants (overfitting at higher epochs).
- Hidden state: mean-pooled *last-layer* (d_φ = 5,120 for Qwen2.5-32B / QwQ-32B; 8,192 for Llama-3.3-70B).
- Reasoning trajectories generated by DeepSeek-R1-671B; correctness labels from Qwen-3-32B as
  teacher; GPT-4.1 for evaluation.
- Train/calibration/test split: 3,000 / 1,000 / 1,000 (5K corpus from s1K + OpenR1 + DeepMath).
- Risk levels swept: δ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20}; failure probability ε = 0.05.
- Code: https://github.com/wzekai99/ORCA (public).

## Proposed PAPER_INDEX.md classification

## 2604.01170 — Online Reasoning Calibration: Test-Time Training Enables Generalizable Conformal LLM Reasoning (Zhou, Wang, Wu, Zhu, Shi, Wang, Wilson, Jaakkola, Bates, 2026)

**Relevance:** ORCA is the strongest single competitor to our static-DoM selective-prediction
framework (F-2, F-8). It frames calibration as an *online* objective optimized at every reasoning step
via TTT (test-time training) inner-loop fast-weight updates and an outer-loop meta-trained
initialization, with finite-sample risk control via Learn-then-Test (LTT). On MATH-500 (our shared
benchmark) it achieves 67.0% step savings at error rate 2.1% with δ=0.1, vs. 24.8% for the static
linear-probe baseline. The static baseline is conceptually identical to our prefill L19 DoM probe
(modulo layer choice — they use mean-pooled last-layer; we use L19), so its dominance by TTT is a
direct challenge to F-2's "single static direction at AUROC 0.7731 is sufficient" framing and F-8's
"71.6% accuracy at 50% coverage" headline. The paper also offers a methodological upgrade we
should adopt regardless of TTT outcomes: LTT calibration gives finite-sample risk guarantees on
selective-prediction rules that F-8 currently lacks.

**Key claim we tested:** A *static* linear probe on hidden states leaves 18–25% relative
selective-prediction efficiency on the table compared to a TTT probe whose weights are
gradient-updated per reasoning step against a Brier loss. On MATH-500 OOD specifically, the gap
widens to 2.6–2.7× under δ=0.1.

**Our result:** **TO TEST.** We have not yet run a TTT update on top of our prefill L19 DoM, nor
have we applied LTT calibration to our selective-prediction pipeline. P11-FE1170 (TTT update on
cached residuals, ~30min CPU) and P11-FE1171 (LTT calibration, ~20min CPU) are READY.
Outcome of these will determine whether ORCA REPLICATES (TTT lifts AUROC, LTT improves
selective-prediction risk control), is CONTRADICTED at our scale (no lift on Qwen-1.5B → ORCA is
≥32B-only), or is PARTIALLY CONFIRMED (LTT works but TTT doesn't transfer).

**Related experiments:** P11-FE1170, P11-FE1171, P11-FE1172, P11-FE1173 (this brief);
H-1170, H-1171, H-1172.

**Status:** TO TEST
