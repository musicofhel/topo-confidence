# Triage brief — 2602.01893 — Geometric Analysis of Token Selection in Multi-Head Attention

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01893
Triaged: 2026-04-29

Authors: Mudarisov, Burtsev, Petrova, State (Feb 2026, arXiv preprint).
Models studied: LLaMA-2-7B, Gemma-7B, Mistral-7B.
Dataset: OpenWebText, context length L = 1024 (decode-step protocol).

## Refutations

1. **Refutes F-3 (prefill / final-token DoM orthogonal, cos = 0.046) by attribution to head-type structure, not "two functional directions".**
   *Mechanism.* The paper categorises heads into Retriever (output ≈ vL, the last token), Mixer (mass shifts from v0 to vL as N grows), and Reset (output ≈ v0, the sink). The depth-wise distribution (Figure 9) places Retriever heads predominantly in the first 1–2 layers, Reset heads in early layers, and Mixer heads from "lower-middle layers onwards" — i.e., L19 in a 28-layer model is in **Mixer-dominated territory**. A Mixer-head output is, by construction, an interpolation between v0 (sink) and vL (last token). The "prefill" hidden state at the last *prompt* token and the "final-token" hidden state at the last *generation* token differ chiefly in *which* token vL refers to and in the resulting attention-mass distribution. The 0.046 cosine could therefore reflect a mechanical sink-vs-content axis induced by Mixer heads, not a learned, content-dependent functional decomposition.
   *Falsifiable test.* Project L19 prefill DoM and L19 final-token DoM onto (a) the Retriever-head sub-projection, (b) the Mixer-head sub-projection, (c) the Reset-head sub-projection, using the paper's classification criteria. If cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) jumps from 0.046 to >0.5 inside any single head-type subspace, the orthogonality is structural (head-type partition) rather than functional, and F-3 needs to be re-stated.

2. **Refutes F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 = "computed correctness") via attention-sink / Reset-head confound.**
   *Mechanism.* Theorem 1 + Theorem 2 + Assumption 3 establish that the strongest geometric separability lives in the small-N regime (typically N ∈ [1, 4]) and that **sink similarity ρ0 chiefly biases Recall** — i.e., much of the variance in attention's selected-token aggregate is driven by how much mass lands on the sink. If Qwen-2.5-1.5B at L19 has any non-trivial Reset-head population, the prefill DoM direction (which is computed *over the prefill activations across heads*) will partially align with "fraction of attention mass routed to the sink token at L19". The AUROC 0.7731 could then be predominantly an attention-sink-mass signal — a correlate of *prompt familiarity / repetition / surface form* — rather than a model-internal correctness signal.
   *Falsifiable test.* For each MATH-500 problem, extract L19 attention weights at the prefill last-token position, sum α_0 across all 12 heads of L19, and call this `sink_mass(p)`. Compute the partial AUROC of prefill_DoM_projection(p) for predicting K=1 correctness *after* regressing out `sink_mass(p)`. If AUROC drops from 0.7731 to ≤0.62, F-2's "computed-correctness" framing collapses into "low-information prompts attract sink mass, and easy prompts are answered correctly".

3. **Refutes F-7 (D-bucket has a distinctive collective geometric signature) via the U-shaped separability theorem.**
   *Mechanism.* Theorem 1's lower bound (eq. 11) and Theorem 2's lower bound (eq. 13) together yield the headline geometric prediction: separability is high at N = 1–4 *and* at N ≈ L (trivially), but is **lowest at intermediate N (tens to ~hundreds)** because the margin ∆ shrinks. F-7's "collective" signature for D-bucket is computed over a bucket of ~12 problems out of ~33 in some splits — i.e., over a number of items that, at the per-token attention level, sits in exactly the regime the paper predicts to be margin-dominated noise rather than true geometric structure. If the D-bucket signature is driven by attention-aggregate properties and not by per-token top-N selection, the paper predicts that signal should be at the geometric noise floor.
   *Falsifiable test.* Re-run F-7's D-bucket signature analysis (PR-67/88, axis-projection, etc.) restricted to (a) the top-N=1, top-N=2, top-N=3 attention-selected tokens at L19 instead of the full residual stream, and (b) the residual stream without any selection (current). If the D-bucket signature *strengthens* under top-N restriction or *vanishes* without it, the current F-7 result is at least partially margin-noise from the wrong operating regime.

4. **Refutes H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits will yield clean partitions) via the paper's own null result on PR differences across head types.**
   *Mechanism.* Appendix A.7 / Figure 15 reports that "differences are modest in global means" between Retriever, Mixer, and Reset heads on Precision and Recall — the regime structure is visible in *alignment geometry* (cos with v0 / vL, leave-one-out norms) but not in raw separability metrics. If correctness signals at L19 ride on top of separability rather than on head-type alignment, then a head-by-head decomposition of prefill DoM into Retriever / Mixer / Reset contributions is unlikely to produce sharp separation between "prefill-DoM heads" and "final-token-DoM heads" — it will produce gradients, not partitions.
   *Falsifiable test.* For each L19 head h, compute (a) the projection of prefill DoM onto head h's value-state output, (b) the projection of final-token DoM onto the same head h's output. Sort heads by |proj_prefill| and |proj_final|. If the top-3 prefill heads and top-3 final-token heads overlap by ≥2 of 3, H-13's clean-partition framing fails and we should switch to a continuous attribution story.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **H-13 (head-level attribution of prefill vs final-token circuits)** — direct overlap. The paper *is* the geometric framework H-13 needs. Promote H-13 from PARKED-ish status with the paper's classification criteria and a labelled head-by-layer table for Qwen-2.5-1.5B as the deliverable. Numerical anchors: cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 (F-3); prefill DoM AUROC = 0.7731 (F-2).
- **F-2 (prefill L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)** — Sink-mass confound directly applies (see Refutation 2). Anchor: AUROC 0.7731.
- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)** — Head-type decomposition reframes orthogonality (see Refutation 1).
- **F-7 (D-bucket collective signature)** — Operating regime mismatch (see Refutation 3).
- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universal across architectures and scales)** — Soft tension. The paper establishes that **value-state norms are nearly position-constant (CV ~0.02–0.03 across all non-sink positions, all heads, all layers, in three 7B models)**. Breathing is a residual-stream / participation-ratio phenomenon, not a value-state-norm phenomenon, so this is not a direct refutation, but it constrains the mechanism: any breathing story that *requires* token-level value norm variation is ruled out by Assumption 1's empirical validation. Anchor: peak PR = 67/88 (F-1).
- **H-17 (DoM rotation during generation is RoPE-mechanical)** — The paper's 4-phase attention profile (sink, plateau, oscillation, recency) and Assumption 3 directly support a "mechanical positional structure" view of attention mass. The oscillatory phase (T1 < i ≤ T2) with cos(ω·i) modulation is consistent with RoPE phase structure leaking into attention weights. This is corroborating, not contradicting.
- **H-21 (D-bucket has narrower prefill attention than A-bucket)** — The paper's piecewise attention template gives a clean test: per-bucket fit (psink, pbase, η, ω, T1, T2) should differ if D vs A buckets differ in attention concentration.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Value-state norm CV diagnostic** — for each layer–head, compute coefficient of variation of ||v_i||₂ across positions i > 0; sink quantified separately as ||v_0|| / E_{i>0} ||v_i||.
  *Replication cost*: <30 min CPU on cached Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill activations from P11 H100 NPZs (need value-state W^V projections).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — provides immediate empirical check that the paper's Assumption 1 holds on Qwen-2.5-1.5B and quantifies how "sink-dominated" each layer is.

- **Cross-token cosine decay fit (β estimation)** — fit cos(v_i, v_{i+d}) = e^{-β d} per layer–head; report MAE.
  *Replication cost*: ~30 min CPU on the same NPZs, given attention values.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes if value states are cached, no otherwise — a cheap predictor for which heads are "well-modelled" by the paper's bounds.

- **Piecewise attention-weight template fit** — fit α_i to {p_sink at 0; p_base on [1,T1]; p_base(1+η cos(ω i)) on (T1,T2]; p_base e^{η(i-T2)} on (T2,L]}. Report per-head MAE and the fraction of heads under a threshold.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2 h CPU on cached attention weights at L19 for 500 MATH problems.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — gives D-bucket vs A-bucket attention-shape test for H-21.

- **Top-N geometric Precision / Recall / F-score (Definitions 1–3)** — for the top-N attention-weighted tokens at a head, compute the radii r_min, r_max in value-state space and the Precision / Recall under those radii. Sweep N ∈ {1,…,L}.
  *Replication cost*: 1 h CPU per (model, layer) on cached attention + value states.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly gives a geometric-separability fingerprint per head that we can correlate with prefill-DoM weight.

- **Retriever / Mixer / Reset head classifier** — classify heads by relative magnitudes of ||α_0 v_0||, ||α_L v_L||, and aggregate of the rest, plus the trajectory of cos(s(N), v_0) and cos(s(N), v_L) as N grows.
  *Replication cost*: <1 h CPU per layer on Qwen-2.5-1.5B.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — load-bearing for both H-13 and Refutations 1–4.

- **Type-guided head sparsification** — remove a fraction p of heads per layer ranked by ablation impact within head-type buckets; measure ∆NLL on next-token loss in a prefill+single-decode protocol.
  *Replication cost*: 1 day H100 (per-head ablation × 28 layers × 12 heads = 336 ablation runs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: maybe — only if we want to validate that prefill-DoM-relevant heads survive type-guided sparsification (i.e., prefill DoM tracks "important" heads). Lower priority than the diagnostics.

- **Sink-similarity vs Recall correlation diagnostic** — corr(R(r_min, N), ρ_0) as a function of N. The paper finds a peak at N ≈ 3 with magnitude up to 0.6 in Gemma/Mistral, ~0.25 in LLaMA-2.
  *Replication cost*: <30 min CPU on cached data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly tests whether Qwen-2.5-1.5B sink-similarity behaviour is in the LLaMA-like or Gemma-like regime.

## Approaches & framings

- **"Attention as a margin-based geometric classifier in value-state space"** — replaces the "attention-as-soft-retrieval" or "attention-as-information-routing" framings with a Precision / Recall / F-score story over a top-N selection set. Connection to F-2 / F-3 / F-7: every existing finding currently lives in residual-stream space; this lens proposes the value-state space is the right place to *first* compute geometric quantities, with the residual stream as a downstream summary. Worth re-asking: are F-2 / F-3 / F-7 visible in value-state space at L19, or only after the o-projection back to the residual stream?
- **"Top-N is a tunable operating regime, not a fixed mechanism"** — Theorem-driven framing that sharpness of separability is non-monotonic in N, with an optimum at N = 1–4 and trivial recovery at N → L. This bears on every probe / classifier we have built over residual aggregates: those aggregates are implicitly using N = L (no selection), which the paper proves to be a low-margin regime. New question: would an explicit top-N=2 attention-restricted activation give a higher AUROC than the full residual stream at L19?
- **"Head taxonomy → depth-pipeline (Retriever → Reset → Mixer)"** — depth-dependent functional progression. Maps onto an interpretability claim that early layers stabilise representations, mid layers integrate context (where L19 likely sits), and late layers refine token-aligned features for prediction. Sharpens H-13's question: which depth's heads carry prefill-DoM signal?

## Datasets & benchmarks

- **OpenWebText** — ~8M docs, public, accessible, used for next-token-prediction / decode-step diagnostics. **Applicable: no** for the correctness-prediction question (no labels), **yes** for any pure attention-geometry replication on Qwen-2.5-1.5B if we want to compare to LLaMA-2-7B / Gemma / Mistral on the same corpus the paper used. Not a substitute for MATH-500.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Decode-step protocol: prefill the entire L = 1024 context, then take a single decode step. Measure attention weights and value states at that one decode position. (This matters: the paper's claims are *not* trained on full autoregressive generation; they are about the attention behaviour at the single next-token-prediction step. Our P11 final-token analyses are doing something similar; our prefill analyses correspond to position L−1 in the prompt, which is comparable.)
- Sink-token compression: ||v_0||₂ = λ · C_{l,h} with 0 < λ ≪ 1. Practical implication for any code that normalises by ||v|| — handle position 0 as a special case or you will divide by a tiny number.
- Exponential cross-token similarity: cos(v_i, v_j) ≈ e^{−β|i−j|}, β > 0. β values are reported per layer–head (not summarised in a single table in the body — would need Appendix A.3 plot digitisation if we want quantitative anchors).
- 4-phase attention profile parameters (Assumption 3): {p_sink, p_base, η (oscillation amplitude / recency growth rate), ω (oscillation frequency), T1, T2}. Note: η is overloaded for both oscillation and recency in the paper's notation — Table 1 in §A.5 lists it once.
- No public code link found in the body of the paper. Their related prior arXiv:2025 (Mudarisov et al, "Limitations of normalization in attention") may have shared infrastructure.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Sink-mass ratio at L19, all layers, Qwen-2.5-1.5B prefill** — needed for Refutation 2. Requires per-layer attention weights at the prefill last-token position; if `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute` cached the residual streams but **not** the attention weights, this is a *new* extraction (~20 min H100 on the existing 500-prompt set).
- **L19 head-type classification on Qwen-2.5-1.5B** — needed for Refutations 1, 4 and for promoting H-13. Requires value-state outputs *per head* at L19 prefill last-token + final-token positions for the 500 MATH-500 prompts. If the cached NPZs are concatenated post-o-projection residual streams, this is a *new* extraction (~30 min H100).
- **Value-state norm CV per head at L19** — Replication of the paper's Assumption 1 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B. Same data dependency as above; if extracted, runs in <10 min CPU.
- **Top-N=2 vs N=L AUROC comparison for prefill DoM** — refutation test for the operating-regime claim. Requires per-token attention weights at L19 + per-token value states (because the top-N=2 output is α_{i_1}v_{i_1} + α_{i_2}v_{i_2} where i_1, i_2 are top by attention weight). If we already have residual streams but not the per-token value states, this is *not* immediately runnable on cached data.

In short: **none of these are runnable on the currently cached NPZs without a re-extraction pass that emits per-head value states and per-head attention weights at L19**. The cheapest thing we can do *right now* is run the value-state norm CV check and the head-type classifier *once* a re-extraction has emitted the right tensors.

## Proposed HYPOTHESES.md additions

### H-83: Prefill-DoM AUROC at L19 is largely an attention-sink-mass surrogate
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2602.01893 + F-2
**Test:** Run P11-FE85. Regress prefill_DoM_projection on sink_mass at L19 across the 500 MATH-500 prompts. Compute (i) R² of sink_mass alone, (ii) partial AUROC of prefill_DoM for K=1 correctness after partialling out sink_mass.
**Requires:** Per-head L19 attention weights at prefill last-token (depends on P11-FE83 re-extraction) + 1.5B Qwen + cached MATH-500 K=1 labels.
**Would change:** Confirm → F-2's mechanism story collapses; the prefill DoM signal should be re-described as a sink-attention surrogate, with implications for F-8 selective prediction. Reject → F-2 stands as a content-dependent computed-correctness signal.
**Blocks:** Promotion of any "prefill DoM = computed confidence" framing in the writeup.

### H-84: Cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) is a head-type-partition artifact, not a functional orthogonality
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2602.01893 + F-3
**Test:** Run P11-FE86. Within each of {Retriever, Mixer, Reset} L19 head-type subspaces, compute cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM). Compare against the unrestricted 0.046.
**Requires:** Head-type labels at L19 (from P11-FE84) + per-head value states at L19 prefill + final positions.
**Would change:** Confirm (any within-subspace cos > 0.5) → F-3 needs to be re-stated. Reject (all within-subspace cosines remain near 0) → F-3's orthogonality is a true functional-direction claim, not a head-type artifact.
**Blocks:** H-13 (the head-level attribution will bottom out at the same answer).

### H-85: Top-N=2 attention-restricted L19 aggregate beats the full residual stream at K=1 correctness AUROC
**Priority:** HIGH
**Motivated by:** 2602.01893 Theorem 1 + Theorem 2
**Test:** Run P11-FE87. Sweep N ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, L}. At each N, re-fit DoM on the L19 prefill last-token aggregate restricted to top-N attention weights. Compute K=1 correctness AUROC.
