# Triage brief — 2604.12016 — Identity as Attractor: Geometric Evidence for Persistent Agent Architecture in LLM Activation Space

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12016
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1 — Mean pooling as confound for F-2 prefill DoM AUROC (0.7731).**
The paper's Ablation 3 (Table 4) shows that last-token pooling produces *no significant geometric signal* (d ≈ 0) while mean pooling produces strong signal (d > 1.88) across the same documents. Our prefill DoM (F-2) is computed from mean-pooled prefill activations. If the mean-pooling mechanism is what concentrates the signal — averaging distributed semantic content into a direction — then the L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731 could partially reflect the aggregation mechanism rather than a genuine single-direction correctness signature. A direct test: compute DoM from the *last prefill token only* at L19 and compare AUROC to the mean-pooled version. If last-token DoM AUROC drops substantially (as their d ≈ 0 predicts for single-token representations), the "single direction" framing of F-2 needs qualification as "single mean-pooled direction."
*Falsification*: Run last-token-only L19 prefill DoM on cached MATH-500 activations; if AUROC ≥ 0.75, the mean-pooling confound is rejected.

**R2 — F-3 prefill/final orthogonality (cos = 0.046) as artifact of pooling strategy, not structural.**
The paper explicitly argues that last-token hidden states encode "next-token prediction context" while mean pooling captures "document-level semantic content" (Section 3, Ablation 3 interpretation). Our F-3 finding that cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046 compares a mean-pooled prefill direction against a final-token direction. If these two pooling strategies inherently capture orthogonal signal types (distributed semantics vs. positional continuation), the near-zero cosine is *expected from the pooling mismatch* rather than revealing a deep structural fact about the model's correctness representations. This would downgrade F-3 from "structurally orthogonal directions" to "different aggregation strategies produce different directions, trivially."
*Falsification*: Compute mean-pooled DoM over the *generation* tokens (not just the final token) and measure cos(prefill_mean_DoM, generation_mean_DoM). If this cosine is also near zero, the structural orthogonality stands; if it jumps substantially, the pooling mismatch explains F-3.

**R3 — F-7 D-bucket "distinctive collective geometry" as generic semantic clustering, not correctness signal.**
Section 4.5 (Table 7) demonstrates that *any* semantically coherent document produces tight paraphrase clustering (the control agent Sigma also clusters significantly tighter than cross-agent distances). If D-bucket problems share semantic features (e.g., problems involving specific mathematical domains, problem-statement structures, or reasoning patterns), their "distinctive collective geometric signature" (F-7) could simply reflect semantic coherence of the problem class, not a correctness-derived geometric property. The paper's Cohen's d = 0.46–0.88 for YAR-vs-Sigma specificity shows the effect scales with document complexity — longer, more structured prompts cluster tighter — which for MATH-500 means problems with more elaborate reasoning chains might cluster by *complexity*, not by *correctness bucket*.
*Falsification*: Shuffle D-bucket labels randomly among problems of matched semantic content (same math subdomain, same difficulty rating); if the "D-bucket geometry" persists under shuffled labels, F-7's correctness attribution is refuted.

**R4 — F-4 "asymmetric collapse" as generic attractor convergence, not correctness-specific.**
The paper shows within-group cosine distance *decreases with layer depth* (0.0106 → 0.0070 from L8 to L24) for semantically coherent inputs — i.e., progressive representational collapse is a generic property of transformer depth, not specific to "correct" trajectories. If the asymmetric collapse (F-4: correct trajectories collapse harder than incorrect) is simply a manifestation of correct answers being more semantically coherent (well-formed reasoning → tighter cluster) while incorrect answers are diverse (many ways to be wrong → looser cluster), then F-4 reduces to "correct reasoning is more semantically self-consistent than incorrect reasoning" — which is nearly tautological and doesn't warrant a geometric/dynamical interpretation.
*Falsification*: Match correct and incorrect trajectories for semantic coherence (e.g., by perplexity or self-consistency score); if collapse asymmetry disappears after matching, F-4's geometric claim is weakened.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731)**: The paper's mean-pooling-vs-last-token finding (d > 1.88 for mean, d ≈ 0 for last-token) directly interrogates the mechanism behind our strongest probe. Their result on Llama 3.1 8B at layers 8, 16, 24 — using 4096-dim activations — is in the same methodological space as our Qwen-2.5-1.5B L19 1536-dim mean-pooled prefill direction.

- **F-3 (cos(prefill_DoM, final_DoM) = 0.046)**: Their demonstration that mean pooling and last-token pooling capture orthogonal signal types (semantic vs. positional) provides a mechanistic explanation for the near-zero cosine. This is neither confirming nor refuting — it reframes the finding from "structurally interesting orthogonality" to "expected pooling-strategy divergence."

- **F-4 (asymmetric collapse)**: Their monotonic within-group convergence with depth (Table 1, both models) is consistent with the collapse direction but suggests it may be a generic transformer property.

- **F-10 (PH null)**: The paper uses only cosine distances, not PH. Their attractor geometry is fully captured by L2/cosine metrics, which is consistent with F-10's finding that topology adds nothing beyond covariance-based measures.

- **H-1 (DoM steering)**: Their exploratory steering experiment (Section 4.6) shows non-monotonic steering effects: α = 5 gives +0.40 improvement, α ≥ 10 degrades or produces incoherent outputs. Directly relevant to H-1's hypothesis about per-position DoM steering — suggests there may be an optimal injection magnitude and that overshooting produces degenerate behavior. They use the same ActAdd framework (2308.10248) as our H-1 baseline.

- **H-17 (DoM rotation is RoPE-mechanical)**: Their last-token null result is consistent with positional encoding dominating single-token representations, which aligns with the hypothesis that per-token DoM changes during generation could be RoPE-driven rather than semantically computed.

- **F-1 (dimensional breathing universal)**: Their non-monotonic layer-16 bump in Llama (but not Gemma) suggests architecture-dependent convergence dynamics at intermediate layers. This intersects with breathing's layer-wise profile — the bump occurs right where breathing effects are typically measured.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Mean-pooled hidden state cosine distance as attractor metric** — compute mean-pooled activations per input, pairwise cosine distance matrix, Welch's t-test for within-vs-between group separation with Bonferroni correction + permutation test + Mann-Whitney U.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU (on cached NPZs).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — directly applicable to cached L19 prefill activations to test whether correct-vs-incorrect MATH-500 clusters show attractor-like separation.

- **Truncation + pooling ablation** — systematically compare mean pooling vs last-token pooling at multiple truncation lengths (full, 512, 256 tokens) to disentangle pooling mechanism from representational signal.
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU (on cached NPZs if last-token activations are available; otherwise needs fresh extraction ~2h on 2060 Super).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — critical for understanding whether F-2's DoM AUROC is mean-pooling-dependent.

- **Centroid-contrastive steering vector** — compute (mean_A+B − mean_C) / ‖...‖ as a steering vector, inject at a target layer via forward hook at varying α scales.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h GPU (needs forward passes with hooks).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: conditional — relevant if H-1 (DoM steering) is prioritized, but the 2060 Super may struggle with hook-injected forward passes on Qwen-2.5-1.5B.

- **Bootstrap length-control analysis** — generate N random length-matched excerpts as null distribution for distillation proximity.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes — useful for testing whether D-bucket "distinctive geometry" (F-7) survives a bootstrapped semantic-content control.

## Approaches & framings

- **Identity-as-attractor framing**: transformer layers as contractive mappings toward concept-specific attractors (following Chytas & Singh 2025 IFS formalization). Intersects our F-1 breathing / F-4 collapse — could reframe "breathing" as approach-to-attractor dynamics where correct solutions converge to a tighter attractor basin.

- **"Knowing about vs operating as" hierarchy**: reading a description of an identity produces ~65–74% of the geometric shift toward the attractor, but doesn't reach the attractor itself. Could reframe F-2: the prefill DoM direction might encode "knowing about the problem structure" (partial attractor approach) vs the generation phase which "operates as" a solver.

- **Distributed-vs-positional signal distinction**: mean pooling captures distributed semantic content; last-token captures positional/continuation context. This is a reusable framing for any probe comparison between aggregation strategies in our pipeline.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Layer selection at 25%, 50%, 75% depth (layers 8, 16, 24 of 32-layer model) provides good coverage without exhaustive sweep.
- Permutation test (n=10,000) and Mann-Whitney U alongside Welch's t-test for robustness against non-normality with small n.
- Cohen's d > 1.88 across all conditions — far above conventional large-effect threshold (0.8).
- Steering injection: non-monotonic α response — α = 5 optimal, α > 10 degrades. Important calibration point for any steering experiment.
- Mean pooling on truncated (256-token) inputs still produces d > 2.3 — signal concentrated in early document sections.

## 2604.12016 — Identity as Attractor: Geometric Evidence for Persistent Agent Architecture in LLM Activation Space (Vasilenko, 2026)

**Relevance:** Demonstrates attractor-like geometry in LLM activation space using mean-pooled hidden states, cosine distance, and controlled paraphrase experiments on Llama 3.1 8B and Gemma 2 9B. Their key ablation finding — that last-token pooling produces no geometric signal (d ≈ 0) while mean pooling produces large effects (d > 1.88) — directly interrogates the mechanism underlying our F-2 prefill DoM probe, which uses mean-pooled prefill activations. Their attractor convergence with depth and generic semantic clustering findings provide alternative explanations for F-3 (prefill/final orthogonality), F-4 (asymmetric collapse), and F-7 (D-bucket geometry).

**Key claim we tested:** Semantically equivalent inputs converge to a tighter representational cluster than semantically distant inputs in activation space, with the signal carried by mean pooling and absent from last-token representations.

**Our result:** **TO TEST** — the mean-vs-last-token pooling distinction has not been tested on our MATH-500 correctness probe. Four FE experiments proposed (P11-FE131016 through P11-FE131019) to assess whether their findings transfer from identity-document geometry to correctness-prediction geometry.
