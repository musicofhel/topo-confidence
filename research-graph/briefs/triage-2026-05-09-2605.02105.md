# Triage brief — 2605.02105 — Sharpness-Aware Pretraining Mitigates Catastrophic Forgetting

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02105
Triaged: 2026-05-09

## Refutations

**R1 — Could refute F-2 (L19 prefill DoM AUROC 0.7731) by showing DoM is an optimizer-induced artifact, not an intrinsic feature.**
The paper demonstrates that SAM fundamentally reshapes the directional curvature profile of converged checkpoints: small-batch SAM reduces Tr(H) and directional sharpness along fine-tuning-relevant directions (Section 4.2, Figure 12). If H-734 holds (DoM ≈ top Hessian eigenvector), then SAM's explicit suppression of λ_max would flatten the spectral gap that produces the dominant PC1 direction (cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216). Extracting L19 DoM from a SAM-pretrained OLMo-1B (their publicly available checkpoint) and finding AUROC ≪ 0.7731 would show F-2's signal is optimizer-contingent, not architecture-universal.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Run the P11 DoM extraction pipeline on the SAM mid-trained OLMo-2-1B checkpoint on MATH-500 at 1024 tok. Compare L19 DoM AUROC to the AdamW baseline checkpoint (also provided). If SAM AUROC drops below 0.70 while AdamW AUROC stays near 0.77, the refutation fires.

**R2 — Could refute F-1 (breathing universality) by demonstrating breathing is a sharpness artifact that disappears under flat-minima optimization.**
The paper shows SAM reduces both trace and directional sharpness monotonically with token budget (Figure 12a), meaning the geometry of SAM checkpoints is fundamentally flatter. If dimensional breathing (F-1's expansion-then-collapse pattern) is driven by high curvature in specific loss-landscape directions, SAM-pretrained models would show attenuated or absent breathing. The paper's OLMo-2-1B SAM checkpoint is publicly available — measuring per-layer participation ratio across tokens on that checkpoint would directly test whether breathing persists at flatter minima. If breathing disappears under SAM, F-1's "universal across architectures" claim must be narrowed to "universal under AdamW-class sharp-minima optimizers."
*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute per-layer participation ratio across sequence positions on SAM vs AdamW OLMo-2-1B checkpoints for 50 MATH-500 problems. If SAM shows <50% of AdamW's breathing amplitude, the refutation fires.

**R3 — Could refute H-735 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B not at EoS) by providing evidence that EoS governs LLM pretraining dynamics at the 1B scale.**
The paper extensively validates EoS (λ_max(H) ≈ 2/η) as a real phenomenon shaping LLM pretraining at 20M–150M scale, and uses it to explain why higher peak learning rates improve the learning-forgetting tradeoff (Sections 2.3.2, 3.3, Figure 6). If the EoS relationship holds at 1.5B scale (which their scaling trends suggest it might — see Figure 3b where SAM's advantage persists or grows with scale), then Qwen-2.5-1.5B's pretraining may indeed operate at EoS, contradicting H-735. This would mean L19's spectral gap (H-743) is constrained by η rather than being a task-structure intrinsic — the DoM direction would be an EoS artifact.
*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute λ_max of Qwen-2.5-1.5B's pretraining-loss Hessian (or a stochastic approximation via Lanczos on the pretraining distribution) and compare to 2/η_peak. If λ_max/η_peak ∈ [1.5, 2.5], H-735 is refuted.

**R4 — Could refute H-734 (DoM aligns with top Hessian eigenvector) by showing SAM suppresses the top eigenvector without affecting correctness signals.**
The paper demonstrates that SAM reduces directional sharpness while maintaining comparable base-model quality (OLMo-2-1B SAM base = 42.9 vs AdamW base = 43.2, Table 2). If SAM flattens the Hessian's top eigenvector but the DoM AUROC on the SAM checkpoint remains high, then DoM cannot be aligned with the top Hessian eigenvector — it must come from a different geometric source (e.g., a mid-spectrum eigenvector, or a feature direction orthogonal to the sharpest loss-curvature direction).
*Falsifiable experiment:* On SAM and AdamW OLMo-2-1B checkpoints: (a) compute top Hessian eigenvector via power iteration on a mini-batch, (b) compute L19 DoM direction, (c) measure cos(DoM, top_Hessian_eigvec) for each. If SAM has cos ≈ 0 but AUROC ≈ AdamW's AUROC, H-734 is refuted.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** The paper's central mechanism — that optimizer choice determines directional curvature — directly questions whether F-2's signal is robust across optimization recipes. The DoM direction's near-identity with PC1 (cos 0.9216) could be a spectral-gap artifact of AdamW's sharp minima.

- **F-1 (breathing universality):** The paper's evidence that SAM-pretrained checkpoints have fundamentally different geometric properties (reduced Hessian trace, directional sharpness) implies breathing's "universality" may be conditioned on optimizer class, not just architecture.

- **F-10 (PH null against Gaussian):** The paper's Hessian analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for F-10: if the loss landscape around the AdamW checkpoint is approximately quadratic (Section 4.1, validated up to moderate perturbation), then activations sampled from this near-quadratic basin would be approximately Gaussian — explaining why PH detects no signal beyond covariance.

- **H-734 (DoM ≈ top Hessian eigenvector):** The paper provides the tooling (directional sharpness computation along arbitrary parameter-space directions) and the experimental design (SAM vs AdamW Hessian comparison) needed to test H-734 directly.

- **H-735 (Qwen-2.5-1.5B not at EoS):** The paper's EoS analysis at 20M–150M scale and validation at 1B scale provides the strongest external evidence to date that EoS dynamics operate during LLM pretraining, bearing directly on H-735.

- **H-739 (breathing is AdamW-specific):** The paper gives the clearest experimental framework for testing H-739: compare SAM vs AdamW checkpoints for breathing presence. SAM's explicit flat-minima bias is the most controlled intervention for isolating optimizer-induced geometry.

- **H-743 (L19 spectral gap is an outlier):** If the spectral gap at L19 is shaped by directional sharpness (as the paper's framework suggests), then SAM would shrink that gap. Testing whether L19 remains a spectral-gap outlier under SAM would determine whether H-743 is optimizer-specific.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Directional sharpness measurement along a specific parameter-space direction** — Given a checkpoint θ and a perturbation direction Δ, compute κ(Δ; H) = Δ⊤HΔ/‖Δ‖² using Hessian-vector products. The paper measures this for fine-tuning-direction perturbations.
  *Replication cost*: 2–4h H100 (need Hessian-vector products on Qwen-2.5-1.5B, ~10 Lanczos iterations).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires H100 forward+backward passes with Hessian computation, high cost relative to information gain for our pipeline.

- **Learning-forgetting Pareto frontier estimation** — Sweep fine-tuning learning rates on a checkpoint, plot (pretraining_loss_post_FT, FT_loss) pairs, extract Pareto frontier.
  *Replication cost*: days of H100 compute per checkpoint.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — requires full fine-tuning sweeps, far outside our budget.

- **Quadratic approximation quality test** — Compare observed post-perturbation loss to L(θ) + ½Δ⊤HΔ; measure the gap as a function of perturbation magnitude and token budget.
  *Replication cost*: 4–8h H100 (Hessian computation + evaluation at perturbed checkpoints).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no at weight level, but yes at activation level (see FE below).

- **Isotropic Gaussian weight perturbation sensitivity profile** — Perturb each layer's weights by γ·‖W‖_F·Z/‖Z‖_F and measure loss degradation; sweep γ.
  *Replication cost*: 1–2h H100 (forward passes at multiple perturbation levels).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: yes at activation-analogue level — perturb cached L19 activations with isotropic noise, measure DoM AUROC degradation as a function of noise magnitude.

- **SAM mid-training** — Switch optimizer from AdamW to SAM during learning-rate decay phase (last 10% of training). Adds ~10% compute overhead to capture most of full-SAM benefit.
  *Replication cost*: days of H100 for a 1.5B model.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: no — we use frozen checkpoints, not our own pretraining.

## Approaches & framings

- **Forgetting as directional curvature in weight space.** The paper reframes catastrophic forgetting as ½‖Δ_FT‖²·κ(Δ_FT; H) — a product of distance traveled and sharpness in the fine-tuning direction. This geometric framing could be adapted to our setting: "correctness-signal fragility" could be understood as the directional curvature of the AUROC surface along the DoM direction in activation space.

- **Base-model quality ≠ post-modification quality.** The paper's central insight — that the best base model (lowest pretraining loss) is not necessarily the best model after modification — is a cautionary framing for H-739/H-734. Our DoM signal may be strong in the AdamW-trained base model but fragile under downstream modification (RLHF, quantization), limiting deployment applicability (cf. APPLICATIONS.md).

- **EoS as implicit sharpness control.** The paper connects learning rate to λ_max(H) ≈ 2/η, giving a quantitative prediction for the sharpness profile. This provides a testable framework for H-735: if Qwen's peak learning rate during pretraining is known, we can predict whether it operated at EoS.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- SAM hyperparameter: ρ = 0.05 selected from small-scale tuning (Appendix C.1), used consistently across all scales.
- SAM mid-training on OLMo-2-1B: 50B tokens on Dolmino mixture, WSD schedule with linear annealing, max context length 2048, batch size 1024, LR 7.4487e-5.
- Annealing-only SAM: apply SAM only during last 10% of WSD schedule (decay phase). Captures most of full-SAM benefit at ~10% overhead.
- Gaussian perturbation protocol: per-layer isotropic noise scaled by γ·‖W‖_F, γ ∈ {0.009, 0.013, 0.017, 0.020, 0.025}.
- OLMo-2-1B SAM checkpoint: pretrained on 4T tokens + 50B token SAM mid-training. Publicly available via OLMo model repository.
- Directional sharpness computed at canonical fine-tuning LR = 4×10⁻⁴.

## Replicable intermediates

- **Activation-space perturbation sensitivity:** Add isotropic Gaussian noise to cached L19 prefill activations (500 × 1536 from `pathway11_h100/prefill_gated_compute/`) at varying magnitudes γ. Recompute DoM AUROC at each γ. This is the activation-space analogue of the paper's weight-perturbation experiment (Section 3.4, Figure 8b). Uses cached NPZs only; 10min CPU.
- **Directional AUROC curvature:** Starting from the cached DoM direction, compute AUROC for DoM + ε·v where v is sampled from random unit vectors orthogonal to DoM. Plot AUROC(ε) to measure the "sharpness" of the AUROC surface around DoM. Uses cached DoM vector + L19 activations; 15min CPU.
- **Quadratic fit of AUROC landscape:** Fit AUROC(θ) ≈ a₀ + a₁·θ + a₂·θ² where θ is the angle from the DoM direction (parameterized along random great circles). Test whether quadratic approximation holds for |θ| < 15°. Analogous to paper's Section 4.1 quadratic approximation test. Uses cached data; 15min CPU.
