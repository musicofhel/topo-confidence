# Triage brief — 2606.04010 — The Variance Brain Foundation Models Forgot: Third-Order Statistics Predict Cognition Where Billion-Parameter Models Fail

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04010
Triaged: 2026-06-06

## Refutations

**Refutation 1 — F-10 (PH Gaussian null): Tucker/HOSVD is a more targeted non-Gaussianity detector than persistent homology.**

F-10 concludes that topology summary statistics on residual streams are not distinguishable from a Gaussian null. For Gaussian distributions, all cumulants κ_n = 0 for n ≥ 3. Marraffini et al. show that even when raw κ₃ tensor entries are individually noisy, the Tucker decomposition of the co-skewness tensor extracts a low-rank subspace that carries structured non-Gaussian signal invisible to both covariance analysis and (by implication) geometric probes like PH that test shape rather than statistical order. The refutation mechanism: compute the co-skewness tensor S_{ijk} = (1/N) Σ z_i(n) z_j(n) z_k(n) on the 500×1536 cached L19 prefill activations, apply Tucker/HOSVD to extract the rank-R factor matrix U, project activations into this κ₃-optimal subspace, and test whether covariance features there predict correctness better than in PCA subspace. If Tucker-basis features outperform PCA-basis features, F-10's null would be weakened — there IS non-Gaussian structure in L19 residual streams, just of a kind PH cannot detect.

*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute Tucker decomposition of L19 co-skewness tensor, compare AUROC of ridge-regression correctness probe on FC-Tucker vs FC-PCA features at matched rank R. If Tucker > PCA by Cohen's d ≥ 0.3, F-10's Gaussian conclusion is too strong.

**Refutation 2 — F-2 (DoM AUROC 0.7731) and cov-spectrum probe (0.7928): PCA basis may be suboptimal for the covariance-spectrum probe.**

The cov-spectrum probe (FE881, AUROC 0.7928) uses PC1-residualized log-eigenvalues from the PCA decomposition — i.e., directions that maximize κ₂ (variance). Marraffini et al. show at matched dimensionality that Tucker basis (κ₃-maximizing directions) consistently outperforms PCA basis for downstream prediction, with Cohen's d = 0.90–1.57 on AOMIC AAL and 0.62–1.21 on HCP AAL. If this transfers to LLM activations, the 0.7928 AUROC is not a ceiling but a lower bound under a suboptimal basis. The refutation fires if covariance features computed in the Tucker subspace of L19 activations exceed 0.7928 AUROC.

*Falsifiable experiment:* Replace PCA with Tucker/HOSVD for subspace selection in the cov-spectrum pipeline. Compare AUROC at matched rank. If Tucker-basis cov-spectrum probe > 0.7928, the current result underestimates the linear ceiling.

**Refutation 3 — F-1 (dimensional breathing is universal): Breathing may track dominant variance (noise) rather than task-relevant structure.**

The paper's central diagnosis is that self-supervised objectives allocate capacity to dominant variance components that are uninformative for the downstream task. Participation ratio (the metric underlying F-1's "breathing") is a summary of the eigenvalue spectrum — it is entirely determined by κ₂. Marraffini et al. show that a κ₂-optimal basis captures different structure than a κ₃-optimal basis, and that the task-relevant signal lives in κ₃. If the breathing phenomenon is dominated by high-variance directions that are computationally active but uninformative for correctness (analogous to physiological noise in fMRI), then F-1's universality describes a computational artifact rather than a task-relevant geometric signal. The refutation fires if breathing metrics computed on Tucker-projected activations (κ₃ subspace) show a different pattern than on full or PCA-projected activations.

*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute participation ratio trajectories for correct vs incorrect problems in (a) full space, (b) top-R PCA subspace, (c) top-R Tucker subspace. If breathing pattern differs qualitatively across subspaces, F-1's universality claim needs qualification.

**Refutation 4 — H-4 (Breathing ↔ Sharpness Dimension / EoS): Breathing may be a κ₂-only phenomenon that misses the κ₃ structure carrying task-relevant signal.**

If the connection between breathing and sharpness dimension (H-4) is real, it would be a second-order (κ₂) phenomenon by construction — both participation ratio and the Hessian eigenvalue spectrum are second-order statistics. Marraffini et al.'s finding that task-relevant information lives at κ₃, not κ₂, would imply that the breathing↔sharpness correspondence, even if confirmed, would explain a computational mechanism unrelated to the correctness-predictive signal. This would make H-4 a dead end for understanding *why* DoM predicts correctness.

*Falsifiable experiment:* Compute spectral gap (sharpness-related) and participation ratio on PCA vs Tucker subspaces of L19 activations. If the κ₃ subspace shows no breathing but retains full correctness-prediction power, breathing is dissociated from the correctness signal.

## Direct connections to F-N / H-N

- **F-10 (PH Gaussian null, AUROC baseline):** The paper's co-skewness tensor analysis is the most direct methodological alternative to PH for detecting non-Gaussian structure. Our PH analysis tested geometric shape; their κ₃ analysis tests statistical order. Both ask "is there structure beyond covariance?" but through orthogonal lenses. The AUROC 0.7731 (F-2) and 0.7928 (cov-spectrum) are purely κ₁/κ₂ features — if κ₃ carries additional signal, both are conservative.

- **F-2 (L19 DoM AUROC 0.7731):** DoM is a first-order statistic (mean direction). The paper's hierarchy κ₁ → κ₂ → κ₃ → ... suggests that each order captures independent structure. Our progression from DoM (0.7731, first-order) to cov-spectrum (0.7928, second-order eigenvalues) matches their prediction. The natural next step is κ₃ features on L19 activations.

- **F-9 (CoE-60 redundant with single-layer DoM):** The paper shows that temporal reduction does not help (Appendix E, Table 8) — the task-relevant signal is spatial, not temporal. This parallels F-9's finding that CoE-60 (a temporal trajectory feature across 60 layers) is redundant with single-layer L19 DoM: the information is in the spatial (cross-dimension) structure at a single layer, not in the temporal (cross-layer) trajectory.

- **H-6 (CoE-60 still beats single-layer DoM at 1024-tok labels):** The paper's temporal vs spatial analysis (Table 8) provides a framework to test H-6: if temporal features are redundant with spatial features at a single layer (as Marraffini finds for fMRI), H-6 is likely to be rejected.

- **cos(DoM, PC1) = 0.9216:** The paper's finding that PCA (κ₂) and Tucker (κ₃) bases differ substantially suggests that PC1 ≈ DoM is a κ₂ phenomenon. The 0.9216 alignment means DoM is almost entirely captured by variance-maximizing directions, consistent with the paper's framework.

## Methodologies extracted

- **Tucker/HOSVD decomposition of co-skewness tensor** — Decomposes the third-order cumulant tensor S ∈ ℝ^{P×P×P} via higher-order SVD to extract factor matrix U ∈ ℝ^{P×R} whose columns span the subspace maximizing third-order structure. Analogous to how PCA maximizes variance (κ₂), Tucker maximizes co-skewness (κ₃).
  *Replication cost*: 30min CPU on our 500×1536 matrix (tensor is 1536³ ≈ 3.6B entries, but rank-R Tucker with R=80 is tractable via iterative HOSVD).
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — directly applicable to cached L19 prefill activations. The paper's code is available.

- **Per-cumulant preservation metrics** — Quantifies how much κ₂ and κ₃ structure a transformation preserves via Log-Cholesky relative error and Frobenius NMSE, computed in the Tucker subspace. Allows diagnosis of *which statistical order* carries a given signal.
  *Replication cost*: 15min CPU once Tucker basis is computed.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — compute on L19 activations to characterize whether the correct/incorrect split lives in κ₂ vs κ₃.

- **Log-Cholesky distance on SPD manifold** — Riemannian distance between positive definite matrices via Cholesky factorization Σ = LL^⊤, combining strict lower-triangular L₂ norm with log-diagonal L₂ norm. Used as both a finetuning loss and an evaluation metric.
  *Replication cost*: 10min CPU implementation, trivial.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — could replace Euclidean distance when comparing per-problem covariance matrices.

- **Dual-moment (κ₂ + κ₃) finetuning loss** — Weighted combination L(λ) = λ · d_LC(FC(Z), FC(Z̃)) + (1−λ) · d_{κ₃}(Z, Z̃) with two κ₃ surrogates: coskew_mse (direct tensor MSE) and fc_tucker (Log-Cholesky on FC computed in κ₃-optimal Tucker subspace). fc_tucker consistently outperforms coskew_mse.
  *Replication cost*: Requires retraining, not applicable to our cached data.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **no** — our pipeline doesn't train models; we extract and probe.

- **Cumulant expansion as a diagnostic hierarchy** — Frame any representation analysis as a cumulant hierarchy: κ₁ (mean), κ₂ (covariance/FC), κ₃ (co-skewness), κ₄ (co-kurtosis)... Each order isolates genuinely new structure. For Gaussians, κ_n = 0 for n ≥ 3.
  *Replication cost*: Conceptual framework, 0 cost.
  *We'd plausibly run this*: **yes** — reframes our DoM → cov-spectrum progression as a κ₁ → κ₂ hierarchy and motivates testing κ₃.

## Approaches & framings

- **Variance allocation problem** — Self-supervised objectives allocate model capacity to dominant variance components, which may be uninformative for the downstream task. This is a general diagnosis applicable to any pretraining → probing pipeline. Intersects our setup: if L19 activations are shaped by the pretraining loss to represent dominant variance (next-token prediction), the correctness signal may live in higher-order residuals that the main variance direction (PC1 ≈ DoM) partially overlaps by accident rather than by design.

- **Basis selection matters more than compression amount** — At matched dimensionality, Tucker (κ₃) basis consistently outperforms PCA (κ₂) basis. The choice of subspace criterion, not the number of features, is the primary lever. Directly relevant to how we select features for the cov-spectrum probe.

- **κ₃ is the useful cue, but it enters the pipeline through the basis, not through its entries** — Direct κ₃ tensor entries are too noisy; the Tucker basis denoises them. The informative object is the subspace geometry, not the tensor values. Parallels our finding that DoM (a direction) carries more signal than the raw activation values.

## Implementation details worth capturing

- Tucker decomposition via higher-order SVD (HOSVD): fit factor matrix U ∈ ℝ^{P×R} on training set, freeze, project test data.
- Co-skewness tensor S_{ijk} = (1/T) Σ_t z_i(t) z_j(t) z_k(t) for z-scored data. For our LLM setting: z_i(n) z_j(n) z_k(n) across N=500 problems, yielding S ∈ ℝ^{1536×1536×1536} — too large to materialize fully. Must use iterative HOSVD or randomized Tucker (tensorly library).
- Rank R=80 is the paper's working point; sweep R ∈ [40, 333] shows broad plateau above R=80.
- Log-Cholesky: Σ = LL^⊤, d_LC = √(||strict(L₁−L₂)||²_F + ||log diag(L₁) − log diag(L₂)||²₂), with εI regularization (ε=10⁻⁴).
- KRR evaluation: Pearson-correlation kernel, nested CV (20 reps × 10 folds), Nadeau-Bengio corrected t-test with BH-FDR.
- Code: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/E4C0/

If ∥S_correct∥ vs ∥S_incorrect∥ differ significantly, there's non-Gaussian structure that PH missed — stress-testing F-10.
  - Cached artifact: same NPZs
  - Runtime: ~15min CPU

Although applied to fMRI (not LLMs), the methodology transfers directly to our cached L19 prefill activations: our probe hierarchy (DoM = κ₁, cov-spectrum = κ₂) naturally extends to κ₃ via Tucker decomposition. The paper's finding that PCA (κ₂-maximizing) is consistently outperformed by Tucker (κ₃-maximizing) at matched dimensionality directly challenges whether our 0.7928 cov-spectrum AUROC is a true linear ceiling.

**Key claim we tested:** Third-order co-skewness tensor decomposition reveals task-relevant structure invisible to second-order covariance analysis, and Tucker basis outperforms PCA basis for downstream prediction.
